# 1．Introduction

With the rapid development of the new energy vehicle sector, power battery thermal safety has become a critical bottleneck restricting technological advancement.Lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) dominate the market due to their high energy storage capacity and excellent energy density-to-weight ratio [l],yet they face inherent safety hazards suchas overheating and fire risks when damaged or improperly used [2]. Operating optimally within $2 5 { \sim } 4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ to balance performance and lifespan [3],LIBs are prone to thermal imbalance during charging/discharging or under varying environmental conditions, leading to shortened service life,reduced efficiency,and even thermal runaway.

Precisely regulating battery temperature within the optimal range via thermal management systems thus becomes crucial for ensuring operational stability and mitigating safety risks.The core goal of BTMS is to keep batteries within their optimal operating temperature range while guaranteeing temperature uniformity both among individual cells and across entire battery packs [4].However,striking a balance between energy consumption and the system's cooling efficiency has become a technical bottleneck.Therefore，developing highly energy-efficient thermal management systems-ones that cut energy use to a minimum while upholding thermal safety for batteries—stands as a key direction for technological breakthroughs,which in turn boosts the market competitiveness of electric vehicles.

Thermal management technologies designed for electric vehicle batteries incorporate air cooling [5],liquid cooling [6,7],and phase change material (PCM) cooling [8,9]. Liquid cooling technology has emerged as the mainstream solution due to the cooling medium's characteristics such as high specific heat capacity,flexible structure,and controllable cost.Based on the contact relationship between the cooling medium and the battery,itis divided into direct cooling and indirect cooling [1O].For direct cooling by cell immersion,dielectric fluids are selected based on their thermal performance (e.g.,high heat transfer coeficient and high thermal stability)and safety characteristics (e.g., high dielectric strength,low toxicity，and non-flammability）[11]. Compared with the stringent requirements for insulation and safety in direct cooling,indirect cooling systems achieve a balance between heat dissipation efficiency and safety through physical isolation,making them the preferred choice for large-scale applications.

Internationally, research on integrating heat-generating batteries into cooling systems has advanced the development of electric vehicles through key technological breakthroughs,including innovations in flow channel designs,optimization of cold plate structures,and algorithm parameter tuning.Fan et al. [12] addresses the issues of overheating and temperature non-uniformity in LIBs via bionic fishbone-channel liquid-cooled plates,and designs four structures (D1-D4) for the cooling of large prismatic battery packs during 6C discharge. The symmetric single-inlet and double-outlet structure (D2) delivers superior cooling performance,outperforming the traditional Z-type plate.Orthogonal tests optimize the parameters of D2, enhancing temperature control and uniformity,reducing pressure drop,and thus improving the efficiency of battery thermal management. Wang et al.[13] investigated the effects of coolant inlet and outlet configurations on the thermal performance of battery modules,and found that the configuration with alternately arranged water inlets and outlets on a single side of the cooling plate achieved the optimal cooling efficiency. Employing a surrogate-assisted method to construct an optimization model and integrating the multi-objective genetic algorithm (MOGA) for optimizing channel parameters and water flow velocity, post-optimization results show reductions in the battery module's maximum temperature, average temperature, and the channel's maximum pressure. Among these improvements,the maximum pressure is reduced by $1 3 . 2 8 ~ \%$ ，thereby enhancing the system's heat dissipation efficiency while lowering its energy consumption. Yang et al. [14] investigated a novel liquid cooling system with taper-type manifold channels dedicated to lithium-ion battery thermal management, comparing rectangular and taper manifold structures with different channel passes.Findings indicate that the three-pass taper structure exhibits superior cooling performance, with an $8 6 . 3 ~ \%$ reduction in power consumption compared to the baseline. Delayed cooling is unsuitable as it causes large temperature differences in a short time. Xin et al.[15] developed a hybrid BTMS integrating liquid and air cooling, using axially distributed heat conducting blocks (HCBs) for liquid cooling and air cooling at edges. Studying HCB number,size,flow rate, etc.,optimization (3 HCBs, 6 mm channel diameter, $0 . 0 0 2 ~ \mathrm { k g } / s$ flow, $1 \ m / s$ wind speed） significantly reduced maximum temperature and temperature difference,improving uniformity.Xu et al. [16] conducted a comparison of serpentine and U-shaped liquid cooling channels for battery heat disspation,identifying serpentine channels as superior.Optimization of the serpentine channel via an adaptive ensemble of surrogate models lowered the maximum temperature difference by $7 . 4 9 \%$ ,yielding more uniform temperature distribution and offering guidance for battery liquid cooling design. Xie et al. [17] develops and refines a liquid cooling structure.First, CFD simulations explore how cooling plate parameters-tube diameter, interval, thickness,and arrangement—influence battery pack heat dissipation. Based on single-factor analysis results,a four-factor,three-level orthogonal array is built for single-objective optimization,targeting minimum maximum temperature.Experiments verify the simulation model. To reduce cooling plate mass, multi-objective optimization (minimizing maximum temperature and mass） is conducted.Compared with single-objective optimization,multi-objective optimization sees a 10.9 $\%$ rise in maximum temperature but an $8 2 . 4 ~ \%$ drop in cooling plate mass.Qian et al.[18] explored a lithium-ion battery liquid cooling system built on mini-channel cold plates,analyzing the impacts of the number of channels and coolant flow rate,direction,and width on battery pack thermal performance.Results showed a 5-channel cold plate suffices for cooling; increasing flow rate significantly reduces temperature.When compared to Design 1,Design 2 decreased the maximum temperature by $1 3 . 3 ~ \%$ and the temperature difference by $4 3 . 3 \%$ ,while achieving better temperature uniformity. Feng et al. [19] developed a honeycomb liquid cooling module for cylindrical batteries, establishing a 3D thermal model via ANsYS and validating it experimentally. An improved structure with deflectors was proposed,and 3D temperature distributions were examined as a function of coolant temperature and flow rate.Results confirmed the model's correctness and structural rationality,the temperature distribution within the battery pack is markedly influenced by the temperature and flow rate of the coolant, requiring proper control. Liao et al. [2O] applied nanofluids to liquid-cooled BTMs for 18650 lithium batteries,determining the Cu water-based nanofluid to have the best cooling effect—this reduced the maximum temperature difference of the battery pack by $1 2 . 6 \%$ and the maximum temperature by $1 . 0 6 6 \mathrm { ~ K ~ }$ in comparison to water.Analysis showed increasing nanofluid volume fraction and flow rate reduced temperature difference and maximum temperature but increased pressure drop；lower nanofluid temperature significantly cooled but damaged uniformity.Jarrett and Kim [21] conducted parametric modeling and CFD analysis of serpentine channel cooling plates,opti-mizing channel width and position. Results revealed that a single design could meet the combined objectives of pressure drop and average tem-perature,though this came at the cost of temperature uniformity. Optimization convergence was affected by initial designs,with multiple local optima showing similar performance but different geometries. Deng et al.[22] evaluated the cooling efficiency of a cold plate-based liquid-cooled battery pack., while examining the system's dependence on coolant flow rate, the number of cold plates,channel distribution, and cooling direction. Results demonstrated that a flow rate of $1 \ g / s$ was well-suited for heat dissipation—specifically,the maximum temperatures measured after 3C and 5C discharge stood at $2 7 . 6 7 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and 32.17 ${ } ^ { \circ } \mathbf { C } ,$ ,respectively.More channels in the middle (e.g.,Design 532) yielded best cooling，and reasonable cooling direction effectively improved temperature distribution uniformity.

Existing liquid-cooled channel designs have developed various typical schemes with remarkable progress:bionic fishbone channels improve cooling uniformity under high discharge rates via bio-inspired optimization but suffer from local pressure drop due to branched structures; serpentine channels achieve efficient heat dissipation through continuous flow paths yet face large temperature gradients, high sensitivity to flow velocity changes,and single-dimensional parameter optimization; tapered manifold channels reduce energy consumption but are limited by complex variable cross-sectional design, high processing costs,and poor scalability for mass production.By contrast,the honeycomb flow channels adopted in this study offer unique academic advantages: the uniform hexagonal units provide suf-ficient heat transfer area and symmetric flow paths to minimize stagnation zones and local eddies,enhancing temperature uniformity; their periodic design balances flow resistance stability and processability, enabling easier large-scale application than tapered manifolds;unlike existing single-dimensional optimization studies on honeycomb channels,this work focuses on the multi-parameter coupling effects of channel width,height,and period.

In this study,a honeycomb cooling flow channel was elaborately designed,and the heat transfer performance of the honeycomb cold plate was analyzed. The coupling laws of key honeycomb channel parameters on heat dissipation efficiency,temperature uniformity,and energy consumption were systematically revealed. Orthogonal experimental design was employed to optimize the structural arrangement of the honeycomb flow channels,and range analysis was conducted for each structural factor to determine the honeycomb flow channel structure with optimal comprehensive performance.

# (3) Boundary Conditions

The computational model includes three solid domains, namely the battery pack,heat sink,and cold plate;and one fluid domain,which is $5 0 \%$ ethylene glycol aqueous solution. All computational domains have the same initial temperature as the ambient temperature,which is set to 298.15 K.The contact surface of the battery pack with ambient air serves as a natural convection boundary,featuring a heat transfer coefficient of $5 \mathrm { W } / ( \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 } { \cdot } \mathrm { K } )$ .Regarding the fluid domain, the inlet adopts a velocity inlet condition,while the outlet uses a pressure outlet condition.

# 2.3.Mesh independence validation

After opening the 3D model of the cold plate in ANsYS SpaceClaim, the closed fluid domain was extracted.Subsequently,the inlet/outlet boundaries of the fluid domain, the cold plate wall,and the solid domain identifier were named separately to complete the geometric preprocessing.The model was imported into ANSYS Fluent Meshing with the Watertight Geometry workflow selected.First, surface meshing was performed: the global base surface mesh size was set,and local size refinement was applied to the honeycomb flow channel region.After generating the surface mesh,a prismatic boundary layer was added to the cold plate wall to accurately capture the near-wall flow.Then, the full polyhedral mesh type was chosen for volume meshing，and curvature-adaptive refinement was enabled for regions with sudden curvature changes such as channel corners.Finally,the mesh was generated.

Fig.2 presents the grid independence verification of the simulation model.The figure illustrates four sets of grid numbers, namely 620,000, 1.81 million, 3.21 million,and 5.O5 million. Compared with larger grid numbers, those with fewer grids show significant deviations.However, once the grid number reaches a certain threshold,the curve stabilizes and approachesa linear trend.Therefore,to guarantee the precision and consistency of the results,a computational grid with approximately 3.21 million elements was ultimately selected.

Based on the hydraulic diameter $D _ { \mathrm { h } }$ ,fluid density $\rho _ { l } ,$ velocity $\nu ,$ and dynamic viscosity $\mu _ { l }$ of the coolant,we calculated the Reynolds number $\begin{array} { r } { R e = \frac { \rho _ { l } \nu D _ { \mathrm { h } } } { \mu _ { l } } } \end{array}$ for all simulation cases, which is far below the critical value of 2300 for laminar-turbulent transition.Therefore,we determine that the flow state is laminar flow.

# 3.2.Effects of the width of the cold plate channel

The channel period is $N = 2$ ,and the channel height is $H = 2 ~ \mathrm { m m }$ Different channel widths W are $4 \ m \mathrm { m }$ ， $5 \ \mathrm { m m }$ ， $6 \mathrm { \ m m }$ ，and $7 \ \mathrm { m m }$ ， respectively,and different inlet flow velocities $\nu$ were studied for verification.

Fig.5 shows that increasing channel width has a positive effect on improving cooling performance: the pack's maximum temperature, maximum temperature difference,and the pressure drop all decrease with increasing width.However,the rate of decrease for each indicator slows down gradually as the width increases,indicating the existence of an optimization threshold,where excessive widening of the channel leads to diminishing marginal returns.Widening the channel expands the flow cross-sectional area,reducing local velocity gradients and suppressing velocity boundary layer separation to improve flow uniformity and temperature consistency.Meanwhile,the increased hydraulic diameter lowers pressure drop (laminar flow resistance is inversely proportional to hydraulic diameter).For heat transfer,the expanded area enhances effective heat exchange,and reduced flow velocity (at constant volumetric flow） slows coolant temperature rise. However,exceeding a width threshold weakens fluid disturbance and saturates heat exchange area growth, diminishing marginal cooling improvements. In terms of maximum temperature,at an inlet flow velocity of $0 . 2 5 ~ \mathrm { m } / s$ ，when the channel width increases from $4 \mathrm { m m }$ to5 mm, the maximum temperature decreases by $3 . 2 \% ;$ when increased to $7 \mathrm { m m }$ ,the total reduction reaches $6 . 6 ~ \%$ ,The higher the flow velocity, the slower the rate of decrease.For example,at a flow velocity of $1 \mathrm { m } / s$ ， the total reduction when increasing from $4 \mathrm { m m }$ to $7 \mathrm { m m }$ is only $2 . 0 ~ \%$ Regarding the maximum temperature difference,ata flow velocity of $0 . 2 5 ~ \mathrm { m } / s$ ，the total reduction in maximum temperature difference is $1 6 . 9 \%$ when the width increases from $4 \mathrm { m m }$ to $7 \mathrm { m m }$ ; at a flow velocity of $1 \mathrm { m } / s$ ,the total reduction narrows to $3 . 8 \%$ ,reflecting the attenuation of the synergistic effect between flow velocity and width.At high flow velocities,the coolant already has strong disturbance capacity and high heat transfer efficiency.Thus,the improved flow uniformity and increased heat exchange area from wider channels exert a weak supplementary effect on cooling performance.For the pressure drop,at a flow velocity of $0 . 2 5 \mathrm { m } / s$ ,the total reduction is $1 5 . 3 \%$ when increasing from $4 \mathrm { m m }$ to $7 ~ \mathrm { m m }$ ；the difference in pressure drop reduction across different flow velocities is small ( $1 3 . 7 \ \%$ at $1 \ \mathrm { m } / \mathrm { s }$ ),indicating that the effect of width on pressure drop is weakly affected by flow velocity.

Fig.6 shows that the effect of increasing channel height on cooling performance follows a similar pattern to that of channel width: as height increases, the pack's maximum temperature,maximum temperature difference,and pressure drop all decrease continuously,but the rate of decrease gradually narrows. Compared to width,pressure drop is more strongly influenced by variations in height,indicating that height is a keyparameter for reducing flow resistance.This arises from the higher sensitivity of hydraulic diameter to height in rectangular channels: under the same dimensional change,increasing height boosts hydraulic diameter more significantly than widening width. Since laminar pressure drop is inversely proportional to the square of hydraulic diameter, flow resistance is reduced more notably.For heat transfer, greater height expands vertical flow space,reducing vertical temperature gradients and enhancing heat transfer uniformity.Reduced flow velocity (constant volumetric flow) also slows coolant temperature rise,though this effect diminishes with excessive height due to weakened disturbance.In terms of maximum temperature,at an inlet flow velocity of $0 . 2 5 \mathrm { m } / s$ ,when the height increases from $2 \ \mathrm { m m }$ to $3 \ \mathrm { m m }$ ,the maximum temperature decreases by $3 . 6 ~ \%$ ：when increased to $5 \mathrm { m m }$ ,the total reduction reaches $6 . 8 \%$ .The rate of decrease is slower at higher flow velocities:at $1 \ \mathrm { m } / \mathsf { s } ,$ ， the total reduction when increasing from $2 \mathrm { m m }$ to $5 \mathrm { m m }$ is only $2 . 1 ~ \%$ Regarding the maximum temperature difference,ata flow velocity of $0 . 2 5 ~ \mathrm { \ m } / s$ ，the total reduction in maximum temperature difference is $2 1 . 3 ~ \%$ when the height increases from $2 \mathrm { m m }$ to $5 \mathrm { m m }$ ,which is better than the improvement effect of width on temperature difference.For the pressure drop,ata flow velocity of $0 . 2 5 \mathrm { m } / s$ ,the total reduction reaches $5 1 ~ \%$ when the height increases from $2 \mathrm { m m }$ to $5 \mathrm { m m } ;$ it still reaches 41.2 $\%$ at $1 \ m / s$ ,indicating that increasing height is an effective means to reduce energy consumption.

# 3.4.Effects of the period of the cold plate channel

The channel width is $W { = } 4 \mathrm { m m }$ ,and the channel height is $H = 2 \mathrm { m m }$ Different channel periods $N$ are 2,3,4,and 5,respectively,and the models of the channels are shown in Fig.7.

Fig.8 shows that the effect of channel period $( N )$ on cooling performance exhibits differentiated characteristics: as the period increases, the pack's maximum temperature and the cold plate's pressure drop decrease monotonically,as the rate of decline progressively lessens; however, the maximum temperature difference displays a fluctuation pattern in “first decreasing,then increasing,then decreasing again", indicating that the improvement of temperature uniformity by the period is uncertain. The channel period denotes the number of hexagonal unit repetitions along the flow direction.More units optimize flow path symmetry,enabling uniform fluid spliting/merging,reducing local eddies and flow separation at honeycomb corners,and lowering pressure drop.For heat transfer,an extended effective heat exchange length allows the coolant to absorb more battery heat,reducing the maximum temperature.Temperature difference fluctuations stem from periodic flow direction changes (altered splitting/merging positions), but the hexagonal structure's inherent flow uniformity limits overall impact.In terms of maximum temperature,at an inlet flow velocity of $0 . 2 5 ~ \mathrm { m } / s$ ,when the period increases from 2 to 3,the maximum temperature decreases by $2 . 0 ~ \%$ ；when increased to 5,the total reduction reaches $3 . 2 \%$ ,and the rate of decrease remains basically stable at high flow velocities (e.g.,the total reduction is approximately $3 . 0 \%$ at $1 \textrm m /$ s).Regarding the maximum temperature difference,when the period increases from 2 to 3,the temperature difference decreases；when increasing from 3 to 4,it rises slightly;when increasing from 4 to 5,it decreases again.The overall fluctuation range is small (maximum variation $\leq 5 \ \% )$ ,implying the period exerts a limited influence on optimizing temperature difference.For the pressure drop,at a flow velocity of $0 . 2 5 ~ \mathrm { m } / s$ ，when the period increases from 2 to 5,the total reduction reaches $56 \%$ ，and this reduction remains basically stable across different flow velocities (still around $5 6 ~ \%$ at $1 \ \mathrm { m } / \mathrm { s }$ ,indicating that the period is one of the key parameters for reducing pressure drop.

# 4. Conclusions

For exploring the impact of coolant flow characteristics on battery thermal management, this study examines the laws governing their effects on cooling performance by varying the coolant inlet velocity $\nu _ { : }$ as well as the channel width W,height $H ,$ and period $N$ of the cold plate. Key conclusions are summarized as follows.

(1)Flow velocity at the coolant inlet has a notable effect on cooling performance.When flow velocity increases,the battery pack's maximum temperature,along with its maximum temperature difference,shows a monotonic decrease,and the cooling effect gradually improves,but the rate of temperature decrease slows down gradually with a tendency toward thermal saturation. Meanwhile,the pressure drop of the cold rises sharply with increasing flow velocity,resulting in a significant energy consumption cost.When flow velocity goes up from $0 . 2 5 \mathrm { m } / s$ to $1 \mathrm { m } /$ s,the pressure drop surges by $5 9 7 \%$ ,given cooling improvements often boost energy consumption,it's essential to balance the cold plate's cooling effect against its energy use.

(2) Increasing channel width has a positive effect on improving cooling performance. The pack'smaximum temperature, maximum temperature difference,and pressure drop all decrease with increasing width,yet the rate of decrease slows gradually, with an optimization threshold existing. Compared with high flow velocity conditions,width shows a more significant improvement on temperature and temperature difference under low flow velocity.Additionally,its impact on pressure drop is weakly affected by flow velocity,with the total reduction stabilizing at $1 3 . 7 \ \% - 1 5 . 3 \ \%$

(3) The impact law of channel height on cooling performance resembles that of channel width,but its effect is more pronounced. As height increases,the rate of decrease for all indicators gradually narrows.Moreover,the impact of height on pressure drop is far greater than that of width: at a flow velocity of $0 . 2 5 ~ \mathrm { { m } } / s ,$ when the height increases from $2 \mathrm { m m }$ to $5 \mathrm { m m }$ ,the total reduction in pressure drop reaches $5 1 \ \%$ ,making itakeyparameter for reducing flow resistance.Meanwhile,height also outperforms width in improving the maximum temperature difference,with a total reduction of $2 1 . 3 ~ \%$ under low flow velocities.

(4) The impact of channel period on cooling performance exhibits differentiated characteristics.As the period increases,the pack's maximum temperature and pressure drop both diminish in a monotonic manner, with the rate of decrease gradually slowing down.Ata flow velocity of $0 . 2 5 ~ \mathrm { m } / s$ ，when the period increases from 2 to 5,the total reduction in pressure drop reaches $56 \%$ ， making it an effective parameter for reducing energy consumption.However,the maximum temperature difference shows a fluctuating trend of “first decreasing，then increasing，then decreasing again",with a limited overall optimization effect and a maximum variation ${ \leq } 5 ~ \%$

(5) Through orthogonal design and range analysis,at an inlet flow velocity of $\nu = 0 . 2 5 \mathrm { m } / s$ ,the optimal parameter combination is determined as channel width $W = 7 ~ \mathrm { m m }$ ,height $H = 5 \mathrm { m m }$ ,and period $N = 5$ .Under this combination, the pack's maximum temperature of is $3 4 . 0 6 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ ,the maximum temperature difference is $7 . 2 8 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ ,and the pressure drop is $1 1 3 . 9 3 \mathrm { P a }$
