The low ion mass transfer rate on the negative electrode side of alkaline zinc-based flow batery accelerates the formation of zinc dendrites and causes serious concentration polarization.Through the intercalation of EDTA, the interlayer spacing of MgAl-LDH nanosheets is expanded,providing arapid transport pathway for large-size zincate ions.Simultaneously,the adsorption efect of EDTA enhances the process of $- \mathrm { O H }$ removal, thereby further promoting uniform zinc deposition.on the electrode surface.When employed as the negative electrode material ina battery,this material significantly improves the efficiencyand lifespanof the battery.Over 300 cycles of testing,the energy efficiency of the battery decreases by no more than $0 . 0 0 4 ~ \%$ per cycle,and zinc dendrite formation is markedly inhibited.These findings emphasize the importance of ion mass transfer rate on the negative electrode side and provide an important approach for zinc dendrite inhibition of alkaline zinc-iron flow batteries.

# 1．Introduction

In the context of the global energy structure transition,the development of energy storage technology has become particularly crucial [1, 2].Among the myriad of energy storage technologies, zinc-based flow batteries stand out due to their unique advantages, becoming one of the most important energy storage solutions [3]. Zinc-based flow batteries meet the demands of large-scale,long-duration energy storage with their highsafety，longlifespan，environmental friendliness，and cost-effectiveness [4,5].

In practical applications,one of the main factors hindering the largescale application of zinc-based flow batteries is their low current density, which leads to low power density of the battery stack, large system footprint,and high ancillary costs [6,7].High current density will promote the formation of zinc dendrites [8].Non-uniform deposition during the deposition process of zinc can form sharp dendritic structures. The presence of dendrites not only facilitates the formation of “dead zinc",leading to low utilization of zinc/zincate ions and capacity decline,but also,the tip of the dendrites has a stronger attraction to zinc/zincate ions,further intensifying the non-uniform distribution of zinc deposition. In severe cases,dendrites may even puncture the battery membrane,causing short circuits and affecting the safety and reliability of battery operation [9].

Therefore,researchers have conducted extensive studies on the dendrite issue of zinc-based flow batteries [1O,11].To prevent the formation of zinc dendrites, researchers have attempted to regulate the zinc deposition process through various methods.Porous electrodes,as an essential component of the battery,are the sites where electrochemical reactions occur and where zinc deposition occurs, significantly influencing the formation and growth of zinc dendrites.Researchers have sought to regulate the deposition process of zinc ions or zincate ions on the electrode surface through surface modifications [l2,13]. Common regulatory approaches include increasing the specific surface area to reduce local current density [14],increasing zinc nucleation sites to improve the uniformity of zinc deposition [15,16],and control the orientation of crystal planes [17].In the process of zinc deposition, the ion mass transfer rate significantly affects the supply of zinc ions at the electrode surface.Therefore,in addition to the methods mentioned above,researchers have also attempted to enhance the mass transfer rate of zinc ions on the electrode surface by constructing ion channels on the electrode surface to promote uniform zinc deposition. For example, Yang et al. used MgAl- Layered Double Hydroxide (LDH) to construct an artificial SEI on the electrode surface,utilizing interlayer channels to accelerate the transfer of zinc ions [18]. Cheng et al. etched Mxene into nanosheets with nanoscale ion channels to improve ion accessibility [19].The ion diffusion distance is reduced by shortening the ion diffusion distance.Lin et al. coated the electrode surface with a layer of perfluorosulfonic acid ion-exchange layer. The protective layer with rich sulfonic acid groups could simultaneously realize the entry of $S O _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - }$ and accelerate the transport of zinc ions,and induce the uniform deposition of Zn under the protective layer [2O]. Zhou et al. used vermiculite to construct negatively charged ion channels on the electrode surface. Under the influence of potential, zinc ions jumped back and forth between the negatively charged walls to achieve rapid transfer [21].While the aforementioned strategies have demonstrated promise in regulating zinc deposition,it is important to note that most, including organic ligand-based SEI layers,ion-exchange membranes,and znophilic coatings,are primarily designed and optimized for systems involving ${ \mathrm { Z n } } ^ { 2 + }$ ions.However,this paradigm is not directly transferable to alkaline zinc-based flow batteries,where the active species is the large,tetrahedrally coordinated zincate ion $( \mathrm { Z n ( O H ) } _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - } )$ . The critical challenges of its substantial size $( { \sim } 4 \ \mathring { \mathrm { A } } )$ and stable solvation shell require a fundamentally different interface design.For example,MgAl-LDH has interlayer channels with a size of only about $2 . 5 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ ，which cannot accommodate zincate ions.Therefore,for alkaline zinc-based flow batteries,it is necessary toconstruct ion channels with suficiently large sizes to regulate the mass transfer process of zincate ions on the electrode surface.

In addition to its large size,which restricts its entry into conventional ion channels,another drawback of the zincate ion is the presence of four hydroxyl groups $\left( - \mathrm { O H } \right)$ on its surface.These $- \mathrm { O H }$ groups form stable solvation structures through hydrogen bonding with water molecules, thereby affecting the uniformity of zinc deposition and causing severe hydrogen evolution corrosion [22]. During zinc deposition, the gradual detachment of these four $- \mathrm { O H }$ groups is required to allow the zinc ions to approach the electrode surface and undergo deposition.Researchers have also attempted to introduce specific interfacial layers [22] or electrolyte additives [23] to reduce the desolvation energy barrier, thereby achieving a more efficient zinc deposition process.However, the fixation and homogenization of these substances is also a challenge.

This study addressed the active material zincate ions in alkaline zincbased flow batteries by employing ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA)-intercalated LDH as an electrode interfacial layer (Fig.1). Unlike generic ${ \mathrm { Z n } } ^ { 2 + }$ -conducting layers or simple hydrophilic coatings, this strategy leveraged the tunable interlayerchemistryofLDH to create a confined nano-environment that addressed both the mass transfer and desolvation bottlenecks unique to zincate ions.EDTA was inserted into the interlayer galleries of LDH through an ion-exchange method.Since the main interaction between the interlayer material and the main layer iselectrostatic interaction,anions with a larger charge usually preferentially insert into the interior of LDH.EDTA usually exists as a 4- valent anion with four hydrogen ions removed in an alkaline environment.Therefore,its priority between LDH layers is significantly higher than other anions,and thus it is less likely to be replaced by other anions in the solution.The interlayer gallery spacing of LDH was expanded to a dimension sufficient to accommodate the entrance and transfer of the large scale zincate ions cluster,a feature absentin dense SEI layers or membranes.The EDTA within the channels also enhanced the adsorption effect on zincate ions-and facilitated the process of removing $- \mathrm { O H }$ ions from zincate ions.The ion channel accelerated the mass transfer of zincate ions at the electrode surface,reduced the concentration gradient atthe electrode surface,and improved the uniformity of zinc deposition. Utilizing this electrode material,the issue of zinc dendrite formation at the negative side of alkaline zinc-based flow batteries was significantly mitigated,and the efficiency of the battery was enhanced,with the cycle life also being further extended.This study provides an effective solution to the zinc dendrite problem in alkaline zinc-based flow batteries,promoting their furtherdevelopmentand application.

# 2．Results and discussion

In the anode of an alkaline zinc-iron flow battery,during charging, zincate ions from the solution undergo redox reactions at the electrode surface through mass transfer processes.The rate of these mass transfer processes directly affects the efficiency of the battery and the morphology of zinc deposition.In this work,carbon felt(CF）was employed as the original porous electrode substrate,onto a composite EDTA@LDH nanosheet structure.In the preparation process,conventional LDH nanosheets were initially synthesized via the co-precipitation method. Subsequently,an excess of EDTA was introduced into the solution,facilitating an ion-exchange process that replaced a portion of the original nitrate ions within the interlayer galleries of the LDHwith EDTA molecules,thereby intercalating them into the interlayer channels of the LDH nanosheets.Prior to treatment,the carbon fiber surface was essentially smooth,with no other substances covering it except fora few impurities (Fig.2b).After hydrothermal treatment,the carbon fiber surface was densely covered with LDH nanosheets,with most of the nanosheets standing upright and interlacing with each other (Fig.2c). Energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) analysis confirmed the elemental composition of the nanosheets as magnesium (Mg),aluminum (Al),and oxygen (O),which are uniformly distributed across the carbon fiber surface (Fig.2d).This elemental distribution aligns with that of the synthesized material, thereby validating the successful growth of LDH nanosheets.The morphology of individual EDTA@LDH nanosheets was captured using transmission electron microscopy (TEM),revealing that the nanosheets exhibited a regular hexagonal shape with neat edges and an approximate side length of around $5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ (Fig. 2e). The shape of the nanosheets is close to that of LDH (Fig. S1 and S2).

Furthermore,the zeta potential of the material was measured.The zeta potential of CF was found to be negative (Fig.3c). Upon adding LDH and EDTA,the zeta potential peak shifted to the right, transitioning from negative to positive.This shift may be atributed to the positive charge being carried by the LDH layers.Consequently,the modified electrode surface material is anticipated to enhance the adsorption effect on zincate ions,which are anions,through electrostatic attraction, thereby intensifying the mass transfer rate of ions at the electrode surface.

The hydrophilicity of three electrode materials was characterized by using contact angle measurements (Fig. 3d-f). The untreated CF material,GFo30,was sourced from Taiwan Carbon Energy Company.This material exhibited poor hydrophilicity,with droplets on the CF surface remaining largely spherical,resulting in a contact angle of $1 2 8 . 5 ^ { \circ }$ corresponding to an obtuse angle.The hydrophilicity of the CF was significantly enhanced after treatment, with all contact angles becoming acute. The contact angle of LDH-CF was reduced to $7 5 . 4 ^ { \circ }$ .Furthermore, the contact angle of EDTA@LDH-CF was even lower,at $4 3 . 1 ^ { \circ }$ .The enhanced hydrophilicity may be attributed to the presence of $- \mathrm { O H }$ groups on the layers of the LDH.The further enhancement of material hydrophilicity after EDTA intercalation into LDH was attributed to a core mechanism wherein the EDTA molecule,acting as a polydentate ligand,not only anchored itself to the positively charged LDH layers via electrostatic interactions through its four carboxylate groups,more critically,the introduction of these strongly hydrophilic groups fundamentally altered the physicochemical nature of the interlayer gallery. On one hand, the bulky molecular volume of the EDTA anion expanded the LDH interlayer spacing,creating more capacious nanochannels that reduced the energy barrier for water molecule diffusion and ingress.On the other hand,its concentrated carboxylate functionalities rendered the interlayer microenvironment highly polar,enabling the formation of an extensive hydrogen-bonding network with water molecules.This synergistic effect,arising from the molecular-level introduction of hydrophilic groups and the nanoscale modulation of the interlayer structure, collectively promoted the adsorption and wetting behavior of water molecules on both the material surface and within its bulk phase. For porous electrode materials,the improvement in hydrophilicity is beneficial for the thorough wetting of the electrolyte.The electrolyte can rapidly and completely wet the electrode surface,promoting the swift arrival of active ionsat the electrode surface,thereby accelerating the internal ion transport within the electrode.

Although a large amount of literature has shown that large-size ions entering the LDH interlayer could widen the size of the interlayer channel, further measurements are needed to verify whether zincate ions can successfully enter the enlarged interlayer_channel. X-ray diffraction (XRD) was employed to examine the crystallographic structure of the material and thus to investigate the impact of EDTA on the crystal structure of LDH nanosheets.The XRD results are depicted in Fig.4a. The LDH nanosheets exhibited a peak at $2 \theta = 1 1 . 7 4 ^ { \circ }$ ,corresponding to the (Oo3) plane,from which the interplanar spacing could be derived using Bragg's law.The interplanar spacing was determined to be $0 . 7 5 \mathrm { n m }$ ,and after subtracting the thickness of the layer plate at 0.48 nm, the height of the interlayer channels was only $0 . 2 7 { \mathrm { n m } }$ . The diameter of the zincate ion was approximately $0 . 4 \mathrm { n m }$ ,which was insufficient to penetrate the interlayer channels of the LDH nanosheets.Compared to LDH,the interlayer channels within the EDTA@LDH were expanded. EDTA molecules entered the interlayer channels through ion exchange, thereby enlarging the channel dimensions.The XRD results indicated that EDTA@LDH showed a peak at $2 \theta = 6 . 1 6 ^ { \circ }$ ,corresponding to the (003) plane that had been expanded by the EDTA molecules,with the interplanar spacing increasing to $1 . 4 3 \mathrm { n m }$ .After accounting for the layer plate thickness,the interlayer channels measured $0 . 9 5 \ \mathrm { n m }$ in height, which was ample for the smooth entry of zincate ions into the interlayer. The simultaneous presence of a peak near $1 2 ^ { \circ }$ ,attributable to the (003) plane of the pristine LDH,indicates that the intercalation process was not fully complete,leading to a mixture of intercalated and nonintercalated domains. Consequently，the interlayer channelsof EDTA@LDH could serve as rapid transport pathways for zincate ions. Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations also corroborated this conclusion (Fig. S3).Because of EDTA, the interlayer size of LDH was different between the two simulation models,as described above.The model for LDH built without EDTA is shown in Fig.4b.The model for LDH with EDTA is shown in Fig.4d.In the untreated LDH model, zincate ions could not enter the LDH interlayers during a 50 ns simulation, remaining only in the exterior of the LDH(Fig.4c). In contrast, in the model where the interlayer spacing of LDH was increased to $1 . 4 3 \mathrm { n m }$ by EDTA, zincate ions spontaneously diffused into the LDH interlayer channels within a 50 ns timeframe (Fig. 4e). This result indicated that the primary limitation for the passage of zincate ions through the LDH interlayers was the insufficient spacing,which prevented their transit.Therefore, zincate ions can successfully enter the LDH interlayer channels upon widening the spacing.

Although the insertion of EDTA changed the LDH main layer spacing, this change did not have a significant impact on the strength of the main layer.Because the dominant force stabilizing the EDTA within the LDH galleries is the strong electrostatic attraction between the anionic carboxylate groups of EDTA and the positively charged brucite-like layers.This interaction primarily involves non-covalent forces and is not expected to directly break or form covalent bonds within the $\mathbf { M g } / \mathbf { A l }$ framework of the layers themselves.The LDH host layers,composed of edge-sharing hydroxides of $\mathbf { M } \mathbf { g }$ and Al,are highly stable.The insertion of guest anions into the interlayer space is a fundamental characteristic of LDH materials.The system accommodates the large EDTA anion primarily through a physical expansion of the interlayer distance,rather than through a reconstruction of the covalent bonding network within the layers.

Inorder to verify the effect of the expanded layer spacing and EDTA on ion mass transfer and electrochemical process,the zinc deposition process was calculated by Density functional theory (DFT).In addition to expanding the interlayer spacing,EDTA also served as a chelating agent for zincate ions,enhancing their adsorption efficacy.DFT calculations confirmed the chelating effect of EDTA,demonstrating that the LDH layers with EDTA molecules exhibited the highest adsorption energy for zincate ions (-7.15 eV) (Fig. 4f).The adsorption of zincate ions on EDTA@LDH was further elucidated by combining diferential charge density with the projected density of states (PDOS) (Fig.S4).In the PDOS calculations,it was found that the d-orbital energy levels of zincate ions could overlap and resonate with the s and p orbital electrons of EDTA at energy levels below the Fermi level, indicating the existence of charge transfer and the formation of stable covalent bonds between them.This was corroborated by the differential charge density,where yellow regions represented charge accumulation and cyan regions represented charge depletion (Fig. $^ { 4 } { \bf g } )$ . It was evident from the figure that zincate ions lost electrons during the adsorption process, with electrons shifting toward the adjacent EDTA. The carboxylate groups (-COO) of EDTA acted as strong Lewis bases,coordinating directly with the $\mathrm { Z n ^ { 2 + } }$ center in the $\mathrm { Z n ( O H ) _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - } }$ complex. This coordination drew electron den-sity away from the Zn-OH bonds, effectively polarizing and weakening them. Simultaneously,the oxygen atoms of the EDTA carboxylate groups acted as potent hydrogen-bond acceptors.They competed for and form strong hydrogen bonds with the hydrogen atoms of the coordinated $- \mathrm { O H }$ groups in $\mathrm { Z n ( O H ) _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - } }$ ，This competition disrupted the original hydrogen-bonding network between $\mathrm { Z n ( O H ) _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - } }$ and its surrounding water molecules,thereby destabilizing the solvation shell and facilitating the deprotonation/dissociation of -OH. Upon calculation, it was discovered that when zincate ions were on the surface of EDTA@LDH, it became activated by accepting electron transfer from EDTA.In the differential charge density maps,the $- \mathrm { O H }$ groups near EDTA exhibited significant charge accumulation (yellow regions),suggesting that these -OH groups were the first to be activated.This could also be confirmed by the changes in the bond lengths of Zn-O bonds,with the distant Zn-O bond length being 1.91 $\mathring \mathrm { A }$ and the proximal ${ \mathrm { Z n } } { \mathrm { - } } 0$ bond length being stretched to $2 . 0 5 \mathring { \mathrm { A } } .$ ，The energy barriers for the dissociation process of the two $- \mathrm { O H }$ groups of zincate ions were calculated to substantiate this effect further (Fig. S5).In essence,EDTA acted as a multi-dentate chelator that “activates” the $\mathrm { Z n ( O H ) _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - } }$ ion by simultaneously attacking the Zn center and the -OH ligands,thereby significantly lowering the energy barrier for $- \mathrm { O H }$ dissociation. In order to assess the efficacy of EDTA in LDH, the interlayer spacing of LDH was expanded, and zincate ions were introduced into it. The results showed that the energy barrier for $- \mathrm { O H }$ group dissociation on EDTA@LDH was the lowest. This indicated that EDTA@LDH not only stably adsorbed zincate ions but also promoted the dissociation of its $- \mathrm { O H }$ ions,thereby accelerating the reduction process of the zincate ion on the electrode surface (Fig.S6). In alkaline zinc-based flow batteries,promoting the dissociation of $- \mathrm { O H }$ groups on zincate ions can not only make zincate ions more easily participate in redox reaction but also accelerate the electrode reaction kinetics and improve the charge and discharge rate and energy efficiency of the batteries.It can also avoid the conversion of zincate ions into non-electrochemically active $\mathrm { Z n O }$ or other by-products in an alkaline environment, resulting in the formation of a passivation layer on the electrode surface,which in turn produces uneven zinc deposition, thereby improving the coulomb efficiency of the battery.Therefore, the calculated results show that EDTA@LDH can not only stably adsorb zincate ions, but also promote the dissociation of $- \mathrm { O H }$ groups, significantly improve battery performance, enhance battery efficiency, and inhibit the formation and growth of zinc dendrites.

Finally,to demonstrate the impact of the interlayer ion channels on the transport rate of zincate ions,the diffusion coefficients of zincate ions on the surfaces of three differentmaterials were calculated at $3 0 0 \mathrm { K }$ (Fig.4h).The results demonstrated that the interlayer channels facilitated the diffusion of zincate ions,with the diffusion coefficient increasing by 10 (from $0 . 1 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } / s$ to $1 . 5 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } \mathrm { ~ c m } ^ { 2 } / s )$ . Moreover, the addition of EDTA was found to be more conducive to the enhancement of the diffusion coefficient. The EDTA@LDH model exhibited the highest diffusion coefficient for zincate ions.Because of high adsorption energy，zincate ions were efficiently and strongly adsorbed by the EDTA sites when they encountered them within the interlayer.For high diffusion coefficient,the expanded interlayer structure ensured that between these strong adsorption sites,the ions experienced a low-resistance,hydrophilic environment.This allowed them to diffuse rapidly along the two-dimensional channels from one site to another,or from the channel entrance to the deposition site on the

electrode surface.

In summary, the calculation results indicated that when EDTA molecules are intercalated into the interlayers of LDH,the intercalated molecules and the increased interlayer spacing alter the surface properties of the material,enhance the capture and diffusion processes of zincate ions on the material surface,and accelerate the reduction of zincate ions.Based on the previously mentioned results such as the improvement of hydrophilicity,the mass transfer rate of zincate ions on the electrode surface has been comprehensively enhanced,and it is expected to significantly improve the morphology of zinc deposition.

In the context of electrochemical performance assessment, various electrode materials were secured within a three-electrode test system. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) was employed to evaluate the impedance characteristics of these materials (Fig.5a).The Nyquist plots of these electrode materials consisted of a semicircle and a linear segment. The diameter of the semicircle in the low-frequency region represented the charge transfer impedance at the interface. Compared to CF,the larger semicircle diameter of LDH-CF indicated a higher charge transfer impedance,likely due to the inability of zincate ions to penetrate the interlayer channels of LDH, thereby introducing an additional layer of charge transfer impedance.Among the materials tested,EDTA@LDH-CF exhibited the smallest semicircle diameter in the low-frequency region，demonstrating the lowest charge transfer impedance.Furthermore,its linear segment in the high-frequency region had the steepest slope,indicating the lowest ion diffusion impedance.The EIS results suggested that among all the electrode materials tested,EDTA@LDH-CF had the fastest ion transfer rate at the surface, which could lead to lower polarization losses and higher efficiency in batteries.The specific equivalent circuit fitting data are supplemented in the supplementary information,as shown in Table S3.

The nucleation overpotential of the electrode materials was also measured (Fig. 5b). In a $0 . 1  { \mathrm { M } }  { \mathrm { Z n O } }$ solution, the nucleation overpotential of EDTA@LDH-CF was only $5 6 \mathrm { m V }$ ,which was the lowest compared to other materials.A lower nucleation overpotential implied that this material had smaller nucleation sizes and more uniform nucleation during the zinc deposition process,reducing the likelihood of excessive non-uniform zinc deposition and inhibiting the formation of zinc dendrites.The zinc deposition morphology on the fiber surface is related not only to the nucleation overpotential of the electrode material but also to the crystal plane orientation during zinc deposition.The CF of the cathode was extracted and cleaned at the end of charging for XRD analysis (Fig.5c).The XRD results indicated that the proportion of the (002) crystal plane on the surface of EDTA@LDH-CF was the highest, demonstrating that EDTA@LDH-CF could induce a greater proportion of zinc deposition on its surface with the (Oo2) crystal plane,contributing to a flatter and more uniform zinc deposition morphology.

To verify the impact of EDTA@LDH on the zinc deposition process, scanning electron microscope (SEM) was used to capture the surface morphology of the electrode after zinc deposition (Fig.5d-f).The electrode was subjected to a constant current density of $1 0 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for $1 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ . Zinc exhibited a more heterogeneous aggregation within the interior of CF,forming large agglomerates between fibers.Upon magnification, the micromorphology of deposited zinc was observed to be predominantly sharp,forming protrusions on the fiber surface.This sharp zinc deposition morphology implied a higher propensity for dendrite formation during prolonged deposition processes,which could pierce the battery membrane,thereby afecting battery safety.In contrast, zinc deposition on the surfaces of LDH-CF and EDTA@LDH-CF primarily occurred between LDH nanosheets,with minimal large zinc agglomerates presented between the fibers.According to the results of DFT calculation,this was mainly due to the enhanced adsorption of zincate ions on the electrode surface,and more nucleation sites guided zinc deposition on the electrode surface.After the deposition of zinc on the fiber surface of LDH-CF,the original morphology of the LDH nanosheets underwent significant alterations.Although the zinc accumulated on the electrode surface,the zincate ions could not enhance the mass transfer through the interlayer channel (see Fig.4c),and the zinc on the electrode surface still showed uneven deposition morphology.Moreover, the zinc deposition was sharp and covered the original nano-sheet on the surface of carbon fiber. In contrast,the morphology of zinc deposition on the fiber surface of EDTA@LDH-CF was more uniform, with zinc being more evenly distributed within the interlayer space of the nanosheets.The deposited zinc was comparatively flat. EDS analysis further confirmed that the change of fiber surface morphology was caused by zinc deposition. As shown in Fig. S7 of the element mapping, Zn element is evenly distributed on the fiber surface and in the nanosheet gap,which proves that during zinc deposition, zincate ion can be transferred to the bottom layer of LDH through interlayer channels, thus forming a bottom-up deposition topography.At the same time,this vertically aligned nanosheet separated the surface of the carbon fiber, preventing the formation of zinc clusters,resulting in a flatter,more uniform distribution of zinc.The uniform distribution of $\mathbf { M } \mathbf { g }$ and Al elements also proved that the MgAl-LDH on the surface of carbon fiber was not significantly affected during zinc deposition,and the element ratio of about 2:1 can still be maintained,which has good stability.EDs analysis showed that Zn was distributed uniformly on the electrode surface,which verified the flat morphology of zinc deposition.This morphology not only facilitated rapid zinc deposition and striping, reducing the formation of dead zinc,but also prevented dendrite generation,significantly enhancing the battery's safety and cycle life.

For the EDTA@LDH-CF electrode material, the enhancement of hydrophilicity enables the electrolyte to wet the electrode more quickly, and the zincate ions in the electrolyte can reach the electrode surface rapidly.Then,under the action of EDTAasa chelating agent, zincate ions can remove the -OH group and undergo adsorption,achieving rapid and uniform deposition. Owing to the more abundant ion supply and enhanced ion adsorption,the morphology of zinc deposition within the porous electrode was markedly improved,preventing the formation and growth of zinc dendrites.This electrode material holds promise for optimizing the performance and extending the lifespan of alkaline zincbased flow batteries.

To further validate the impact of EDTA@LDH-CF on the regulation of zinc deposition and its subsequent effects on battery performance, EDTA@LDH-CF was employed as the negative electrode material, specifically integrated into a single alkaline zinc-iron flow battery.The electrodes and membrane were sandwiched between two graphite plates with serpentine flow channels. Single cells equipped with varying electrode materials were subjected to charge-discharge testing at different current densities.The quantitative values of battery efficiencies across all samples and current densities were summarized in Table S4,which clearly demonstrated the superior and consistent performance of the EDTA@LDH-CF electrode.Compared to conventional CF materials,the voltage efficiency of a battery equipped with LDH-CF was not only not enhanced but even slightly lower (Fig.6a).This might be due to the inability of zincate ions to cross into the LDH layer,so this layerinterface between the electrode and the electrolyte added additional resistance to the transfer of charge,resulting in additional polarization loss.This was also consistent with EIS test results.The battery equipped with EDTA@LDH-CF exhibited a significant enhancement in voltage eficiency.This improvement was attributed to the accelerated mass transfer rate of zincate ions at the electrode surface.At a low current density of $8 0  { \mathrm { ~ m A ~ } }  { \mathrm { c m } } ^ { - 2 }$ ，concentration polarization hada negligible effect on the battery,hence no significant difference in voltage eficiency was observed between the two.However，as the current density increased, the role of concentration polarization became more pronounced,and the benefits of enhanced ion mass transfer became more evident, leading to an increasing disparity in voltage efficiency between the two types of batteries.Ata current density of 320 $\operatorname { m A } \operatorname { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ,this difference can be maximized to $8 . 2 ~ \%$ . To assess the influence of zinc deposition morphology, the battery underwent long-term charge-discharge cycling tests at an ultra-high current density of 320 mA $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ . The cyclic stability of the electrode material and the battery life were determined in this test.Due to the higher current density, zinc deposition became more uneven,and the issue of zinc dendrites became more pronounced. The battery equipped with only CF exhibited anomalies after just over 15O cycles.The battery voltage surged dramatically, and all eficiency metrics decayed rapidly (Fig. 6b), likely due to severe uneven zinc deposition leading to an increase in dead zinc and a significant deficiency in the supply of oxidized ions in the solution, resulting in battery over-polarization.The battery with LDH-CF also showed a similar phenomenon at a similar time. Under the same operating conditions,the battery equipped with EDTA@LDH-CF had lower charging voltage and higher discharging voltage,a result of its enhanced mass transfer efficiency of zincate ions and reduced battery concentration polarization.This is also the reason for its enhanced voltage efficiency (Fig. 6c). Furthermore,after undergoing a longer cycle of chargedischarge,the battery could still maintain stable operation without significant changes in its performance metrics (Fig. 6d). After 300 cycles,the battery was able to keep an energy efficiency of $5 3 . 8 \%$ ,with an average energy efficiency decay of only $0 . 0 0 4 ~ \%$ per cycle.Additional validation was performed to ensure the robustness of this result. The long-term cycling test was reproduced using a independently fabricated EDTA@LDH-CF electrode,which exhibited similarly high stability with an similar average decay rate (Fig.S9). In the long cycle process, the battery could always maintain a stable capacity and energy release (Fig.6e).The presented cycling durability under ultra-high current density conditions demonstrated a superior performance benchmark when compared to existing studies on AZIFBs,as benchmarked in Table S5.This robust performance under such demanding operational parameters provided strong evidence for the efficacy of the novel electrode material in effectively suppressing zinc dendrite formation. While absolute performance ceilings may vary due to differences in experimental design across the literature,the data obtained in this work convincingly demonstrated the material's exceptional cycle stability and its significant potential in addressing the critical issue of zinc dendrite growth.The results of the long-term cycling tests confirmed the regulatory effect of EDTA@LDH-CF on zincate ions.After the long cycle, the EDTA@LDH-CF electrodes were removed and rinsed,and SEM images were captured (Fig. S1O).The surface structure of carbon fiber remains largely intact, confirming the structural stability of this material during long cycles.On the one hand,by enhancing ion mass transfer to reduce battery concentration polarization and improve battery efficiency,and on the other hand,by reducing nucleation overpotential and improving the uniformity of zinc deposition,the formation of zinc dendrites and dead zinc was avoided, significantly enhancing the battery's cycle life.

# 3．Conclusion

This work has designed an electrode material composed of EDTAintercalated LDH,which utilized the expanded interlayer spacing to regulate the transport of zincate ions at the negative electrode of alkaline zinc-based flow batteries.The adsorption of EDTA enhanced the induction of zincate ions and promoted the dissociation process of $- \mathrm { O H }$ This will further promote the rapid deposition of zinc on the electrode surface.This electrode material not only effectively suppressed the formation and growth of zinc dendrites but also improved the efficiency and extended the lifespan of the battery.Ata current density of $3 2 0 \mathrm { m A }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ,the maximum voltage efficiency can be increased by $8 . 2 ~ \%$ .Over 300 cycles,the battery energy efficiency can be maintained with a loss of less than $0 . 0 0 4 \%$ per cycle. With continued research and development, this material can be optimized to meet the growing demands of energy storage technologies,contributing to a more sustainable and efficient energy future.

# 4.Experimental section

# 4.1． Materials

In this work,a series of reagents including potassium hydroxide (KOH), sodium hydroxide (NaOH),sodium ferrocyanide $( \mathrm { N a } _ { 4 } \mathrm { F e } ( \mathrm { C N } ) _ { 6 } )$ ， and zinc oxide $\left( \mathrm { Z n O } \right)$ ,were employed to formulate the electrolyte solution.Additionally,magnesium nitrate $( \mathrm { M g } ( \mathrm { N O } _ { 3 } ) _ { 2 } )$ ,aluminum nitrate $( \mathsf { A l } ( \mathsf { N O } _ { 3 } ) _ { 3 } )$ ,urea $\mathrm { ( C O ( N H _ { 2 } ) _ { 2 } ) }$ ,and EDTAwereutilized in the preparation of electrode materials.All aforementioned reagents were of analytical grade and were used without further purification.

# 4.2.Preparation of EDTA@LDH-CF

MgAl-LDH nanosheets were synthesized via the co-precipitation method.The LDH nanosheets intercalated with ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA@LDH) were prepared through an ion-exchange process.To prevent the dissolution of carbon dioxide,the preparation was conducted under a nitrogen atmosphere.Magnesium nitrate $( \mathrm { M g } ( \mathrm { N O } _ { 3 } ) _ { 2 } )$ and aluminum nitrate $( \mathsf { A l } ( \mathsf { N O } _ { 3 } ) _ { 3 } )$ were dissolved indeionized waterata molar ratio of 2:1,and the solution pH was adjusted to 11 using sodium hydroxide (NaOH) to generate LDH nanosheets.The reactants were thoroughlymixed and allowed to age at $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ .The solution pH was then adjusted to 5.5 using EDTA,and CF was immersed into the solution and aged at $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for an additional $^ { 8 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ .The CF,after the reaction,was rinsed with deionized water and dried in a vacuum oven to yield the final product, EDTA@LDH-CF.

# 4.3.Material characterization

The morphological and elemental mapping of the samples were examined using a ZEIss Sigma 30o SEM. The nanosheets’ morphology and diffraction patterns were captured using a high-resolution transmission electron microscope.The materials were characterized using the following instruments:XPS was performed on a Thermo Scientific K-Alpha XPS system,XRD was conducted on a Rigaku SmartLab SE diffractometer,and FTIR was carried out ona Thermo Fisher Scientific Nicolet iS20 spectrometer.

# 4.4.Electrochemical tests and battery tests

A three-electrode system was employed to perform cyclic voltammetry and EIS tests to investigate the electrochemical characteristics of the electrode materials.The working electrode was made of CF,the counter electrode was a platinum plate,and the reference electrode was a saturated calomel electrode (SCE),with a $0 . 1 \mathrm { ~ M ~ Z n O }$ solution serving as the electrolyte.The frequency range for EIS was selected from 200 kHz to $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { m H z } }$ .The test voltage was the open-circuit voltage and the amplitude of the AC disturbance voltage was $1 0 \mathrm { m V }$ In the alkaline zinciron flow battery,the catholyte consisted of $0 . 8 \mathrm { M } \mathrm { K _ { 3 } F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } } .$ while the anolyte was a $0 . 4 \mathrm { ~ M ~ Z n O ~ }$ solution.The electrode material was CF measuring $5 \ \mathrm { c m } \times 5 \ \mathrm { c m }$ ,and the separator was Nafion 211.The electrodes and separator were sandwiched between graphite plates engraved with serpentine flow channels.The battery underwent chargedischarge tests at various current densities $( 8 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ to $3 2 0 \ \mathrm { m A }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ to evaluate its rate performance.Additionally, two working currents, $8 0  { \mathrm { \ m A } }  { \mathrm { \ c m } } ^ { - 2 }$ and $3 2 0 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { \ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ，were selected for long-term charge-discharge cycles to assess the battery's cycling stability.The charge-discharge tests were conducted under constant current conditions,with the charging process controlled by time( $( 2 4 ~ \mathrm { m i n }$ at $8 0 ~ \mathrm { { m A } }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 \cdot }$ and the discharging process controlled by a cut-off voltage,which was set at $_ { 0 . 1 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ 、The flow rate of the electrolyte was controlled by a peristaltic pump,with varying flow rates corresponding to different current densities.The specific calculation method for the flow rate was determined in Ref.[27].Under the test conditions,the flow rate was set at $1 0 0 \mathrm { \ m L / m i n }$ .Furthermore,the battery was charged to different states of charge (SOC) and subjected to linear voltammetry scanning tests to obtain the polarization curves and power density curves.

# 4.5.Molecular dynamics simulations and density functional theory calculations

To investigate the movement of zincate ions, the GROMACS package was used to simulate their position change near the tunnel of LDH with or without EDTA.The starting configurations were generated with the PACKMOL software.The force fields for the ions in the aqueous solution were based on the intramolecular parameters derived from the AMBER force field.The Restrained Electro Static Potential charge was generated by Multiwfn.

Because of EDTA, the tunnel size of LDH was different between the two simulation models,as described above.The atoms were visualized in figures using the open-source visual MD.Periodic boundary conditions were used along all three axes.In this MD simulation,the nonbonding interactions between ions and channels include Coulomb interactions and van der Waals.The 12-6 Lennard-Jones (LJ) potential was used to describe the van der Waals interaction.The LJ parameters of LDH were taken from the CLAYFF force field.The NVT ensemble was used with a Nose-Hoover thermostat to maintain the temperature at $2 9 8 \mathrm { K } .$ A cut-off distance of $1 . 2 \mathrm { n m }$ was employed for the van der Waals term via direct summation.The long-range electrostatic interactions were computed via Particle Mesh Ewald method.A Fast Fourier Transform grid spacing of $0 . 1 6 \ \mathrm { n m }$ and cubic interpolation were used to calculate the electrostatic interaction in the reciprocal space.A cut-off length of $1 . 2 \ \mathrm { n m }$ was adopted in the calculation of electrostatic interactions in real space.The leapfrog integration algorithm was taken to solve the equations of motion with a time step of 2 fs.The system is simulated in the NVT ensemble for 5O ns after the process of energy minimization.

The computations were conducted using the Vienna ab initio Simulation Package based on the spin-polarized DFT.The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof exchange correlation functional among generalized gradient approximations was introduced to describe electron exchange.The projector augmented wave method was employed to characterize the properties of valence electrons.Brillouin zone was sampled by $3 \times 3 \times 1$ k-points for geometry optimization.Convergence conditions in the structure relaxation were set to residual energy and force less than $1 0 ^ { - 4 }$ eV and $0 . 0 2 \mathrm { e V } / \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$ ,respectively. $2 0 \mathring { \mathrm { A } }$ vacuum layer was set to avoid the interactions between the adjacent periodic sections. The cut-off energy was set to $4 0 0 ~ \mathrm { e V }$ for the structural optimization and self-consistent calculation.The DFT-D3 method was used to correct the van der Waals interactions.
