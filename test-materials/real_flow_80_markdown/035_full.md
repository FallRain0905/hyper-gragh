The performance of vanadium redox flow bateries (VRFBs) is decisively influenced by the electrode.The key properties of electrode,including surface activity,electrical conductivity,and mass transfer performance,often displayacontradictory trade-off relationship.Gradientdesign can effectively harmonize these critical properties of electrodes and achieve enhanced battery performance.In this study,graphite microparticles with several micrometers in size,are concurrntly introduced into the PAN-based electrospinning precursor solution. By controlling the content of graphite microparticles (GMPs）and employing stepwise electrospinning,carbon nanofiber electrode with gradient components and pore structures can be fabricated.The side with more GMPs and larger pore size interfaces with the bipolar plate and liquid inlet,enhancing masstransfer and reducing contact resistance. Conversely,the dense side without GMPs interfaces with the membrane,shortening the ionelectron transport path and protecting the membrane from damage caused by vertical fibers.The gradient structure is also advantageous for preventing fiber breakage and structural collpse that may result from an excesive amount of GMPs. The VRFB assembled with a gradient electrode demonstrated an energy efficiency $4 . 1 8 \%$ higher than that of the conventional battery,which verifies the feasibility of this gradient electrode design.

# 1．Introduction

The demand for clean, sustainable energy has increased over the past few decades,as the environment and climate have deteriorated.The development of effective energy storage systems has emerged as a crucial field of study,as global demand for energy rises and the urgency to switch from fossil fuels to renewable energy sources becomes more pressing [1,2]. Redox flow batteries (RFBs) are unique among various energy storage technologies,owing to their high energy efficiency, scalability,and potential for long-duration energy storage [3]. Because of their superior cycling stability and environmental benignity,vanadium redox flow batteries (VRFBs),which are seen as a promising technology for large-scale energy storage applications like grid-level energy storage and integration of renewable energy sources,have attracted a lot of attention [4,5].

Despite the appealing advantages of Vanadium RedoxFlow Batteries (VRFBs),their widespread commercial application is limited by their high costs.To mitigate these costs,operating the battery at high current densities has been proposed to minimize the usage of expensive active materials.However,an increase in operating current density is accompanied by an increase in battery polarization,leading to high overpotentials and reduced capacities [6-9].As a critical component of VRFBs,the electrode not only provides active sites for redox reactions but also influences ion and mass transport within the porous medium [10]．Therefore,a well-conceived electrode design is essential for reducing excessive cell polarizations and enhancing the overall performance of VRFBs.

Carbon-based materials,particularly carbon nanofibers (CNFs), have emerged as promising candidates due to their high electrical conductivity,large surface area,and tunable pore structures [11]. However, traditional carbon nanofiber electrodes are limited by low porosity and poor mass transfer,which restrict their electrochemical performance [12].Previous studies have shown that the performance of CNF electrodes can be improved by doping with heteroatoms or incorporating conductive particles (e.g.,graphite microparticles, GMPs)[13]. Nonetheless,the introduction of functional components within the CNF matrix often results in non-uniform current distribution and poor utilization of active sites.And there are contradictions in the key performance of the electrodes,where one aspect improves while another deteriorates.Additionally,the mechanical stabilityof these electrodes under the harsh operating conditions of vanadium redox flow batteries (VRFBs）isa concern [l4].Therefore,there is an urgent need for carbon-based electrodes with a large surface area and excellent mass transfer performance to improve electrode performance and increase the power density of VRFBs.Recently,there has been increasing attention paid to the regulation of the three-dimensional structure of porous carbon fiber electrodes.

Electrospinning is a powerful technique for fabricating nanofibers, particularly suitable for creating high-surface-area electrodes for energy storage applications.This method utilizes an electric field to stretch and thin a polymer solution,resulting in fibers with diameters ranging from micrometers to nanometers.Zeng et al.[15] regulated the diameter of Polyacrylonitrile (PAN) electrospun carbon fibers by adjusting the concentration of the PAN precursor solution，and indicated that increasing the fiber diameter could improve the mass transfer performance of vanadium ions without affecting the electrochemical activity of the electrode. Zhao et al.[16] enhanced the mass transfer performance and electrical conductivity of electrospun carbon nanofibers (ECNFs) by adding graphite carbon spheres to the electrospinning precursor solution,designing a highly porous carbon nanofiber electrode (PCNFs) with an internally loose structure [17,18].The process allows for precise control over fiber morphology and porosity,which are critical factors in determining the electrochemical performance of electrodesin vanadium redox flowbatteries(VRFBs).Therefore, electrospinning technology exhibits strong design flexibility,offering robust support for the preparation of carbon nanofiber electrode materials with synergistic performance [19].

The unique architecture and operational principles of flow batteries dictate stringent requirements for the composition and pore structure of electrode materials.Typically,the plate side-functioning as the electrolyte inlet/outlet terminus-necessitates large pores to facilitate deep electrolyte penetration and transport. Concurrently,this region demands relatively high electrical conductivity to minimize interfacial contact resistance between plates and electrodes [2O].These two requirements,however, frequently exhibit an inherent trade-off relationship.Achieving simultaneous enhancement of electrode conductivity alongside increased porosity is therefore critically important. Employing highly conductive graphite microparticles as spacers may effectively separate adjacent fibers,significantly reducing fiber-to-fiber contact resistance and electrode-to-bipolar plate interfacial resistance.This strategy concurrently improves both mass transport performance and electrical conductivity.Nevertheless,excessive graphite particle incorporation risks compromising fiber continuity.An appropriately engineered gradient structure may prove more advantageous for preserving the overall structural integrity of the fiber electrode [21].

To achieve the above-mentioned purposes,in this study,a novel gradient carbon nanofiber electrode featuring a longitudinal gradient composition and structure was developed.This was achieved by modulating the concentration of highly conductive graphite microparticles (GMPs） within a PAN-based electrospinning precursor solution and utilizing a multi-step continuous electrospinning technique, followed by subsequent heat treatment processes. The gradient structure design allows the two sides of the electrode to possess distinct compositions and properties,rendering it more appropriate for the typical structure of a flow battery.By contacting the side with high porosity and high carbon content with the bipolar plate,and the side with high density and no graphite particles with the separator,it is possible to effectively balance the mass transfer and charge transfer processes duringbatteryoperation， thereby furtherimprovingbattery performance.

# 3．Results and discussion

To identify variations in fiber structure with differing contents of GMPs,the morphologies of samples are captured using SEM,as shown in Fig.1.Each type of electrospun carbon nanofiber displays a threedimensional network structure.The fiber diameters of the original ECNFs electrode materials,which lack GMPs,exhibit a relatively uniform distribution,ranging from approximately $2 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ to $3 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ (Fig. 1 (al-a3)).Upon the introduction of larger-sized GMPs,the diameter distribution of the fibers becomes more uneven,with an increase in thicker fibers.This phenomenon may be related to the suspension and dispersion of GMPs in the electrospinning solution,which makes the spinning process more irregular. Fibers in the vicinity of graphite particles are more prone to adhere together and form bundles.Moreover, the pores around the graphite particles become larger,a result of the larger size of the graphite particles providing support for the surrounding nanofibers (Fig.1(bl-c3)).Carbon nanofiber electrodes with larger pores are more conducive to electrolyte penetration.It can be anticipated that adding more graphite particles within an appropriate concentration range will offer greater support for the fibers,resulting in more voids.Consequently,by adjusting the content of graphite particles in the precursor solution and performing continuous layer-by-layer electrospinning,carbon nanofiber electrode materials with a gradient structure in the thickness direction can be achieved.Furthermore, the surface of electrospun fibers containing graphite particles is rougher. This may be due to the release of some graphite nanoparticles from the ultrasonically dispersed electrospun precursor solution during the process,which then adhere to the surface of the fibers.Following subsequent heat treatment, these particles are released.A rougher surface may provide a larger specific surface area,which is more beneficial for the electrochemical response process.

# 4.Conclusion

An integrated gradient carbon nanofiber electrode,designated as GG-ECNFs,was fabricated via a one-step continuous electrospinning process.This method circumvents interface defects inherent to multistep fabrication while ensuring the intrinsic continuity of the gradient architecture,thereby substantially enhancing electrode stability and reproducibility.The gradient design strategy promotes continuity within the internal electron conduction network of the electrode and concurrently minimizes the tortuosity of electrolyte penetration pathways.This approach synergistically optimizes both microstructure and macroscopic performance,achieving precise spatial alignment between material structure and electrochemical functionality.Experimental characterization reveals that the GG-ECNFs electrode exhibits compositional and structural gradation along the longitudinal axis,consequently demonstrating differentiated electrical conductivity and wettability profiles.When employed as the electrode material within a VRFB,the GG-ECNFs electrode manifests superior electrochemical reaction kinetics compared to conventional uniformly structured carbon nanofibers (14-ECNFs). The corresponding VRFB attained an energy efficiency of $8 0 . 3 7 \%$ at a current density of $2 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A c m ^ { - 2 } }$ ,representing a $4 . 1 8 \%$ enhancement over batteries assembled with conventional 14-
