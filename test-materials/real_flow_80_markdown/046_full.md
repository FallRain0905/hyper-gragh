All-soluble alliron redox flow bateries are considered a promising long-duration,large-scale energystorage technology due to their fully decoupled energyand power design and low-cost active materials.However,the anoderequires chelating ligands with electron-donating capabilities to establish a potential difference with ferocyanide,but ligand crossover results in their oxidation,which subsequentlycauses failureand restricts stable operation. This work proposes a method based on $K ^ { + }$ -induced aggregation of sulfonate groups in perfluorosulfonic acid membranes,eliminating the need for additional additives.By cycling the membrane through the flow battery process,the barier effect against anode ligands is enhanced,enabling stable cycling for 3227 cyclesat $8 0 \ \mathrm { m A } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ and ensuring long-term stability.Molecular dynamics simulations and cross-membrane diffusion experiments reveal the impact of different metal cations on the morphology of water channels in the membranes.A $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ -exchange membrane treated with $K ^ { + }$ from the electrolyte achieves a high average energy efficiency of $7 3 . 4 \%$ , maintaining battery stability. This work provides a simple yet effective strategy to improve the stability of all-soluble all-ron redox flow battries.By uncovering the underlying microscopic mechanisms through simulations,it paves the way for the practical implementation of this technology in large-scale energy storage systems.

# 1．Introduction

The expansion of renewable energy sources such as solar and wind power is essential for reducing carbon emissions and promoting sustainable development[1,2].However,their inherent variability presents challenges for grid stability as their capacity grows.Redox flow batteries (RFBs) offera promising solution due to their safety,scalability,and flexibility [3-8].Among RFBs,vanadium redox flow batteries (VRFBs) are the most commercially advanced,yet their high-cost limits widespread adoption [9].In comparison,iron-based flow batteries,such as zinc-ferrocyanide and hybrid all-iron systems,utilize the low cost of iron to significantly reduce upfront expenses [1O-14]. Despite this advantage,the precipitation of active materials on the anode hinders the decoupling of energy capacity and power.By modifying chelating ligands on the anode to lower the redox potential during the trivalent-to-divalent conversion of iron,and pairing it with ferrocyanide on the cathode,an all-soluble,all-iron flow battery with complete decoupling at both electrodes can be developed [15-19]. This innovation could position iron-based batteries as a cost-effective alternative to VRFBs for large-scale energy storage.

Recent research has concentrated on the development of various chelating ligands for all-soluble,all-iron redox flow batteries.Yan et al. were the earlier to develop an alkaline al-soluble,all-iron system using the Fe(III/II)-triethanolamine (TEA) complex $( - 0 . 8 6 \ : \mathrm { V }$ vs.SHE)and the Fe(III/II)-cyanide complex $\mathrm { ( F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } } ^ { 3 \mathrm { - } } / \mathrm { F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } } ^ { 4 \mathrm { - } }$ ,0.48 V vs. SHE)[17].Fe (TEA) has attracted significant attention due to its favorable redox potential, fast electrochemical kinetics,and low cost [2O].However,the absorption of alkylamines by cation exchange membranes leads to ligand crossover,resulting in rapid oxidation by $\mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { \mathsf { s } } { } ^ { 3 \mathrm { - } }$ under alkaline conditions,causing capacity fade and eventual battery failure [21-23].To mitigate this issue,researchers have developed TEA-derived ligands with increased steric hindrance,such as 3-[bis(2-hydroxyethyl) amino]-2-hydroxypropanesulfonic acid (DIPsO),triisopropanolamine (TIPA)，andbis(2-hydroxyethyl)amino-tris(hydroxymethyl)methane (BIS-TRIS) [24-29].However, these derivatives not only increase costs but also reduce electrochemical kinetics,risk contamination of proton exchange membranes,and consequently raise membrane resistance [15].Ligands like nitrilotris(methylphosphonic acid) (NTMPA),N,N-bis (phosphonomethyl)glycine (BPMG),and tetra-sulfonated ethylenediamine derivatives (EDTS) partially alleviate crossover issues by introducing weaker electron-donating groups,but they still result in lower output voltage compared to TEA [19,21]. Thus,TEA remains one of the most promising anode ligand candidates,although strategies to reduce ligand crossover are needed.Among these,the development of cation exchange membranes resistant to TEA holds significant potential.

Notably,the unique operational characteristics of redox flow batteries,where electrolyte cations continuously interact with ion exchange membranes,provide a natural platform to explore strategies for mitigating ligand crossover and enhancing membrane performance.This cyclical process offers opportunities to enhance membrane performance, enabling long-term operation without additives.In fuel cell research, cations such as $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ ， $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ ， $\mathbf { M } \mathbf { g } ^ { 2 + }$ ，and $\mathrm { { C a ^ { 2 + } } }$ have been studied as contaminants that disrupt proton conduction channels,forming water clusters that increase activation energy for ionic conductivity [3O-34].For example, $\mathsf { M } \mathsf { g } ^ { 2 + }$ can react with sulfonic acid groups on proton exchange membranes,forming new sulfonate structures that degrade membrane performance [32].Cation contamination also increases membrane resistance,impairs mass transfer,and reduces electrochemical active surface area (ECSA),ultimately harming fuel cell performance [30]. However,in alkaline all-soluble,all-iron redox flow batteries,these cations are not contaminants but are integral to the electrochemical circuit (Fig.1).Thus,their role in RFBs should be reconsidered, particularly in terms of their dynamic behavior within the membrane's pore structure. Optimizing the design of cation exchange membranes to improve their ligand-blocking properties could offer new strategies for enhancing the long-term operation of all-iron redox flow batteries.

Herein,we propose a novel approach to improve the stability of allsoluble all-iron redox flow batteries by leveraging $K ^ { + }$ -induced aggregation of sulfonate groups in perfluorosulfonic acid membranes.This method eliminates the need for additional additives，providing a straightforward solution to prevent ligand crossover and oxidation, which are common challenges for anode ligands.Through repeated cycling in the flow battery system,the membrane's barrier effect is progressively enhanced,enabling the system to maintain stable operation for over 3200 cycles at $8 0 \ \mathrm { m A } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ ，Molecular dynamics simulations and cross-membrane diffusion experiments further elucidate the influence of metal cations,particularly $K ^ { + }$ ,on the morphology of the membrane's water channels.A $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ -exchange membrane treated with $K ^ { + }$ from the electrolyte exhibits a high average energy eficiency of 73.4 $\%$ ,ensuring long-term stability of the battery.This work not only advances the understanding of membrane-enhanced stability mechanisms but also offers a simple and effective strategy to enhance the performance of all-soluble all-iron redox flow batteries,bringing them closer to commercial viability.

# 2.8.Computational methods

The density functional theory (DFT) calculations were performed using Gaussian 16,employing the $6 { - } 3 1 1 \mathrm { G } ^ { \ast }$ basis set with the B3LYP functional,and geometry optimizations were carried out with the DFT-D3(BJ) correction [36-4O].Based on the optimized molecular structures,the Independent Gradient Model based on Hirshfeld partition (IGMH) and Electrostatic Potential (ESP) analyses were performed using Multiwfn 3.3.8 and VMD 1.9.3 to visually elucidate the interaction strength and active sites between impurity cations and the Nafion membrane [41-44]. To account for the basis set superposition error (BSSE),single-point energy calculations of the complexes were performed using the aforementioned basis set and method.Harmonic frequency analyses were conducted to confirm the absence of imaginary frequencies in all optimized structures.The binding energy calculation formula is as follows:

$$
\mathrm { E = E _ { N a f i o n + c a t i o n } - E _ { N a f i o n } - E _ { c a t i o n } + E _ { B S S E } }
$$

The Nafion chains used for molecular dynamics simulations consist of four subunits (Supplementary Figure 3) with a regular polymer structure,where the side chains are evenly spaced along the main chain. The equivalent molecular weight is $1 1 0 0 \mathrm { ~ g ~ m o l ^ { - 1 } }$ ,which is the most commonly studied model [45].A bilayer Nafion membrane model comprising 36 monomers per layer was constructed using the Packmol packages [46].The water content of the membrane was set to $\lambda = 1 0$ ,as shown in Supplementary Figure 4. Outside the membrane, $1 0 0 \ \mathrm { N a C l }$ (or KCl) molecules and 2880 water molecules were randomly placed in the simulation box to model the impact of impurity cations on the membrane.The potential model for Nafion is derived from the DREID-ING force field,as referenced in previous studies [47,48]. The water molecules are modeled using the SPC three-point water model,while ions are represented by the merz force field corresponding to the water model [49].The simulations are conducted using the GROMACS software package，with temperature and pressure controlled by the V-rescale thermostat and the Parrinello-Rahman barostat,respectively [50-52]. The simulation employs a time step of 1 fs.After energy minimization using the steepest descent algorithm,equilibrium molecular dynamics (MD) simulations are performed to allow the system to reach a stable state.Finally,production MD runs are carried out under NPT ensemble conditions at $3 0 0 \mathrm { K }$ and 1 bar for 1O ns,with results from the last 2 ns used for data analysis presented in this study.

# 3.1.2. Failure mechanisms and optimization strategies

Insights into the causes of battery failure were gained from electrode analysis.XRD and SEM tests were performed on the electrodes after cycling.Fig.3a and Fig.3b show that iron deposition occurred at the anode with Nafion-Na,while no deposition was observed at the cathode with Nafion-K. This phenomenon is attributed to Nafion-Na's stronger adsorption of triethanolamine compared to Nafion-K,resulting in unstable coordination at the anode.The migration of triethanolamine to the cathode leads to its reaction with ferrocyanide,which is oxidized, initiating iron deposition and reducing coulombic eficiency.

In the $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ electrolyte,a different failure mechanism emerges.Potassium ferrocyanide,with its lower solubility $( 0 . 7 6 ~ \mathrm { M } )$ , undergoes crystallization due to water migration from the cathode to the anode during cycling.Evidence of this is shown in Fig.3c,where crystalline deposits lead to passivation and capacity decay.Sodium ferrocyanide's solubility is even lower $( 0 . 5 6 \mathrm { M } )$ ,exacerbating its susceptibility to crystallization. SEM imaging of the cathode surface in Fig.3d reveals significant crystalline growth,underscoring this issue.

The stability of the electrolyte is highly sensitive to pH variations.As shown in Supplementary Figure 5,when the anolyte $\mathtt { p H }$ is adjusted using different NaOH/KOH concentrations,an excessively low pH induces $\mathrm { F e ( O H ) } _ { 3 }$ precipitation,causing the electrolyte to become turbid and unusable.Even at a NaOH/KOH concentration of $^ { 1 \mathrm { ~ M ~ } }$ ，chelation instability persists (Supplementary Figure 6a), leading to iron deposition on the glassy carbon electrode and significantly reducing reversibility.This instability arises from insufficient alkaline conditions to maintain strong coordination between iron ions and their ligands.To address this issue,we optimized the electrolyte composition to $2 \textbf { M }$ NaOH/KOH,which provides a sufficiently alkaline environment to stabilize iron chelation while preventing precipitation.The effectiveness of this optimization is demonstrated by the improved cycling stability shown in Supplementary Figure 6b For the catholyte,however,a higher pH introduces another challenge: ligand exchange between OHand CN-.Based on previous studies and our experimental observations, excessive OH' concentrations can competitively displace CN- ligands, altering the coordination environment of ferrocyanide and accelerating its degradation.Therefore,achieving an optimal pH balance is crucial—not only to ensure robust chelation in the anolyte but also to minimize undesirable ligand exchange reactions in the catholyte [53, 54].

# 3.2.Mechanisms of cation-induced membrane enhancement

To investigate the influence of cations on membrane performance, the pretreatment process was analyzed in detail.Neutralization of sulfonic acid groups with hydroxide ions,followed by ion exchange with $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ or $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ ，produces Nafion-Na and Nafion-K membranes,as depicted schematically in Fig.4a.Swelling experiments,summarized in Supplementary Figure 8a,show that both membranes swell the most in pure water and the least in KOH solution. Nafion-Na exhibits greater swelling and electrolyte absorption, which correlates with its higher ion transport capabilities. Diffusion experiments (Supplementary Figures 9 and Supplementary Figure 1O) confirm faster diffusion of $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ compared to $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ ， contributing to reduced internal resistance and enhanced VE with Nafion-Na membranes.Additionally,ionic conductivity measurements of Nafion-X reveal that Nafion-Na exhibits a higher ionic conductivity $( 0 . 4 7 ~ \mathrm { m S / c m } )$ compared to Nafion-K $( 0 . 1 7 ~ \mathrm { m S / c m } )$ ,further confirming that the larger channels in Nafion-Na facilitate cation transport and contribute to reduced internal resistance in batteries. Unfortunately,due to its strong swelling properties,Nafion-Na exhibits a higher adsorption rate for the triethanolamine ligand in the anolyte,as shown in Supplementary Figure 8b This not only weakens the chelation stability of the anolyte but also facilitates the crossover of triethanolamine to the catholyte,ultimately resulting in redox failure of the catholyte.

To better understand these efects,an independent gradient model based on Hirshfeld partitioning (IGMH) was employed to examine the interactions between cations and membrane side chains (Fig.4b).The analysis highlights stronger interactions between $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ and $\cdot S { 0 3 } ^ { - }$ groups, indicated by darker isosurfaces at the bond critical points.Density functional theory (DFT) calculations further reveal binding energies of $- 1 2 5 . 6 3 \mathrm { k c a l m o l ^ { - 1 } }$ for $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ and $- 1 1 0 . 5 2 \mathrm { k c a l m o l ^ { - 1 } }$ for $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ ,confirming a stronger affinity of $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ for $- S O _ { 3 } { } ^ { - }$ groups.

Molecular dynamics simulations provide additional insights into the structural effects of cations on the Nafion membrane.The simulations (visualized in a supplementary GIF) show that K+,due to its lower binding energy,leads to slower adsorption/desorption kinetics and local aggregation of $- S O _ { 3 } { } ^ { - }$ groups.This aggregation results in horn-shaped channels (Fig.4c),which hinder ion transport but enhance stability by blocking crossover. In contrast, $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ forms uniform rectangular channels (Fig.4d) due to weaker aggregation,promoting faster ion transport.

In a mixed $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } / K ^ { + } }$ environment, the membrane exhibits a centring phenomenon (Fig.4e). $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ influences the channel structure to resemble Nafion-K,effectively inhibiting ligand crossover. Simultaneously, $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ within the channels facilitates faster ion conduction.Quantitative analysis (Fig.4f) shows that $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ aggregates predominantly in the narrowest regions of the channels $_ { - 2 }$ to $2 \ \mathrm { n m }$ ),obstructing ligand transport while increasing resistance. $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ ，in contrast，is more evenly distributed,further enhancing ion transport.

Radial distribution function (RDF) analysis and cation coordination numbers (Fig. 4 g) reveal that $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ induces more pronounced local aggregation of $- S O _ { 3 } { } ^ { - }$ groups than $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ In mixed environments, $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ aggregation is reduced,improving the uniformity of cation distribution and benefiting ion transport. Despite this, $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ -induced local aggregation effects persist, highlighting its role in improving stability.

The morphology of water channels further supports the centring phenomenon (Supplementary Figure 11). $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ causes localized blockages in water channels,reducing ionic conductivity and increasing energy loss.Fig.4h illustrates the coordination numbers and RDF of water molecules around different cations.Although both $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ and $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ carry $a +$ 1 charge, $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ ,with its smaller ionic radius and higher surface charge density,exhibits stronger hydration,reflected in higher coordination numbers and RDF peaks at shorter distances.Water density analysis shows that the density in the narrowest channel sections decreases from $1 5 5 ~ \mathrm { k g } / \mathrm { m } ^ { 3 }$ (Nafion-Na) to $1 1 4 ~ \mathrm { k g } / \mathrm { m } ^ { 3 }$ (Nafion-K). In a mixed $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } / K ^ { + } }$ environment, the water density adopts an intermediate value of $1 3 6 \mathrm { k g / }$ $\mathbf { m } ^ { 3 }$ ，while the channel diameter narrows from $0 . 5 8 \ \mathrm { n m }$ to $0 . 5 1 \ \mathrm { n m }$ (Supplementary Figure 12).

Charge density analysis (Supplementary Figure 13) provides further evidence for these efects. Nafion-K exhibits a positive surface charge density,aligning water molecules such that hydrogen atoms face the electrolyte.This orientation reduces triethanolamine crossover. In contrast,Nafion-Na displays an opposite charge orientation,destabilizing catholyte chelates and influencing performance.Under mixed $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ and $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ conditions,the charge density trends also reflect the centring phenomenon.Supplementary Figure 14 further analyzes the distribution of ions within the pores when the $\mathrm { N a ^ { + . } K ^ { + } }$ ratios are 0.5:1.5,1.0:1.0,and

1.5:0.5.The results indicate that as the $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ content increases,ions tend to accumulate on the membrane surface,whereas higher $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ content facilitates ion penetration into the pore interiors.During actual battery cycling, excess $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ and $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ are typically present in the electrolyte, leading to gradual permeation and redistribution of ions within the membrane. Specifically,in Nafion-Na, $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ is gradually lost and replaced by $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ ， forming a protective layer on the membrane surface that enhances stability.Meanwhile,in Nafion $\cdot \mathrm { K } ,$ partial $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ loss occurs,allowing $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ to penetrate into the membrane interior to improve ionic conductivity. This finding further validates the feasibility of the cation-enhancement strategy.

The permeability of triethanolamine through Nafion-X was tested using the H-cell shown in Supplementary Figure 2,with the results displayed in Supplementary Figure 15.The experiments show that the permeability of Nafion-Na is $6 . 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 7 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } / \mathrm { m i n }$ ,while that of Nafion-K decreases to $4 . 5 \times 1 0 ^ { - 7 } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } / \mathrm { m i n }$ .This indicates that,under the same concentration gradient,the amount of triethanolamine migration allowed by Nafion-K is significantly reduced,meaning the introduction of $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ effectively lowers the triethanolamine crossover in Nafion-X, thereby enhancing the battery's stability and providing experimental support for the feasibility of the aforementioned calculations.In addition to the perfluorosulfonic acid ion exchange membrane (Nafion), the non-fluorinated cation exchange membrane sPEEK,due to its excellent alkaline stability,is gradually being applied in alkaline iron-based flow batteries [57,58]. Furthermore,the sulfonic acid groups,which are discretely distributed along the main chain of sPEEK,also offer new possibilities for the cation-enhanced strategy proposed in this study. Experimental results show that the permeability of triethanolamine through the enhanced sPEEK-K further decreases to $5 . 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 8 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } / \mathrm { m i n } _ { \cdot }$ ， demonstrating a good suppression effect.Therefore,the sPEEK membrane,after the cation-enhanced strategy described in this study,is expected to become an important research direction for the next generation of alkaline iron-based flow batteries.

Insummary，Nafion-Na enhancesenergy efficiency through improved ion transport,while Nafion-K significantly prolongs cycling stability by narrowing channels and inhibiting ligand crossover. The mixed $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } / K ^ { + } }$ environment balances these effects,achieving both stability and effciency.This synergy arises from $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ forming a protective layer on the membrane surface and $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ promoting faster ion transport within the channels.

Compared to previous studies,the simplest anode material—iron-triethanolamine (Fe(III/II)-triethanolamine)—was employed in this study, and a simple cation-induced membrane enhancement method was used to modify the commonly used Nafion membrane,allowing the battery to maintain good performance after 233O cycles,surpassing most studies based on TEA ligands,as shown in Supplementary Table 1[16-19,21, 59-63]. It is noteworthy that, only the supporting cations $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ and $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ ,which are commonly used in alkaline iron-based flow batteries,are discussed in this study.In fact, the surface charge density of Group Imetal cations decreases as the ionic radius increases,leading to an interesting nonlinear variation in the hydrated ionic radius when carrying a single positive charge.This nonlinear effect influences the exchange process of sulfonic acid groups in the Nafion membrane,thereby altering the pore structure of the hydrated membrane.In this study，the unique positioning of $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ and $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ at this nonlinear transition point is utilized to optimize battery performance.However,in more complex application scenarios,larger cations (such as $\mathrm { R b ^ { + } }$ and ${ \boldsymbol { \mathrm { C } } } { \boldsymbol { s } } ^ { * }$ ）can be introduced in combination with smaller cations (such as Li+） to construct a more diverse,stepwise pore structure,thereby further optimizing ion transport properties and expanding the applicability of membrane performance across different conditions [64-66].However,as shown in Supplementary Figure 18,images of the electrolyte before and after cycling reveal a significant water migration phenomenon.An increase in the water content of the anolyte was observed,leading to a decrease in pH,which severely compromised the chelation stability of triethanolamine.Simultaneously,a rise in the pHof the catholyte accelerated the ligand exchange of ferrocyanide,ultimately resulting in its degradation, as indicated by the deepening red color and the formation of precipitates in the storage tank.This phenomenon further limited the enhancement of battery stability.Therefore,in addition to utilizing the membrane contamination mechanism to induce membrane reinforcement and prevent ligand crossover,further research is required to suppress water migration and developa ferrocyanide-based catholyte with improved pH stability to enhance the long-term stability of the battery [53,54].

# 4.Conclusion

In this study,we explored the performance and stability of all-soluble all-iron redox flow batteries (AIFBs) by evaluating the influence of pretreated cation-exchange membranes and the diverse ion effect in sodium and potassium-based electrolytes. Our findings demonstrate that while Nafion-Na membranes provide superior ionic conductivity and voltage efficiency,Nafion-K membranes enhance stability by mitigating ligand crossover,leading to longer cycling lifetimes.The mixed Na+/K\* electrolyte further optimizes the system by improving solubility and stability without requiring additional additives,balancing energy efficiency and cycling durability.By leveraging cation-induced membrane reinforcement, we achieved a significant improvement in battery performance,with an optimized $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } / K ^ { + } }$ system showing stable cycling for over 2300 cycles at $8 0 ~ \mathrm { m A } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ ，with an average energy efficiency of $7 3 . 4 ~ \%$ .These results highlight the potential of combining ionic conductivity and stability in cation-induced membranes to create highperformance,long-lasting AIFBs.This work provides a simple yet effective strategy to enhance the practical viability of all-soluble redox flow batteries for large-scale energy storage applications.
