All-ironaqueous redox flow battries (AI-ARFBs)areatractive for large-scaleenergy storage due to their low cost,abundant raw materials,and the safety and environmental friendlinessof using water as the solvent. However,traditional deposition-type AI-ARFBs sufer from limitations incharge and discharge depth due to the coupling of energyand power.In contrast,all-soluble AI-ARFBs (ASAI-ARFBs),which feature full soluble iron species throughout charge and discharge cycles,achieve the decoupling of energy and power, thus overcoming the limitations and improving operational scalability.Despite their benefits,challenges remain in redox species solubility,electrolyte stability,electrode reactivity,membraneselectivity andcapacity decay mechanism.This review provides a comprehensive overview of current research on ASAI-ARFBs,focusing on the needs for robust electrolytes,advanced electrode structures,durable membrane materials and in-situ characterisation techniques to address these challenges and enhance their performance.Additionaly,this review highlights the importance of integrating renewable energy technologies with ASAI-ARFBs to boost their commercialisation potential.

# 1． Introduction

The rising global demand for clean energies drives the urgent need for large-scale energy storage solutions [1]. Renewable resources, e.g. wind and solar power,are inherently unstable and intermittent due to the fickle weather [2-4]. To meet the demand of effectively harnessing these clean energies,it is crucial to establish eficient,large-scale energy storage devices capable of storing and releasing surplus energy according to actual needs [2]. Traditional electrochemical energy storage technologies,such as lithium-ion batteries,rely on storing energy within solid-state electrodes,which poses challenges related to scalability and long-term cost-effectiveness for large-scale applications [5].Redox flow batteries (RFBs),which store energy in liquid of external reservoirs, provide alternative choices to overcome these limitations [6].A RFB single cell primarily consists of the anode and cathode,the anolyte and catholyte stored in separate tanks,and the membrane for separating two half-cells [7]. In a typical RFB,pumps circulate the electrolytes to the electrode chambers,where redox reactions occur at the electrode/electrolyte interface,and the membrane serves the function of ion conduction [7].The modular design of RFBs allows for flexible system configurations,cost eficiency,and the ability to adjust energy and power output as needed [8]. Therefore,RFBs are considered a competitive candidate for sustainable and large-scale energy storage.

Aroused from the intrinsic safety and high conductivity of aqueous electrolytes,RFBs using aqueous electrolytes offer distinct advantages for real-world applications [9,1O].Various aqueous redox flow batteries (ARFBs),including vanadium-based [11,l2],zinc-based [13-16] and iron-based [17-2O] systems,have attracted great interests.Among them,all-iron ARFBs (AI-ARFBs),where both the catholytes and anolytes are water-soluble iron salts,have garnered significant attention for their cost-effectiveness and abundance of raw materials,and can be categorized into acidic,alkaline,and near-neutral types based on supporting electrolytes (Fig.1).The acidic AI-ARFBs,also referred as deposition-type AI-ARFBs,operate based on $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { F e } ^ { \circ }$ redox pair at the anodic side and $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 _ { + } } / \mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 _ { + } }$ redox pair at the cathodic side [18].A considerable issue is,under acidic media,parasitic hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) causes both electrolyte depletion and low coulombic efficiency [18].Additionally,uneven Fe deposition compromises battery performance,safety and reliability aroused from the restricted capacity and the increased risk of short circuits [18].Although some improvements have been achieved toward optimising the robustness of acidic AI-ARFBs [17-24], they still sufer from the inherent coupling of energy and power with limited scalability.Further from this aspect,alkaline andnear-neutral AI-ARFBs, known asall-solubleAI-ARFBs (ASAI-ARFBs),are competitive candidates because they maintain soluble iron species $( \mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + } )$ in both half-cells during charge and discharge processes,presenting the ability to entirely decouple energy and power.

Table 1 compares the key performance indicators of ASAI-ARFBs and deposition-type AI-ARFBs,highlighting the unique advantages of ASAI-ARFBs. One notable advantage is the adjustable cell voltage,which allows for customisation of the working voltage.This flexibility improves system integration with grid and renewable energy sources, reducing energy conversion losses.Additionally,ASAI-ARFBs benefit from higher electrolyte utilisation,meaning a larger proportion of redoxactive species is effectively engaged in the charge/discharge process.This enhances energy eficiency,reduces waste,and makes the system more cost-effective by requiring fewer chemicals to achieve the desired energy output.The enhanced power and energy densities of ASAI-ARFBs provide significant advantages for energy storage applications. Higher power density enables rapid energy delivery during peak demand, making these batteries ideal for grid stabilisation and frequency regulation.Simultaneously,increased energy density maximises storage capacity within a given electrolyte volume,benefiting installations with limited space while improving scalability for large-scale energy storage systems.Lastly,the longer operational lifespan of ASAI-ARFBs reduces the need for frequent system replacements and lowers maintenance requirements.This extended longevity results in a lower total cost of ownership,making ASAI-ARFBs a durable and economically viable solution for long-term energy storage.These features collectively position ASAI-ARFBs as a sustainable,cost-effective option for various energy storage applications.

The decoupling of energy (capacity $\times$ voltage) and power in RFBs can also be referred to as the decoupling of capacity and power.Power output is predominantly determined by active area in the electrochemical cell stack while energy storage is governed by the volume of the electrolyte reservoirs,concentration and redox potential of redoxactive species [25].This decoupling enables independent optimization of power and energy to accommodate specific application demand. However,in deposition-type RFBs,this decoupling is less pronounced due to the nature of capacity storage.As a result of solid-phase deposition,the charged active species become confined within the electrochemical cell,leading to the recoupling of energy and power [26]. Additionally,thicker deposition layers may exhibit physical instability, increasing the possibility of detachment,which ultimately reduces capacity. In contrast，ASAI-ARFBs operate without metal deposition/dissolution during cycling,enabling true decoupling of energy and power.Among energy-power decoupled RFB technologies， the all-vanadium RFBs (VRFBs) serve as commercially mature examples. However,VRFBs face challenges such as the limited availability and high cost of vanadium resources [27],as well as the occurrence of water-related side reactions in acidic electrolytes [28].ASAI-ARFBs employ water-soluble iron salts that are abundant and cost-effective and their operation in alkaline conditions reduces the severity of water-related side reactions compared to acidic systems,owing to the differences in the thermodynamics and kinetics of water decomposition [29,30]. These advantages position ASAI-ARFBs as a promising complementary technology to VRFBs in the energy storage sector.

# 3．Research progress of ASAI-ARFBs

# 3.2. Electrode, membrane modification and cell configuration

Current efforts to enhance performance of ASAI-ARFBs primarily focus on modifying the electrolytes,with electrodes and separators receive comparatively less attention.However,electrodes,where electrochemical reactions occur,are critical to overall performance of RFBs. Typically,commercial graphite or carbon felts are used as cathode and anode in ASAI-ARFBs.However, they suffer from poor wettability and low catalytic activity [78,79],highlighting the urgent need for modifications or development of new electrode materials.

Thermal treatment is a key method for electrode modification due to its low cost and suitability for large-scale production [8O].For example, as shown in Fig.8Aand 8B,graphite felts show more pores/high surface area after heat-treating at $6 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 1 \mathrm { h } }$ [81].Additionally,the increase in oxygen-containing functional groups (Fig.8C) enhances the hydrophilicity of the graphite felts,thereby promotes the electrode'sreactivity.This modification of the cathode and anode led to an increase in EE, from $5 6 . 1 \%$ to $6 2 . 9 \%$ at $2 0 0 \mathrm { m A c m ^ { - 2 } }$ in $\mathrm { F e ( D I P S O ) } | |$ Fe(CN)e RFBs. Carbon nanotubes (CNTs)，which are rolled-up layers of graphene sheets,are classified into single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs) and multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs) based on the number of graphene layers [79]. Due to their low cost, high specific surface area, and stability,CNTs are widely used for electrode modifications in various RFBs.Yongchai Kwon et al.reported the catalytic activity of original MWCNTs and carboxylic acid-functionalized MWCNTs (CACNTs) on the Fe(BIS-TRIS) redox couple [82].As shown in Fig.8D and 8E,the higher current density,narrower peak potential difference, and lower charge transfer resistance all indicate that CNTs enhanced the redox kinetics of Fe(BIS-TRIS).In Fe(BIS-TRIS) $| | \mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { \mathfrak { s } }$ RFBs,MWCNTs achieved $8 4 \ \%$ EE,while CACNTs achieved $8 3 . 5 ~ \%$ at $8 0 \mathrm { \ m A } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ (Fig.8F).The slightly lower performance of CACNTs can be attributed to the electrostatic repulsion between the anionic Fe(BIS-TRIS) and the COO" groups.In another study,Musthafa Ottakam Thotiyl et al. proposed a novel strategy to enhance performance of ASAI-ARFBs by triggering electrostatic forces at the electrode-electrolyte interface [83]. As shown in Fig.8G and 8H, this strategy involved the use of positively charged poly diallyldimethylammonium chloride to modify CNT-coated carbon paper as electrodes,attracting negatively charged redox species in the electrolyte.By introducing additional electrostatic flux at the interface,this method significantly improved the mass transfer kinetics of redoxactive molecules,resulting in a $1 4 0 ~ \%$ increase in energy density.

Ion exchange membranes,functioning as separators,play a crucial role in determining the robustness of ASAI-ARFBs.Nafion cation exchange membranes are commonly used in ASAI-ARFBs. Different from earlier studies that utilised Nafion membranes,Rajaram K.Nagarale et al.developed a sulfonated polyethylene styrene-divinylbenzene cation exchange membrane for $\mathrm { F e ( T E A ) | | F e ( C N ) _ { \mathfrak { s } } }$ RFBs [85]．Although the membrane exhibited lower performance ( $9 2 \%$ CE, $7 5 \ \%$ EE, $8 2 ~ \%$ VE) compared to Nafion membrane $9 9 \ \%$ CE, $7 5 \ \%$ EE, $7 6 \ \%$ VE),it demonstrated the potential for fluorine-free membranes in future alkaline AI-ARFBs.

Cell configuration is a critical yet often underappreciated aspect in the design of RFBs.As shown in Fig.8I and 8J,a "flow-through" (FT) configuration is defined by using flow frame manifolds that deliver and receive electrolyte from two opposing sides of a cavity containing a porous electrode.In contrast,a "flow-over"(FO) configuration directs the electrolyte around a machined graphite flow field,allowing it to diffuse into a thinner electrode cavity,typically using graphitized papers or occasionally thin carbon felts.The study highlights that in ASAI-ARFBs,the FO configuration with carbon papers demonstrated higher maximum power density,EE and electrolyte utilisation compared to the FT configuration with graphite felts [84].

# 3.3. Capacity decay mechanism

Capacity decay in ASAI-ARFBs is caused by complex reasons, previous studies have shown that factors such as ligand dissociation in the anolyte and the stability of $[ \mathrm { F e } ( \mathrm { C N } ) _ { 6 } ] ^ { 4 - / 3 - }$ in the catholyte under varying pH levels impact the performance of ASAI-ARFBs.However,comprehensive,systematic studies of capacity decay mechanism remain limited.In the earliest study of $\mathrm { F e ( T E A ) } | | \mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { \mathfrak { s } }$ RFBs,capacity decay was attributed to the crossover of free TEA molecules due to their higher diffusion coeficient and greater driving force [54]. Subsequent study employing spectroscopy and electrochemical techniques found that undesirable contact between charged Fe(BIS-TRIS) and oxygen led to self-discharge,which significantly contributed to capacity decay in Fe (BIS-TRIS) $| | \mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { \mathfrak { s } }$ RFBs [86].

Further research explored deper insights into the capacity decay mechanism within the Fe(DIPSO) $| | \mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { 6 }$ RFBs [87].As shown in Fig.9A,compared to Fe(TEA),Fe(DIPSO) as the anolyte shows almost no HER or iron deposition,yet significant capacity decay was still observed.Throughcyclingtestsusingconcentration-and volume-unbalanced cells,several common mechanisms of capacity decay were ruled out,including the decomposition of Fe(DIPSO), self-discharge of Fe(DIPSO) under oxygen exposure,and HER in the anolyte,as well as the decomposition of $\mathrm { [ F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } ] ^ { 3 - } }$ in the catholyte. Fig.9B shows that in $\mathrm { F e ( D I P S O ) | | F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } }$ RFBs,capacity decay is primarily caused by the crossover of free DIPsO ligands,which react with $\mathrm { [ F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } ] ^ { 3 - } }$ to form $\mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { 6 } ^ { 4 - }$ ,leading to increasing capacity imbalance between the anolyte and catholyte in subsequent cycles.UV-VIS spectroscopy indicates the accumulation of the discharge product [Fe(CN) $6 ] ^ { 4 - }$ caused by the side reaction at $1 0 0 ~ \%$ state of charge,as shown in Fig.9C.Although the oxidising ability of $\mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { 6 } { } ^ { 3 - }$ is significantly enhanced under alkaline conditions,the specific reaction process between $\mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { 6 } { } ^ { 3 - }$ and DIPSO remains unclear. Interestingly, $\mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { 6 } { } ^ { 3 - }$ was found to spontaneously convert into $\mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { 6 } { } ^ { 4 - }$ under alkaline conditions (a reversible process),and the ligand DIPsO further accelerates the formation of $\mathrm { F e } ( \mathrm { C N } ) _ { 6 } { } ^ { 4 \cdot }$ (Fig.9D). The confirmation of hydroxyl radicals in the alkaline $[ \mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { 6 } ] ^ { 3 - }$ solution (Fig.9E) was used to link the spontaneous reversible reaction of $\mathrm { [ F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } ] ^ { 4 - / 3 - } }$ and the side reaction between DIPSO and $\mathrm { [ F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } ] ^ { 3 - } }$ As shown in Fig.9F,in alkaline conditions, $\mathrm { [ F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } ] ^ { 3 - } }$ oxidises OH to ·OH, then ·OH oxidises $[ \mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { 6 } ] ^ { 4 - }$ back to $\mathrm { [ F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } ] ^ { 3 - } }$ ,creating a reversible equilibrium.The introduction of DIPSO rapidly consumes ·OH, leading to the accumulation of [Fe(CN) $6 ] ^ { 4 - }$ .This mechanism of capacity decay has also been proven to be applicable to TEA and BIS-TRIS ligands.Finally,it is important to emphasize that the aforementioned studies have confirmed that the [Fe $( \mathrm { C N } ) _ { 6 } ] ^ { 4 - / 3 - }$ in the catholyte exhibits excellent stability in alkaline electrolytes,without crossover and unaffected by oxygen exposure.

# 4. Summary and perspective

ASAI-ARFBs,despite being developed less than a decade ago,have quickly garnered significant attention in the field of energy storage due to their innovative all-soluble mechanism and the utilisation of low-cost raw materials.The developments in recent years have confirmed that ASAI-ARFBs offer significant advantages in terms of efficiency, cycle life,and scalability compared to traditional deposition-type AI-ARFBs. This review provides a comprehensive overview of the fundamental conceptsunderpinning ASAI-ARFBs, elucidating theirworking mechanism and summarising the current state of research.However, while promising advancements have been made,optimal performance has not yet been fully realised,highlighting the need for further investigation.To expedite the development and application of ASAI-ARFBs,it is crucial to conduct in-depth studies in the following areas (Fig.10).

(1）Exploring new anolytes and catholytes.Enhancing the solubility of both anolytes and catholytes is critical,as higher solubility directly increases the capacity of ASAI-ARFBs.For anolytes,viscosity is a key factor influencing energy density,while stability directly affects the operational lifespan.To address these challenges,optimising the ratios of iron ions,ligands,and supporting electrolytes,is vital to achieving high electrolyte concentrations without significantly increasing viscosity.Additionally,to meet the commercialisation requirement of at least 2o,ooo cycles,it is important to explore more stable anolyte ligand structures.For catholytes,the commonly used $\mathrm { [ F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } ] ^ { 4 - } } / ^ { 3 - }$ couple shows a relatively low positive potential, limiting the voltage of ASAI-ARFBs.Introducing multiple ligands to the metal ion center has been shown to achieve higher positive potential. For instance, $^ { 2 , 2 ^ { \prime } }$ -bipyridine $^ { 4 , 4 ^ { \prime } }$ -dicarboxylic acid-modified CN-demonstrates significant potential as catholytes for ASAI-ARFBs [88].Given that the precise correlation between the molecular structures of ligands (L) and the stability constants of $\mathrm { F e ( L ) }$ complexes is not yet fully understood, further experimental and computational chemistry are required to further elucidate this relationship and guide the design of ligand structures with optimal properties [89].

(2)Designing new membrane.Membranes used in RFBs should possess good chemical stability,sufficient mechanical strength,excellent ion selectivity,high conductivity,low permeability of redox species,and cost-effectiveness.Although Nafion membranes exhibit good performance in ASAI-ARFBs,it has been observed that crossover of organic ligands during prolonged operation leads to a poor lifespan.Modifying existing commercial Nafion membranes,exploring other widely-reported membranes (PBI, SPEEK,etc.）[90,91],or developing entirely new membrane materials,is expected to suppress the crossover of organic ligands and further enhance the operational lifespan of ASAI-ARFBs.

Additionally,it is essential to highlight that membrane modifications need to balance ionic conductivity and selectivity.

(3) Developing new electrode materials.For electrode materials,since both the cathodic and anodic reactions in most ASAI-ARFBs operate via an outer-sphere electron transfer mechanism,the primary focus is on facilitating the mass transport of redox species.Consequently，electrode modification efforts center on improving the mass transport rate of redox species at the electrode interface.While increasing hydrophilicity of electrodes is a widely recognised goal in RFBs,it is important to consider whether the approach might negatively impact the redox reactions of the redox species in ASAI-ARFBs.Additionally,the hydrophilicity of the electrodes may decrease during operation due to the adsorption of non-redox species [92].Recent research has demonstrated that achieving permanent superhydrophilicity in carbon electrodes through the covalent attachment of 4-(diazonium)benzenesulfonic acid at room temperature can improve performance in alkaline ARFBs [92]. Such strategy hold promise forhigh-performance ASAI-ARFBs.

(4)Applying in-situ characterisation techniques.In-situ characterisation techniques are more accurate,specific,and convincing than exsitu techniques for exploring battery mechanisms and are widely used in various battery systems.Currently,several in-situ characterisation techniques are being applied to various RFBs [93-101].Techniques such as in-situ nuclear magnetic resonance, in-situ electron paramagnetic resonance,and in-situ X-ray diffraction provide real-time insights into electrochemical reactions,ion transport,and the stability of redoxactive species under operating conditions.Therefore,utilising advanced in-situ characterisation techniques (in-situ imaging,in-situ spectroscopy) will be beneficial for deeply investigating the underlying causes ofcapacity decayin ASAI-ARFBs,therebypromoting their faster development and application.

(5)Integrating renewable energy technologies with ASAI-ARFBs. The integration of renewable energy technologies,such as solar, wind,and hydropower,with ASAI-ARFBs has shown promising potential for enhancing energy storage systems.A recent study demonstrated the use of triple-junction solar cells to charge Fe (TEA) $| | \mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { \mathfrak { s } }$ RFBs [1O2]．The solar ASAI-ARFBs operated stably for $^ { 1 2 0 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ ，with only minor performance degradation attributed to the dissociation of Fe(TEA). Such finding underscores the viability of coupling renewable energy sources with ASAI-ARFBs to mitigate the intermittency of energy generation while maintaining operational efficiency.By optimising the integrationbetween renewable energytechnologiesand ASAI-ARFBs,the development of more sustainable,low-carbon energy infrastructures can be furtheradvanced.
