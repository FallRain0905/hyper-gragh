Iron-chromium flow bateries are emerging as a promising technology for large-scale energy storage,ofering high safety and long cycle life.However,their performance is being strongly influenced by electrolyte flow rate: insufficient flow is exacerbating concentration polarization,while excessive flow is increasing pumping losses andreducing system eficiency.This study is establishing an integrated framework that is combining experimental characterization with three-dimensional multiphysics simulations. Specifically,an $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ symmetric celltest platform was constructed,and a numerical model coupling fluid dynamics,ion transport,and electrochemical kinetics was developed onamultiphysics platform.Using this framework,key physical parameters are being systematicaly analyzed under varying flow rates per unit electrode area, including flow characteristics, reactant concentration distribution, current density distribution, polarization behavior,and long-term cycling stability. Results show that an optimal flow rate of $1 . 5 ~ \mathrm { m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } }$ is enhancing flow uniformity and reactant distribution,achieving a ${ \sim } 1 3 . 0 6 ~ \%$ increase in concentration uniformity and $8 3 \ \%$ voltage efficiency,while maintaining low pressure drop $( 1 3 5 4 . 5 \mathrm { P a } )$ and pumping losses.These findings confirm $1 . 5 \ : \mathrm { m L } / \mathrm { m i n } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ asthe optimal balance between performance and energy cost, providing a valuable parameter reference for flow-field design and operational control of small-scale Fe-Cr flow bateries with similar configurations and fixed flow channel designs.

# 1．Introduction

The iron-chromium redox flow battery (Fe-Cr RFB),as a typical electrochemical energy storage device for renewable energy,demon-strates broad application prospects in large-scale grid energy storage and peak shaving due to its advantages of low cost, good environmental friendliness,and high energy conversion efficiency [1-5]. The positive and negative electrode reactions are the $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + }$ and $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ redox pairs,respectively,offering good reaction reversibility and chemical stability.However,in practical operation,the iron-chromium flow battery still faces challenges such as significant concentration polarization,mass transfer limitations,and high pump energy consumption. These botlenecks directly limit its eficiency and commercialization process [6-9].Previous studies have shown that flow rate is one of the key parameters influencing the performance of iron-chromium flow batteries.Alower electrolyte flow rate often leads to an increased concentration gradient of reactants on the electrode surface,thereby causing significant concentration polarization,which affects the battery's output stability and energy efficiency [1O-l3]．Although increasing the flow rate can effectively reduce mass transfer resistance, it also imposes additional pump load, which decreases the overall system efficiency [14,15]. Chen et al.used CFD simulations and experimental validation to investigate the impact of electrolyte flow rate on the performance of vanadium redox flow batteries (VRB).The study found that increasing the electrolyte flow rate could stabilize the distribution of the electrolyte,reducing the effects of flow irregularities and thereby improving battery performance [16].Gao etal.,in their study of alkaline electrolyzers,used three-dimensional two-phase CFD simulations to investigate the effect of electrolyte flow rate on electrolyzer performance.The study pointed out that appropriately increasing the electrolyte flow rate helps to reduce the cell voltage,but excessively high flow rates lead to increased energy consumption.This further supports the view that an optimal balance between mass transfer and energy consumption needs to be found for the flow rate [17]. Kim et al. developed a transient model combined with experimental data to study the effects of flow rate,current density,and effective area size on the performance of vanadium redox flow batteries.The study found that increasing the flow rate could enhance energy efficiency,but excessively high flow rates increase pump energy consumption,reducing overall system efficiency [18].Therefore,this study systematically explores the polarization behavior and mass transfer evolution mechanisms of the iron-chromium flow battery at different flow rates, clearly identifying $1 . 5 \ \mathrm { m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } }$ as the optimal operating condition. This flow rate effectively mitigates concentration polarization while balancing energy consumption control, providing direct parameter references for engineering applications.

In recent years,using multiphysics simulation methods to study the internal processes of flow batteries has become a mainstream approach. Platforms such as COMsOL can couple fluid dynamics,charge transfer, and electrochemical reactions to accurately simulate key variables such as velocity fields,ion concentration,and current density [19-22].This study implements a quantitative evaluation of reactant distribution uniformity and mass transfer efficiency based on the "concentration uniformity coefficient."This innovative approach not only reveals the sensitive window for mass transfer optimization but also assists in optimizing flow channel structures and pump control strategies. Similar attempts have been made in the literature;for example,Zhou et al. proposed the "uniformity field index" based on concentration gradients to assess the distribution of electrode reactions in vanadium redox flow batteries [23].

The polarization during the operation of iron-chromium flow batteries mainly includes ohmic polarization, electrochemical polarization, and concentration polarization.Among them,concentration polarization dominates in the medium to high current density regions,significantly affecting output stability [24-26]. By constructing a symmetric cell,this study effectively isolates the differences in reaction kinetics and focuses on the coupling relationship between flow rate and concentration polarization,providing a novel pathway for the quantitative identification of polarization behavior.In addition,this study,based on the symmetric cell, successfully decouples the polarization of the iron system in the iron-chromium flow battery,effectively separating ohmic polarization,activation polarization,and concentration polarization.It also identifies the sensitivity of $\mathrm { { Z _ { w } } }$ to flowrate variations,a feature that israrely seen in the literature [27-29].For example,Huang fitted and modeled the concentration polarization characteristics in Zn-Br batteries,but they did not achieve an analysis framework that dynamically matches with flow rate variations [30].

Flow rate regulation,as a key means to optimize the mass transfer efficiency and enhance the energy efficiency of flow batteries,has established a systematic research foundation in mature systems such as vanadium redox flow batteries (VRFBs).It not only provides critical support for flow rate optimization in the broader flow battery field but also promotes the development and application of the“voltage efficiency (VE)-pump power consumption $( \mathrm { P _ { \ p u m p } ) ^ { \ast } }$ coupling model. In terms of flow rate regulation mechanisms and practical applications: Guarnieri[31] proposed a dynamic flow rate control strategy based on flow factor modulation on a kW-class VRFB test platform,quantified the correlation between the flow factor,stack current,and state of charge (SOC),and achieved a ${ \sim } 2 ~ \%$ improvement in the battery round-trip efficiency via software-based dynamicregulation—providinga feasible experimental method for large-scale applications.Polyakov [32] on the theoretical front,deepened the connotation of the flow factor control strategy by treating it as a generalized extension of Faraday's electrolysis law.They clarified the mathematical relationships between the flow factor and parameters (e.g., battery conversion efficiency [single-pass conversion] and stack/tank volume ratio),resolving the issue that traditional flow factors relied on empirical selection and providing a universal theoretical basis for flow rate parameter design in different flow battery structures.In engineering applications,the“VE-P pump” coupling model has been widely used in pump control strategy design for VRFB systems,becoming a core tool for optimizing system energy efficiency.

However,most existing research and technical solutions focus on VRFB systems,and targeted research on iron-chromium redox flow batteries (ICRFBs) remains relatively scarce.ICRFBs differ significantly from VRFBs in electrolyte physicochemical properties (e.g.,viscosity, ion diffusion coefficient) and electrode reaction kinetics;additionally, they face unique issues such as ion cross-contamination and electrolyte instability.These factors make the mechanism bywhich flow rate affects internal mass transfer and polarization characteristics more complex, leading to potential compatibility risks if VRFB-based flowrate regulation strategies are directly adopted.Accordingly,this study focuses on iron-chromium redox flow batteries (ICRFBs).A coupled fluid flowelectrochemical model is first established to systematically investigate the effects of flow rate on internal velocity,concentration,and electric field distributions.Based on multiple metrics including voltage efficiency, pump power consumption, concentration polarization, and longterm cycling stability,optimal flow rate parameters are precisely determined via combined steady-state numerical simulation and experimental validation.Notably,energy eficiency and pump power consumption are not integrated into a single net system efficiency index, as system efficiency involves unquantified,unsystematically studied factors (e.g.,heat loss,side reactions),and forced integration would compromise result accuracy and conclusion rigor. The proposed coupled model, multi-index evaluation system,and optimal flow rate findings provide a comprehensive parameter reference for ICRFB flow rate optimization and lay a foundation for its commercialization.Future research will furtherrefine flow factor evaluation criteria to establish a complete ICRFB flowrate regulation technical system.

# 2.1. Experiment

Fig.l(a) shows the basic structure of the iron-chromium flow battery.The oxidation reaction of the chromium redox couple $\scriptstyle ( \mathbf { C r } ^ { 2 + } \to \mathbf { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ $+ \ e ^ { - } )$ occurs at the negative electrode,while the reduction reaction of the iron redox couple $( \mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + } + \mathbf { e } ^ { - }  \mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + } )$ takes place at the positive electrode.The electrolyte is circulated from the negative electrode storage tank containing $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ and the positive electrode storage tank containing $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ ,with $\mathrm { H } ^ { + }$ migrating through the ion-exchange membrane to maintain charge balance.Fig.1(b) shows the symmetric cell structure of the iron system,where both sides use the $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ redox couple.The system is simplified by membrane separation to study electrode kinetics and polarization differentiation.The use of the iron ion symmetric cell $( \mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ system) in the polarization differentiation experiments is mainly based on its unique electrochemical characteristics and its high compatibility with the experimental objectives.Firstly, the iron ion redox couple $( \mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + } )$ exhibits excellent stability within the conventional potential window and is less prone to side reactions such as oxygen evolution or hydrogen evolution,thereby avoiding interference signals introduced by oxidation or hydrolysis of the chromium redox couple $( \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } )$ . Secondly, the experiment focuses on the specific impact of flow rate on concentration polarization: by fixing the temperature, electrolyte concentration,and electrode materials,and only adjusting the electrolyte flow rate,the relationship between flow rate variation and concentration polarization can be precisely quantified.The symmetric cell design further eliminates differences in the reaction kinetics of the positive and negative electrodes,ensuring that polarization changes are solely due to the mass transfer process,thus providing direct experimental evidence for the optimization of flow channels in flow batteries.It should be noted that this symmetric cell model does not fully replicate the synergistic effect of dual redox couples $( \mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } )$ in the Fe-Cr full cell, nor does it account for the osmotic pressure difference across the ion-exchange membrane. Fig.1(c) illustrates the schematic diagram of the $1 0 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ small cell used in the experiment.The core components include graphite bipolar plates (for conductivity and electrolyte flow distribution),carbon cloth porous electrodes (providing the reaction interface),and perfluorosulfonic acid membranes (which the electrolyte while allowing $\mathrm { H } ^ { + }$ conduction). Additional components such as seals,positioning pins,and end plates ensure the cell's sealing and structural integration.

# 2.2.Simulation

The process of building the numerical simulation system includes geometry modeling,establishment of physical fields, mesh generation, definition of data sampling paths,model validation,and mesh independence analysis.This resulted in the development of a reliable and repeatable iron-chromium flow battery simulation platform, laying the theoretical and engineering foundation for subsequent simulation studies.Fig.2(a) presents the complete three-dimensional geometric model of the $1 0 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ flow battery used in the simulation, encompassing the anode,cathode,and ion-exchange membrane.The flow channel structure adopts an interdigitated design with a "one inlet and two outlets" configuration,which is characteristic of a flow-through setup. Specifically,the electrolyte enters the single main channel from the inlet,and under the forced flow effect of the interdigitated structure,it actively penetrates the porous microstructure of the electrodes (rather than merely flowing along the electrode surface,as in a flow-by configuration).After passing through the porous electrode matrix,the electrolyte splits into two branch channels,eventually converging and exiting through the two outlets.Fig.2(b) presents a close-up view of the geometric modeling. The model includes the negative electrode flow channel, negative electrode,membrane,positive electrode,and positive electrode flow channel. The channel morphology was modeled based on the structure of the actual battery. # 3．Results and discussion

This study systematically analyzes the impact of electrolyte flow rate on the performance of iron-chromium flow batteries,covering flow characteristics,concentration distribution, current density,polarization behavior,and cycling stability.The results show that flow rate adjustment not only affects mass transfer and polarization but also determines the overall energy efficiency and operational stability of the battery.The optimal flow rate for comprehensive performance is found to be $1 . 5 \mathrm { m L } /$ $\mathrm { { m i n / c m ^ { 2 } } }$

# 3.2. Flow-rate effects on $C r ^ { 3 + } / C r ^ { 2 + }$ concentration felds and uniformity

After clarifying the flow characteristics,the next step is to analyze the distribution of reactant concentration in the electrolyte under different flow rates,as the concentration distribution directly affects the reaction efficiency of the battery.Fig.4 shows the concentration changes of $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ under different flow rate conditions.By comparing the images,it is clear to see the impact of flow rate on the supply of reactants.Fig. 4(a) shows that at a flow rate of $0 . 7 5 \mathrm { m L / m i n / }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ the $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ concentration significantly decreases, exhibiting a clear concentration gradient. This indicates that at low flow rates,the diffusion of the electrolyte within the electrode is limited,especially in the region near the outlet, where reactant supply is insufficient,leading to concentration polarization.In contrast,at a flow rate of $1 . 5 ~ \mathrm { m L / m i n / }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ (Fig. 4(b)), the $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ concentration gradient significantly decreases, with a noticeable increase in concentration at the outlet. The concentration distribution becomes more uniform,and the supply of reactants is effectively improved.When the flow rate is further increased to 2.25 $\mathrm { m L } / \mathrm { m i n } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ (Fig.4(c)),although the average concentration increases, an enrichment phenomenon occurs at the inlet. The overall uniformity of the concentration distribution does not improve further.This indicates that while increasing the flow rate can reduce the concentration gradient,excessively high flow rates may lead to the accumulation of reactants in localized areas,which,in turn,negatively impacts the uniformity of the reaction.Fig.4(d) and (e) show the concentration variations of $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ and $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ on the electrode cross-section, respectively. At a flow rate of $0 . 7 5 ~ \mathrm { m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } }$ ,the $\mathrm { C r ^ { 2 + } }$ concentration exhibits a significant uneven distribution,with lower concentration in the center and higher concentration at the edges.As the flow rate increases to 1.5 $\mathrm { m L } / \mathrm { m i n } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ ,the concentration becomes gradually more uniform, with the outlet concentration rising to $0 . 5 6 \mathrm { m o l / L }$ ,indicating more sufficient reactant supply.However,further increasing the flow rate does not result in a significant increase in $\mathrm { C r ^ { 2 + } }$ concentration. In contrast, the $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ concentration at low flow rates exhibits a "bimodal" structure (Fig.4(e)),reflecting uneven diffusion of reactants within the electrode. As the flow rate increases, the $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ concentration distribution gradually smooths out, with a significant rise in concentration in the center region, and the uniformity improves.Fig.4(f) further uses the $^ { 1 1 } { \mathrm { C r } } ^ { 2 + }$ ion concentration uniformity curve" to quantify the changes in concentration uniformity under different flow rate conditions.The results show that between flow rates of $0 . 7 5 \mathrm { \ m L } / \mathrm { m i n } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ and $1 . 5 \ \mathrm { m L } / \mathrm { m i n } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ ，concentration uniformity improves significantly,with the $\mathrm { C r ^ { 2 + } }$ uniformity change rate increasing byapproximately $7 \%$ .However,when the flow rate is increased to $2 . 2 5 ~ \mathrm { m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } }$ ,the uniformity curve levels off, and the improvement in the uniformity change rate is only about $2 \%$ indicating that further increases in flow rate do not lead to significant additional improvements. In summary,increasing the flow rate effectively improves the concentration distribution,especially ata moderate flow rate of $1 . 5 \ \mathrm { m L } / \mathrm { m i n } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ ，where the concentration distribution becomes more uniform,and the reactant supply capability is significantly enhanced.Although high flow rates increase concentration, the local accumulation phenomenon suggests that excessively high flow rates may not further optimize reaction efficiency.Based on this,the next step is to evaluate the current density distribution and system energy efficiency to fully understand the impact of flow rate on battery performance.

# 3.4.Flow-rate-dependent polarization: EIS signatures and component deconvolution

In battery performance analysis,polarization behavior isa key factor influencing efficiency and stability.To further quantify the contribution of concentration polarization to the overall performance and verify the mechanism revealed by steady-state simulations,independent experimental electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) tests were conducted to decompose the polarization components,rather than transient simulations using the proposed model. Fig.6 illustrates the regulatory effect of flow rate on concentration polarization and ohmic polarization bypresenting polarization curves,impedance spectra,and polarization decomposition under different flow rate conditions.

Fig.6 systematically illustrates the polarization behavior of the ironchromium flow battery under different current densities and electrolyte flow rates,as well as the corresponding decoupling mechanism.The aim is to reveal the influence of flow rate on polarization characteristics—particularly concentration polarization—in order to determine the optimal operating parameters.In Fig.6(a)-(f),the polarization curvesunderunit flowrates of 0.5,1.0,1.5,2.0,2.5,and $3 . 0 \ \mathrm { m L / m i n / }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ are presented,showing their variation trends with increasing current density.It can be observed that all the curves in the low current density region $( < 6 0 \mathrm { m A } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ rise linearly,indicating that the system is mainly governed by ohmic polarization.In contrast,in the medium-tohigh current density region $\mathrm { ( > 1 0 0 ~ m A / c m ^ { 2 } } \mathrm { ) }$ ，the polarization curves exhibit a nonlinear and steep upward trend,suggesting that concentration polarization gradually becomes the dominant factor.As the flow rate increases,the overall polarization curves become progressively flatter,particularly at $3 . 0 ~ \mathrm { m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } }$ ，where the voltage loss in the high current density region is significantly alleviated.This reflects the suppression of mass transfer resistance by enhanced electrolyte convection.However,when the flow rate increases from1.5 to $3 . 0 \mathrm { m L / m i n / }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ ,the extent of curve improvement decreases markedly,showing a trend of diminishing returns.This indicates the existence of an optimal point or inflection point for flow rate.

To further decompose the polarization components,Fig. 6(g） presents the electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) of the battery under different flow rate conditions.The Nyquist plots consist of a typical semicircle and a sloped tail.The high-frequency intercept corresponds to ohmic resistance $\left( \operatorname { R } _ { \Omega } \right)$ ，the mid-frequency semicircle represents the charge transfer resistance $\mathrm { ( R _ { c t } ) }$ ,and the low-frequency tail reflects the diffusion resistance $( Z _ { \mathrm { w } } )$ induced by concentration polarization.With increasing flow rate,the slope of the diffusion tail gradually decreases, indicating improved mass transfer conditions and effective mitigation of concentration limitations.This result provides a theoretical basis for the subsequent polarization decomposition analysis.Fig.6(h) presents the decomposition of total polarization,electrochemical polarization,and ohmic polarization at a typical operating point of $1 4 0 \mathrm { \ m A } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ under different flow rate conditions.The method of polarization separation is illustrated in Fig.S1,where concentration polarization is obtained as: Concentration polarization $=$ Total polarization- Ohmic polarization -Activation polarization. # 3.5.Long-term cycling stability and effciencies vs.flow rate

Long-term cycling tests represent a critical methodology for evaluating the long-term operational stability and durability of ironchromium flow batteries,as they yield direct evidence of a battery's ability to sustain energy efficiency,voltage efficiency，and capacity retention across repeated charge-discharge cycles while also reflecting the system's potential for practical, large-scale deployment.To further investigate the impact of flow rate on the long-term operational stability of Fe-Cr flow batteries,we performed independent long-term cycling experiments.

Fig.7 presents the long-term cycling performance of the battery under different flow rates,including energy efficiency,voltage efficiency,coulombic efficiency,and capacity retention,further revealing the impact of flow rate on battery performance.Fig.7(a) shows the variationof energy efficiencywith the number of cycles.Ata low flow rate $( 0 . 5 \mathrm { m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } } )$ ,the energy efficiency decreases fromabout $7 6 \%$ to around $7 0 \%$ ,with a clear downward trend as the cycle number increases.In contrast,at a moderate flow rate $( 1 . 5 ~ \mathrm { m L } / \mathrm { m i n } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ ，the energy efficiency remains stable at approximately $8 2 \%$ ,with only minor fluctuations and little change as the number of cycles increases.Under the high flow rate condition of $2 . 2 5 ~ \mathrm { m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } }$ ,although the energy efficiency can be maintained at a slightly higher level (about $8 0 \%$ ,it begins to decline slightly with continued cycling,reflecting the additional energy consumption associated with higher flow rates. Fig. 7(b) shows the variation of coulombic efficiency. Under all flow rate conditions,the coulombic eficiency remains above $9 5 ~ \%$ .However,at a low flow rate of $0 . 7 5 \ \mathrm { m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } }$ ，the efficiency exhibits larger fluctuations, indicating uneven reactions and an increase in side reactions. At a higher flow rate of $2 . 2 5 \mathrm { \ m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } }$ ，the coulombic efficiency decreases slightly,suggesting that excessively high flow rates have a certain negative impact on battery stability.In contrast, at $1 . 5 \mathrm { m L } / \mathrm { m i n } /$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ ,the coulombic eficiency remains relatively stable in the range of $9 7 \ \% - 9 8 \ \%$ ,reflecting a more uniform reaction distribution and fewer side reactions.Fig.7(c) illustrates the variation of voltage efficiency with cycle number.At a flow rate of $0 . 7 5 ~ \mathrm { m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } }$ ,the voltage efficiency decreases from $8 2 ~ \%$ to $7 8 \%$ . At a flow rate of $2 . 2 5 ~ \mathrm { m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } }$ ,however, the voltage efficiency declines slightly to $8 3 ~ \%$ .This trend further confirms that a moderate flow rate has a positive effect on the long-term stability of battery performance.Fig.7(d) compares the charge-discharge curves at the 30th cycle.At a low flow rate,the voltage gap is relatively large and the plateau is short, indicating severe polarization and insufficient reactions. At $1 . 5 \ \mathrm { m L } / \mathrm { m i n } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ ，the voltage gap is minimized and the plateau is the most stable,suggesting sufficient reactant supply and reduced polarization. At $2 . 2 5 ~ \mathrm { m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } }$ ，the voltage gap increases again,and the reaction uniformity slightly decreases.Fig.7(e) presents the discharge capacity retention under different flow rate conditions.At low flow rates, the discharge capacity retention is significantly lower, indicating reduced reaction efficiency. Under the high flow rate condition of $2 . 2 5 ~ \mathrm { m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } }$ ,although the discharge capacity is slightly improved,the excessive pump power results in a slight decline in energy efficiency. In contrast,at $1 . 5 \ : \mathrm { \ m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } }$ ，the discharge capacity retention remains at around $9 8 \ \%$ ，demonstrating relatively ideal stability.Fig.7(f) integrates energy efficiency,coulombic efficiency,and voltage efficiency.The results indicate that ata flow rate of $1 . 5 \mathrm { m L } / \mathrm { m i n } /$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ ,all three efficiencies reach their optimal levels and exhibit minimal variation during long-term cycling，demonstrating that the battery operates with the greatest stability under this flow rate condition.

# 4.Conclusion

This study combined experiments with three-dimensional multiphysics simulations to systematically analyze the flow characteristics, reactant concentration distribution,current density distribution,polarization behavior,and long-term cycling stability of the battery under different unit flow rates.The results indicate that:Low flow rates (unit flow rate $< 0 . 7 5 \ \mathrm { m L / m i n / c m ^ { 2 } } )$ lead to significant concentration gradients inside the battery.The energy efficiency is only $7 6 \%$ and decreases to about $7 1 \ \%$ after3O cycles.High flowrates (unit flowrate $> 2 . 2 5 \mathrm { m L } /$ $\mathrm { m i n } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 \cdot }$ ）improve the average flow and concentration,but intensify local flow disturbances.The inlet-outlet pressure drop rises to 2762.4 Pa,pump consumption increases,and the overall energy efficiency declines.Moderate flow rate $( 1 . 5 ~ \mathrm { m L } / \mathrm { m i n } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ markedly improves flow and reaction uniformity.The concentration uniformity coefficient increases by ${ \sim } 1 3 . 0 6 \ \ \ \%$ ；the current density distribution becomes smoother; peak values and overpotentials decrease;and the voltage efficiency rises to ${ \sim } 8 3 ~ \%$ .Meanwhile,the inlet-outlet pressure drop remains at $1 3 5 4 . 5 \mathrm { P a }$ with relatively low pump consumption. Long-term cycling tests further show that at this condition,the energy efciency is $8 2 \%$ ,the voltage efficiency $8 5 ~ \%$ ,and the capacity retention close to $9 8 ~ \%$ ,clearly superior to low flow ratesand comparable to high flow rates.

In summary,a moderate flow rate of $1 . 5 \ : \mathrm { m L } / \mathrm { m i n } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ is confirmed to be the optimal balance point between performance and energy consumption in this system. The extrapolation of the optimal flow rate (1.5 $\mathrm { m L } / \mathrm { m i n } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } $ to the Fe-Cr full cell is based on the consistent core bottleneck—concentration polarization—shared by both the symmetric cell and the full cell. This optimal flow rate has been validated by full-cell experiments under the same parameters.This condition not only enhances flow and reaction uniformity but also maintains low pump power while ensuring high energy and voltage efficiencies with excellent capacity retention.These findings provide a valuable parameter reference forflow-field design and operational parameter selection in small-scale iron-chromium flow batteries with similar configurations and fixed flow channel designs.
