# ABSTRACT

Alkaline zinc-iron flow bateries (AZIFBs)are regarded as one of the most promising candidates for energy storage systems (ESs).Although they have advantages,such asscalability,safety,and long cycle life,there are remaining disadvantages-crossover and zinc dendrite formation.To address these issues,we introduce a blend polymer membrane for a stable zinc-iron flow battery system with enhanced alkaline stability and dendrite-free operation.The SPEEK/Polysulfone (SPK/PS) blend membrane demonstrates balanced properties of high ion selectivity,high mechanical strength,and chemical stability,which enhances its cyclability and durability, showing stable long-term performance with energy efficiency of $8 0 . 0 \%$ for 700 cycles at a current density of 80 mA $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ using a state of charge (SOC) of $5 0 \%$ .This study offers an effective approach for alkaline zinc-based batteries,providing stability in alkaline environments.

# 1．Introduction

The importance of renewable energy sources has grown significantly in the pursuit of carbon neutrality in recent days [1,2]. However, renewable energy technologies such as solar,wind,and tidal power are inherently intermittent,which can result in irregular and unstable power supply.To address this challenge,energy storage systems (ESSs) have emerged as promising solutions for ensuring grid stability and

energy reliability.

Among various ESS technologies,redox flow batteries (RFBs) have attracted considerable attention [3-6].RFBs are electrochemical systems that store energy in redox-active species dissolved in liquid electrolytes,which are stored in external tanks.During operation,these electrolytes are pumped into an electrochemical cell,where redox reactions occur at the electrodes to charge or discharge the battery.These RFBs offer several advantages,including scalability,design flexibility, safety,and long cycle life.Despite these advantages,RFBs also face several challenges that hinder their widespread commercialization, including electrolyte crossover，active-species self-degradation，and electrode side reactions,which can accelerate eficiency loss and compromise long-term performance. These challenges remain as a problem to be solved.

Among the representative types of RFBs,Vanadium Flow Batteries (VFBs)and Zinc-based Flow Batteries (ZBFBs) are the most prominent technologies.VFBs have been extensively studied and demonstrated for their high reversibility and excellent electrochemical activity,owing to the use of vanadium ions in both half-cells,which effectively mitigates cross-contamination issues [5,7-9]. Nevertheless,the high cost of vanadium and the complex electrolyte management significantly limit their large-scale deployment.In contrast，ZBFBs have attracted increasing attention as a promising,low-cost,and non-toxic alternative, utilizing more abundant and less expensive materials,such as zinc [2]. Theyalso offer the advantage of increased energy density due to the use of multivalent zinc ions [2,1O-12]. This substantial advantage makes ZBFBs a compelling candidate for grid-scale energy storage applications.

Within the category of ZBFBs,alkaline Zinc-Iron Flow Batteries (AZIFBs) are particularly promising due to their cost-effectiveness, longterm cyclability,and the potential to achieve relatively high energy densities $( \sim 5 0 \mathrm { ~ W h ~ L ^ { - 1 } } )$ ,attributed to a high theoretical open-circuit voltage (OcV) of $1 . 7 4 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ [5,13-15].AZIFBs utilize ferrocyanide anion as acathodic material and zinc hydroxide anion as an anodic material, respectively (Fig.1).During the charge and discharge process,the following reactions occurred in the catholyte and anolyte side,respectively [14].

However, they still face critical issues such as zinc dendrite formation,high membrane resistance,and active material crossover,which must be addressed for practical applications.To overcome these challenges,the membrane,which separates both sides,must have high chemical and mechanical stability,as well as high ion selectivity.

Various membrane-based strategies have been developed to meet these criteria. One approach involves controlling zinc ion transport to prevent localized deposition； for instance,composite membranes incorporating inorganic fillers have been studied to block dendrite growth physically [15].Another strategy focuses on suppressing the crossover of ferrocyanide ions by employing ion-selective membranes with tailored pore structures or surface functionalization.Anion exchange membranes (AEMs) have been developed to selectively transport hydroxide ions while blocking negatively charged active species, thereby minimizing cross-contamination during cycling [14,16-18]. Researchers have attempted to develop the membrane by using functionalized polymers with diverse cationic groups,such as quaternary ammoniums, imidazoliums, phosphoniums,and guanidinium.

Additionally, some researchers have attempted to develop a negatively charged membrane to suppress zinc dendrite accumulation at the anode by utilizing repulsion between negatively charged groups,such as sulfonate groups and phosphate groups,and zincate ions $( \mathrm { Z n ( O H ) } _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - } )$ [19,20].Building upon these developments,this study focused on optimizing a sulfonated poly (ether ether) ketone (SPEEK)-based membrane for application in AZIFBs.SPEEK offers several advantages, including high chemical and thermomechanical stability,good oxidation resistance,and cost-effectiveness [21-24]．Also,the negatively charged sulfonic groups in SPEEK contribute to mitigating zinc dendrite formation by inducing electrostatic repulsion against zincate ions in the alkaline electrolyte,thereby reducing the accumulation of zincate ions near the membrane surface during cycling [19].However,it exhibits chemical instabilityunderalkaline conditions due to degradation of the sulfonic acid groups and backbone cleavage [25,26].To enhance the mechanical and chemical stability of the membrane in an alkaline environment,polysulfone (PS) was introduced into the SPEEK matrix [27,28].Polysulfone is a widely used thermoplastic known for its excellent mechanical strength, thermal resistance,and chemical stability.Nevertheless,its inherent hydrophobicity can lead to membrane fouling and reduced ion transport efficiency.To balance these characteristics,we systematically fabricated the SPEEK/PS (SPK/PS) membrane with the optimized ratio to tailor membrane performance for AZIFB operation.The optimized membrane composition was selected based on structural integrity,ion transport properties,and compatibility with alkaline electrolyte conditions.

# 2.2.Synthesis of sodium zincate

Sodium zincate $\mathrm { ( N a _ { 2 } Z n ( O H ) _ { 4 } ) }$ was synthesized following previously published papers [29]. $5 . 8 2 ~ \mathrm { g }$ of $\mathrm { Z n O }$ was weighed on the evaporating dish. The $4 0 \%$ NaOH solution was poured into it. The evaporating dish was heated at $1 5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ overnight to evaporate water.After that,white powder was collected.

# 2.3．Synthesis of SPEEK

SPEEK was sulfonated from the PEEK polymer.To synthesize SPEEK, $1 5 g$ of PEEK was dried overnight at $6 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ After that,PEEK and $2 0 0 \mathrm { m L }$ of sulfuric acid were poured into the three-neck round flask. The reaction proceeded at $6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for an adjusted time with stirring.After the reaction, the vigorous solution was poured into DI water with stirring, and the mixture was continuously washed until the pH became neutral.

The synthesized SPEEK was analyzed by $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ -NMR to confirm its degree of sulfonation (DS). The DS was calculated by the following equation [30].

$$
\frac { \mathrm { P e a k \ a r e a \ ( H } _ { s } ) } { \sum \mathrm { p e a k \ a r e a \ ( H } _ { x } ) } { = } \frac { y } { 1 2 - 2 y }
$$

Here, $\mathbf { y }$ represents the fraction of the repeat units substituted with the sulfonic acid group $\mathrm { ( - } S 0 _ { 3 } H )$ among all the repeating units in the polymer. The proton signal denoted as $\mathbf { H } _ { \mathbf { x } }$ corresponds to all proton signals except the one adjacent to the $- S O _ { 3 } \mathrm { H }$ group,which was represented by $\mathbf { H } _ { s }$ .DS was calculated as ${ \bf y } ^ { * } 1 0 0 ( \% )$

# 2.4.Membrane fabrication

The membrane fabrication procedure is demonstrated in Fig.S1.The SPK/PS membrane was fabricated by blending two polymers,SPEEK (SPK) and Polysulfone (PS).Each polymer was separately dissolved in DMAc at $7 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } .$ After complete dissolution, the solutions were mixed and stirred at $7 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 1 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ .The solution was cast into the Petri dish and cured in the vacuum oven with a gradually increasing temperature: $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 1 0 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ ， $9 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ \textrm { \scriptsize 1 h } _ { \cdot }$ and $1 0 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 1 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ ，After curing,the fabricated membrane was peeled off and stored in deionized water. The fabricated membrane was designated as SPK/PS- $x _ { i }$ ，where $x$ represents the weight percentage of PS.The average thickness of the membrane is ${ \sim } 5 0 \pm 2 \mu \mathrm { m }$

# 2.5．Alkaline stability test

The alkaline stability of the membrane was measured using an ex-situ immersion test.The membrane was cut into $2 \times 2 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ size,and immersed in 4 M NaOH solution at $5 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } .$ The degradation of the membrane was measured by $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ -NMR and FT-IR (Thermo Scientific，Nicolet Continuum).

# 2.6. Permeability test

Ion permeability test was conducted using a diffusion cell. $2 0 ~ \mathrm { m L }$ of $0 . 4 \mathrm { M } \mathrm { K } _ { 4 } \mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { 6 }$ and $3 . 0 \mathrm { ~ M ~ N a O H }$ solution, and $2 0 ~ \mathrm { m L }$ of $0 . 4 \mathrm { ~ M ~ K _ { 2 } S O _ { 4 } }$ and $3 . 0 \mathrm { M N a O H }$ solution were used and they were filled into the left and right compartments,respectively.Every $^ { 1 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ ,1 mL of the sample was collected from the right cell component,and another $1 \ \mathrm { m L }$ of fresh $\mathrm { K _ { 2 } S O _ { 4 } }$ solution was added to the right cell. The collected samples were diluted three times and measured using UV-Vis (Cary 50oo UV-Vis-NIR).

The permeability of the ferrocyanide anion was calculated by the following equation.

$$
\mathbf { V _ { B } } \frac { \mathbf { d C _ { B } ( t ) } } { \mathbf { d t } } { = } \mathbf { A } \frac { \mathbf { p } } { \mathbf { L } } ( \mathbf { C _ { A } } - \mathbf { C _ { B } ( t ) } )
$$

${ \bf V _ { B } }$ represents the solution volume in the right compartment, $\bf C _ { B } ( t )$ represents the concentration of $\mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { 6 } ^ { 4 - }$ in the right reservoir, and $\mathbf { C _ { A } }$ represents the concentration in the left reservoir.A represents the effective area of the membrane,and L represents the thickness of the membrane.p represents the permeability of $\mathrm { F e ( C N ) } _ { 6 } ^ { 4 - }$ against the membrane.

# 2.7. Ion conductivity test

Ion conductivity of the membrane was measured by Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy(EIS) analysis (Bio-Logic VSP Potentiostat). Before taking EIS measurement,membranes need to be cut and soaked in 3 M NaOH solution.To measure the EIS test, the cell was assembled without electrodes and filled with 3 MNaOH solution on both sides.And then,EIS was measured with the frequency range from $1 0 0 0 \mathrm { k H z }$ to 10 mHz.The ion conductivity of the membrane was calculated by the following equation.

$$

Also,ion selectivity was calculated by dividing ion conductivity by ion permeability.

# 3．Results and discussion

# 3.1．Optimization of SPEEK

First,the degree of sulfonation (DS) of SPEEK was optimized by varying the sulfonation time and temperature (Fig.2a).The synthesized SPEEK was named SPK- $x ,$ where $x$ represents the calculated DS of the SPEEK.During the sulfonation procedure,a sulfuric acid group was introduced into the polymer backbone,and the DS was determined by comparing the integrated areas of $\mathrm { H } _ { s }$ (proton adjacent to the $- S O _ { 3 } \mathrm { H }$ group) and $\mathrm { H } _ { \mathrm { x } }$ (all other aromatic protons) in the $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ -NMR spectra.

The SPK membranes with different DS values were fabricated and subjected to 50-cycle performance tests in AZIFBs (Fig. 2b). Among them,the SPK-7O membrane exhibited the highest energy eficiency (EE)of $8 5 . 1 \%$ ，outperforming SPK-50 $( 7 3 . 7 \% )$ and SPK-60 $( 7 7 . 5 \% )$ . However,when the DS exceeded $8 5 \%$ ,a decline in performance was observed,with SPK-85 showing a lower EE of $8 0 . 0 \%$ (Fig. S2). This decline is attributed to excessive membrane swelling at high DS,which can deteriorate mechanical stability and increase ionic crossover during operation.

Based on the results of the DS optimization,the SPK-7O,which exhibited the highest energy efficiency,was selectedas the base polymer for the blend membrane.For simplicity,the SPK-7O is hereafter referred to as SPK.Using the optimized SPK,SPK/PS blend membranes were fabricated by incorporating varying amounts of polysulfone，as described in the previous section.

# 3.2. Characterization of membrane

The cross-sectional morphology of the membranes was examined by FE-SEM to investigate the internal structure and compatibility between SPEEK and polysulfone components (Fig.S3).The pristine SPK membrane (Fig.S3a) exhibits a dense and homogeneous cross-sectional structure without visible phase separation,characteristic of a singlecomponent polymer membrane.In contrast, the SPK/PS-1O membrane (Fig.S3b-c) displayed a distinct phase-separation morphology with spherical PS-rich domains dispersed throughout the SPEEK matrix.This micro-phase separation is typical of immiscible or partially miscible polymer blends and arises from the thermodynamic incompatibility between the hydrophilic SPEEK and relatively hydrophobic PS components during the membrane casting process.The magnified image (Fig.S3c) clearly reveals the uniform distribution of the PS domain, embedded within the continuous SPEEK phase.This matrix-dispersed morphology is beneficial for the membrane performance: the continuous SPEEK phase provides pathways for ion transport,while the dispersed PS domains contribute mechanical reinforcement and dimensional stability.

In Fig.3,the chemical properties of the membrane were evaluated by $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ -NMR andFT-IR analysis.In the $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ -NMR spectra (Fig. 3a), there is no difference in the intensity of the $\mathrm { H } _ { s }$ peak between the SPK-70 and the SPK/PS-10.

It means that PS does not influence the $- S O _ { 3 } \mathrm { H }$ group of the SPEEK. Afterassessing alkaline stability over 15 days,some degradation of the ${ \cdot } S O _ { 3 } \mathrm { H }$ groups was observed.Although the SPK membrane showed a decreased intensity of the $\mathrm { H } _ { s }$ proton peak,the SPK/PS-1O membrane maintained its intensity.For easier comparison, the DS of the before and after samples was calculated (Table S1).The SPK-7O membrane showed $5 8 . 6 \%$ DS after the test with a $1 2 . 8 \%$ decrease,but the SPK/PS-10 membrane maintained the DS,showing $6 7 . 8 \%$ DS after the test. Such phenomena imply that the SPK membrane can maintain its sulfuric acid group when it is blended with the PS.Here,PS acts as a mechanical shield against alkaline features,thereby prohibiting degradation by blocking hydroxide anions from attacking the SPEEK matrix.Also,the degradation ratio was calculated based on the DMSO-d6 peak (Table S2).When comparing the integration ratio,the SPK/PS-10 membrane maintained its backbone more than the SPK-7O membrane. It means that blending SPEEK with PS can enhance not only the stability of sulfuric acid groups but also the stability of the backbone in the alkaline condition.

Even in the FT-IR spectra (Fig.3b),there are no significant changes between the membranes before the alkaline stability test.However, distinct differences in peak intensities were observed between the membranes before and after the alkaline stability test.The peaks located at approximately 1650,1590,and $1 4 9 0 ~ \mathrm { { c m } ^ { - 1 } }$ are associated with the aromatic backbone of SPEEK,which comes from the PEEK backbone. Compared to the SPK membrane,the SPK/PS-1O membrane showed better retention of these peaks,indicating enhanced backbone stability after alkaline exposure.The peaks in the range of $1 3 2 0 { \cdot } 1 2 5 0 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ and 1150-1250 $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ correspond to the symmetric and asymmetric stretching of sulfone $\scriptstyle \left( 0 = \mathbf { S } = \mathbf { O } \right)$ ）and sulfonic acid groups, respectively, while those in the $1 1 5 0 { \cdot } 1 0 8 0 \ \mathrm { ~ c m } ^ { - 1 }$ range are attributed to ether (C-O-C) bonds [31].These characteristic absorptions of the SPK/PS-10 contributed to improved chemical stability of both the backbone and the functional groups,without impacting the structure of the SPEEK matrix. In addition,the broad peak around $3 4 0 0 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ ，assigned to $\mathrm { O - H }$ stretching in sulfonic acid groups,exhibited a noticeable increase in the SPK membrane after the test,implying the loss of $- S \mathrm { O _ { 3 } H }$ groups. Such degradation may negatively affect ion conductivity and cycling performance.

The mechanical properties of the membrane were also evaluated,as summarized in Fig. 4 and Table S3. First, Fig. 4a and Table S4 present the results of the ion permeability test.After $1 4 4 \ \mathrm { h }$ of testing, the SPK and SPK/PS-1O membranes exhibited minimal increases in ferrocyanide ion concentration $\mathrm { \Omega } ^ { } ( 0 . 0 5 6 \ \mathrm { m M }$ and $0 . 0 5 4 ~ \mathrm { m M }$ ,respectively),indicating excellent barrier properties.In contrast, the commercial Nafion membrane (NRE2l2) exhibited rapid ion crossover,reaching $1 . 2 6 \mathrm { m M }$ within $^ { 1 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ and continuing to increase to $2 . 4 3 \ \mathrm { m M }$ at $1 4 4 \ \mathrm { h }$

Ion conductivity values were measured and used to calculate ion selectivity (Fig.4b and Fig. S4). The SPK/PS membrane exhibited a conductivity of $1 . 8 3 \mathrm { m S c m } ^ { - 1 }$ ,while SPK and NRE212 showed $4 . 1 7 \mathrm { m s }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ and $8 . 3 0 \mathrm { m } S \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ ,respectively.Based on the ratio of conductivity to ion permeability at $^ { 1 4 4 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ ,the calculated ion selectivity values were $0 . 3 7 6 \times 1 0 ^ { 8 } \mathrm { m } S \mathrm { m i n } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 3 }$ for NRE212, $4 7 . 7 \times 1 0 ^ { 8 } \mathrm { m } { \mathrm { s } } \mathrm { m i n } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 3 }$ for the SPK, and $4 6 . 0 \ \times \ 1 0 ^ { 8 } \ \mathrm { m } { \cal S } \ \mathrm { m i n } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 3 }$ for the SPK/PS-10 membrane, demonstrating that SPK and SPK/PS-1O membranes exhibit significantly higher ion selectivity compared to the commercial NRE212 membrane. After the alkaline stability test,the SPK/PS-1O membrane relatively maintained its ion conductivity $( 1 . 1 4 ~ \mathrm { m S ~ c m } ^ { - 1 } )$ ,which comes from its chemical stability,although the SPK membrane showed considerably decreased conductivity $( 0 . 8 2 \mathrm { m } S \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 } )$ (Fig. S4 and Table S3).

Water uptake and swelling degree results (Fig.4c and Table S3) further support the dimensional stability of the membranes.The SPK membrane exhibited the highest water uptake $( 4 3 . 9 ~ \% )$ and swelling degree $( 1 4 . 9 \% )$ due to its strong hydrophilic nature.In comparison, the SPK/PS-10 membrane showed lower water uptake $( 3 7 . 4 \% )$ and a reduced swelling degree $( 1 3 . 9 \% )$ ,indicating that the incorporation of hydrophobic PS helped mitigate excessive water absorption while maintaining ionic pathways.Contact angle measurements (Fig.4e and Fig.S5) revealed moderate hydrophilic surfaces,with values of $9 4 . 9 ^ { \circ }$ for NRE212, $9 1 . 3 ^ { \circ }$ for SPK,and $9 2 . 0 ^ { \circ }$ for SPK/PS-10,indicating comparable wettability among the samples.

The mechanical strength of the membrane was evaluated using a tensile strength test (Fig.4d and Table S3).The SPK/PS-1O membrane exhibited the highest maximum stress of $2 7 . 8 2 \ \mathrm { M P a }$ ,followed by SPK (23.77 MPa)and NRE212 (22.78 MPa),confirming the reinforcing effect of PS in the blend membrane.

These results collectively highlight the advantages of the SPK/PS-10 membrane in balancing ion selectivity,mechanical strength,and dimensional stability.While the ion conductivity of SPK/PS-1O is lower than that of NRE2l2 and SPK,its ion permeability is significantly reduced,resulting in a much higher ion selectivity than that of the NRE121 membrane.

# 3.3． ZIRFBs single-cell performance

The cycling performance of the membranes was evaluated under varying state of charge (SOC) conditions at a fixed current density of 40 $\mathbf { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ (Fig. 5a-c and Fig. S6). At $8 0 \%$ SOC, the SPK/PS-10 membrane exhibited the highest energy efficiency (EE) of $8 9 . 0 \%$ ，outperforming both the SPK membrane $( 4 9 . 2 \% )$ and the commercial NRE212 membrane $( 8 5 . 5 \% )$ ．Notably，at $1 0 0 \%$ SOC，the SPK/PS-1O membrane maintained a high coulombic efficiency (CE) of $9 3 . 0 \%$ and EE of $8 7 . 7 \%$ ， whereas the SPK membrane underwent mechanical failure and was unable to sustain operation.In contrast, the NRE212 membrane showed a considerable drop in performance at $1 0 0 \%$ SOC,with a CE of $7 2 . 0 \%$ and an EE of $5 9 . 1 \%$ .Also,among the SPK/PS $x$ membranes (Fig. S6), the SPK/PS-1O showed the most stable cyclability even at $1 0 0 \%$ SOC.

Based on this experiment, the SOC was set at $5 0 \%$ ,and the cycle test was conducted for 15O cycles at a current density of $8 0 \ \mathrm { \ m A } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ (Fig.5d-f). The SPK/PS-10 membrane exhibited a higher average efficiency $( 9 8 . 2 \%$ of CE and $8 1 . 7 9 \%$ of EE) compared to the SPK membrane $( 9 8 . 1 \%$ of CE and $7 8 . 9 \%$ of EE).Also,as shown in Fig. S7,polarization curves obtained under various SOC conditions indicate that the SPK/PS-10 membrane exhibits superior voltage stability compared to the NRE212 and SPK membranes.This indicates that the SPK/PS-1O membrane is more effective in minimizing voltage losses over prolonged operation.

Additionally,voltage profiles (Fig.5g-i) showed that the SPK/PS-10 membrane maintained a more stable charge-discharge voltage with lower overpotentials during cycling.Although the SPK/PS-1O membrane exhibited lower ion conductivity,its enhanced stability in alkaline conditions likely reduced membrane degradation and suppressed changes in interfacial resistance during operation.These findings further confirm the enhanced long-term electrochemical stability of the SPK/PS-10 membrane under demanding operational conditions.

To comprehensively evaluate the electrochemical performance of the membranes,additional tests were conducted,including rate performance,power density tests,and self-discharge measurements (Fig.6). Asshownin Fig.6a-c,the rate capability test was evaluated at various current densities ranging from 10 to $1 6 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ . The SPK/PS-10 membrane demonstrated superior performance across all current densities,maintaining higher eficiencies even at high current densities up to $1 6 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ .In contrast, the SPK membrane showed significant performance degradation at current densities above $8 0 \ \mathrm { m A } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ,with efficiency dropping below $7 0 \%$ .Additionally,upon returning to the low current density of $1 0 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ,the SPK exhibited a dramatic decrease in its efficiencies,indicating a crossover of active species (Fig.S8),whereas the SPK/PS-1O maintained its original efficiencies without a change in electrolyte color.This behavior suggests that the SPK membrane cannot effectively suppress current-induced crossover,where the applied current accelerates ion migration through the membrane,exacerbating active material permeation [32].The excellent rate capability of the SPK/PS-1O is attributed to its maintained low internal resistance and superior structural stability even under high current operation.

As shown in the polarization curves measured at $5 0 \%$ SOC (Fig. 6d), the SPK/PS-1O membrane achieves a maximum power density of $4 7 1 . 0 7 \mathrm { m W } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ,which is approximately $1 3 . 4 \%$ higher than that of the SPK membrane $( 4 1 5 . 2 3 \mathrm { m W } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ . The lower overpotential and higher limiting current density of the SPK/PS-1O indicate reduced internal resistance and improved mass transport,consistent with its enhanced membrane properties.

Additionally,as shown in Fig. 6e,the SPK membrane exhibited rapid voltage decay and failed approximately 16 h due to instability at high SOC,as shown in Fig.5.In contrast, the SPK/PS-1O membrane maintained a stable open-circuit voltage for over $^ { 2 6 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ ，demonstrating exceptional resistance to dendrite penetration and superior structural integrity that minimizes self-discharge.This improvement in operational stability at high SOC validates the effectiveness of the PS incorporation for practical long-duration energy storage applications.

In the long-term stability test (Fig.7a),the SPK/PS-10 membrane retained $8 0 . 0 2 \%$ of its energy effciency (EE) over 7Oo cycles,whereas the SPK membrane exhibited a sharp decline in performance around 100 cycles.Furthermore,at the $4 5 0 \mathrm { { t h } }$ cycle,the SPK membrane showed a significantly higher overpotential of $6 0 8 ~ \mathrm { { m V } }$ compared to $4 2 9 ~ \mathrm { m V }$ for the SPK/PS-1O membrane (Fig.7b).This result indicates that the incorporation of the PS contributes to reducing internal resistance by enhancing electrochemical stability during extended cycling. The introduction of hydrophobic PS into the SPEEK backbone effectively suppresses excessive swelling under the alkaline conditions, thereby maintaining stable ion transport pathways for $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ and $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ ions and preserving low internal resistance during prolonged operation. Furthermore,the enhanced alkaline stability resulting from PS addition helps maintain the structural integrity of the membrane matrix,preventing degradation of ionic conductivity over time.Consequently, the SPK/PS-10 blend membrane exhibits significantly lower overpotential compared to the pristine SPK membrane,demonstrating superior electrochemical stability and performance retention during long-term

To further investigate the structural integrity of the membranes,we analyzed the membrane surfaces before and after cycling using FE-SEM (Fig.7c-h and Fig. S9).As shown in Fig. 7d and g, the SPK membrane exhibited large pores on the anode-facing surface after cycling,whereas the SPK/PS-1O membrane retained a pore-free morphology. Similarly, on the cathodic side (Fig.7e and h),numerous pores were observed on the cycled SPK membrane,while the SPK/PS-1O membrane remained intact without visible structural damage.Also,as shown in the crosssectional images (Fig.S9),the SPK membrane displayed significant internal structural degradation and reduced thickness after cycling,while the SPK/PS-1O membrane maintained its uniform,dense cross-sectional morphology with well-preserved phase-separated structure,demonstrating superior structural integrity throughout the membrane.These results suggest that incorporating PS into the SPK matrix effectively enhances the mechanical and electrochemical stability of the membrane under long-term operation.

To complement the structural analysis,Fig.S1O illustrates the surface morphology of zinc deposition on anode surfaces after cycling.As shown in Fig.SlOa, the SPK membrane leads to sharp and irregular dendrite-like crystal formations, indicating uncontrolled zinc nucleation and growth.Furthermore,the SPK/PS-10 membrane exhibits modified surface properties due to increased hydrophobicity and altered pore structure upon PS incorporation. The addition of non-polar polysulfone (PS) to SPEEK reduces ionic conductivity and slows down the transport kinetics of $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ and $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ ions,preventing rapid ion accumulation at localized sites and promoting gradual and uniform ion distribution across the membrane surface.Consequently，as demonstrated in Fig.SlOb,the zinc deposition exhibits a relatively more uniform and compact surface structure. The controlled nucleation and growth process facilitated by the modified membrane properties results in suppressed dendrite formation and enhanced zinc deposition uniformity, which contributes to improved cycling stability and reduced internal resistance during long-term operation.

# 4.Conclusion

In summary,we have fabricated a blend membrane with high mechanical and chemical stability for alkaline Zn-Fe flow batteries.By incorporating PS with SPK,the SPK/PS-1O membrane obtained good mechanical properties with enhanced dimensional stability.Although the ion conductivity was slightly lower than that of the pristine SPK membrane,the SPK/PS-1O membrane showed enhanced chemical stability with less degradation in the polymer backbone and sulfonic acid groups under alkaline conditions.During the AZIFB operation,the SPK/ PS-10 membrane demonstrated stable cyclability $( 8 1 . 8 \%$ of EE)at 80 $\mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ of current density compared to the SPK membrane $( 7 8 . 8 2 \%$ of EE).The SPK/PS-1O membrane also exhibited superior rate capability, maintaining $> 8 0 \%$ energy efficiency up to $1 6 0 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ,and achieved ${ \sim } 1 3 . 4 \%$ higher maximum power density $( 4 7 1 . 0 7 \mathrm { \ m W } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ compared to the SPK membrane.Furthermore,the cell with the SPK/PS-1O performs stably over 7Oo cycles,showing higher efficiency $( \sim 8 0 . 0 \%$ of EE) at an $8 0 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ current density than the cell with the SPK membrane $( \sim 7 5 . 2 \%$ of EE),due to its enhanced chemical stability.The strategy of blending PS with SPK in this work provides a practical approach to designing durable and stable membranes for long-term operation in alkaline zinc-based battery systems.
