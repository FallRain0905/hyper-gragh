Iron-chromium redox flow battery (ICRFB)offers anatractive technological pathway for low-cost,grid-scale energy storage integrated with renewable energy sources. However,the slow $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ redox kinetics at the anode and the coupled parasitic hydrogen evolution reaction (HER),particularly at high current densities, remain major obstacles to their practical application.This study reports alayered structure coupled with oxygen vacancy(OV)engineering,which boosts Cr redox activation and suppresses the HER to fully unlock thecatalytic potential of halogenated bismuth (BiOX, $\mathbf { X } = \mathbf { C } \mathbf { l }$ ,Br,I).The designed BiOX catalyst leverages lattice distortion induced by halogen elements to create abundant OVs,while the asymmetric Bi-O-Cr bridging structure accelerates electron transfer during the $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ conversion. The mesoporous layered framework ensures rapid accessibility of redox-active species.By in situ anchoring this OV-rich BiOX catalyst onto porous carbon fiber,a high-performance electrode is successfully fabricated. Consequently,the ICRFB with OVs-rich BiOX-modified electrodes achieves an energy efficiency of $8 4 . 5 \%$ at $1 4 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ ,with exceptional stability over 40o cycles.

# 1．Introduction

The global transition to renewable energy sources,including solar, wind,and hydropower,represents one of the defining challenges of the 2lst century [1,2]．As an emerging energy storage technology, iron-chromium redox flow batteries (ICRFBs) are regarded as one of the strong contenders for future energy storage devices due to their flexible design,long service life,and large-scale energy storage capability [2-4]. However, the commercialization of ICRFB is hindered by two key bottlenecks. One is the sluggish redox kinetics of the $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ couple at the negative electrode,which is characterized by high overpotential and low reaction rates and limits power density.The other is parasitic reactions such as the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) that cause severe battery degradation. These two factors synergistically restrict the energy efficiency and cycling stability of the battery [5-7].

Carbon-based materials,particularly carbon cloth (CC),are widely used as electrodes in redox flow batteries (RFBs) owing to their electrical conductivity,porosity,and chemical inertness [2,4].However, their low intrinsic catalytic activity and hydrophobicity limit the rate of redox reactions [8-lO].To date,approaches such as surface oxidation,heteroatom doping,and deposition of metal or metal oxide catalysts have been explored to address this limitation [6,7,11-l3]．While some progress has been made,these methods often involve trade-offs: surface oxidation reduces electrical conductivity,doping may introduce structural defects that compromise stability,and metal-based catalysts are typically costly or prone to leaching during long-term cycling [2,5,6,14].

In recent years,layered bismuth-based materials have attracted attention in electrocatalysis due to their unique electronic structures, tunable surface chemistry,and earth abundance [15,l6]．Its open interlayer channels and large specific surface area can significantly expose surface active atoms,providing sufficient active sites for electrochemical reactions.The layered framework can construct a multilevel layered synergistic structure with porous carbon substrates, forming continuous electrolyte mass transfer channels and electron transfer paths,effectively reducing concentration polarization and charge transfer resistance [17].Bismuth oxyhalides (BiOX, $\mathbf { X } = \mathbf { C l } .$ Br,I) possess a typical layered structure: $\mathbf { B i } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { O } ^ { 2 - }$ form ${ \mathrm { \mathbf { B i O } ^ { + } } }$ layers, and halide ions $\left( \mathbf { X } ^ { - } \right)$ stack with ${ \mathrm { \mathbf { B i O } ^ { + } } }$ layers via weak van der Waals forces, resulting in a layered arrangement of $\mathrm { \small { [ B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } ] } ^ { 2 + } } \mathrm { X } _ { 2 } \mathrm { { ^ - } }$ . This open twodimensional layered crystal structure endows them with unique physical and chemical properties,such as a high specific surface area and a large number of atoms exposed on the surface [18].Meanwhile,OVs can be easily introduced into this structure through heat treatment,defect engineering,or electrochemical processes [19-22]. Owing to their high surface area,good chemical stability,and favorable band alignment, BiOX shows promise in applications ranging from photocatalysis to lithium-ion batteries.Through optimization of electronic structures,an increase in active sites,acceleration of mass transfer,and synergistic effects of vacancies,they can significantly enhance electrocatalytic performance and cycling stability [23-26].

Owing to these prominent characteristics,BiOX exhibits substantial potential as a superior photocatalyst and electrocatalyst [18,27].The introduction of OVs on the surface of BiOX can further enhance their catalytic activity [28].Introducing OVs into BiOX is promising to modulate its local electronic structure, thereby improving the inherent catalytic activity of the BiOX[29].OVs can affect the charge transfer process by regulating the band structure of metal oxides.Furthermore, OVs can also serve as activation sites for reactant species,where localized electrons may alter the coordination environment of reactant species [3O].Consequently, OVs play a vital role in the reaction kinetics and catalytic mechanisms of $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ .Huang et al. showed that such modifications can optimize the electronic structure of the electrode surface,reduce charge transfer resistance,and accelerate the kinetics of reactive species.In transition metal oxides,OVs regulate electronic states via atomic-scale polarization,enhancing ion adsorption and activation.At the surface-pore interface,OVs can also decouple the activation and mass transfer processes of redox reactions,enabling efficient operation under high current densities and comprehensively improving the overall performance of the battery [31].

Pan et al. prepared metal-organic framework (MOF)-derived highentropy oxide (HEO)-modified graphite felt,and Demeku etal.synthesized (BiZrMoWCeLa) $O _ { 2 }$ high-entropy oxides; both utilized OVs to provide abundant active sites for vanadium redox reactions,accelerated charge transfer,reduced interfacial resistance,and exhibited excellent kinetic performance and stability [32,33].Li et al.demonstrated via first-principles studies that MXenes with OVs (e.g., $\mathrm { V } _ { \mathrm { o } } { - } S \mathrm { c } _ { 3 } \mathrm { C } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } )$ significantly enhance the adsorption energy of active species and exhibit more pronounced electron transfer,which can effectively promote the redox kinetics of electrolyte active species,offering a potentially efficient catalytic pathway for optimizing the performance of RFBs electrodes [34].Demeku et al.fabricated Cu-doped $\mathrm { C o } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 }$ nanowire-modified electrodes,through the synergistic effect of OVs and metal components,these electrodes enhanced hydrophilicity and conductivity, accelerated electron transfer,increased active sites,and displayed excellent long-term charge-discharge cycling stability [35].

Despite these attributes,research on integrating BiOX into RFBs electrodes remains unexplored,and this work provides an opportunity to bridge this knowledge gap.In this study, we introduce a structurallyengineered surface catalyst for the first time aimed at synergistically utilizing the high catalytic activity of bismuth and the unique structural advantages of BiOX to create a layered surface catalyst，thereby enhancing the overall performance of RFBs.By incorporating an optimal amount of OVs,we further optimized the catalytic properties of BiOX. The introduction of OVs facilitates the more efficient adsorption of iron/ chromium ions,thus significantly improving the electrocatalytic performance of the BiOX/TCC electrode.As shown in Fig.la-b,we prepared BiOX-functionalized carbon cloth electrodes with different structural characteristics by introducing KCl,KBr,and KI precursors,successfully achieving halogen-dependent system regulation [36].The study reveals that the key lies in achieving a decoupling of catalytic activity and mass transfer through hierarchical interfaces,from atomic-level catalytic surfaces to mesoporous and microporous structures. The halogendependent surface chemistry of BiOX determines its interaction with redox species in the ICRFB electrolyte.Iodide,with the lowest electronegativity, promotes surface electron enrichment, thereby enhancing the adsorption of redox intermediates.In contrast, chloride, with higher electronegativity,stabilizes anionic species but suppresses the electron transfer process [26,36-38]. Experimental results show that the ICRFB using BiOI-modified carbon cloth as the electrode maintains an excellent energy efficiency (EE)of up to $7 7 \%$ at a current density of $2 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ significantly outperforming the ICRFB using the original carbon cloth electrode $( \mathrm { E E } ~ = ~ 6 7 . 9 \% )$ .Moreover，this electrode demonstrated outstanding durability after more than 40o cycles,owing to the stability of its surface structure in terms of mechanical and electrochemical performance.By addressing key challenges in electrode kinetics,this study not only significantly enhances the performance of RFBs but also provides a new design concept for catalyst integration in electrochemical energy storage systems.The BiOX/TCC electrode represents a cost-effective and scalable solution,capable of accelerating the application of RFBs in grid-scale energy storage and driving the global transition to a low-carbon future.

# 2.1．Materials

All chemicals used were of analytical grade and can be used without further purification: Bismuth Nitrate Pentahydrate $( \mathrm { B i } ( \mathrm { N O } _ { 3 } ) _ { 3 } . 5 \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }$ $\geq 9 9 . 0 \%$ ,Aladdin Scientific),Potassium Bromide (KBr, $2 9 9 . 0 \%$ ,Aladdin Scientific),Potassium Iodide (KI, $2 9 9 . 0 0 \%$ ,Aladdin Scientific),Potassium Chloride (KCl, $\geq 9 9 . 5 \%$ ,Aladdin Scientific),and Ethylene Glycol (EG,anhydrous, $9 9 . 8 \%$ ,Aladdin Scientific).

# 3．Results and discussion

# 3.1．Morphological characterizations of BiOX catalysts

We employed Grazing Incidence Wide-Angle X-ray Scattering (GIWAXS) to investigate the surface structure of thermally treated carbon cloth electrodes and BiOX/TCC electrodes.As shown in Fig.lc, the 1D GIWAXS patterns reveal the presence of the (O11),(11O),and (012) crystal planes in the BiOCl/TCC electrode.Meanwhile, the BiOBr/TCC electrode displays distinct (O12)and (11O) crystal planes.The BiOI/TCC electrode exhibits diferent (O12) and (11O) crystal planes,indicating the formation of the BiOI phase,which is further corroborated by the 2D GIWAXs data in the corresponding patterns. Surface morphology analysis of the modified carbon cloth electrodes was conducted via scanning electron microscopy (SEM),with representative images shown in Fig.2. The unmodified carbon cloth electrode (RCC) has a smooth surface (Fig. S2a-b) and no obvious defects. The contact angle test (Fig.S2c) shows that the electrode surface has strong hydrophobicity,which will greatly hinder mass transfer and exhibit chemical inertness.The lack of reactivity results in low efficiency of RCC electrodes when used directly as reaction electrodes in flow batteries [4]．Fig.2a illustrates the morphology of the TCC following high-temperature activation of the RCC.The thermally treated electrode surface reveals distinct defects, which can be attributed to the decomposition of carbon on the electrode surface and the alterations in surface structure and composition under the high-temperature,oxygen-rich environment. High-temperature treatment modified the chemical inertness of the carbon cloth electrode and enhanced its afinity for polar molecules such as water, thereby significantly improving its surface performance [39].

However,basic surface activation treatment has a weak effect on enhancing the catalytic activity of the electrode surface and does not further improve the electrode's conductivity or reduce its resistance (Fig.S3).So we chose to use the BiOX catalyst to modify the electrode surface based on this.Fig.2b presents the surface microstructure of the BiOCl/TCC electrode.The as-prepared BiOCl features a flower-shaped microsphere structure constructed from nanosheets.As observed in Fig.2e,a large quantity of loose BiOCl nanoflower is loaded onto the carbon cloth electrode.Compared with the pristine TCC electrode,this configuration significantly enhances the energy efficiency (EE) of the ICRFB.Nevertheless,the thick bismuth catalyst particles restrict the diffusion of water molecules during charge-discharge processes,potentially causing electrode oxidation and catalyst particle detachment. This may also,to some extent, impede electron transfer and reduce the kinetics of the $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ redox pairs, thereby reducing battery efficiency [40,41].Fig.2c shows the surface microstructure of the BiOBr/TCC electrode.Compared to the looser catalyst particles on the BiOCl/TCC electrode,the surface structure of the BiOBr/TCC electrode is relatively more compact.As shown in Fig.2f, the BiOBr catalyst was successfully distributed on the surface of the BiOBr/TCC electrode.

Further comparison of SEM images reveals the surface structure of the BiOI/TCC electrode (Fig.2d).Ultrathin stacked nanosheets are uniformly distributed across its surface,with consistent thickness.These features provide abundant reactive sites,thereby enhancing the catalytic activity of the electrode.Concurrently,the ultrathin stacked nanosheets on the BiOI/TCC electrode surface demonstrate high stability,which can be attributed to the strengthened adhesion between the carbon cloth electrode and the catalyst,which is caused by the effect of ultra-thin stacked nanosheets formed via hydrothermal treatment. Consistent with the uniform and dense distribution of Bi elements on the electrode surface (Fig.2g),this further confirms the successful fabrication of the BiOI/TCC electrode.Furthermore,four-point probe resistance measurements show that among the BiOX/TCC electrodes,the BiOI/TCC electrode exhibits the lowest resistance compared to RCC and TCC electrodes,indicating its excellent electrical conductivity.The core reason lies in the ultrathin stacked nanosheet structure of BiOI,which shortens the electron transport path,and the strong adhesion between the nanosheets and carbon cloth reduces contact resistance,thereby synergistically enhancing electrical conductivity.

# 3.2. Characterization of BiOX catalysts

In order to gain further insight into how the unique $\mathrm { \small { [ B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } ] } ^ { 2 + } } \mathrm { X } _ { 2 } \mathrm { { ^ - } }$ layered structure of bismuth oxyhalide (BiOX) regulates the surface microstructural properties,Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET） measurements were conducted to evaluate the specific surface area (SSA) variations of the carbon cloth electrodes,as depicted in Fig.3a-d.The isotherms for BiOX/TCC electrodes (BiOCl/TCC,BiOBr/TCC,BiOI/TCC) exhibit markedly higher $\Nu _ { z }$ uptake compared to pristine thermally TCC, indicative of a substantial increase in specific surface area upon BiOX loading (Fig.3a). In the high relative pressure range $( \mathrm { P } / \mathrm { P } _ { 0 } \approx 0 . 8 – 1 . 0 )$ ， the steep rise of the adsorption curve conforms to the characteristics of type IV isotherms,corresponding to the capillary condensation phenomenon in the mesopores $\left( 2 \mathrm { - } 5 0 \mathrm { n m } \right)$ formedby the layered stacking of BiOX nanosheets.By contrast, the pristine TCC showed a nearly type III isotherm,attributable to the absence of such microporous architectures. The BET specific surface areas of the electrodes are summarized in Table S1.The results reveal that all BiOX-modified electrodes exhibit increased SSA compared to the pristine TCC,owing to the introduction of BiOX onto the carbon cloth surface.Notably,BiOI/TCC achieved the highest SSA of $1 . 9 7 3 \ \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 } / \mathrm { g }$ ，whereas the pristine TCC only exhibited $0 . { \overset { \cdot } { 9 } } 6 3 ~ \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 } / \mathrm { g }$ ,representing an approximate twofold increase. This superiority of BiOI/TCC may stem from the stronger van der Waals interactions between the $[ \mathrm { B i } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ] ^ { 2 + }$ layer and $\Gamma ^ { - }$ layer in its $[ \mathrm { B i } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ] ^ { 2 + } \mathrm { I } _ { 2 } ^ { - }$ structure,which can inhibit the agglomeration of BiOI nanosheets and retain more open porous structures.The enhanced SSA of BiOI/TCC not only provides abundant anchoring sites for $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ redox species but also offers a structural platform for the subsequent catalytic effects of OVs introduced via defect engineering,laying a dual foundation for accelerated reaction kinetics.

The differential pore volume (dV/dD) profiles (Fig.3b-c) reveal that BiOX modification introduces a more heterogeneous pore architecture relative to TCC.In the mesopore regime( $( \sim 0 . 3 \ – 2 \ \mathrm { n m }$ ,Fig.3c),BiO/TCC displays the most prominent peak (centered at ${ \sim } 0 . 9 \ \mathrm { n m }$ ), followed by BiOBr/TCC,whereas BiOCl/TCC shows a weaker signal. Such mesopore enrichment in BiOI/TCC is critical.The mesopores match the diffusion length scale of redox species in electrolytes,thus minimizing masstransport resistance,which is the key prerequisite for fast electrochemical kinetics.The cumulative pore volume of BiOI/TCC is notably larger than that of BiOBr/TCC,BiOCl/TCC,and pristine TCC (Fig.3d).A higher cumulative pore volume not only provides enhanced electrolyte storage capacity but also expands the accessible interfacial area for $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ redox reactions, both of which are key factors in accelerating reaction kinetics.BET characterization results confirm that BiOX modification,particularly BiOl,can effectively tailor the pore structure of carbon cloth by virtue of its intrinsic $\mathrm { \small { [ B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } ] } } ^ { 2 + } \mathrm { X } _ { 2 } { } ^ { - }$ layered advantage, endowing it with a larger SSA,more abundant mesopores,and a higher cumulative pore volume.These structural merits synergistically facilitate electrolyte penetration,ion diffusion,and active site exposure.More importantly,they establisha “physical scaffold"for the synergy between the layered structure and OVs.This structural foundation directly addresses the core challenges of sluggish $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ kinetics and poor mass transfer in ICRFB,thus highlighting the innovation of the BiOX-based electrode design in this work.

Raman spectroscopy was performed on the as-prepared carbon cloth electrode samples. As shown in Fig.3e, each sample exhibits a strong band at $1 3 5 3 \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ ,designated as the D band,which corresponds to the $\mathsf { s p } ^ { 3 }$ -hybridized disordered carbon structures in the carbon cloth.Additionally，a strong band at $1 5 8 0 ~ \mathrm { { c m } ^ { - 1 } }$ ，the $\mathbf { G }$ band,is observed, corresponding to the $\mathsf { s p } ^ { 2 }$ -hybridized ordered carbon structures. The $\mathrm { I _ { D } / }$ $\mathrm { I _ { G } }$ ratio for each sample (Fig.3e) reflects the degree of graphitization. Compared to the TCC electrode,the $\mathrm { I _ { D } / I _ { G } }$ ratios of BiOCl/TCC and BiOBr/TCC are reduced,indicating increased graphitization and decreased defect density.In contrast, the $\mathrm { I _ { D } / I _ { G } }$ ratio of the BiOI/TCC electrode increases,suggesting a more abundant defect density,which would provide additional active sites for electrolyte species.To confirm the presence of OVs on the surface of BiOX/TCC electrodes,electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) measurements were conducted (Fig. 3f). The enhanced EPR response in BiOX/TCC electrodes is primarily attributed to the presence of OVs with unpaired electrons,indicating that modification with BiOX induces the formation of abundant oxygen defects [42]. The EPR spectra exhibit a resonance signal at $g = 2 . 0 0 4$ ， which is ascribed to unpaired electrons trapped at surface OVs [43].This is because the larger atomic radius of $\Gamma ^ { - }$ weakens the interaction between the $\scriptstyle \left[ \mathrm { B i } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } \right] ^ { 2 + }$ layer and the $\Gamma ^ { - }$ layer, rendering the $[ \mathrm { B i } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ] ^ { 2 + } \mathrm { I } _ { 2 } ^ { - }$ structure more prone to generating OVs during defect engineering treatment.As electron enrichment centers,these abundant OVs can optimize the adsorption energy of $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ and $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ redox couples on the electrode surface,and form a synergy with the high SSA of the layered structure,thereby accelerating charge transfer kinetics and suppressing the parasitic HER.

# 3.3. Calculation and catalytic mechanism of oxygen vacancies

The stable adhesion of catalysts on the electrode surface is particularly critical for the durability of batteries in high-speed flowing electrolytes.The BiOCl/TCC structure exhibits a simple solvated structure and tends to form large aggregates.These aggregates are readily soluble in the electrolyte and difficult to stably adhere to the electrode surface, which is detrimental to the long-term stable operation of the battery.As shown in Fig.4a,the BiOBr/TCC structure shows an abnormal morphology and inferior cycling performance.Under high-speed electrolyte flow,this unfavorable structural morphology leads to unstable bonding between the catalyst and the electrode,making it prone to detachment and thus impairing the battery's cycling performance.In the BiOI/TCC structure,ultra-thin stacked nanosheets are orderly decorated on the electrode surface in a building block-like manner,with excellent structural stability. The ultra-thin stacked nanosheets are a characteristic feature of the $[ \mathrm { B i } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ] ^ { 2 + } \mathrm { I } _ { 2 } -$ layered structure,which exhibits strong interlayer interactions.These nanosheets are ordered in a brick-like fashion on the electrode surface,contributing to their exceptional stability.Additionally,the synergistic enhancement resulting from the abundant OVs introduced via defect engineering significantly strengthens the interfacial bonding.

Furthermore,to unravel the intrinsic synergy between BiOX's $\mathrm { \small { [ B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } ] } } ^ { 2 + } \mathrm { X } _ { 2 } ^ { - }$ layered structure and OVs,based on density functional theory (DFT) calculations on the mechanism,we elucidated at the atomic level the underlying mechanism by which BiOX promotes the reactivity of Crat the anode and suppresses the HER.DFT calculations (Fig.4b-e) show that the overlap area between Cr-3d and Bi-6p orbitals in BiOI is 8.21,significantly larger than that between $\mathrm { C r } { - } 3 \mathrm { d }$ and $\mathrm { C } { \cdot } 2 \mathrm { p }$ orbitals in TCC (5.55), between $\mathrm { C r } { - } 3 \mathrm { d }$ and Bi-6p orbitals in BiOCl(6.19), and between $\mathrm { C r } { - } 3 \mathrm { d }$ and Bi-6p orbitals in BiOBr(7.11).The enhanced orbital overlap arises from the orbital orientation induced by the layered structure and the electron redistribution mediated by OVs,a unique feature of BiOX-based catalysts,which fills the research gap in the field of flow batteries. When $\mathrm { C r } ( \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } ) _ { 5 } \mathrm { C l } ^ { 2 + }$ adsorbs onto the Bi-based catalyst surface,the increased orbital overlap results in stronger d-p orbital hybridization,which synergistically accelerates charge transfer in the anode $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ redox reaction. This breakthrough directly addresses

the kinetic bottleneck in ICRFBs.

To verify the role of OVs in improving reaction performance,we further performed DFT calculations without OVs (Fig.S5).The overlap area between Cr-3d and Bi-6p orbitals in BiOI without oxygen vacancies is 7.57,which is smaller than that in BiOI with oxygen vacancies. Similarly,the overlap areas in TCC (Cr-3d/C-2p:5.55), BiOCl (Cr-3d/Bi-6p:6.19),and BiOBr (Cr-3d/Bi-6p:7.11)are all smaller than those in their OVs-rich counterparts,further confirming the performance enhancement effect of OVs.The adsorption configurations and energies of $\mathrm { H } ^ { + }$ on different surfaces in the electrolyte are shown in Fig.4f. $\mathrm { H } ^ { + }$ exhibits a stronger adsorption energy on the BiOI surface $( - 2 . 6 8 ~ \mathrm { e V } )$ compared to TCC,BiOCl,and BiOBr surfaces,favoring the acquisition of electrons by $\mathrm { H } ^ { + }$ to form adsorbed species.Transition state calculations were used to simulate the migration of $\mathrm { H } ^ { + }$ on these surfaces (Fig. 4g-h). The HER on BiOI exhibits a higher activation energy barrier,making it more difficult for $\mathrm { H } ^ { + }$ to migrate and combine to form $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 }$ on the BiOI catalyst surface.This indicates that BiOI can effectively suppress HER compared to TCC,BiOCl,and BiOBr.

During charging, the EDL on the negative electrode surface is negatively charged, causing positively charged $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ ions to be atracted by the negative electrostatic field,migrate to the EDL region,and then gain electrons in the EDL to be reduced to $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ .However, the presence of specifically adsorbed $\mathrm { C l ^ { - } }$ in the outer Helmholtz plane (ohp) forms a tightly bound layer with a strong negative charge.The $\mathrm { C r } ( \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } ) _ { 5 } \mathrm { C l } ^ { 2 + }$ complex ion,due to its ligand $\mathrm { C l ^ { - } }$ and thick hydration shell,experiences electrostatic repulsion with the negative charge of the ohp,making it difficult to penetrate the ohp and enter the core reaction zone,which hinders the electron transfer pathway.

The introduction of the BiOI catalyst, especially its oxygen-rich vacancy characteristics,can break through this bottleneck through a dual mechanism.On the one hand,the OVs on the surface of BiOX act as electron-rich sites,precisely regulating the charge density on the electrode surface.During charging,the OVs can weaken the accumulation intensity of negative charges in the negative electrode EDL,reduce the negative potential gradient in the OHP region, thereby alleviating the electrostatic repulsion of $\mathrm { C r } ( \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } ) _ { 5 } \mathrm { C l } ^ { 2 + }$ complex ions and promoting their migration towards the inner side of OHP.On the other hand, due to the strong coordination interaction between BiOl and $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ polarizes the hydration shell of the complex ions,weaken the binding strength between $\mathrm { C l ^ { - } }$ ligands and water molecules,and prompt the dissociation of some $\mathrm { C l ^ { - } }$ and their detachment from the hydration shell,reducing the spatial hindrance of the complex ions and making them more easily penetrate OHP to reach the reactive sites.Additionally,the OVs in BiOI can also act as bridges for electron transfer,shortening the electron transfer distance between Cr ions and the electrode surface.Even if some complex ions are still near OHP,they can quickly complete electron exchange through OVs,significantly reducing the charge transfer resistance. Ultimately,this interfacial regulation effect effectively unblocks the redox reaction path of $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ and enhances the electrochemical kinetic performance of the electrode.Consequently，the reduction reaction $( \mathbf { C r } ^ { 3 + } \xrightarrow { } \mathbf { C r } ^ { 2 + } )$ proceeds sluggishly, accounting for the slow kinetics of the negative electrode reaction during charging.

# 3.5.Redox flow battery performance

To evaluate the performance of the as-prepared BiOX/TCC electrodes in practical applications,we integrated TCC,BiOCl/TCC,BiOBr/ TCC,and BiOI/TCC electrodes into redox flow battery prototypes for charge-discharge tests,aiming to further investigate,in particular, the behavior of the $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ redox couple at the electrode surface.Fig. 6a illustrates the structure of the prototype,which primarily consists of electrolyte reservoirs,current collectors,graphite plates,a membrane, and electrodes.During charging and discharging，the positive and negative electrode electrolytes continuously flow through the electrode surface under the drive of the circulation pump. During charging, electrical energy drives the oxidation and reduction of $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ to $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ respectively,which are stored in the electrolyte. During discharging,the active substances undergo reverse reactions to release electrical energy.Fig. 6b-c show the coulombic eficiency (CE,Fig. 6b), voltage efficiency (VE,Fig.S22),and energy efficiency (EE,Fig.6c) of the electrodes tested at current densities of 80,100,120,140,160,180, and $2 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ ，with a recovery test conducted by restoring the current density to $8 0 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ after completing charge-discharge cycles at $2 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ . This enables the ICRFB equipped with the BiOI/TCC electrode to operate stably for over 40o cycles at a high current density of $1 4 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ ,demonstrating excellent long-term cycling stability.This renders ita viable candidate for long-duration and large-scale energy storage applications.

# 4. Conclusions

This study reports an engineered BiOX-modified carbon fabric electrode with OVs engineering,which integrates the $\mathrm { \small { [ B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } ] } ^ { 2 + } } \mathrm { X _ { 2 } } ^ { - }$ layered structure design with defect engineering to address the kinetic limitations and HER in ICRFB.The unique electronic structure and chemical properties of the bismuth-based $\mathrm { \small { [ B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } ] } } ^ { 2 + } \mathrm { X } _ { 2 } ^ { - }$ materials, coupled with a precise lattice distortion strategy induced by the relatively large ionic radius of $\Gamma ^ { - }$ ,enable a breakthrough in ICRFB performance through three synergistic mechanisms.First,the multi-electron transfer characteristic of $[ \mathrm { B i } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ] ^ { 2 + }$ combined with the OVs sites as electron transport bridges reduces the $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + }$ redox energy barrier.Second, the ordered layered framework and Bi coordination enhance the activation of reaction intermediates through selective adsorption.Third,the optimized OVs concentration facilitates atomic polarization,improving the adsorption and desorption of $\mathrm { c r }$ species,while the Bi-O-Cr bridging intermediates within the layered structure shorten charge transfer distances. Furthermore,the cooperative regulation of surface charge density by Bi-OVs suppresses HER side reactions.Collectively,these features significantly enhance the electrode kinetics and stability.This research not only provides a low-cost alternative to noble metal catalysts but also establishes a design blueprint for high-activity,low-side reaction, longlifetime multifunctional electrodes,which is crucial for advancing the commercialization of ICRFB and their applications in grid-scale energy storage.
