Hydrocarbon proton exchange membranes (PEMs) have been extensively employed in redox flow batteries (RFBs)due to their environmental benefits and ion selectivity towards redox species.However,high cost of polymer monomers and complex synthesis processes continue to hinder the widespread adoption of such PEMs in RFBs.In this work,aseries of sulfonated polybenzimidazole membranes (SNPBI-x)are simply designed through direct sulfonation and the corresponding application in iron-chromium redox flow battry (ICRFB) has been verified. The SNPBI- $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ membrane is composed of rigid polybenzimidazole and naphthalene rings, which not only mitigate the reduction in mechanical properties caused by sulfonic acid groups but also ensure the chemical stability of the membrane during operation.Also,SNPBI- $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ membrane exhibits relatively high ion exchange capacity (IEC),high water uptake and low swelling ratio compared to Nafion 212.Meanwhile,the permeability of SNPBI-1.42 is notably low,with values of only $0 . 7 6 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 7 } \mathrm { c m } s ^ { - 1 }$ for $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ and $0 . 4 3 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 7 } \mathrm { c m } s ^ { - 1 }$ for ${ \mathrm { C r } } ^ { 3 + }$ over $^ { 9 6 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ .The high ion selectivity is atributed to the Donnan effect introduced by the protonation of imidazole groups,which enhances the ion barier properties of the membrane and battery performance.Moreover,optimized SNPBI-1.42 demonstrates the best battery performance among all the membranes tested,achieving a coulombic efficiency (CE) of $9 7 . 5 5 ~ \%$ ,an energy efficiency (EE) of $8 6 . 8 8 ~ \%$ and a voltage efficiency (VE) of $8 9 . 0 6 \%$ at $1 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ Furthermore,SNPBI-1.42 maintains an EE of over $8 0 ~ \%$ for more than 1600 cycles in ICRFB,further confirming its superior chemical stability in ICRFB.

# 1．Introduction

The continuous consumption of fossil fuels and the associated environmental challenges pose significant barriers to the sustainable development of humanity [1-3]. Although diverse clean energy sources including solar energy，wind energy，geothermal energy and hydroelectric power etc.has been widely adopted globally,the intermitent nature of these energy sources remains a major obstacle especially when considering human electricity consumption patterns during peak and off-peak hours [4,5]. Hence,large scale energy conversion and storage devices are urgently demanded as the medium in the utilization of these diverse clean energy sources [6].

Among all kinds of large scale energy conversion and storage devices,redox flow batteries (RFBs) play a vital role in future power grid due to their advantages of easy scalability,long lifespan,fast response and high efficiency [7].Intermittent supply of clean energy is easily stored and continuously offered via RFBs to support the energy usage [8,

9].In detail, RFBs are charged on the electrode through the flowing of electrochemical active species in electrolyte and stored in external tanks [10,11].During peak electricity demand,a constant energy supply is achieved through the discharge of the redox species [l2,13]. Over the past few decades,a variety of RFBs including all-vanadium RFBs (VRFBs)[14,15]，all-iron RFBs[16,17]，zinc-based RFBs[18,19], iron-chromium RFBs [20,21],organic RFBs [22,23] have been extensively developed.Notably, iron-chromium redox flow battery (ICRFB) was introduced by NASA in 1973 as the first modern flow battery [24]. Compared to the commercialized VRFBs,the raw materials of redox species $( \mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } )$ in ICRFB are relatively easy to be obtained and the corresponding costsare appreciably lower than that of vanadium-based counterparts [25,26].Also,after more than fifty years of development, ICRFBs exhibit comparable performance to other RFBs especially in terms of battery efficiency [26-29].The electrode reaction of ICRFB is listed below:

Positive electrode $: F e ^ { 2 + } - e ^ { - }  F e ^ { 3 + } \quad E ^ { 0 } = + 0 . 7 7 1 ~ V \nu s . ~ S H E$

Negative electrode $: C r ^ { 3 + } + e ^ { - }  C r ^ { 2 + } \quad E ^ { 0 } = - 0 . 4 0 8 ~ V \nu s . ~ S H h$

Overall reaction $: F e ^ { 2 + } + C r ^ { 3 + }  F e ^ { 3 + } + C r ^ { 2 + } \quad E ^ { 0 } = + 1 . 1 7 9 \ V$

The overall performance of ICRFB is determined by the properties of electrochemical active materials,electrodes,membranes and other supporting components including current collectors and gaskets [30]. Among them,the membrane placed between positive and negative electrodes is one of the key components.As a carrier of supporting ions in the electrolyte,the ion-selective membrane is expected to prevent the crossover of redox active species simultaneously.Thus,high conductivity which reduces the internal resistance of ICRFB and excellent ion selectivity are essential characteristics of a qualified membrane.Also, both excellent mechanical properties and chemical stability are required for long-term operation of ICRFB [31-34]． Commercialperfluorosulfonic acid membranes e.g.Nafion are widely used in RFBs due to their high conductivity.However,Nafion tends to suffer from significant ion crossover in ICRFB,resulting in reduced coulombic efficiency(CE） and energy efficiency (EE) [35-37].Meanwhile,the mechanical properties of Nafion are relatively low,leading to degradation of PEM during operation.Besides,environmental concerns regarding the utilization of per-and poly-fluorinated alkyl substances (PSFA)and the relatively high cost of Nafion limit its widespread adoption in ICRFB.

In recent years, non-fluorinated hydrocarbon PEMs including SPEEK [38], SPBI [39],SPI [4O] and SPSF [41] have been extensively investigated for the application in RFBs. Specifically,Wang et al. prepared a composite membrane of sulfonated polyether ether ketone and amphoteric polyether ether ketone (SPEEK/APK) for VRFB [42].This non-fluorinated PEM exhibits excellent structural compatibility and lower vanadium permeability compared to Nafion 212.An EE of $8 1 . 3 \%$ at a current density of $1 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ was achieved in VRFB. Li et al. designed a sulfonated polyimide (SPI) membrane containing flexible aliphatic chain segments for the application in VRFB_[43]. The membrane possessed superior chemical stability and excellent battery performances. At a current density of $1 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ ,the battery assembled with SPI demonstrated a higher CE $( 9 6 . 8 3 ~ \% )$ and EE $( 7 9 . 4 7 ~ \% )$ than Nafion 212 (CE: $9 3 . 1 4 \ \mathrm { ~ \ } 9 6 $ $\operatorname { E E } { : 7 4 . 1 3 } \ \ \mathrm { ~ \% } )$ ．Asa representative of non-fluorinated hydrocarbon PEMs,sulfonated polybenzimidazole (PBI) membranes become a hotspot to achieve optimal RFB performance due to its considerable proton conductivity, high ion selectivity,remarkable mechanical properties and cost-effectiveness [44-46]．Extensive research has been conducted to optimize PBI molecular design and enhance RFB efficiency [47-5O]. Specifically,the protonated imidazole groups in acid-based RFBs repel electroactive cations within the electrolyte,thereby preventing the crossover of redox couples.For instance, Yang et al.[51] synthesized a series of ether-free poly $\boldsymbol { \mathrm { \Delta } }$ -triphenyl methylimidazole) (PTMIm) membrane by one-pot Friedel-Crafts polyhydroxyalkylation mechanism. This process led to the formation of pendant imidazole groups on an all-aromatic backbone,resulting in electrostatic repulsion of vanadium ions.The obtained membrane possessed superior selectivity $( 8 . 7 \ \times \ 1 0 ^ { 5 } \ \mathrm { S } \ \operatorname* { m i n } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 3 } )$ compared to Nafion 115 $( \bar { 6 } . 6 \times 1 0 ^ { 3 } \mathrm { ~ S ~ m i n ~ } \mathrm { { c m } ^ { - 3 } ) }$ ,achieving a CE of $9 9 . 5 ~ \%$ over 500 cycles at $1 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A c m ^ { - 2 } }$ in VRFB.In addition to imidazole side chains, the introduction of flexible side chains sealed by sulfonic acid on PBI backbone is also an efective method for the promotion of battery performance.He etal.[52] grafted hydrophilic side chain onto PBI for the achievement of a microphase separation structure that significantly improved proton conductivity.Meanwhile,the corresponding PEM exhibited ultra-low vanadium permeability due to the Donnan ffect caused by imidazole groups.This membrane achieved an EE of $8 9 . 1 3 \%$ and a voltage efficiency (VE) of $9 0 . 7 3 \ \%$ in VRFB at $8 0  { \mathrm { \ m A } }  { \mathrm { \ c m } } ^ { - 2 }$ Moreover,such efficiencies were maintained for 2Oo cycles under the same current density.Although the chemical grafting of PBI gains impressive RFB performance,the introduction of side chains requires precise control of synthesis conditions,which inevitably causes the complexity of membrane preparation process.To simplify the synthesis process,Li et al.[53] designed a sulfonated PBI membrane with a controllable sulfonation degree via a one-pot synthesis of sulfonated monomers.The as-synthesized membrane employs electrostatic repulsion between the sulfonic acid groups and imidazole groups,which effectively promoted proton transport while preventing the crossover of redox species.This membrane exhibited a CE of $9 8 . 2 ~ \%$ and an EE of $8 3 . 1 7 \%$ in ICRFB.However,the degree of sulfonation (DS) is depended on the sulfonated monomer,resulting in restricted sulfonated sites in PBI.

In this work,a sulfonated polybenzimidazole polymer (SNPBI-x) with different DS was reported viaa simple sulfonation method.The obtained membrane possesses multiple sulfonation sites,which increase the ion exchange capacity (IEC).Meanwhile,the introduction of rigid naphthalene rings in SNPBI- $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ prevents the loss of mechanical strength caused by high DS.Characterizations including Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR),H nuclear magnetic resonance spectra $\cdot ^ { 1 } \mathrm { H } \mathrm { N M R } )$ , IEC and ion permeability were conducted to analyze the assynthesized SNPBI- $\mathbf { x }$ ，Furthermore,membrane properties including water uptake (WU),swelling ratio (SR)and mechanical properties were also evaluated.Afterwards, the membrane was placed in an ICRFB and the key metrics such as surface resistance,self-discharge performance, battery CE,EE and VE were all tested and compared to Nafion 212.Also, a long-term stability test was conducted at $1 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ with both Nafion 212 and SNPBI $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ ，The comparison of both membranes was demonstrated by FT-IR and $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ NMR before and after the stability test.

# 2.2.Preparation of NPBI

The synthesis of NPBI is referred to the method reported previously [54].A schematic of the synthesis process and the membrane of NPBI in shown in Fig.S1.In detail, DAB (2.1427 g,10 mmol),NDA $( 2 . 1 6 1 9 g , 1 0$ mmol) and Eaton's reagent $( 2 5 \ : g )$ were added to a $1 0 0 \mathrm { m L }$ three-necked flask.The mixture was heated gradually to $1 7 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ under mechanical stirring ina nitrogen atmosphere and maintained at this temperature for $^ { 2 7 \mathrm { h } }$ .The resulting viscous black-green liquid was then cooled to room temperature and poured into ultrapure water,yielding a fibrous black-green polymer.The polymer was stirred in $5 \ \mathrm { w t \% N a H C O _ { 3 } }$ for 12 h to remove excess acidic substances and subsequently washed and filtered with ultrapure water for several times until the polymer was neutral. Both NPBI and SNPBI $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ membranes were fabricated by solution casting method.The polymers were dissolved in DMSO to achieve a solid content of $4 \mathrm { w t } \%$ and the solution was heated to $1 2 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ with stirring for $^ { 8 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ .The defoamed solution was poured onto a flat glass plate and the solvent was allowed to evaporate at $6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $4 8 \ \mathrm { h }$ ,resulting in transparent brown membranes as illustrated in Fig.1.The membranes were immersed in1M $\mathrm { H _ { 2 } S O _ { 4 } }$ solution and maintained at $6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $2 4 \ \mathrm { h }$ to obtain an average wet membrane thickness of approximately $6 0 ~ { \mu \mathrm { m } }$

# 3.2.Membrane properties of SNPBI $_ { \cdot x }$

WU and SR of Nafion 212 and SNPBI-x membranes were compared at various temperatures as indicated in Fig. 4a and b and Tables S2-S3. Both WU and SR of all membranes increase significantly with rising temperatures.Relatively large WU percentage is conducive to proton transport across the membrane.Nevertheless,excessive WU results in swelling issues,which has an adverse effect on membrane mechanical properties as well as the performance of RFB.Notably,Nafion 212 possesses the lowest WU compared with any SNPBI $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ membranes while its swelling ratio is largest among all membranes involved as shown in Fig.4b.For instance,the WU and SR of SNPBI-1.42 reach $4 6 . 0 9 \%$ and $5 . 6 1 \%$ ,respectively at $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } .$ In comparison,Nafion 212 only possesses a WU and SR of $3 0 . 0 2 ~ \%$ and $1 0 . 0 8 ~ \%$ ,respectively at the same temperature.Such difference in WU can be rationalized by the lower IEC value of Nafion 212 compared with SNPBI $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ membranes as indicated in Table S1.Meanwhile,the fatty structure of Nafion 212 results in an obvious growth in SR,which inevitably causes instability in energy device stacks.For all SNPBI- $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ membranes, the increase in IEC leads to significant improvements in both WU and SR.In detail, SNPBI-1.7O has a WUof $5 8 . 0 6 ~ \%$ and a relatively low SR of $6 . 6 0 ~ \%$ .This excellent resistance to swelling is attributed to the rigid polymer backbone structure, which limits the swelling effect caused by the water within the membrane [56].

Next, mechanical properties of Nafion 212 and SNPBI $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ membranes were revealed by the stress-strain curves as depicted in Fig.4c and Tables S1-S2.Clearly, the tensile strength of Nafion 212 $( 1 0 . 0 0 \ \mathrm { M P a } )$ become inconspicuous in comparison with any other SNPBI-x membranes,which are stemmed from the superior mechanical properties of all PBI matrix membranes.The rigid polymer backbones of SNPBI $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ membranes enhance their tensile strength.Furthermore,although the tensile strength of SNPBI- $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ membranes decreases with the introduction of sulfonic acid groups from 214.26 MPa to $9 3 . 1 3 ~ \mathrm { M P a }$ as displayed in

Table S1, the unique II-II stacking interactions of imidazole and naphthalene within the rigid polymer backbone improve the membrane mechanical properties [57].Thus,SNPBI- $\mathbf { x }$ membranes still maintain a high level of tensile stress even with higher IEC compared with Nafion 212.

# 3.3. Ion permeability

As illustrated in previous section,ions selectivity of the membrane in RFB determines the level of cross-contamination of redox species. Moreover,enhanced transportation of charge-balanced ions is essential for maintaining high RFB efficiencies.Herein,permeability tests of $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ using Nafion 212 and SNPBI- $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ membranes were conducted. Similar increasing tendency towards experimental times is observed after a $9 6 \mathrm { h }$ permeability test as displayed in Fig.5a and b for both $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ ions. Notably,Nafion 212 possessed highest salt concentrations compared to the SNPBI- $\mathbf { x }$ membranes on the receiving side for both $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ . The increased crossover of redox species can be rationalized by the existence of effective ion channels formed by the hydrophilichydrophobic phase separation structure of Nafion 212.Although such structure facilitates the transportation of $\mathrm { H ^ { + } }$ ,it also boosts the permeation of $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ ions. Among all SNPBI-x membranes, SNPBI-1.42 exhibited the lowest permeability for both $\mathrm { F e ^ { 3 + } } ( 0 . 7 6 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 7 } \mathrm { c m } s ^ { - 1 } )$ and $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ $( 0 . 4 3 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 7 } \mathrm { ~ c m ~ } s ^ { - 1 } )$ ， demonstrating enhanced ion selectivity.Although the introduction of sulfonation groups inevitably facilitate the transportation of all positively charged ions including $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ ， $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ and $\mathrm { H } ^ { + }$ ，the positively charged imidazole groups in SNPBI $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ effectively prevent the penetration of $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ owing to the Donnan effect [48].Thus,such unique structure enables superior permeability properties of SNPBI- $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ membranes for both $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ and the detailed data are listed in Table S4.

# 3.4.Performance of ICRFB

In this section,SNPBI- $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ membranes were tested in ICRFB and a comparison of battery efficiencies was conducted with Nafion 212.CE, EE and VE of ICRFB was measured at current densities scoping from 40 to $1 4 0  { \mathrm { \ m A } }  { \mathrm { \ c m } } ^ { - 2 }$ for both Nafion 212 and SNPBI- $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ membranes as illustrated in Fig. 6a-c and Table S5.Apparently,CE of all membranes increases along with increasing current density,which can be ascribed to the less charge-discharge time used for the permeation of redox ions. Also,SNPBI-1.32, SNPBI-1.42 and SNPBI-1.53 possess higher CE across all current densities compared with SNPBI-1.7O and Nafion 212,which is in consistent with the permeability results in last section.Moreover, CE of these three membranes remains almost invariable with the increasing IEC，implying SNPBI-1.32,SNPBI-1.42 and SNPBI-1.53 possess excellent ion barrier properties.The results reveal that within the IEC range of $1 . 3 2 { - } 1 . 5 3 \mathrm { m m o l } \mathrm { g } ^ { - 1 }$ ,the amount of sulfonic acid groups has minimal impact on CE.Notably, SNPBI-1.42 exhibits the highest CE $( 9 8 . 0 9 \% )$ at a current density of $1 4 0 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ .In contrast, CE of SNPBI-1.70 drops steeply to $9 1 . 6 3 ~ \%$ at the same current density due to the excessive sulfonic acid groups which facilitates the permeation of redox species including $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ in addition to $\mathrm { H } ^ { + }$ . In contrast to the increasing trend of CE with rising current density,EE of ICRFB demonstrates a descending trend with the increase of current density as depicted in Fig.6b.Besides,all cells using SNPBI-x membranes except SNPBI-1.7O possess significantly higher EE than that using Nafion 212 especially under low current densities.For example,battery using SNPBI-1.42 demonstrates the highest EE( $( 8 3 . 3 3 \% - 8 9 . 9 0 \%$ among all membranes across current density ranging from 140 to $4 0 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ Itis worth mentioning that EE of the cell using SNPBI-1.7O is even lower than that of Nafion 212,mostly owing to high ion permeation of $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ . Similar tendency towards CE of the cell also verifies the decreased EE using SNPBI-1.7O.As illustrated in Fig.6c,VE of ICRFB was then derived from the CE and EE.A decreasing tendency of VE is observed along with the increased current density,whose trend is similar to EEof the cell.The decline in VE is ascribed to the variation of applied current and the ohmic resistance of the cell. Since the electrochemical activation polarization and concentration polarization of the ICRFB are the same with different membranes,ohmic polarization mainly caused by the resistance of different membranes is responsible for the VE drop.For instance,as the current density is elevated up to 140 $\mathbf { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ,the potential drop introduced by cell resistance results in a VE of $8 7 . 9 9 ~ \%$ with Nafion 212.Additionally,all batteries using SNPBI $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ membranes exhibit lower VE than that using Nafion 212,which is in consistent with the cell ohmic resistance as demonstrated in Fig. 6d, thus evincing that the drop of VE is dependent on the ohmic polarization. Specifically,the area resistance of SNPBI-1.42 and Nafion 212 was $3 4 0 . 7 9 0 ~ \mathrm { m } \Omega \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ and $2 9 6 . 7 7 3 \ \mathrm { m } \Omega \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ ,respectively,aligning with the trend observed in Fig.6c.Furthermore,the membrane conductivity of SNPBI- $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ in electrolytes was significantly lower than that of Nafion 212 as depicted in Table S6.Moreover,VE based on SNPBI-1.7O surpasses those of the other SNPBI- $\mathbf { x }$ membranes while CE of SNPBI-1.70 is only close to that of Nafion 2l2,ultimately resulting in the lowest EE of cells with all membranes employed.The resultant decreasing EE is mainly attributed to the low ion selectivity,which facilitates the transportation of both redox species and $\mathrm { H } ^ { + }$ across the membrane.Although the ohmic resistance of SNPBI-1.7O is reduced as exhibited in Fig.6d,the evidently low ion selectivity (also low CE of the corresponding battery) leads to a comparable EE with Nafion 212.

# 4．Conclusion

Insummary，a series of simply designed sulfonated polybenzimidazole membranes (SNPBI-x) were designed and synthesized using sulfuric acid as a sulfonating agent for the application in ICRFB. The IEC of SNPBI-x membranes was tailored by controlling the sulfonation reaction time.The successful introduction of sulfonic acid groups significantly improved the water uptake of SNPBI membranes,thus promoting the proton transport capacity of PEMs.Meanwhile, SNPBI $\mathbf { \nabla } \cdot \mathbf { X }$ exhibited lower swelling ratio and superior mechanical properties compared to Nafion 2l2,owing to the inherent characteristics of PBI membranes.In addition,SNPBI-x membranes exhibited excellent ion selectivity due to the Donnan effect induced by protonated imidazole structure on the backbone.As applied in ICRFB,SNPBI-1.42 demonstrated conspicuous battery performance (CE: $9 7 . 5 5 \%$ EE: $8 6 . 8 8 \%$ VE: $8 9 . 0 6 ~ \% \textcircled { \varpi } ~ 1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { m A c m } ^ { - 2 } ) }$ compared to other membranes in this work. Moreover,SNPBI-1.42 displayed a cycling stability of more than 1600 charge-discharge cycles with competitive battery efficiencies ( $\mathrm { C E : } { \mathsf { \Omega } } > 9 4$ $\% ;$ EE: $> 8 0 \ \% \ @ \ 1 0 0 \ \mathrm { m A } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ . The membrane was subsequently analyzed by FT-IR and $\mathrm { ^ { 1 } H N M R }$ ,clearly verifying its superior chemical stability.In brief,this work provides a simple and effective sulfonation method to construct selective proton transport channels in PBI and verifies that SNPBI-1.42 is a promising candidate for application in ICRFBs.
