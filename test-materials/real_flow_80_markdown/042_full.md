ABSTRACT: Lithium-rich layered oxides (LLOs) are fascinating highenergy-density cathode materials for next-generation lithium-ion batteries (LIBs). However, the high voltage causes severe decomposition of conventional carbonate-based electrolytes at $\mathbf { L D s ^ { \prime } }$ surfaces, often producing a non-uniform, unstable,and non-protective cathode-electrolyte interphase (CEI)，hindering the ${ { \bf { L } } _ { \bf { i } } } ^ { + }$ diffusion and reducing the electrochemical efficiency. Various side reactions, such as the release of lattice oxygen， dissolution of transition metals (especially manganese), and irreversible structural transformations, also occur and severely attenuate the electrochemical performance. Electrolyte additives provide a facile and effective approach to optimize the battery performance by in situ regulating the physical/chemical structures/properties of CEI. This paper reviews the research progress in functional electrolyte additives for LLOs and discusses the mechanisms of CEI construction with diverse functios. Finall, we tentatively propose suggestions toconstruct CEI by screeing andcustomizing electrolyte additives to promote the large-scale application of LLOs for LIBs.

L ithium-ion batteries (LIBs） have occupied a leading position in the portable electronic products and electric vehicle markets.1-4 To meet the strategic projection for long-term use, there is an urgent need to develop high-energydensity cathode materials.5-7 Among various candidates, lithium-rich layered oxides (LLOs）have attracted much atntion due to their high specific capacity and low cost.89 However, poor cycle stability， severe voltage decay，and slow rate capability greatly hinder their commercialization.10,11

Many factors affect the electrochemical properties of LLOs, amongwhichthesurfaceplaysavitalrole.1213During electrochemical cycling，especially at high voltage，highly oxidative species such as high-valence transition metal (TM) ions and active oxygen moieties are easily generated. These oxidative species can react with the organic electrolyte at the LLO surface,accelerating diverse structural deteriorations such as the dissolution of TMs,irreversible transformation of layeredstrucesdpticelr non-uniform, thick,and loose cathode-electrolyte interphase (CEI）can often be formed during the electrolyte decomposition process，which may severely reduce the initial Coulombic efficiency，capacity， discharge voltage，and rate performance ofLOs ifnot welleguated17

One of the unique characteristics of LLOs compared with traditional cathode materials is that,in addition to the TM redox,the lattice oxygen could also be activated to deliver “extra” capacities, i.e., oxygen anionic redox (OAR).18 OAR usually refers to the reversible process of $\mathrm { O } ^ { 2 - }  \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { n - } .$ ，while the irreversible products of oxygen can strongly react with the electrolyte.19 During electrochemical cycling， the activated oxygen on the LLO surface can easily escape from the lattice and destabilize the crystal framework.20 Simultaneously，TMs will migrate to the adjacent Li layer or even dissolve into the electrolyte,resulting in the irreversible layer-to-spinel transformation and reducing the capacity and discharge voltage.21 What's worse，the released high-activity oxygen species will further accelerate the oxidation of electrolyte to generate $\mathrm { C O } _ { 2 }$ and $_ { \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } }$ 22,23 Thus,thehydrolysis of $\mathrm { L i P F } _ { 6 }$ can be accelerated to produce HF,which severely corrodes LLOs and aggravates the TMs'dissolution.24

The electrochemical stability of LLOs can be enhanced not only by bulk design but also by surface modification through either pre-coating or in situ CEI construction.25-27 7Although surface coating of LLOs can effectively weaken the irreversible structural transformations and interface side reactions,in situ CEI construction provides an effective and more convenient way.28,29 The ideal CEI protective structure must be thin, continuous，uniform，and compact，favoring high ${ \mathrm { L i } ^ { + } }$ conductivity，suppressing the TM and O ions’release，and separating LLOs and electrolyte to inhibit the continuous side reactions.30,31 Also， the CEI should balance mechanical strength and flexibility to tolerate the morphology changes of LLOs.32 Moreover, it should be electrochemically stable in the harsh battery environment.33 The high capacity of LLOs comes from both cation and anion redox reactions,which is much more complicated than for other high-voltage cathode materials.Therefore，in situ CEI construction with elegant control of the composition and structure can be efficiently adjusted by using electrolyte additives.34,35 In particular, the selection and application of electrolyte additives that inhibit the dissolution of TMs and release of oxygen are critical to regulating the electrochemistry of LLOs.

Additives are crucial to change the properties of electrolytes, through profoundly adjusting the interfacial reactions of LLOs and the CEI structures.36 First, additives can optimize the characteristics of the electrolyte，playing multiple roles including electrolyte stabilizers, $\mathrm { H F / H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }$ scavengers, ${ \mathrm { L i } ^ { + } }$ solvation structure regulators, flame retardants，and so forth.30,3739 Seconddiivescanbedesigedtopeeren tially adsorb on the LLO surface and undergo chemical/ electrochemical structural transformations， thereby in situ fabricating the CEI structure with multiple functions.14,31 Also,multiple additives can be coupled to display synergistic effects in the CEI. A survey of effective additives with rationalized elements and functional groups is of primary importance for the regulation of CEI structures,requiring deep understandings on the mechanisms of the formation and effects of CEI，which is still challenging owing to the complicated and dynamic process at the interface.40,41 Therefore,the further development of additives specifically for LLOs requires great attempts and enhanced efficiency.

Compared with the numerous studies on the construction and adjustment of solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) flms by additives on the anode side, there is a lack of cognition for the design of CEI by additives， especially for the high-voltge LLOs. Therefore，a comprehensive understanding of the interphase regulation of LLOs by electrolyte additives based on updated progress is highly required. This Focus Review aims to provide an overall picture of this topic. We first introduce the interface problems of LLOs and the side reactions with electrolytes.Then,the latest progress in the optimization of electrolyte additives for LLOs and the requirements of an ideal CEI for maintaining electrochemical stability are discussed in detail.Finally,future strategies for in situ CEI construction with electrolyte additives are prospected. It is hoped that through this review,the significance and application prospects of electrolyte additives for LLOs will be clarified，and the development of advanced electrolyte additives will be enlightened.

# CHALLENGES OF INTERFACIAL ELECTROCHEMISTRYIN LITHIUM-RICHLAYERED OXIDES

The conventional carbonate electrolyte for LLOs has a low oxidation onset potential and undergoes oxidative decomposition at high voltages. Therefore，the interfacial side reactions between the electrolyte and the strongly oxidizing LLOs often lead to the loss of lattice oxygen, the reduction of surface TMs,and the irreversible transformation from the layered to the spinel and rock-salt structures. These processes may produce micro-cracks and result in serious cycle instability and voltage drop.

Since LLOs with electron and ion channels are equipotential, for generation of the CEI, it can be regarded as a parallel ellticduberEDiteaceD charge and discharge processes of the battery, the electrolyte in contact with the surface of the LLO cathodes will experience a series of structure and component evolutions to form the CEI. The formation of the CEI can be divided into two stages.31 (1) Before the electrochemical process，solvent molecules and lithium salt anions undergo specific adsorption, which is the main factor for controlling the initial structure and chemical composition of the CEI. (2）As the voltage increases,LLOs exhibit strong oxidizing properties.The specifically adsorbed species on LLOs will be preferentially oxidized and decomposed,resulting in the reduction of surface TMs.Due to the interface side reactions,the traditional electrolyte forms an uneven CEI,and a portion of the LLO surface will be exposed to the electrolyte,causing continuous decomposition of the electrolyte (Figure 1).

During charging in the first cycle,with the activation of $\mathrm { L i } _ { 2 } \mathrm { M n O } _ { 3 } ,$ oxygen anions are activated to participate in the reaction $( \mathrm { O } ^ { 2 - / - } )$ of the de-lithiation process43 Oxygen on the LLOs’surface is released from the lattce sites and even oxidized to $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { \bullet - }$ and $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ This unavoidable oxidation increases the irreversible capacity in the first cycle.44 Moreover， the highly oxidized ${ \mathsf { O } } ^ { n - }$ ions can further accelerate the decomposition of the electrolyte，producing more $\mathrm { C O } _ { 2 }$ and $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }$ .The hydrolysis of $\mathrm { L i P F } _ { 6 }$ is thus aggravated to produce a large amount of HF and $\mathrm { P O } _ { x } \mathrm { F } _ { y } ^ { z - } .$ ，which (（especially HF） will corrode the LLO surface to promote the dissolution of TMs.23,24 Alsotheescapeof latice oxygendestabilizes the crystal structure.Accompanied by irreversible oxygen release, the binding energy of TMs and oxygen is reduced, TMs will migrate from the TMlayer to the adjacentLi layer to balance the framework,and the cations’mixing is increased,thus causing the layered-to-spinel-like and/or rock-salt transformation.21 The Jahn-Teller effect is widely present in the manganese-based oxides,and the disproportionation reaction of Mn caused by the Jahn-Teller effect aggravates the $\mathrm { M n } ^ { 2 + }$ dissolution.45 The dissolved TMs willcontinue to deposit on the anode surface，which will cause the destruction and reconstruction of the SEI film.46 The battery will thus experience capacity decline，voltage drop，and large dynamic resistance.

The electrolyte of LIBs is an ionic conductor,in which lithium salt is dissolved in organic solvents.34 Commonly used inorganic lithium salts include $\mathrm { L i P F } _ { 6 } ,$ $\mathrm { L i B F } _ { 4 } ,$ and $\mathrm { L i C l O } _ { 4 }$ The overall performance of $\mathrm { L i P F } _ { 6 }$ with carbonate solvents, including ionic conductivity and electrochemical stability，is the best, and thus they are widely used. Organic solvents are the major component of the electrolyte and largely determine the performance.47 Generally，it is considered to use highdielectric-constant cyclic carbonates (ethylene carbonate (EC) and polycarbonate (PC)）mixed with low-viscosity chain carbonates (diethyl carbonate (DEC)，dimethyl carbonate (DMC),and ethyl methyl carbonate (EMC)).However, due to the poor compatibility of PC with the graphite anode, the usage of PC is decreasing.

Xing and other research groups calculated the electron affinities and orbital energies of several carbonate solvents by density functional theory (DFT)，revealing their oxidation stability and decomposition mechanism.4 n.48-50 They found that EC has an obviously higher oxidation onset potential than linear carbonates,but this is inconsistent with the experimental conclusion that the main oxidation product detected in the mixed-solvent electrolyte comes from EC.The reason is that the EC molecule is more polar and has a strong electrostatic force with the anionic groups. Therefore，as the charging potential increases $( E < \mathsf { \bar { 4 } } . 3 \bar { \mathrm { ~ V } } )$ ，ECand negatively charged $\mathrm { P F } _ { 6 } ^ { - }$ preferentially accumulate on the surface of the LLOs, starting to form the CEI structure (Figure 2a).Borodin and Xing found that the lithium salt anions (including $\mathrm { P F } _ { 6 } { } ^ { - }$ ， $\mathrm { B F _ { 4 } } ^ { - } .$ and $\mathrm { C l O } _ { 4 } ^ { - }$ ）can significantly reduce the oxidation onset potential of EC,mainly because the EC that participates in the solvation structure will undergo H transfer when it loses electrons,and the surrounding lithium salt anions react to generate $\mathrm { H F / H C l O } _ { 4 } ,$ which makes traditional electrolytes mismatch with the high-voltage LLOs.48,51 In particular, $\mathrm { L i P F } _ { 6 }$ is prone to autocatalytically decompose to form $\mathrm { P F } _ { 5 }$ and LiF.24 $\mathrm { P F } _ { 5 }$ will undergo a series of hydrolysis reactions to produce highly active $\mathrm { P O F } _ { 3 } ,$ HF，and phosphoric acid compounds $\left( \mathrm { H P O } _ { 2 } \mathrm { F } _ { 2 } , \right.$ $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { P O } _ { 3 } \mathrm { F } ,$ and $\mathrm { H } _ { 3 } \mathrm { P O } _ { 4 } )$ .

When $E \ge 4 . 3 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } _ { \cdot }$ ,the EC molecules preferentially enriched on LLOs will undergo severe oxidative decomposition and dehydrogenation reactions (Figure 2b). On the one hand, Metzger summarized and proposed the mechanism of the generation of CO2 and CO by ring-opening oxidation of EC.52 In the whole process，some organic radicals are initially formed, such as $\cdot _ { \mathrm { O - C H _ { 2 } - C H _ { 2 } } } +$ and $\mathbf { \partial } ^ { + \bullet } \mathrm { O = C H } _ { 2 }$ . # PERFORMANCEOPTIMIZATIONBYTAILORING INTERPHASEWITHELECTROLYTEADDITIVES

Effective electrolyte additives are crucial for in situ controling the CEI layer toward high stability.54 The CEI produced by the carbonate electrolyte is usually non-uniform，and thus the electrolyte continues to decompose，resulting in resistance increase and capacity attenuation. In order to solve the interfacial problems of LLOs, considering the various performance failure mechanisms including the electrolyte decomposition,oxygen evolution,TMs’dissolution,and irreversible phase transition， the influences of electrolyte additives are classified.The analysis of the existing additives reveals that one or more characteristic elements such as B,P,S,N, Si, and F and functional groups such as $\mathrm { C } { = } \mathrm { C } _ { \mathrm { \lambda } }$ ，phenyl,ester,and so on determine their functions in the CEI formation. One of the most effective strategies is the introduction of cooperative functional groups into the CEI to synergistically protect LLOs from harmful side reactions.

During the first charge of LLOs,the high cutoff voltage of $4 . 6 { - } 4 . 8 \ \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ can cause severe oxidative decomposition of a $\mathrm { L i P F } _ { 6 } .$ /carbonate-based electrolyte,which usually occurs above $4 . 3 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ (vs $\mathrm { L i / L i ^ { + } }$ ).This process consumes the limited ${ \mathrm { L i } ^ { + } }$ source in the battery and forms a non-uniform CEI layer on the cathode material. Continuous electrolyte decomposition and overgrowth of CEI eventually cause the invalidation of the battery.In recent years,by using trace amounts of electrolyte additives，the adverse reactions of the electrolyte on the cathode surface have been suppressed,and the electrochemical performance of the LIBs has been significantly improved.As shown in Figure 3a，on the one hand，many electrolyte additives can serve as sacrificial agents to scavenge HF and $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { \cdot }$ ，which can inhibit the decomposition of $\mathrm { L i P F } _ { 6 }$ and the corrosion of HF on the LLOs.On the other hand, the additives as the first sacrificial component could be pre-oxidized before the electrolyte when its HOMO is higher than those of the solvents. The decomposition products of additives may spontaneously form a thinner and stronger CEI film in subsequent cycles, which is beneficial to promote the migration rate of $\mathrm { L i ^ { + } }$ ions and reduce electrode interface impedance.

The principles for selection of additives could be guided by the theoretical molecular orbital calculations and electrochemical testing of linear sweep voltammetry/cyclic voltammetry $\mathrm { ( L S V / C V ) }$ to screen the oxidizing properties of oxidants or high-voltage solvents.The effects of electrolyte additives with different characteristic elements are often different.For example, the empty $p$ orbital of the central boron atom in the borate compounds is in an electron-deficient state, showing Lewis acidity，and also acts as an anion acceptor.55 It can complex $\mathrm { P F } _ { 6 } ^ { - }$ or $\mathrm { F ^ { - } }$ ，which is beneficial to the dissociation of lithium salt, thereby reducing the formation of $\mathrm { P F } _ { 5 } ,$ LiF,and other byproducts.Phosphate or phosphite organic compounds could be more easily oxidized than solvent molecules and can preferentially undergo electrochemical decomposition on the surface of LLOs to form a stable CEI,which can improve electrochemical performance.38,56In addition，phosphorus additives can also be used as electrolyte flame retardants to capture combustion free radicals, thereby preventing LIBs from burning and exploding due to thermal runaway.40 Sulfonates and sulfates are two types of sulfur-containing additives,which have a good modification effect on the CEI layer and can significantly reduce the impedance, even at high voltages.57,58

This excellent film-forming property prevents the subsequent decomposition of electrolyte.Fluorocarbonate-based additives benefit from the strong electronegativity and weak polarity of the fluorine atom,which leads to the decrease of activation energyand HOMO level of the electrolyte.59 They can enhance the anti-oxidation stability of the electrolyte under high voltage and effectively forma LiF-rich CEI.

Phosphate additives are designed to reduce the chemical/ thermal reactivity of $\mathrm { P F } _ { 6 } ^ { - }$ while maintaining its film-forming advantages. The carbonate molecules and $\mathrm { P F } _ { 6 } { } ^ { - }$ anions are continuously decomposed by the catalytic $\mathrm { M n } ^ { 2 + }$ cations. $\mathrm { M n } ^ { 2 + }$ can also deposit onto the anode surface with lower electrophilicity than those of carbonate molecules or $\mathrm { P F } _ { 6 } { } ^ { - }$ anions. The mechanism of TMs’ dissolution revealed in recent years and possible strategies to eliminate it are discussed below.

The use of additives to remove HF and construct a uniform CEI could enhance the electrochemical stability of LLOs in $\mathrm { L i P F } _ { 6 }$ -based electrolytes.HF causes fatal interface damage to LLOs and dissolution of TMs.To solve these problems, Yim's group used the (trimethylsilyl)methanesulfonate (TMSOMs) multifunctional additive,which contains $- S O _ { 3 } -$ and $_ { \mathrm { S i - O } }$ functional groups (Figure Sa).58 Electrochemical oxidation of the sulfonate will effectively reduce the oxidative decomposition of the electrolyte by generating a stable protective layer on the surface of the $0 . 5 \mathrm { L i } _ { 2 } \mathrm { M n O } _ { 3 } { \cdot } 0 . 5 \mathrm { L i N i } _ { 0 . 4 } \mathrm { C o } _ { 0 . 2 } \mathrm { M n } _ { 0 . 4 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ electrode,which will prevent continuous electron transfer from the electrolyte to the electrode.In addition,based on the strong binding affinity between Si and F, the $_ { \mathrm { S i - O } }$ component effectively removes the remaining fluorine from the electrolyte. This wil greatly reduce the reaction of $\mathrm { F }$ with TMs. Li et al. used bis(trimethylsilyl)carbodiimide (BTMSC）as an electrolyte additive, in which the N-containing group has the ability to scavenge $\mathrm { H } ^ { + }$ or HF.74 These capabilities enable BTMSC to form a protective film containing Si and $_ \mathrm { N }$ on the cathode surface，suppressing the TM dissolution caused by HF for $\mathrm { L i _ { 1 . 2 } M n _ { 0 . 5 5 } N i _ { 0 . 1 5 } C o _ { 0 . 1 } O _ { 2 } }$ ，Li's team used dimethylacetamide (DMAC）as an electrolyte film-forming additive to improve the_cycle stability of high-voltage LLO cathode materials at 25 $^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } . ^ { 7 5 ^ { \prime } }$ They also found that amines as electrolyte additives can form a stable $\mathrm { F ( O ) \cdots H - N }$ hydrogen bond with HF $\left( \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } \right)$ ， inhibiting the destructive effect of HF on battery performance.

Boron-based additives have also been widely used in LLObased LIBs.Yuan et al. chose lithium bis(oxalato)borate (LiBOB）as an effective interfacial additive for stabilizing the $\mathrm { L i _ { 1 . 2 } M n _ { 0 . 5 4 } C o _ { 0 . 1 3 } N i _ { 0 . 1 3 } O _ { 2 } }$ cathodematerial.76Thecapacity retention of this LLO reaches $9 6 . 4 \%$ at $0 . 5 ~ \mathrm { C }$ after 100 cycles, which is much better than that without any additive. Certain nitrile compounds are very effective in protecting high-voltage cathodes due to their good thermal properties, high redox stability,and low flammability.Additives based on nitriles have been extensively studied, such as succinonitrile (SN)，glutaronitrile (GLN)，and adiponitrile (ADN).The nitrile compound reacts with $_ { \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } }$ under acidic conditions $\left( \mathrm { H } ^ { + } \right)$ to reduce the $_ { \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } }$ content, and thus suppresses the decomposition of $\mathrm { L i P F } _ { 6 }$ into HF.At the same time，the electronegative $- \mathrm { C N }$ has strong coordination ability with the TMs on the surface of LLOs, thereby inhibiting the dissolution of TMs.Yu et al.used the nitrile-based electrolyte additive 1,3,6-hexanetricarbonitrile (HTCN）and the siloxy-based additive tris(trimethylsilyl)phosphate (TMSP） to propose an “anchoring $^ +$ pouring” synergistic strategy to construct a stable and ultrathin CEI for $\mathrm { L i _ { 1 . 1 3 } } \mathrm { \check { M } n _ { 0 . 5 1 7 } N i _ { 0 . 2 5 6 } C o _ { 0 . 0 9 7 } O _ { 2 } }$ 27 The CEI inhibits the dissolution of TMs and interfacial side reactions and significantly improves the electrochemical stability.As a result，an average voltage drop of only $0 . 5 ~ \mathrm { \ m V / \Omega }$ cycle was realized.Theoretical calculations have shown that the key “anchor”effect of HTCN is enhanced during the surface electrochemical evolution of LLO with lattce densification. This information on the roles and suppression of TMs dissolved in the electrolyte gives us new understandings about the failure mechanism of the electrochemistry of Mn-based cathode materials and provides important insights into the electrolyte design of future LIBs.

One of the crucial problems on the surface of LLOs is the metastable transition from the layered to the spinel $\mathrm { T M } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 }$ structure, which causes irreversible capacity and voltage decay during cycling. Since the oxidation reaction of superoxide radicals $( \boldsymbol { \mathrm O _ { 2 } } ^ { \bullet - } )$ on the electrolyte is inhibited, less $_ { \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } }$ and $\mathrm { C O } _ { 2 }$ are generated,and the hydrolysis of $\mathrm { L i P F } _ { 6 }$ will not be triggered, thereby cutting off the HF generation.The TMs' dissolution problem，largely associated with HF corrosion，is thus controlled.

Great progress has been made in the design of electrolyte additives to in situ construct CEI for optimizing the electrochemical performance of LLOs.As discussed above, phosphite and borate compounds can capture the release of ireversible lattice oxygen. Silicon and amine electrolyte additives can reduce the generation of HF,inhibit its corrosion on the LLOs,and prevent the dissolution of TMs.Borates and nitriles can coordinate with TMs to inhibit their dissolution.It can be seen that an electrolyte additive may have multiple effects，not only stabilizing the electrolyte itself but also forming a stable CEI film comprised of various functional elements and groups.As a result,the interfacial side reactions can be reduced to achieve perfect protective effects.However, there is still blindness in the selection of electrolyte additives.It isnecessary to start from specific interfacial problems, especially the dissolution of TMs and the irreversible release of oxygen, to further rationalize strategic solutions.

# THEORETICAL AND EXPERIMENTAL STUDY TECHNIQUES

To achieve long cycle life，excellent energy storage，and outstanding safety performance,LIBs place very high requirements on the electrolyte.In particular,achieving compatibility between the electrolyte and electrode materials in a highvoltage environment is a tough task.The entire electrolyte system is very complex, including several solvents, lithium salts, and various additives，and there are strong interactions between the reaction products.It is diffcult to obtain specific chemical components with existing chemical characterization methods，which greatly restricts the development of electrolytes for LIBs.

High-throughput calculation can accelerate the additive screening to finely regulate the interfacial structure of LLOs to meet diverse requirements for practical applications.

The current development of electrolyte additives relies more on scientific intuition and trial-and-error experiments,which take more time and effort. High-throughput calculation can accelerate the additive screening to finely regulate the interfacial structure of LLOs to meet diverse requirements for practical applications (Figure7).79,80 We can also extract new knowledge from existing material data or build predictive models.The high-throughput screening technology refers to the application of high-throughput calculation based on large composite libraries through the use of automation,miniaturization analysis，and large-scale data analysis，which can increase the speed by more than an order of magnitude,as shown in Figure7.81The high-throughput research and development method uses self-developed automation equipment and corresponding software to perform a large number of high-throughput calculations，systematic experimental design $> 1 0 0 0$ materials/batch),battery testing ( $> 5 0 0 0$ in parallel), and data analysis in parallel and forms closed-loop feedback to make larger improvements in less time.82 The screening strategy for electrolyteadditives is to selecta material database based on continuous evaluation obtained from highthroughput calculations.In this strategy, three levels of issues should be considered.

1. The systematic selection principles of additive structures compatible with specific battery environments should be elucidated, since the elements and functional groups of additives have profound effects on the behaviors of electrolyte and the CEI construction. Zaghib et al. have used theoretical calculations to figure out the properties and effects of a series of functional groups as the functionalcompoentsofaditiveoreample thiophene-based additives favor the ion-conductivity of the CEI after oxidative radical polymerization, $\mathrm { N O } _ { 2 } .$ containing additives are highly compatible with the graphite anode,and additives containing -COOH can exchange $\mathrm { H } ^ { + }$ with $\mathrm { L i ^ { + } }$ to serve as lithium storage materials to compensate $\mathrm { L i ^ { + } }$ during cycling.

2.The redox potential is a label of the tendency of matter to gain or lose electrons. The molecules in the electrolyte should be relatively resistant to reduction and oxidation to provide a wider redox window than the battery operating voltage range. They also need to exhibit good structural stability in the charged/discharged state to ensure the reversibility of redox.When the functional additives are oxidized before the base electrolyte， the as-formed CEI may alleviate the decomposition of electrolyte at high voltages，thereby improving the high-voltage stability of the battery. Although their amount might be minor,some additives can still participate in the solvation structure of the electrolyte，and thus the solubility needs to be considered in order to effectively transfer $\mathrm { L i ^ { + } }$ ions between electrodes.

3.The ionic conductivity is the most practical parameter foran electrolyte，which determines the internal resistance and rate characteristics of the battery. Furthermore,the formation of an ultra-thin and evenly distributed CEI is required to avoid the contact between cathode materials and electrolyte, suppress the interfacial side reactions,and prevent the continuous increase of interface impedance,which is conducive for transporting ${ \mathrm { L i } ^ { + } }$ and improving the rate performance. The chemical and electrochemical stability of the electrolyte is also crucial to the cycle life and safety of the battery. In addition，structural stability of the components and functional groups in the CEI must be ensured to prevent CEI damage during the cycles.

The compatibility of additives with other battery materials such as anodes should also be considered. Regarding the compatibility between electrolyte and additives, the following aspects should be considered at least: (a） Solubility. The additives must have suitable solubilities in a certain electrolyte. (b）Viscosity and conductivity. The viscosity of the additive should be considered because a large viscosity affects the ${ \mathrm { L i } ^ { + } }$ transport capacity. Conductive additives can be added to improve the interfacial conductivity. (c） Solvation structure. The introduction of an additive should not adversely affect the solvation structure of the ${ \mathrm { L i } ^ { + } }$ greatly to maintain/improve the ion transfer.For example，lithium nitrate $\left( \mathrm { L i N O } _ { 3 } \right)$ isa particularly preferred inorganic additive which can change the solvation structure of $\mathrm { L i ^ { + } }$ by introducing $\mathrm { N O _ { 3 } } ^ { - }$ and promote the effective de-solvation of ${ \mathrm { L i } ^ { + } }$ .Besides, $\mathrm { L i } _ { x } \mathrm { N } _ { y } \mathrm { O } _ { z }$ a decomposition product of $\mathrm { L i N O } _ { 3 } ,$ can significantly improve the ${ \mathrm { L i } ^ { + } }$ conduction efficiency and stability of the interface, thereby improving the battery performance.84,85 (d) Thermal stability. Some additives can be selected to improve the thermal stability of the electrolyte and even play a flame retardant effect.For the compatibility of balanced electrolyte additives with anode materials，on the one hand，stable electrode/electrolyte interphases are constructed simultaneously. On the other hand, the electrolyte additives applied to the LLOs can well control the gas production and TMs'dissolution during the cycle and will not damage the stability of the SEI.

Generally speaking,in order to achieve special effects, additives have certain chemical activity, such as CEI formation, polymerization,and reaction with certain substances,and thus they are somewhat toxic and unsafe. It is， of course, very important to manage and control these risk points and meet the requirements of battery performance.The reduction of additive amount is also important. Moreover, when conducting high-throughput screening of electrolyte additives，formula designers should carefully consider and balance their advantages and disadvantages.

Affected by factors such as the electrolyte composition and the charge/discharge stage,the chemical structures of the CEI are highly complex and dynamic,making its precise characterization challenging.8 In addition,the thin CEI attached on LLOs could be easily damaged when taken out from the volatile electrolyte in LIBs.It is very important to characterize and understand the relationship between the CEI and battery performance,which is still a problem to be solved.

In addition to traditional characterization methods,some new characterization techniques have been developed in recent years. These methods have been used to examine the chemical structureunits of CEI with high spatial resolution.487-89 To better realize the development of electrolyte additives for LLOs，more advanced methods must be explored. For example,although traditional TEM can obtain atomicresolution images of materials,its characterization of the CEI is still not ideal because of the CEI's chemical activity and beam radiation sensitivity. Therefore，the traditional TEM method for studying CEI has been limited to the decimeter level.However, cryo-scanning TEM (cryo-STEM) can be used to study the CEI structure at the atomic level by freezing the sample in liquid nitrogen to preserve the original structure of the CEI.89,90 This technology can accurately detect the CEI's thickness，composition，uniformity，and spatial distribution. Moreover， time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (TOF-SIMS） is powerful to detect the CEI's composition.17,91,92 TOF-SIMS provides specific information about fragments from the CEI layer，and using TOF-SIMS to comprehensively study the surface of LLOs helps in understanding the evolution of CEI structures.

The development of advanced in situ characterization techniques to systematically study the formation and characteristics of CEI is critical to the development and optimization of electrolyte additives for Li-rich cathodes.Many analysis techniques， including in situ surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy (in situ SERS)，in situ Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (in situ FTIR),in situ liquid secondary ion mass spectrometry (in situ liquid-SIMS)，and in situ atomic force microscopy (in situ AFM) have the potential for probing the formation and evolution of CEI.More specifically,in situ SERS can realize in situ observation of the dynamic changes between the electrolyte and cathode material interface,providing new ideas for the interface research of the energy system. In situ FTIR can detect the oxidation process of electrolyte,which helps to understand the formation mechanism of CEI.94 In situ liquid-SIMS provides the dynamic process of interface formation in LIBs,and the established in-depth analysis of chemical components can reveal the structure of the interface; the dynamic image formed by the interfacial layer of a LIB can also be simulated by operating liquid-SIMS combined with molecular dynamics.9S In order to adapt to the high voltage and electrode volume changes during the charge and discharge cycles, the CEI layer should balance mechanical flexibility and hardness, which can be welldetected by in situ AFM.96,97 It can detect the micro-topography of the cathode surface in real time and provide physical and chemical information on the nanoscale.

# SUMMARYAND PERSPECTIVE

LLOs have the characteristics of high-discharge specific capacity， low cost，and environmental friendliness，making them promising cathode materials for next-generation LIBs. In the wide voltage range of $2 . 0 { - } 4 . 8 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ ，the behavior of the CEI has important influences on the electrochemical performance and safety of LLOs.For traditional electrolytes such as $\mathrm { L i P F } _ { 6 } /$ carbonate electrolyte, the main challenges are their instability under high voltage, the oxidative decomposition caused by the surface irreversible oxygen of LLOs, transition metal migration and dissolution,and irreversible structural transformation.

The addition of electrolyte additives in small amounts can effectively inhibit the occurrence of the aforementioned side reactions between the electrode and the electrolyte,which helps to regulate the CEI structure toward high chemical/ electrochemical stability,high $\mathrm { L i ^ { + } }$ conductivity,and uniform distribution. Several requirements are necessary for effective additives.First,an excellent electrolyte additive should increase the oxidation potential of the electrolyte and can be preferentially adsorbed to the LLOs surface rather than the electrolyte.Second，the CEI regulated by the electrolyte additive is expected to suppress the active oxygen released from the cathode lattice to prevent the oxidation of electrolyte from the gas generation and increase of internal pressure. The formation of harmful side products HF and the dissolution of transition metals could also be suppressed to effectively protect the LLOs.

In view of the current research progress, it is proposed that the following aspects should be paid attention to when developing and screening effective additives: (1） Using in situ characterization technology. The relationship between electrolyte additives and the electrochemical performance of LLOs lacks in-depth analysis.As a result，researchers cannot accurately understand the mechanism of action and cooperation of various components introduced by electrolyte additives. In situ characterization methods are necessary to conduct more detailed investigations on the CEI reaction process. (2） Theoretical calculations closely coupled with experiments.For calculations,it is very important to consider the binding energy between the electrolyte additives and the anions/cations on the surface of the LLOs during the electrochemical dynamic process.Besides， the choice of additives is largely in the“trial and error”stage，with a lack of effcient theoretical guidance，and thus extremely low efficiency.In the future, theoretical calculations should be used more to screen additives，and high-throughput screening methods should be used to assess additives for LLOs. (3) The synergistic effects of various components of the electrolyte.The improvement of electrolyte performance depends not only on additives but also on new lithium salts and solvents in formulation development. (4) The combination of a variety of process technologies. New binder and conductive additives technologies are used as synergistic additives to improve the overall performance of the battery. Electrolyte additives containing different characteristic elements and functional groups have different effects on the various interface problems of LLOs. Choosing and adjusting appropriate electrolyte additives is of great significance for constructing an ideal CEI film.In short，through unremitting research on the above aspects,electrolyte additives will make an indelible contribution to the commercial development of LLOs-based LIBs.
