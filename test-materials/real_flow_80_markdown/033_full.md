# ABSTRACT

Titanium-cerium flow bateries have garnered significant atention in the energy storage field due to the high potential of the $\mathrm { C e } ^ { 4 + } / \mathrm { C e } ^ { 3 + }$ redox pair,the higher potential of the $\mathrm { T i O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ redox pair compared to the hydrogen evolution reaction potential,and the abundant reserves of titanium and cerium.However,traditional carbonfelt (CF)electrodes have limited active sites and poor hydrophilicity,which restrain theredox kinetics of the $\mathrm { T i O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ redox pair. To address this, we built a 3D nitrogen-doped carbon nanofiber composite electrode (N-CNF/CF) based on carbon felt.The nitrogen-doped functional group of N-CNF/CF electrode with high specific surface area significantly improves contact between electrolyte and electrode and electron transfer,thus enhances the $\mathrm { T i O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ reaction kinetics and exhibits excellnt electrochemical stability. Also, DFT calculations uncover that N-doped modulates the electron transfer and enhances the titanium adsorption. After 2O0 cycles, the average voltage efficiency and energy efficiency remain at $8 5 . 9 ~ \%$ and $8 4 . 0 6 \%$ ,respectively,representing a significant improvement over the CF electrode;the average discharge capacity also increases by $1 4 ~ \%$ Additionally,under conditions of $\mathbf { 0 . 7 5 M }$ active material,the capacity and efficiency decay over 3O days is negligible. This study demonstrates that the N-CNF/CF composite electrode holds great promise for practical applications in titanium-cerium flow batteries.

# 1．Introduction

In recent years,titanium-cerium redox flow batteries (Ti-Ce RFBs) have garnered significant attention as a highly promising new energy storage system due to their unique advantages.Ti has a crustal abundance far exceeding that of vanadium (approximately 5O times higher), and its $\mathrm { T i O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ redox pair exhibits a half-cell potential of $0 . 1 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ (vs. SHE),significantly higher than the $\mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { V } ^ { 2 + }$ pair (-0.26 V vs. SHE). This endows Ti-based flow batteries with an inherent advantage in suppressing the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER),thereby enhancing system eficiency and stability [1,2]．Meanwhile,Ce,a rare earth element with abundant reserves and relatively low cost (its crustal abundance is comparable to that of copper),possesses the $\mathrm { C e } ^ { 4 + } / \mathrm { C e } ^ { 3 + }$ redox pair with high potential of 1.61 V(vs.SHE),laying the foundation for achieving higher operating voltages in the Ti-Ce RFBs [3,4].However,the performance improvement of Ti-Ce RFBs faces a critical challenge: the inherently slow kinetics of the $\mathrm { T i O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ redox reaction at the negative electrode severely limit the overall battery performance [5].Since electrochemical reactions primarily occur at the electrode/electrolyte interface,the electrode serves as the core site for charging and discharging processes.Its physicochemical properties (such as catalytic activity,specific surface area,and hydrophilicity) exerta decisive influence on the kinetics of the $\mathrm { T i O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ reaction and, consequently, on system efficiency [6,7].

Carbon felt (CF)and graphite felt (GF) are widely used as RFB electrodes due to their excellent chemical stability and low cost.

However,the inherent defects of bare CF/GF,such as poor hydrophilicity and insufficient reactive sites,limit the effective penetration of electrolytes and the full utilization of active materials,making it particularly difficult to meet the requirements of slow kinetic reactions such as $\mathrm { T i O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ for efficient electrode interfaces [8,9]. To overcome these limitations,researchers have developed various modification strategies.For example,air thermal treatment or acid treatment can increase the number of oxygen-containing functional groups on the surface,thereby improving hydrophilicity and reducing reaction overpotential [1O,11]. Introducing dopants such as nitrogen,oxygen,and boron,can regulate electronic structure and surface chemical properties to enhance electrochemical performance [12,13].Also,depositing metals or metal oxides on the electrode surface can suppress hydrogen/oxygen evolution side reactions and improve battery eficiency [14-16].Additionally,surface modification of carbon nanofibers (CNFs) effectively enhances electrode conductivity,thermal stability,specific surface area,and hydrophilicity,while its unique tubular structure provides abundant reaction sites [17-19]. In particular, nitrogen-doped carbon nanofibers (N-CNFs) have shown great potential in flow battery electrodes due to their excellent electrical conductivity,large surface area,hydrophilicity,and abundant active sites introduced by heteroatom doping [2O-22].However,research into related studies has primarily focused on more extensively studied flow battery systems,such as vanadium flow batteries [23,24].

As mentioned above,it is worth noting that although electrode modification strategies have made progress in traditional flow batteries, research on electrodes for the Ti-Ce RFB system,especiallyaddressing its key bottleneck-the slow kinetics of the $\mathrm { T i O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ -remains insufficient [5].Currently,research in this field mainly focuses on separators,while there is litle attention paid to the key electrode materials for addressing this kinetic bottleneck [25,26].In this study,three-dimensional nitrogen-doped carbon nanofibercompositecarbon felt electrodes (N-CNF/CF) were prepared using a simple pyrolysis method to address the kinetic bottleneck of the $\mathrm { T i O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ reaction. This method avoids the complexity of binder-assisted coating or chemical vapor deposition (CVD) processes commonly used in previous studies,providing a more efficient and scalable approach for electrode fabrication.This design integrates three-dimensional N-CNFs into a carbon felt (CF) substrate, significantly increasing the specific surface area and promoting ion diffusion and electron transfer.Moreover,through systematic XPS and DFT analyses,we reveal that the enhanced performance is not only due to the introduced active sites but also related to the strong adsorption between N species and Ti ions.As a result,the N-CNF/CF electrode demonstrated excellent electrochemical stability over 2Oo cycles,with the average voltage efficiency and energy efficiency remained at $8 5 . 9 \%$ and $8 4 . 0 6 ~ \%$ ,respectively,and a $1 4 ~ \%$ increase in discharge capacity compared to the bare CF (BCF). During continuous operation for up to 30 days of $0 . 7 5 \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ active material,negligible capacity decay and efficiency decaywere observed.This work not only provides an effective method for synthesizing nitrogen-doped carbon nanofibers on CF but also significantly enhances the redox kinetics of $\mathrm { T i O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ ，thereby substantially improving the electrochemical performance of Ti-Ce RFBs.

# 2.2.The preparation of N-CNFs on CF through a one-step thermal treatment process

Firstly,commercially available carbon felt $( 3 \times 3 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ is placed in an air environment and pretreated by calcination at $5 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for 4 hours. Subsequently, the specimen is immersed in a $2 \mathrm { m o l } \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { H C }$ I solution fora period of 24 hours,after which it is thoroughly washed with deionized water and ethanol in order to remove surface impurities.The subsequent stage of the process is to prepare the nickel (Ni)-doped precursor solution.To do so,11 mL of deionized water and $1 1 \mathrm { m L }$ of methanol are to be mixed,with $0 . 0 5 { \ g }$ of solid NaOH then added.The mixture is then to be stirred thoroughly for10 minutes,after which $0 . 6 8 g$ of $\mathrm { N i C l } _ { 2 } { \cdot } 6 \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } 0$ is to be added.The mixture is then to be left to stir for a further 3O minute. The pretreated carbon felt is then to be immersed into the mixture solution, sonicated for a period of 3O minutes,and left to soak fora further 30 minutes.Following the removal of the carbon felt,the component should be dried at ${ 1 3 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } }$ for a period of 8 hours in order to complete the nickel loading process.Subsequently, the nickel-loaded carbon felt and 10 times its mass of melamine should be placed in a quartz boat. The temperature should then be increased in an argon atmosphere at a rate of $2 \mathrm { \mathrm { ~ } ^ { \circ } C \ m i n ^ { - 1 } }$ to $8 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ 、The temperature should be maintained for 2 hours in order to promote the catalytic cracking of melamine and the growth of N-CNFs.Following thermal treatment, the sample was immersed in $3 ~ \mathrm { m o l ~ L ^ { - 1 } }$ HCl for 24 hours in order to remove residual nickel.Thereafter,the sample was thoroughly washed with deionised water and ethanol until it was neutral,and then dried.The target product N-CNF/CF was then obtained.

To determine the optimal nitrogen source and carbon source dosage, we compared the effects of different melamine dosages $( 5 \times , 1 0 \times ,$ and $1 5 \times$ relative to carbon felt) on the growth of N-CNFs on carbon felt.As shown in Fig.Sl,experimental results indicate that carbon nanotube growth density was markedly insufficient under 5 times conditions. Whilst 15 times promoted formation,excessive melamine led to pronounced agglomeration and accumulation of the product. Therefore, considering both growth density and morphological uniformity,this study selected a 1O times melamine dosage as the optimal condition.

To determine the optimal growth temperature $( 8 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ ，we compared the growth of N-CNFs on carbon felt at $6 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ ， $8 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ and $1 0 0 0 \ { } ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } .$ The results in Fig. S2d-f show that N-CNFs could not be successfully generated at $6 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathbf { C }$ conversely,they could be generated at both $8 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and $1 0 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ (Fig. S2g-l). However,at $1 0 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ the elevated temperature resulted in multiple fractures within the carbon fibers of the carbon felt (Fig.S2j-l).Consequently, $8 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ was designated as the optimal condition in this preliminary study.The cyclic voltammetry (CV) test results of carbon felt modified at different temperatures,as demonstrated in Fig.S3,further substantiate that $8 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ is the optimal temperature for N-CNF growth.

# 3．Results and discussion

# 3.1． Characterization of morphological and chemical properties

This work employs a simple synthesis strategy,using bare carbon felt (BCF)as the substrate,and first loads metal nickel (Ni) onto its surface as a catalyst. Subsequently,melamine is introduced as a nitrogen source and carbon precursor.Through an one-step controlled pyrolysis process, a three-dimensional nitrogen-doped carbon nanofiber network is successfully grown in situ on the carbon felt substrate,resulting in the preparation of a three-dimensional nitrogen-doped carbon nanofiber composite carbon felt electrode (N-CNF/CF).The schematic diagram of the preparation process is shown in Fig.1.This method ingeniously achieves the effective doping of nitrogen atoms and the simultaneous construction of a three-dimensional nanofiber structure, laying the foundation for enhancing the electrochemical performance of the electrode.

BCF is composed of micron-sized carbon fibers with a smooth surface (Fig.2a-c). Following the aforementioned synthetic modification,N-CNFs exhibit dense and extensive growth on the surface of the carbon fibers (Fig.2d-f) and intertwine with each other (Fig.2g). This structure significantly increases the number of reactive sites,enhancing charge transfer efficiency.To measure the variation of specific surface areaafter modification,BET tests were carried out and the result were shown in Fig.2j and 2k.Compared with BCF,the specific surface area of the modified carbon felt (N-CNF/CF) increases from $3 . 9 6 \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { g } ^ { - 1 }$ to $2 3 . 7 5 \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 }$ ${ \bf g } ^ { - 1 }$ ,far exceeding that of BCF.Analysis of the N-CNF/CF pore size distribution (BJH model) (Table S1） reveals that the distribution is concentrated within the mesoporous range,further supporting the formation of a three-dimensional porous framework.Concurrently,the modification process successfully introduces N heteroatoms into the carbon framework.As shown in Fig.2h-i, the uniform distribution of C and N elements in the prepared N-CNF/CF was confirmed,providing the effective synthesis of N-CNF/CF.

The XRD patterns of N-CNF/CF and BCF are compared in Fig.3a. The modified sample exhibited no new distinct diffraction peaks,and its main diffraction peaks continued to belong to carbon materials,further confirming that the modification effect primarily manifests in the nanostructured layer on the surface of the CF and in the low content and highly dispersed nitrogen doping [27].At the same time, the position of the broad XRD peak near $2 6 ^ { \circ }$ (002) crystalline plane of the modified carbon felt (derived from the graphitic nature of carbon) did not shift, but the peak intensity increased slightly,indicating that the modification process slightly improved the crystalline order of the material [28]. Furthermore,as shown in Fig.3b,the Raman spectrum exhibits a strong G band near $1 6 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { c m } ^ { - 1 } }$ ,corresponding to the graphitization characteristics of the nanofibers in the material; a strong D band is observed near $1 3 4 0 ~ \mathrm { { c m } ^ { - 1 } }$ ,indicating structural defects in the carbon nanofibers [29, 30].Notably,the intensity of the G band in N-CNF/CF is significantly higher than that of the D band,and the $\mathtt { G } / \mathtt { D }$ intensity ratio is significantly increased compared to BCF.This result indicates that the graphitization degree and crystallinity of N-CNF/CF are enhanced,while graphite impurities and nanofiber defects are reduced.

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was used to characterize the elemental composition and nitrogen bonding configuration of the N-CNF/CF sample.Fig.3c shows that the XPS full spectrum of N-CNF/CF exhibits three distinct characteristic peaks corresponding to C 1s,N 1s, and O 1s,confirming that nitrogen has been successfully incorporated into the carbon felt framework.The N 1s fine spectrum (Fig.3d) can be fitted by deconvoluting into four peaks: pyridinic N $\left( 3 9 8 . 3 \mathrm { e V } \right)$ , pyrrolic N (399.6 eV),graphitic N (401.1 eV),and oxidized pyridine N (403.2 eV)[31-33]. Notably,pyridinic N and pyrrolic Naccount for over $6 0 \%$ of the total N content (Fig. S4),and abundant electronegative nitrogen atoms generated from these two nitrogen configurations serve as active sites through strong interaction [33-36].Additionally,the strong electron affinity of N atoms improves the electronic conductivity of the carbon matrix [36]. The presence of C-N and C-O polar bonds,in conjunction with the three-dimensional nanofiber structureof N-CNF/CF,has been demonstrated to result in a substantial enhancement of the hydrophilic wetting properties of the sample [37].The hydrophilicity transformation of the material was characterized using contact angle measurements.As shown in Fig.3e,on the BCF surface, the water droplet formed a spherical shape with a contact angle of approximately $\boldsymbol { 1 3 5 ^ { \circ } }$ .However,on the N-CNF/CF surface (Fig.3f), the water droplet was completely absorbed upon contact,making it impossible to measure the contact angle (see the attached video).This phenomenon fully demonstrates the superhydrophilicity of N-CNF/CF.

To compare the adsorption activity of titanium species on different nitrogen-doped sites on carbon felt and on undoped carbon felt,we performed density functional theory (DFT) calculations for geometric optimisation and adsorption energy assessment. The calculations employed a monolayer graphene as the carbon substrate model.In this model,carbon atoms at different positions in the substrate were systematically replaced with nitrogen atoms to simulate nitrogen doping under different chemical environments [38].As shown in Fig.4a-c,in the nitrogen-doped carbon (NC) model,the adsorption energies for pyridinic N,pyrrolic N,and graphitic N sites have adsorption energies of -6.54,-6.17,and -0.77 eV,respectively,for Ti(III).For Ti(IV)-O,the adsorption energies are -5.47，-6.73，and -2.46 eV，respectively (Fig.4e-g).In contrast,the adsorption energies for Ti and Ti-O on undoped graphene are only-0.64 and $- 2 . 4 6 \mathrm { e V }$ ,respectively (Fig. 4d and 4h).It is evident that the presence of pyridinic N,pyrrolic N,and graphitic N significantly enhances the adsorption energy of the carbon substrate for titanium (especially pyridinic N and pyrrolic N).This further confirms that nitrogen doping can effectively enhance reaction kinetics.

# 3.2. Electrochemical Characterization and Ti-Ce RFB testing

To evaluate the electrochemical performance of the prepared samples, the electrical double-layer capacitor (EDLC) of BCF and N-CNF/CF was first measured to verify whether N-CNF/CF is conducive to promoting the redox reaction of titanium ions $\mathrm { ( T i O ^ { 2 + } / T i ^ { 3 + } ) }$ (Fig. 5a-b). The experimental results (Fig.5c) show that the EDLC of N-CNF/CF electrode is significantly higher than that of BCF electrode (N-CNF/CF:2.24 F $g ^ { - 1 }$ Vs. BCF: $0 . 0 4 \mathrm { ~ F ~ g ~ } ^ { - 1 } .$ ). This significant difference in EDLC further confirms that growing N-CNFs on CF substrate effectively increases the specific surface area and the number of active sites of electrode,thereby significantly enhancing the charge storage capacity of electrode and providing robust support for electrochemical reactions.CV testing (Fig.5d) shows that the BCF electrode exhibits extremely low activity and slow reaction kinetics in the $\mathrm { T i O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ redox reaction. In stark contrast,the modified N-CNF/CF electrode exhibits significantly enhanced redox peak currents,primarilyattributed to its larger specific surface area and abundant active sites.Additionally,the redox peak potential difference of N-CNF/CF electrode is significantly reduced, further indicating that nitrogen doping effectively accelerates the redox reaction kinetics of the $\mathrm { T i O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ redox pair [39]. The electrode kinetics were further analyzed implementation through the electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) test. In the Nyquist plot of Fig.5e,the diameter of the semicircle in the high-frequency region reflects the charge transfer resistance $\mathrm { ( R _ { c t } ) }$ [40]. The $\mathrm { R _ { c t } }$ of N-CNF/CF electrode is significantly smaller than that of BCF electrode,indicating a significant reduction in $\operatorname { R } _ { \operatorname { c t } }$ and higher charge transfer efficiency.A simplified equivalent circuit and partial fitting data can be found in Fig. S5 and table S2.

These results collectively indicate that the redox behavior of active species is simultaneously controlled by electrochemical kinetics and diffusion processes [41].Based on the Randles-Sevcik equation (Eq.1) [42] and Eq.2[43], we calculated the diffusion coefficient and chemical reaction kinetic constant (specific values are shown in Table S5).The results indicate that both the diffusion coefficient and chemical reaction kinetic constant of N-CNF/CF have significantly improved.This means that the modified electrode not only significantly improves the mass transfer diffusion process of $\mathrm { T i O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ but also accelerates its redox reaction kinetics. These performance improvements can be attributed to the high porosity, large specific surface area, excellent hydrophilicity,and abundant active sites of the N-CNF/CF electrode.

Ti-Ce RFBs with BCF or N-CNF/CF electrodes were assembled to evaluate their cycling performance.A comparison of cycling efficiency over 200 cycles (Fig.6a-c) shows that,under the same current density, the coulombic efficiency (CE) remains stable atapproximately $9 8 . 6 0 \%$ ， regardless of whether the electrodes are modified.This demonstrates the inherent high reversibility of the active materials in Ti-Ce RFBs and the minimal occurrence of side reactions during the reaction process,which is crucial for the battery's long-term cycling stability.Interestingly, there are significant differences in voltage efficiency (VE) and energy efficiency (EE).The VE and EE values for the N-CNF/CF electrode (85.92 $\%$ ， $8 4 . 0 7 ~ \%$ ）are significantly higher than those for the BCF electrode $( 7 8 . 6 6 \% , 7 7 . 5 6 \% )$ .Additionally,the discharge capacity of the modified electrodes increased by $1 4 ~ \%$ (Fig.S1O),clearly demonstrating the advantages of the modified electrode.A comparison of the chargedischarge curves also shows that the voltage efficiency of the modified electrode has improved,which further increases its battery capacity.

# 4.Conclusion

This study proposes a low-cost,easy-to-operate,and highly efficient electrode modification strategy for Ti-Ce RFB,and its effectiveness has been verified through experimental characterisation and electrochemical experiments.By loading N-CNFs onto carbon felt,the specific surface area and number of active sites of the electrode were significantly increased,while also enhancing hydrophilicity.The specific surface area of the N-CNF/CF electrode reached five times thatof the BCF.Also,DFT calculations uncover that N-doped modulates the electron transfer and enhances the titanium adsorption.Single-cell performance testing demonstrated that the Ti-Ce RFB using the N-CNF/CF electrode exhibited excellent EE and cycling stability:2Oo cycles,the average VE and EE reached $8 5 . 9 2 \%$ and $8 4 . 0 6 ~ \%$ ,respectively,significantly outperforming the BCF electrode's $7 8 . 6 6 ~ \%$ and $7 7 . 5 6 \ \ \%$ Additionally，the discharge capacity of the modified electrodes increased by $1 4 ~ \%$ .During continuous operation spanning 30 days, the battery's capacity and efficiency degradation were negligible.The N-CNF/CF electrodes demonstrate significant commercial potential not only in Ti-Ce RFBs but also in Ti-based RFBs,and hold promising prospects for engineering applications.
