Bromine-based flow bateries (Br-FBs) have been widely used for stationary energy storage benefiting from their high positive potential, high solubility and lowcost.However,theyare stillconfronted with serious challenges including bromine cross-diffusion,sluggish reaction kinetics of $\bf { B r _ { 2 } / B r ^ { - } }$ redox couple and sometimes dendrites. To impelthe further industrial developmentof Br-FBs,great efforts have been made in their key materials of the electrodes, membranes and electrolytes.Among them, electrolyte optimization is one of the most promising strategies becauseof its great economyand feasibility,which includes electrolytecomposition optimization and electrolyte flow optimization.Herein,we first summarize the physicochemical properties and composition of electrolytes for Br-FBs. Notably,the spectroscopic characterization methods are also overviewed in this review, in orderto provide the comprehensive analysis methods to guide the electrolytedesignand modification.Then we analyze the main challenges and summarize corresponding strategies related to electrolytes.The promising opportunities for electrolyte optimization to achieve high-performance and long-lifespan Br-FBs are outlined as well.As äresult,this review provides inspiration and guidance for further demonstration applications of Br-FBs.

# 1．Introduction

Efficient utilization and storage of renewable energies are critical to achieve the goals of emission peak and carbon neutrality. Consequently, large-scale energy storage technologies are receiving increasing attention [1,2]. That is because energy storage technologies can effectively overcome the intermittence and instability of renewable energies and stabilize the output of as-generated electricity.Flow batteries (FBs) have surged much attention ascribing to their attractive advantages of flexible design,environmental benignity,long life,fast response speed,inherent safety and so on [3-5].Among them, the $\bf { B r _ { 2 } / B r ^ { - } }$ redox couple is often used as the positive active species to construct a Br-FB (Fig.1),which is an ideal candidate for stationary energy storage with following advantages: [6-8].

(i) Wide voltage window: high electrode potential of $\mathrm { B r _ { 2 } / B r ^ { - } }$ redox couple (1.08 V). (ii) High solubility: e.g. $\mathrm { Z n B r } _ { 2 } > 8 \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ in $_ \mathrm { H _ { 2 } O }$ at $2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ (iii) High energy density: based on high voltage and high solubility.

(iv) Low cost: abundant sources of bromine in seawater (Table 1) [8, 9].
(v) High reliability: the safety hazard of Br-FBs mainly results from the volatility and corrosiveness of bromine molecules.This issue can be overcome by adding complexing agents into electrolytes and optimizing the battery system.In addition，the batery components with high stability will not be corroded during the long-term operation [10,11].

Of note,for metal-based anode active materials,such as zincbromine (ZBFBs), lithium-bromine (LBFBs) and tin-bromine flow batteries(TBFBs),the reactions at the negative side are based on the dissolution/deposition mechanisms [2O-26].The redox reactions and features of different Br-FB systems are summarized in Table 2[10, 27-30].

Nowadays, ZBFBs and HBFBs have been at the demonstration stage [32,33,41,42].In recent years,ZBFBs have made great progress.In 2023,the 1Oo kWh ZBFB system for user-side energy storage developed by Dalian Institute of Chemical Physics has been connected to the grid successfully.However,in order to accelerate the further development of

Br-FBs,there are still many common and thorny issues that need to be solved. The sluggish kinetics of $\mathrm { B r _ { 2 } / B r ^ { - } }$ redox couple limit the working current density and power density of batteries.The easy bromine cross-diffusion causes serious self-discharge,leading to low coulombic efficiency (CE),high capacity decay rate and short lifespan [6,33,43].In addition,for metal-based hybrid Br-FBs,the metal accumulation/dendrite issue during the charge process affects the battery life and reliability [28,44,45].In order to solve above issues,great efforts have been devoted to the key components of Br-FBs,including the membranes, the electrodes as well as the electrolytes [31,46-48].The modifications on membranes are efficient in inhibiting bromine crossover or regulating the metal deposition behavior by designed functional layers on porous substrates [43].However,the membranes suitable for Br-FBs are limited,because of the extremely high stability requirements caused by the strongly oxidized and corrosive bromine [43,45]. Nowadays, porous polyolefin membranes (such as Daramic membranes）are commonly used in Br-FBs.Nonetheless,the modified functional layers will influence the pore structure and ion conductivity of membranes and restrict the ion transport,causing high membrane resistance and battery polarization [45].In recent years,the modifications on electrodes have evolved from the initial enhancement of catalytic activity to the simultaneous improvement of bromine retention capacity [6,41].Even so, electrode modification methods are faced with the deficiencies of complexity,high cost and difficulty in commercialization.The electrolyte is the carrier of the active substances of FBs,upon which its concentration and volume directly determine the energy storage capacity of the battery.The solubility,conductivity and viscosity of electrolytes are closely related to the overall performance of the battery [49,5O].As a result,the electrolyte optimization is an effective,simple and feasible method to deal with above challenges and improve battery performance, mainly including adjusting their composition or concentration.As a result,herein,we summarized the progress of electrolytes for Br-FBs and propose prospective directions for their future exploration (Fig.2).

# 2.Overview of electrolytes for Br-FBs

Generally speaking,the electrolytes for Br-FBs mainly consist of active substances, bromine complexing agents (BCAs) and supporting electrolytes (Table 3).Active substances participate in electrochemical redox reactions on electrodes.During the charge process, $\mathtt { B r } ^ { - }$ are oxidized to form bromine molecules,which then combined with $\mathtt { B r } ^ { - }$ to form polybromide ions $( \mathtt { B r } _ { 2 \mathrm { n } + 1 } ^ { - }$ ： $\mathrm { B r } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ ， $\mathrm { B r } _ { 5 } ^ { - }$ ， $\mathrm { B r } _ { 7 } ^ { - }$ ,etc.)by electrochemicalchemical mechanisms [51].During the discharge process,the reversible process of the above reactions occurs [23].Differently,the purpose of adding BCAs to electrolytes is to inhibit bromine diffusion.At present, the commonly used BCAs are based on organic quaternary ammonium cation compounds,because their bromide or chloride salts are usually highly soluble in aqueous electrolytes.The addition of BCAs can reduce the saturated vapor pressure [52]. The larger organic cations and $\mathrm { B r } _ { 2 \mathrm { n + 1 } } ^ { - }$ forma dense,stable and hydrophobic oily phase ( $\mathbf { B C A - B r _ { 2 n + 1 } }$ fused salt), which is separated from the aqueous phase due to the density difference [52]. Namely,most of bromine is complexed at the bottom and very litle bromine remains in the aqueous phase, consequently inhibiting bromine diffusion.Moreover,BCAs will combine with $\mathrm { B r } _ { 2 \mathrm { n } + 1 } ^ { - }$ to form larger BCA $\boldsymbol { \mathrm { B r } } _ { 2 \mathrm { n } + 1 }$ complexes,which can be excluded effectively by a porous membrane based on the size-sieving effect (Fig.2) [21].The supporting electrolytes play a role in improving the ion conductivity of electrolytes and reducing the internal resistance of batteries,which will not participate in the electrochemical reactions [53].Now,chloride salts like KCl and $\mathrm { N H } _ { 4 } \mathrm { C l } _ { \mathrm { \ell } }$ ，are commonly used as supporting electrolytes in Br-FBs [2O].The electrolyte compositions for various Br-FB systems are summarized in Table 3.In addition, the electrolyte utilization ratios are related to the economic viability of Br-FBs,which is directly relevant with their charge areal capacity [37].At present, the anode structure designs,the electrolyte optimizations and the membrane modifications are effective ways to increase the areal capacity namely the electrolyte utilization ratios of Br-FBs [45,54,55].

# 3. Spectroscopic analysis methods of electrolytes

The addition of BCAs will exert a great impact on the physicochemical properties of electrolytes,while exploring the active substance variations during the charge and discharge process is significant for elucidating the kinetic process of $\mathrm { B r _ { 2 } / B r ^ { - } }$ redox reactions.At the same time,the elucidation of the interactions between BCAs and electrolyte components is instructive for BCA selection.Consequently,spectroscopic analysis of electrolytes is very necessary.At present,spectroscopic analysis tools,including Raman spectroscopy,ultraviolet-visible (UV-vis） absorption spectroscopy and nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy (NMR), have been used for the quantitative and qualitative analysis of electrolytes for Br-FBs.

# 3.1． Raman spectroscopy

Raman spectroscopy is commonly used to analyze the electrolyte composition as well as the interaction between different components during the redox reaction process [64-66].For example,in ZBFB systems,various zinc bromide complexes in the aqueous phase could be confirmed by analyzing the Raman spectra of 14 solutions with varying bromide/zinc ratios,including $\mathrm { Z n B r ^ { + } }$ ， $\mathrm { Z n B r } _ { 2 }$ ， $\mathrm { Z n B r } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ ， $\mathrm { Z n B r } _ { 4 } ^ { - }$ and $\scriptstyle [ Z \ n B r _ { 4 } ] ^ { 2 - }$ [67-69].Furthermore,the distribution of zinc bromide complexes was affected by temperatures, state-of-charges (SOCs) and electrolyte concentrations rather than the addition of BCAs.Li et al.used Raman spectroscopy to explore the compositions of positive electrolytes at different SOCs [21]. The results showed that the charge products at the early charge stage (lower SOC) were mainly $\mathrm { B r } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ $( 1 6 0 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 } )$ ,which reacted with $\mathbf { B r } _ { 2 }$ to form $\mathsf { B r } _ { 5 } ^ { - }$ $\left( 2 5 7 \thinspace \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 } \right)$ at higher SOC. Raman spectroscopy was also used to study the solvation structures of $\mathrm { Z n ^ { 2 + } }$ in $\mathrm { Z n B r } _ { 2 }$ solutions with different concentrations by $\mathrm { Y u }$ Liu et al. [64]. According to the analysis of $\mathrm { Z n B r _ { x } ^ { 2 - x } }$ proportion by peak fiting of Raman spectra, they found that the solvation structure of $\scriptstyle { \mathrm { Z n } } ^ { 2 + }$ in concentrated electrolytes changed from $\mathrm { Z n ( H _ { 2 } O ) } _ { 6 } ^ { 2 + }$ $( 3 9 1 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 } )$ to $\mathrm { Z n B r _ { x } ^ { 2 - x } }$ $( 2 0 0 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 } )$ ， thus changing the zinc deposition potential (Fig. $_ { 3 a - c ) }$ . In addition, in-situ Raman spectroscopy is helpful to investigate the variations of electrolyte compositions as well as the reversibility and mechanism of electrochemical reactions.Li et al. have made great efforts to study the electrode-electrolyte interface of ZBFBs and the active substance variations during electrochemical reactions.The catalytic activity and bromine immobilization capacity of electrodes were thus verified [6, 41].Also, they studied the electrochemical reaction mechanism in a TiBCFB with two-electron transfer by in-situ Raman spectroscopy. Raman spectra showed $\mathrm { B r } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ and $\mathrm { B r } _ { 5 } ^ { - }$ peaks during the first charge process and $\mathrm { B r C l } _ { 2 } ^ { - }$ peak (with the polybromide peaks disappeared) during the second charge process.Based on that,they determined that the two-step reactions corresponded to the electrochemical reactions of $\mathrm { B r _ { 2 } / B r ^ { - } }$ and $\mathrm { B r C l } _ { 2 } ^ { - } / \mathrm { B r } _ { 2 }$ redox couples (Fig.3d) [37]. Jung Hoon Yang et al. observed that the peak intensity of $\mathbf { Z } \mathbf { n } \mathbf { B } \mathbf { r } _ { 2 }$ in the Raman spectra was proportional to $\mathbf { Z } \mathbf { n } \mathbf { B } \mathbf { r } _ { 2 }$ concentrations and the SOCs of electrolytes (Fig. $3 e { \mathrm { - } } f )$ [70]. Then,they further carried out in-situ Raman analysis on the negative electrolyte for the highly accurate control on the real-time SOC,which was essential to achieve stable battery operation.

# 3.2.UV-vis absorption spectroscopy

UV-vis absorption spectroscopy can be used to analyze the solution compositions qualitatively and quantitatively based on the specificity of absorption wavelength $\mathrm { { ( B r ^ { - } } }$ ： $1 8 6 \ \mathrm { n m }$ ， $\mathrm { B r } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ ： $2 6 6 \ \mathrm { \ n m }$ ， $\mathbf { B r } _ { 2 }$ ： $3 9 0 \ \mathrm { n m } \mathrm { \Omega }$ (Fig.4a) [71-73]. The formation and dissociation kinetics of $\mathrm { B r } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ were explained with the help of reflection absorption UV-vis spectroscopy [72,74].The electrolyte constituent changes (namely active substance evolutions) during the charge and discharge processes were continuously analyzed by in-situ UV-vis absorption spectroscopy [75].Most importantly,the combination of various spectroscopic analysis methods contributes to a more comprehensive and deep analysis of electrochemical reactions.For instance,UV-vis spectroscopy was used to characterize the electrolyte adjacent to the zinc electrode during charging，which could quantitatively characterize the bromine cross-diffusion (Fig.4b) [76].Meanwhile,the electrolyte collected from the cathode was analyzed by Raman spectroscopy,verifying the effective conversion of $\mathtt { B r } ^ { - }$ to $\mathbf { B r } _ { 2 }$ and the further growth of polybromide ions (Fig. 4c).

# 3.3.NMR

The structural changes and interaction strengths of the molecules can also be revealed by monitoring the intensity and position of the NMR peaks over time [77,78].Li et al. used two-dimensional (2D) NMR to study the interactions between ChCl and different BCAs,including MEPBr and MEMBr. The 2D $\mathrm { ^ { 1 } H ^ { 1 } H _ { - } }$ Nuclear Overhauser Effect spectroscopy provided obvious signals of $[ 5 - 2 ^ { \prime \prime } ]$ ， $[ 7 - 2 ^ { \prime \prime } ]$ and $[ 2 / 3 - 2 ^ { \prime } ]$ in the mixture of ChCl and MEMBr (denoted as CCA-M).They deduced that there should be a strong interaction between $\mathbf { M E M } ^ { + }$ and choline when $\mathrm { B r } _ { 2 \mathrm { n } + 1 } ^ { - }$ existed (Fig.5a and b). The only one weak signal of $[ 2 ^ { \prime } / 3 ^ { \prime }  – 2 ^ { \prime } ]$ observed in the mixture of ChCl and MEPBr (denoted as CCA-P) suggested an inadequate dispersive effect of ChCl on MEPBr (Fig.5a and c) [79].Thus,they concluded that choline could effectively act as the dispersant for $\mathbf { M E M } ^ { + }$ and prevent the formation of the molecular matrix while MEP- $\cdot \mathrm { B r } _ { 2 \mathrm { n } + 1 }$ was easy to form due to the inadequate dispersive effect of ChCl on MEPBr at low temperatures (Fig. $5 a { - } d )$ .Evan Zhao et al.estimated the degradation of 2,6-dihydroxy-anthraquinone (DHAQ) on a compact $4 3 ~ \mathrm { M H z }$ benchtop system,since inline NMR could directly analyze the chemical structures as well as the reaction kinetics of $\mathrm { D H A L } ^ { 2 - }$ and $\mathrm { D H A } ^ { 2 - }$ products [77].Also,NMR was a non-destructive and quantitative analytical tool to monitor the crossover rate through the membrane.Based on that, they established a negative correlation between the molecular size and the crossover rate,demonstrating that the benchtop NMR system was practical for the operando study of FBs.

# 4.Challenges of Br-FBs

# 4.1．Bromine cross-difusion

The diffusion and migration of soluble bromine species to the negative side and the reactions with the anode active substances lead to the battery self-discharge,resulting in capacity degradation and low CE (Fig.6a).When taking ZBFB as an example, from Fig.6b, the opencircuit voltage kept a decreasing trend due to the self-discharge during the resting process,causing the disappearance of discharge platform and low capacity retention [64].Similarly,Li etal.explored the crossover behavior of $\mathrm { B r ^ { - } }$ and $\mathbf { B r } _ { 2 }$ in a QBFB [8O]. The crossover speed during discharging was higher than that during charging because that the strong electric field during charging prevented bromine crossover. Combined with molecular dynamics (MD) simulation, they found that $\mathrm { B r } ^ { - }$ cross behavior accounted for a large proportion of the total cross behavior,and the CE was affected by the self-discharge reactions between $\mathbf { B r } _ { 2 }$ and $\mathsf { A O S H } _ { 2 }$

# 4.2. Sluggish kinetics of $B r _ { 2 } / B r ^ { - }$ redox reactions

The reactive activity of the $\bf { B r _ { 2 } / B r ^ { - } }$ redox couple is relatively low because that the reactions on the electrode involve adsorption and

desorption processes,resulting in large electrochemical polarization and low power density of the battery [7,23,81]. The reaction equations of $\mathrm { B r _ { 2 } / B r ^ { - } }$ redox couple are as follows:

$$
\begin{array} { l } { { \mathrm { B r } _ { 2 } \ + \ \mathrm { e } ^ { - }  \mathrm { B r } _ { \mathrm { a d s } } + \mathrm { B r } ^ { - } } } \\ { ~ } \\ { { \mathrm { B r } _ { \mathrm { a d s } } } \ + \ \mathrm { e } ^ { - }  \mathrm { B r } ^ { - } } \end{array}
$$

Among them, the formation of $\mathrm { B r _ { a d s } }$ is the rate-determining step, namely,the electrodes with poor bromine adsorption capacity may not accelerate the reaction kinetics of the $\mathrm { B r _ { 2 } / B r ^ { - } }$ redox couple (Fig. 6c) [8]. Worse still, the $\mathbf { B C A - B r _ { 2 n + 1 } }$ complexes are adsorbed on the electrode surface and cover the limited reactive sites.The phase separation of the oily $\mathbf { B C A - B r _ { 2 n + 1 } }$ phase and aqueous phase or other adverse changes in physicochemical properties also affect the conductivity and mass transfer, further aggravating the problem of slow reaction kinetics of $\mathrm { B r _ { 2 } / B r ^ { - } }$ redox couple.

# 4.3.Dendrite

Dendrite is a tricky problem for metal anodes involving deposition and dissolution reactions,reducing efficiencies,cycle life and safety of batteries [7,28,82-85]. The uneven distributions of active substances and charges lead to heterogeneous ion concentrations and electric field distributions,contributing to dendrite formation,which will further exacerbate these uneven situations [86,87].Herein,taking the Zn anode as an example,the formation process of zinc dendrites consists of four steps: $\scriptstyle { \mathrm { Z n } } ^ { 2 + }$ diffusion, ${ \mathrm { Z n } } ^ { 2 + }$ reduction, zinc nucleation and zinc growth. When $\scriptstyle { \mathrm { Z n } } ^ { 2 + }$ begins to deposit, $\mathrm { Z n ^ { 2 + } }$ prefers to nucleate at the sites with higher concentrations or stronger local current density (Fig. 6d)[44, 88-90].The initially formed zinc protrusions lead to localized zinc deposition and induce ramified zinc growth.In addition,on the cathode side, the oily phase obstructs the interaction between the aqueous phase and the electrode surface,preventing the reduction from $\mathbf { B r } _ { 2 }$ to $\mathtt { B r } ^ { - }$ . Asa result, the zinc dendrites formed in the corresponding region cannot be easily oxidized to $\mathrm { Z n ^ { 2 + } }$ ，exacerbating the dendrite issue [87].The growing dendrites will puncture the membrane and result in internal short circuits,decreasing the battery safety [85,91].Worse still, the formation of dead zinc and the detachment of zinc dendrites from the electrode lead to the capacity loss of batteries.

# 5.Mitigation strategies for the challenges of Br-FBs

# 5.1.Strategies related to bromine cross-diffusion

# 5.1.1.Electrolyte concentration adjustment

In general, the viscosity and density of the electrolyte increase with its concentration,which restricts the cross-diffusion of soluble $\mathrm { B r } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ .For example,by increasing the concentration of $\mathbf { Z } \mathbf { n } \mathbf { B } \mathbf { r } _ { 2 }$ from 2Mto $^ { 2 0 \mathrm { M } }$ ,the electrolyte density increased from 1.18 to $2 . 6 0 \ \mathrm { g \ c m ^ { - 3 } }$ ,while its viscosity increased dramatically from1.24 to $6 8 . 1 3 \mathrm { m P a } s$ .The interaction forces between the molecules also increased, resulting in slower diffusion rate of $\mathrm { B r } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ [92].Therefore,it was difficult for $\mathrm { B r } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ to diffuse in $\mathrm { Z n B r _ { 2 } }$ solution and confined to the cathode area, effectively inhibiting the cross-diffusion of bromine (Fig.7a and b) [64].The zinc-bromine battery (ZBB) with a $2 0 \mathrm { M } \mathrm { Z n B r } _ { 2 }$ electrolyte had a high capacityretention rate of $7 4 . 9 8 \%$ after resting for $2 4 \mathrm { h }$ .Differently,the ZBBwith a $^ { 2 \mathbf { M } }$ $\mathbf { Z } \mathbf { n } \mathbf { B } \mathbf { r } _ { 2 }$ electrolyte showeda sharp decline in capacityretention beyond 2-h resting and exhibited only $0 . 1 3 ~ \%$ capacity after $2 4 \mathrm { h }$ (Fig. 7c). As a result,the battery achieved higher CE,energy efficiency (EE)and superiorcycle stability,verifying the effectiveness of increasing the electrolyte concentration to inhibit self-discharge (Fig.7d).

Notably,the faded capacity of Br-FBs caused by bromine crossdiffusion could be recovered to a certain degree.Xie et al.used a 4.0 MHBr solution as a positive electrolyte and a $0 . 5 \mathrm { M } \mathrm { S n C l } _ { 2 } + 3 . 0$ MHBr solution as a negative electrolyte to construct a TBFB [28].To solve the cross-contamination problem， they proposed a Sn reverse-electrodeposition method,which could transfer the crossed $\sin ^ { 2 + }$ from the positive side back to the negative side.As shown in Fig.7e, the battery was fully discharged first.Then,after the positive and negative electrolytes were exchanged, the battery was charged to deposit the crossed $\sin ^ { 2 + }$ at the negative side. Finally,they exchanged back the electrolytes and $\mathsf { S } \mathsf { n } ^ { 2 + }$ was produced by the chemical reaction of Sn and $\scriptstyle \mathbf { S n } ^ { 4 + }$ . As a result, the $\mathsf { S } \mathsf { n } ^ { 2 + }$ that crossed through the membrane could be transferred back to the negative side,realizing the in-situ capacity recovery during battery operation.

# 5.2.Strategies related to sluggish kinetics

The main reason for the low power density of Br-FBs is the large internal resistance due to the low electrolyte conductivity and high polarization of bromine cathode. The proper choices of supporting electrolytes (like KCl and $\mathrm { N H } _ { 4 } \mathrm { C l }$ )could effectively reduce the resistance and boost the reaction kinetics of $\bf { B r _ { 2 } / B r ^ { - } }$ redox couple.For instance,in a deep eutectic solvent (DES), $\mathrm { C l ^ { - } }$ combined with bromine species to form polyinterhalide anions,accelerating the bromine reduction reaction (Fig.13a) [106].Meanwhile, the presence of $\mathrm { C l ^ { - } }$ could reduce the size of Zn-halide complex anions and promote the ion conduction and diffusion processes,thus boosting the reactions at the negative side of ZBB.As a result, the DES electrolyte containing $\mathrm { Z n C l } _ { 2 }$ endowed the ZBB with much higher rate capability and longer lifespan.As discussed above,the addition of BCAs generally leads to phase separation and decreases the electrolyte conductivity,which is also one of the main reasons for the slow kinetics.A type of BCAs that could maintain the uniform distribution of $\mathbf { B C A - B r _ { 2 n + 1 } }$ complexes is expected. Interestingly, CHA not only effectively captured bromine but also made the electrolyte almost homogeneous due to the presence of hydroxyl groups,in favor of suppressing capacity decay and reducing battery polarization (Fig.13b) [10].Therefore,the TiBFB using the electrolyte containing CHA showed more stable performance with higher VE and EE than that with MEPBr, confirming the superiority of homogeneous complexation (Fig.13c and d).

In short,the key to dealing with the sluggish kinetics of $\mathrm { B r _ { 2 } / B r ^ { - } }$ issue is to reduce the resistance.This can be achieved by adding suitable supporting electrolytes to increase electrolyte conductivity.Another strategy is to utilize novel BCAs that can keep the electrolyte phase homogeneous,thus reducing the battery polarization.

# 5.3.Strategies related to dendrites

As for Br-FBs using metallic anodes,the almost inevitable dendrite issue affects the efficiencies,working current density,capacity, power density and cycle life of batteries [lO7].The electrolyte properties have great effects on the solvation structure of metal ions,the ion distribution at the electrode-electrolyte interface,the ion conductivity and the ion diffusion process,all of which play a key role in zinc deposition process. Asa consequence,the optimizations of electrolyte compositions and flow rates are important to affect the electrodeposition process,thereby solving the dendrite issue [108].

# 5.3.1．Electrolyte composition optimization

Based on aforementioned four steps of the anodic metal deposition process,the commonly used strategies related to electrolytes can be divided into three types [83,84,109,110]:

(i) to introduce polar ions or molecules to form the electrostatic shield effect;
(ii) to increase the nucleation rate to achieve more uniform metal grains;
(iii) to regulate the surface texture by increasing the ratios of (002) crystal planes.

5.3.1.6.Electrostatic shield mechanism. The cations of additive with lower reduction potential than that of $\mathbf { M } ^ { \mathrm { n + } }$ are preferentially adsorbed on the initially formed protrusions to form an electrostatic shield, which acts as a barrier to the access of $\mathbf { M } ^ { \mathrm { n + } }$ [111-113]. $\mathbf { M } ^ { \mathrm { n + } }$ will be repelled from the protrusions by the positively charged shield effect and deposit in the adjacent regions until a smooth deposition layer is formed. Nowadays,the commonly used additives with such an electrostatic shield mechanism mainly include organic and inorganic additives [46, 82,84,111,114,115].

Similarly,the surfactant $\mathrm { T P A } ^ { + }$ had higher surface activity and polarity, forming a shield layer with zincophobic repulsion effect and achieving more uniform and flatter zinc deposition morphology than using MEPBr [103].

Similarly,metal ionadditives such as $\mathrm { P b } ^ { 2 + }$ ， $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ , $\mathsf { S } \mathsf { n } ^ { 2 + }$ , $\mathrm { L i ^ { + } }$ ， $\mathbf { B i } ^ { 3 + }$ ,etc., also tend to adsorb on the metal protrusions and form electrostatic protective layers 64,84,l22-124].Dae Sik Kim et al. proposed that $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ formed a positively charged electrostatic shield layer around the zinc seeds without co-deposition, which repelled ${ \mathrm { Z n } } ^ { 2 + }$ approaching the seeds and drove $\mathrm { Z n ^ { 2 + } }$ to the adjacent regions of the anode [84]. In Liu's report, $\mathrm { L i ^ { + } }$ migrated faster than ${ \mathrm { Z n } } ^ { 2 + }$ and was inclined to adsorb on zinc protrusions,blocking the strong electric field and regulating the ion distribution at the interface (Fig.l4b) [64].Furthermore,Hyeong-Jin Kim et al.systematically investigated the effects of $\mathrm { L i ^ { + } }$ ， $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ ， $K ^ { + }$ ， $\bar { \bf M } \bar { \bf n } ^ { 2 + }$ ,and $\mathrm { C r ^ { 3 + } }$ on zinc deposition [l25]. They found that the higher charge and the smaller ion radius of metal ions contributed to the better electrostatic shield effect.For comparison,they also studied the electrostatic shield effect of $\mathrm { M E P ^ { + } }$ ,which was less effective due to its large molecular size.However, the synergistic effect of $\mathrm { M n ^ { 2 + } }$ and $\mathrm { M E P ^ { + } }$ achieved more controlled nucleation process of $\scriptstyle { \mathrm { Z n } } ^ { 2 + }$ and more uniform zinc deposition, then realized high and stable CE of batteries (Fig.14c and d).The above results showed that the mixture of organic and inorganic additives could inhibit the growth of metal dendrites on anodes more effectively.

In addition to the electrostatic shield effect, metal ions also exert an analogous effect on metal nucleation.The addition of Pb influenced the zinc polarization and nucleation at the initial stage,especially at higher concentrations.The deposited Pb on the zinc surface had a higher nucleation overpotential than the original zinc,postponing the nucleation process of zinc.Furthermore,as for the supporting electrolytes, chloride might adsorb on zinc surface,affecting zinc nucleation process and growth rate [129].Additionally,R.H. Another effective way to increase the nucleation rate is to limit the 2D diffusion of $\mathbf { M } ^ { \mathrm { n + } }$ on the anode surface. This is due to the fact that during the metal deposition process, $\mathbf { M } ^ { \mathrm { n + } }$ are adsorbed and diffused on the anode surface,and then aggregated into large nucleation sites. Typical examples are polyethylene glycol 20o (PEG20o,M.W. $= 2 0 0$ ， tetrabutylammonium sulfate $\mathrm { ( T B A _ { 2 } S O _ { 4 } ) }$ and polyacrylamide etc.[111, 118,13O].They adsorbed on the anode surface,which hindered the migration and aggregation of $\scriptstyle { \mathrm { Z n } } ^ { 2 + }$ on the anode surface, resulting in uniform zinc deposition.

5.3.1.8.Surface texture regulation. The texture represents the preferred orientation of the metal deposition at the anode-electrolyte interface, further affecting the surface morphology and dendrite growth (Fig.15a) [110,131].The larger angle between the matrix and the crystal plane will cause the easier zinc dendrite formation on anodes.In general, the (100)and (11O) crystal orientations of zinc surface are very conducive to dendrite growth because the angles between the crystal growth direction and the matrix are $7 0 { \sim } 9 0 ^ { \circ }$ [132].In contrast, the (002),(103)and (105)crystal orientations of zinc surface show the angles of $0 \sim 3 0 ^ { \circ }$ ， meaning less dendrite growth.Some inorganic additives like indium sulfate,tin oxide and boric acid are favorable to form (Oo2) basal plane, resulting in uniform zinc deposition distribution (Fig.15b) [131].In addition，the electrolyte concentrations can also affect the crystal orientation.Notably,3D carbon felts (CFs) are widely used as anodes in zinc-based FBs because they can produce a larger contact area between the electrode and the electrolyte,thus providing more nucleation site. However,the studies on the surface texture regulation are mainly focused on zinc foil electrodes since the substrate flatness is important for the in-situ study of zinc deposition process.Notably,the mechanisms of the surface texture regulation obtained by zinc anodes are instructive for 3D CFs in zinc-based FBs.In-depth explorations of the surface texture regulation of zinc deposition on 3D CFs are necessary in the future. Li et al.investigated the zinc deposition morphological evolution and mechanisms when changing the $\mathrm { Z n B r } _ { 2 }$ concentration during the zinc plating process [55].In concentrated electrolytes $( { \geq } 0 . 4 ~ \mathrm { M } )$ ,deposited zinc was dense due to instantaneous nucleation. On the contrary,as the plating process continued,the electrolyte became dilute gradually $( \leq 0 . 3$ M) and zinc became mossy due to the progressive nucleation (Fig.15c and d).Meanwhile,the dominant plane of zinc crystals changed from (002) to (lol)as decreasing the $\mathbf { Z } \mathbf { n } \mathbf { B } \mathbf { r } _ { 2 }$ concentration,which tended to grow at $3 0 { - } 7 0 ^ { \circ }$ to the substrate instead of parallel to the substrate (Fig.15e).As a result, with a critical $\mathrm { Z n B r } _ { 2 }$ concentration of $\ge 0 . 4 \mathbf { M }$ the zinc deposition morphology was relatively flat and dense,ensuring high reversibility of Zn plating/stripping and achieving high CE (Fig.15f).

# 5.3.2.Electrolyte flow optimization

As mentioned above,the diffusion and ion concentration gradients trigger the formation of dendrites,which are also afected by the electrolyte flow.Therefore,the diffusion control process and ion concentration gradient can be regulated by electrolyte flow optimization, helping to inhibit dendrite formation.

Increasing the electrolyte flow rate will accelerate the ion transport, thus accelerating the mass transfer process in the bulk electrolyte and the diffusion process around the electrode surface.Hence,the ion concentration gradient is reduced and the formation of dendrites is ultimately inhibited [133].In addition, the electrolyte flow reshapes the direction of zinc deposition.Yasumasa Ito et al. found that dendrites tended to twist along the direction of electrolyte flow when its velocity was higher than $1 5 \ \mathrm { c m } \ s ^ { - 1 }$ [134].Moreover, the low electrolyte flow rates will lead to poor mixing of the aqueous phase and the oily $\mathbf { B C A - B r _ { 2 n + 1 } }$ phase.The accumulated $\mathbf { B C A - B r _ { 2 n + 1 } }$ at the bottom of the cathode reduces the electrolyte conductivity and increases the ohmic overpotential,hindering zinc electrodeposition and dissolution reactions.For this reason, Jung Hoon Yang etal.increased the electrolyte flow rate to $1 0 0 \mathrm { m L m i n } ^ { - 1 }$ ,upon which zinc was uniformly deposited on the electrode surface and did not pierce the membrane (Fig.lOa) [99]. Moreover,the electrolyte flow optimization was not only achieved by changing the flow rates.For example,by adding polyoxyethylene (20) sorbitan monolaurate (Polysorbate 2O),the miscibility between the aqueous phase and the oily phase was significantly improved, promoting the homogeneous electrolyte flow.This uniform mixing especially at the bottom contributed to more uniform zinc oxidation over the entire electrode surface (Fig.1Oa) [31].Namely,Polysorbate 20 prevented the inhomogeneous zinc dissolution and reduced zinc residues.

The electrolyte composition optimizations can also regulate the growth direction of the deposited metal parallel to the anode surface, thus inhibiting dendrites.As a result, the electrolyte flow optimization is an efficient method to regulate diffusion and ion concentration gradients,thus achieving uniform metal deposition.

# 6. Summary and prospects

Br-FBs have attracted increasing attention nowadays,but further efforts should be made to solve the thorny issues of bromine crosscontamination,sluggish reaction kinetics of the $\mathrm { B r _ { 2 } / B r ^ { - } }$ redox couple and metal dendrites.Electrolytes are crucial for Br-FBs and the current strategies in terms of electrolyte optimizations are as follow:

(i) Cross-diffusion: to alter electrolyte concentrations or compositions; to design online self-recovery methods.
(ii) Sluggish reaction kinetics: to elevate temperature; to add supporting electrolytes or BCAs that can form homogeneous complexes.
[iii) Dendrites: to optimize electrolyte compositions by adding appropriate supporting electrolytes or additives,which can be

Although much progress has been made in improving the performance of Br-FBs via electrolyte strategies,further improvements are still needed for electrolyte optimizations because of the following deficiencies:

(i) BCA:high cost; the oily or solid complexes increase the ohmic polarization and aggravate dendrites; poor temperature suitability (freezing at low temperatures and decreased complexation capacity at high temperatures)； trade-off effect between promoting reaction kinetics and mitigating bromine diffusion.
(ii) Dendrite:lack of systematical research on mechanisms and structures of electrode-electrolyte interfaces for Br-FBs; increase the battery polarization because of the excessive adsorption of organic additives on the electrodes.

Thus,there still exists a huge potential for the development of advanced electrolytes of Br-FBs and more efforts should be devoted to the following aspects:

(i) Further exploration of the balance between the complexing process and the reaction kinetics is quite necessary and urgent to guide the development of more suitable BCAs.
(ii) To study the effects of various functional groups and chain lengths of BCAs on the morphology and stability of bromine complexes and reveal the complexing mechanisms.
(iii) To develop new multifunctional BCAs with low freezing temperatures,high temperature stability,high electrolyte conductivity and homogeneous complexes.
(iv) Further development of BCAs that form solid complexes is necessary to achieve better bromine sequestration capacity.
(v) To investigate the quaternary ammonium salts with redox activity and quinone-based organics containing ammonium groups, which can not only act as anode active substances to realize the multi-electron transfer and improve the energy density,but also complex with bromine to alleviate its volatilization and difusion.
(vi） To design bifunctional BCAs that can coordinate with metal ions at negative side to regulate their uniform deposition and effectively complex with bromine at the same time.
(vii) To develop additives that enable $\mathtt { B r } ^ { - }$ transferred to $\mathbf { B } \mathbf { r } ^ { + }$ in one step with two electrons,fundamentally eliminating the generation of volatile bromine.
viii) Various in-situ characterization techniques are needed to visualize and understand the dynamic evolution of bromine reaction kinetics and dendrites.
(ix) To study and regulate the solvation structure of metal ions, so that the rapid growth of dendrites can be prevented by increasing the desolvation energies and nucleation overpotentials.
