The vaadium redox flow batery (VRFB) is a promising technology for large-scale energy storage,but the sluggish kinetics and poorcatalytic activityofcarbonfelt electrodes limit high-current-density operation.Herein, a simple sulfuric acid impregnation and segmented thermal treatment strategy is developed to achieve synergistic mild etching and sulfur doping. The $3 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ stage induces surface etching and defect formation, while the subsequent $6 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ treatment promotes C-S bond formation.The optimized electrode exhibits improved redox reversibility,reduced charge transfer resistance,and an increase in the diffsion coeficient for the vanadium ion reduction process.In full-cell tests,it delivers an energy efficiency of $7 8 . 6 \%$ at $3 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ， $8 . 1 ~ \%$ higher than pristine carbon felt,and remains stable over 8Oo cycles.This work provides a facile route for constructing highperformance sulfur-doped carbon felt electrodes for VRFBs.

# 1． Introduction

Among various energy storage technologies,flow batteries are widely regarded as an ideal electrochemical energy storage solution for large-scale and long-duration applications.This is mainly due to their high safety,independent scalability of power and capacity,long cycle life,and recyclable electrolytes [1-7].Therefore,they have attracted significant attention from both academia and industry.The vanadium redox flow battery (VRFB),as the most technologically mature flow battery system，has progressively entered the stage of commercial demonstration and application [8].Although VRFBs exhibit excellent performance and practical value in multiple application scenarios, their relatively high system cost remains a key bottleneck hindering widespread adoption [9].

As one of the core components in a VRFB stack,the electrode is typically made of carbon-based fibrous materials,such as carbon felt or carbon paper [1O].The electrode serves as an electron-conducting mediumand provides electrocatalytic active sites for vanadium ion redox reactions.Therefore,it plays a critical role in determining battery performance [11,l2].Enhancing the catalytic activity of electrodes toward vanadium ion reactions can accelerate the reaction kinetics,increase the operational current density of the battery. This,in turn, can reduce the required electrode area and stack size,offeringan effective pathway to lower the installation cost of VRFB systems [13,14].However,as the current density increases,polarization effects become more pronounced.In particular, the voltage efficiency is often limited by the sluggish kinetics of the vanadium negative electrode reaction [15-17]. Moreover,porous carbon felt electrodes used in VRFBs offer good chemical stability and a large surface area.But their low intrinsic catalytic activity,insufficient active sites,slow reaction kinetics,and poor hydrophilicity collectively restrict their electrochemical performanceat high current densities.These limitations further impede the operation of VRFBs at higher current densities [18,19].To address these challenges, researchers have conducted extensive studies on carbon felt electrodes, mainly focusing on heteroatom doping [2O-28],oxidation treatment [29-33],and deposition of metallic catalysts [34-4O].Nevertheless, existing modification strategies suffer from several limitations, includingcomplicatedprocessesandlowdurabilityunder

high-flow-rate acidic electrolytes.

To address the aforementioned challenges,this study employs a simple and widely accessible dilute sulfuric acid impregnation followed by segmented thermal treatment on carbon felt,achieving a synergistic effect combining mild etching with sulfur doping. This process introduces C-S bond active sites,generates uniform defects,and significantly increases the specific surface area.These modifications effectively enhance the electrode performance,providing a facile and effective design strategy for developing high-performance,durable VRFB electrodes.

# 2．Experimental section

# 2.1．Preparation of S5CF300-600

The process started with commercial PAN-based carbon felt (CF) being immersed in a $5 \ \mathrm { w t } \%$ sulfuric acid solution for $3 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ .Subsequently, the samples were dried in an oven at $1 2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 4 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ to allow substantial evaporation of moisture.Following this,the carbon felt samples were placed in a tube furnace under an argon atmosphere.They were heated to $3 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and maintained for $^ \textrm { \scriptsize 1 h }$ to completely remove residual moisture,thereby forminga highly viscous,nearly anhydrous layer of concentrated sulfuric acid with strong oxidizability adsorbed on the carbon fibers.At this relatively moderate temperature,the concentrated sulfuric acid slowly reacted with the carbon surface,leading mainly to mild oxidative etching and preliminary surface activation.The temperature was then raised to $6 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and maintained for $^ { 1 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ to promote further thermal transformation of residual sulfur-containing species and facilitate sulfur doping at elevated temperature.The resulting sample was designated as S5CF3oo-6oo,and its preparation procedure was illustrated in Scheme 1.To evaluate the role of the segmented thermal treatment, control experiments with single-temperature treatments were conducted: samples impregnated with $5 \ \mathrm { w t } \%$ sulfuric acid and treated at $3 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for1h (S5CF3oo);and samples impregnated with $5 \mathrm { \ w t \% }$ sulfuric acid and treated at $6 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for 1 h (S5CF600). Additionally,a sample subjected only to the segmented thermal treatment without sulfuric acid impregnation (TCF) was prepared asa reference. The preparation conditions for sulfuric acid treatments at different mass fractions and temperatures were presented in Table S1.

# 2.2.Characterization

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM, ZEISs Sigma 30o,Germany) was used to observe the surface morphology of the carbon felt samples and examine the formation of surface defects. Raman spectroscopy (Horiba LabRAMHR Evolution, Japan) was used to analyze the defect density and degree of disorderin the electrode material.The specific surface area of the carbon felt was quantitatively determined by nitrogen physisorption measurements conducted on a Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) surface area analyzer (Micromeritics ASAP 2460,USA). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPs,Thermo Scientific K-Alpha, USA)was performed to confirm the successful incorporation of sulfur into the carbon fibers,quantify its content,and identify the resulting chemical states and functional groups.The hydrophilicity of the electrodes was assessed by measuring the water contact angle using a contact angle goniometer (Lauda Scientific LSA1oo,Germany).

# 2.3.Electrochemical measurements

The electrochemical testing methods employed in this study included cyclic voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS).The electrolyte for the positive-electrode CV test consisted of 0.1 M $\mathrm { V O S O } _ { 4 }$ in3M $\mathrm { H _ { 2 } S O _ { 4 } }$ ，while that for the negative-electrode CV test comprised 0.1 M $\mathrm { V } _ { 2 } ( \mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } ) _ { 3 }$ in3M $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } S 0 _ { 4 }$ .All measurements were performed usinga DH7oo electrochemical workstation ina standard threeelectrode configuration,with a mercury/mercurous sulfate electrode (MSE) as the reference electrode and a platinum mesh as the counter electrode.The potential was scanned from $- 0 . 2$ to $_ { 1 . 2 \mathrm { V } }$ for the positive electrode and from $- 0 . 4$ to $- 1 . 5 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ for the negative electrode,at scan rates of 5,10,15,and $2 0 \mathrm { m V } s ^ { - 1 }$ . Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements were carried out at the open-circuit potential overa frequency range of O.01 to $1 0 0 \mathrm { k H z }$ ,with an amplitude of $5 \mathrm { m V }$ All electrochemical measurements were performed in triplicate.

# 2.4.Details of calculation

For a diffusion-controlled electrochemical process,the peak current obeys the Randles-Sevcik equation [41]:

$$
\mathbf { i } _ { \mathrm { p } } { = } \left( 2 . 6 9 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 } \right) \mathbf { n } ^ { 3 / 2 } \mathbf { A } \mathbf { D } ^ { 1 / 2 } \mathbf { C } \mathbf { v } ^ { 1 / 2 }
$$

where $\mathbf { i } _ { \mathrm { p } }$ is the peak current (A), $\mathbf { n }$ is the number of electrons transferred (taken as 1 for the $\mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ and $\mathrm { V } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + }$ couples), A is the geometric electrode area $( 1 \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ , C is the bulk concentration of vanadium ions (0.1 $\times ~ 1 0 ^ { - 3 } ~ \mathrm { m o l ~ c m ^ { - 3 } }$ ,v is the scan rate $( \nabla \mathsf { s } ^ { - 1 } )$ ,and D is the diffusion coefficient $( \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } \ s ^ { - 1 } )$ . The constant $2 . 6 9 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ incorporates fundamental constants (Faraday constant, gas constant) at $2 9 8 \ \mathrm { \ K } .$ By plotting $\mathrm { i _ { p } }$ against $\mathrm { { v } } ^ { 1 / 2 }$ ,a linear relationship can be obtained. The slope K of this plot was determined from the peak currents measured at different scan rates.The diffusion coeficient D was then calculated by substituting K into the rearranged equation.

# 2.5.Battery performance test

The carbon felt samples were assembled into single cells for chargedischarge testing.The battery testing system used was LAND CT3002N. The electrode dimensions were $1 1 . 5 \times 3 . 3 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ ，the thickness of the electrode was $3 . 6 \ \mathrm { m m }$ ,the electrodes were compressed by $3 5 ~ \%$ .The PFSA membrane was supplied by Suzhou Tuoji New Material Technology Co.,Ltd.Both the positive and negative half-cells were filled with $2 0 0 ~ \mathrm { m L }$ of a commercial electrolyte containing $1 . 0 \mathrm { ~ M ~ V ~ } ^ { 3 . 5 + }$ in $4 . 3 \ \mathrm { M }$ $\mathrm { H _ { 2 } S O _ { 4 } }$ .The charge-discharge voltage window was set between 1.O and $1 . 5 5 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ 、All tests were conducted at room temperature $( 2 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ . The performance at various current densities (150,200,250,300,350,and $4 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { \ c m ^ { - 2 } } )$ was evaluated.The long-term cycling stability was assessed at a constant current density of $3 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ .To accurately evaluate the intrinsic long-term cycling stability of the electrode material and eliminate interference from changes in electrolyte composition (such as valence imbalance of active ions or accumulation of impurities), the electrolyte in the cell system was fully replaced after 40o cycles.The composition,concentration,and volume of the fresh electrolyte were identical to those of the original electrolyte.The battery test was performed in duplicate.

# 3．Results and discussion

To investigate the effect of sulfuric acid impregnation and thermal treatment on the morphology of carbon felt, SEMcharacterization is first carried out.As shown in Fig.la-g and Fig.S1,the pristine CF,TCF,and S5CF300 all exhibit smooth surfaces,whereas S5CF300-600 exhibits numerous uniformly distributed pores.Notably,when examining the overall fiber integrityatamagnification of $2 0 0 0 \times$ (Fig. 1h,i), it can be observed that the surface of S5CF6oo displays large pits due to excessive etching,a phenomenon that is not observed in S5CF3oo-6oo.These morphological results demonstrate that the segmented thermal treatment strategy enables the mild and effective etching of carbon fibers.In contrast, the single-temperature treatments lead to either under-etching (S5CF300) or over-etching (S5CF60O).EDS elemental mapping is performed to evaluate the elemental distribution of electrode.As shown in Fig.S2,a uniform distribution of sulfur is observed on the surface of S5CF300-600.

To further verify the extent of etching at the microscopic level, Raman spectroscopy is conducted to investigate the defect characteristics of the electrode samples.As shown in Fig.2a and Fig. S3,two characteristic peaks are detected near 135O and $1 5 9 0 ~ \mathrm { { c m } ^ { - 1 } }$ ，corresponding to the D band and G band of carbon materials,respectively. The G band is associated with the in-plane stretching vibration of $\mathsf { s p } ^ { 2 } .$ hybridized carbon atoms within the graphitic network [42].The D band originates from structural defects or disorder in the carbon lattice [43]. The intensity ratio $\mathrm { ( I _ { D } / I _ { G } ) }$ ,calculated from the integrated area ratio after peak deconvolution,is commonly used to evaluate the degree of disorderand graphitization level of carbon materials [44,45].After sulfuric acid impregnation and thermal treatment, the $\mathrm { I _ { D } / I _ { G } }$ ratio increases from 0.63 for CF to 1.78 for S5CF300-600,indicating a rise in defect density on the fiber surface.

Building on this,BET analysis is also performed.As listed in Table 1 and Table S2,consistent with the increased defect density,the specific surface area of the carbon felt increases from $0 . 9 4 6 \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 } \mathrm { g } ^ { - 1 }$ for CF to 3.62 $\mathbf { m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathbf { g } ^ { - 1 }$ for S5CF3oo-6oo.In addition, the mesoporous surface area of S5CF300-6oO is significantly larger than that of CF.These mesopores are beneficial for reducing mass transfer resistance,enabling vanadium ions from the electrolyte to diffuse more readily into the highly active reaction sites,thereby enhancing the electrode performance [23].

Furthermore,the extent of sulfur doping is studied via XPS.The elemental compositions are listed in Table 2, the XPS survey spectra are shown in Fig. S4,and the S 2p XPS spectra are shown in Fig.2b-e. Notably,although the sulfur content in S5CF3oo is relatively low,the appearance of C-S bonds indicates that limited C-S-bonded sulfur structures are formed through the reaction between concentrated sulfuric acid/sulfur-containing species and the carbon surface at $3 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ (Fig.2c).However,due to the limited extent of reaction, $4 8 . 9 8 \%$ of the sulfur remains as sulfate-like oxidized sulfur species,which are likely adsorbed or weakly bound to the surface.The sulfur content increases markedly in S5CF6oo,yet the proportion of C-S bonds accounts for only $2 8 . 2 1 ~ \%$ (Fig.2d).This is because,without the pre-activation effect of the $3 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ treatment,sulfuric acid undergoes rapid thermal transformation at high temperature,and the etching effect is more pronounced than effective C-S bond formation.This is consistent with the uneven and excessive etching phenomenon observed in the SEM images. In contrast, S5CF3oO-6oO exhibits the highest sulfur content,with the proportion of C-S bonds reaching $6 6 . 7 0 ~ \%$ (Fig.2e). It is reasonably proposed that the initial treatment at $3 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ pre-activates the carbon surface by inducing mild oxidative etching,generating initial C-S bonds, and creating defect-rich reactive sites.This facilitates the capture of active sulfur species by the pre-activated carbon in the subsequent 600 $^ \circ \mathrm { C }$ treatment,promoting their incorporation into the carbon framework and resulting in more abundant C-S bonds.Moreover, because part of the sulfuric acid may have been consumed or transformed in the first step,which moderates the oxidative etching effect in the subsequent 600 $^ \circ \mathrm { C }$ treatment and leads to the formation of abundant, dense,and uniform mesopores.These findings reveal that the precise control of the thermal treatment sequence and temperature can achieve the dual optimization of mild etching and effective sulfur doping in CF.

Furthermore, the surface defects and porous structure introduced on the carbon fibers partially improve the apparent wettability of the electrode.As shown in Fig.3a-d,contact angle measurements reveal a decrease from $1 5 2 . 9 ^ { \circ }$ for CF to $1 3 4 . 9 ^ { \circ }$ for S5CF300-600,indicating improved but still limited hydrophilicity.The defect formation, increased surface area,and enhanced surface roughness collectively enhance capillary forces,favoring electrolyte penetration.CF cannot be fully wetted by the electrolyte,while all sulfuric-acid-impregnated and thermally treated samples exhibit improved electrolyte wettability (Fig.S5).This improve electrolyte accessibility is also an important factor contributing to the enhanced electrochemical activity.Previous studies have generally attributed the improved wettability of modified carbon felt electrodes to the introduction of polar oxygen-containing functional groups.However, Seong-Cheol Kim etal.demonstrated that the intrinsic vanadium redox kinetics of carbon electrodes are more strongly correlated with surface defects than with oxygen functionalities [46,47].Therefore,although the water contact angle of S5CF300-600 remains relatively high,the enhanced electrochemical performance should be mainly attributed to the defect-rich porous structure,enlarged surface area,and C-S active sites rather than to a significant improvement in hydrophilicity.

To more comprehensively evaluate the modified electrodes,full-cell tests are conducted.Fig.5a-c and Fig. S7a-f illustrate the chargedischarge performances of cells assembled with different electrodes at current densities ranging from 150 to $4 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ .As the operating current increases,the overall polarization of the cell becomes more significant.Therefore,the performance at high current density better reflects the effectiveness of the electrode modification in mitigating polarization. At $3 0 0 \mathrm { m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ,the cell with S5CF300-600 shows marked improvements over the cell with CF:the discharge capacity increases from 5.41 to 11.82 Ah $\mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 }$ ,the voltage efficiency increases from $7 1 . 6 \%$ to $7 9 . 8 ~ \%$ ,and the energy efficiency increases from $7 0 . 5 ~ \%$ to $7 8 . 6 \%$ Fig.5d displays the charge-discharge profiles of cells with different electrodes at $3 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ .The S5CF300-600 cell exhibits a notably lower initial charge potential and a significantly higher discharge potential compared to the cell with CF.The reduced charge-discharge voltage gap indicates lower cell polarization,confirming that the treatment successfully reduces polarization in the vanadium battery.

# 4. Conclusions

In conclusion,this work presents a simple,cost-effective,and scalable strategy for modifying carbon-based electrodes for vanadium redox flow batteries.Through dilute sulfuric acid impregnation followed by segmented thermal treatment,a synergistic modification combining mild surface etching and sulfur doping was successfully achieved on carbon felt electrodes.The $3 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ treatment promotes surface etching and defect generation,while the subsequent $6 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ treatment facilitates the formation of $\mathsf { C } { \ - } { \boldsymbol { s } }$ active sites,leading to an enlarged specific surface area,increased defect density,and enhanced electrocatalytic activity toward vanadium redox reactions.As a result, the optimized carbon felt electrode delivers an energy efficiency of $7 8 . 6 \%$ at $3 0 0 \mathrm { m A c m ^ { - 2 } }$ ,which is $8 . 1 ~ \%$ higher than that of the pristine carbon felt $( 7 0 . 5 ~ \% )$ ，and maintains stable performance over 8oo cycles.More importantly,this modification method relies only on inexpensive sulfuric acid and conventional thermal treatment,without requiring noble-metal catalysts, complex chemical synthesis,or sophisticated equipment.These features make the process highly compatible with large-scale electrode preparation and practical stack manufacturing. Therefore,the proposed segmented thermal treatment strategy provides an efficient and feasible route for producing high-performance and durable carbon felt electrodes.It shows promising potential for reducing electrode-related costs and promoting the practical commercial application of vanadium redox flow batteries.
