High-concentration electrolytes (HCEs） have been pro-posed to optimize the solvation structure and to obtain a stable CEI.18,19 Choi et al. found that the anodic stability of an ultrahigh-concentration electrolyte， 5 M bis(fluorosulfonyl)- imide (LiFSI） in glyme (DME)，showed a high oxidation potential ${ \tt > } 4 . 9 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ (vs $\mathrm { N a } ^ { + } / \mathrm { N a }$ ）and favorable compatibility with a high-voltage cathode charged to $4 . 3 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ .20 The concentrated Na salt increases the proportion of anions to solvent molecules, leading to formation of a solvation structure with enriched contact ion pairs (CIPs）and ion aggregates (AGG).With more anions entering the primary solvation sheath (PSS),an inorganic-rich CEI is formed and accounts for much improved interfacial stability. Nevertheless，most HCEs involve high viscosity, severe corrosion of aluminum foil (current collector) and elevated costs that restrict their practical use.Localized high-concentration electrolytes (LHCEs)，which employ nonsolvating or weakly solvating solvents as diluent agents, have been proposed to address the above issues.21,22 For example, Li et al. introduced hexafluoroisopropyl methyl ether (HFME) as a diluent to a 1.5 M NaFSI in diethylene glycol dimethyl ether (DEGDME） electrolyte，which helps to modulate the ${ { \bf { N } } { \bf { a } } ^ { + } }$ solvation structure to form a robust inorganics-rich CEI and simultaneously reduce the electrolyte viscosity.23Similarly, Zhou et al. introduceda (perfluorobutoxy)methane (MTE）diluent into a NaFSI/
