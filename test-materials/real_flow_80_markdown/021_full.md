The intermittency of renewable energy sources demand a scalable and durable energy storage technology capable of long-term operation with safety and reliability.Redox flow bateries (RFBs)are well suited for gridscale energy storage; however,their reliance on ion-exchange membranes introduces a significant capital cost $( > 3 0 ~ \% )$ ,resistance,and long-term stability challnges.On the other hand,membrane-less batteries based on biphasic electrolyte design ofer apromising alternative by exploiting their liquid-liquid interface as an intrinsic separator,eradicating the need foran ion-exchange membrane.Herein, we report a fully soluble,allinorganic aqueous biphasic membrane-less redox flow battery employing $\mathrm { \Phi _ { T i } } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 4 + , - }$ and $\scriptstyle \cdot \bigvee ^ { 4 \dot { + } } / \bigvee ^ { 5 + }$ redox couples. Phase separation is induced by LiTFSI,forming two immiscible aqueous phases with a stable liquid-liquid interface that inherently suppresses crossover.Comprehensive physical characterizations confirm the densitydriven stratification and high viscosity contrast.Electrochemical studies demonstrated efficient cycling over 2000 cycles with coulombic eficiencies $\left( \mathrm { C E } ~ \% \right)$ exceeding $9 0 \%$ at a maximum discharge current density of 3.18 $\mathrm { \ m A } { \bullet } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ,and a stable potential of $_ { 0 . 8 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ for ${ > } 2 0$ h at zero current condition, indicating effective crossover suppression. These results underscore the potential of aqueous biphasic inorganic systems as an alternative to ion-exchange membranes for chemically robust redox flow batteries.

The sporadic nature of renewable energy sources such as the solar and wind necessitates the development of a large-scale,efficient,and scalable energy storage technology,capable of delivering the stored energy in accordance with the user requirements.Among the various electrochemical energy storage devices, batteries emerge as the leading contenders owing to their optimal energy，and power densities as compared to supercapacitors,or fuel cells [1-5].

While the current marketis dominated byLi-ion batteries,which are rather suitable for portable,and shorter duration energy storage applications,their deployment in large-scale is constrained by safety concerns,economics,long-term degradation,and waste disposal subsequent to the utilization [6-8].These limitations motivate an exploration towards an alternative energy storage application which are suitable for grid-scale energy storage applications,while demonstrating a cyclic stability for thousands of charge-discharge cycles without compromising on the efficiencies.In this regard,redox flow batteries (RFBs),particularly vanadium-based RFBs (VRFBs) offera promising solution.Ina RFB, the energy storage capacity is primarily determined by the volume of active liquid electrolyte being utilized, while the power is governed by the cell stack.This architecture aids in the decoupling of energy,and power densities while offering an unique advantage of scalability,while providing stability for numerous cycles of operation $( > 3 0 , 0 0 0$ cycles) without compromising on the safety [9-l2].However, the price volatility of inherent redox active material viz.vanadium,along with its low open circuit potential (OCP),and energy density restricts its effective implementation on a grand-scale.Moreover,conventional redox flow batteries rely on ion-exchange membranes to physically separate the catholyte,and anolyte compartments while facilitating the necessary ion transport to maintain the charge neutrality.Despite of its useful characteristics,membranes impose several challenges including high capital cost $( > 3 0 ~ \% )$ ,structural degradation,ohmic resistances,and gradual crossover of redox active species.These cumulative disadvantages reduce the overall energy eficiency of the flow battery while hindering its large-scale deployment.

To address these critical issues,numerous researchers around the globe either modified the flow architecture,or preferred a membraneless alternative [13-26],in addition to altering the component combination of redox active materials. The early designs for the membrane-less flow batteries involved the utilization of a microfluidic flow channel architecture,wherein, the laminar flow of the electrolytes prevents their mixing [23-26].However, these designs suffer from an inevitable problem of low scalability,and crossover of redox active species,making them unsuitable for large-scale.

In the later alternative,the elimination of membrane simplifies the cell design while offering an economically viable solution.This scalable membrane-less strategy exploits immiscible liquid electrolyte systems, wherein,the phase separation is driven by thermodynamics rather than hydrodynamics.Subsequently,the concept has been extrapolated to hybrid systems employing organic redox species,and ionic liquids to induce the necessary phase separation [14,17,18,21-26].However, the lack of membrane presents its own set of challenges including,but not limited to,active species crossover,and self-discharge.Regardless,a thorough research in this direction can provide fruitful outcomes,while offering a reduction in the material procurement cost incurred due to the membrane incorporation. In connection to this, biphasic systems provide a compelling approach to membrane-less RFB design, wherein, two immiscible aqueous phases are formed by adopting a salting-out route [13,19,2O,27]. This technique creates a stable liquid-liquid aqueous interface that spatially,and thermodynamically separates the redox active materials.Among the various salts,LiTFSI,has emerged as an effective salting-out agent due to its extremely high solubility in aqueous solutions $( > 1 7 \mathrm { M } )$ ,large and bulkyanionasymmetry,highly delocalised anion,and chaotropic nature (ability to disrupt the hydrogen-bonding network in water).These unique properties makes LiTFSI a suitable agent to synthesize a biphasic electrolyte system for membrane-less RFBs.

Further,to address the inherent material volatility costs along with eradicating the necessity of a membrane,the study utilizes titanium as an anolyte solution in combination with vanadium,due to its abundance,and comparable standard redox potential [28]. Herein,we report a novel membrane-less biphasic aqueous inorganic RFB by utilizing completely soluble redox active species such as,titanium $( \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 4 + } )$ [28-31], and a vanadium $\mathrm { ( V ^ { 4 + } / V ^ { 5 + } ) }$ as anolyte and catholyte respectively,spatially separated bya LiTFSI-induced liquid-liquid interface. The biphasic system enables inherent suppression of vanadium,and titanium crossover while maintaining electrochemical activity across the interface.Comprehensive electrochemical,physical,and spectroscopic analyses were performed along with long-term cycling behaviour to assess its performance.This work provides a new insight into the design of inorganic membrane-free biphasic RFBs utilizing completely soluble redoxactive materials.

# 1.Physical and electrochemical characterization

In our earlier works,the V-Ti RFB has been extensively characterized,and optimized by employing various sustainable alternative. However,the inherent cost incurred during the membrane procurement needs to be minimized to effectively utilize inorganic V-Ti RFBs.In this regard,an aqueous membrane-less biphasic static system has been developed, by employing salting-out method.

To induce the salting out effect,LiTFSI has been employed due to various reasons as mentioned in the introduction.Upon the addition of LiTFSI at a concentration ${ \bf \mathrm { > } } 5 \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ to a solution comprising ${ \mathrm { T i C l } } _ { 3 } + { \mathrm { H C l } } +$ $_ \mathrm { H _ { 2 } O }$ ,the system spontaneously separates into two distinct phases.The resulting phases consists of TFSI’rich,and‘water/ $\mathrm { { \ C l } ^ { - , } }$ rich phases along with their cation counterparts.The concentration of $^ { \mathrm { \scriptsize ~ \cdot ~ } } \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + , }$ in the TFSI'rich phase is low as compared to the‘water/ $\mathrm { \Omega } ^ { \prime } \mathrm { C l } ^ { - , }$ rich phase,due to its preferential chloro-aquo complex formation [28] to minimize the free energy of the system.The low concentration strongly indicates that $^ { \mathrm { \scriptsize ~ \mathfrak { s } + \mathfrak { s } } }$ remains predominantly solvated in $\mathrm { \mathrm { ^ { * } H _ { 2 } O / C l } } ^ { - , }$ rich phase due to a strong hydration,and unfavourable solvation in the ‘TFSI'environment.As‘TFSI’is a large,weakly hydrated and hydrophobic anion,at high concentrations of LiTFSI,along with $\mathrm { T i C l } _ { 3 }$ and HCl in the solution, the ions compete for the water molecules,and due to the lack of sufficient free water molecules to hydrate the species in the solution, the system thermodynamically splits into two distinct phases to reduce the free energy,as showcased in the Fig.1.

In terms of the average and maximum discharge capacity $( 0 . 2 5 \mathrm { m A h }$ and $2 \ \mathrm { m A h }$ ),average discharge current density $( 3 . 1 8 \ \mathrm { m A } \bullet \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ ，and nominal discharge potential $_ { ( \sim 0 . 9 9 ~ \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ at $1 . 2 7 \ : \ : \mathrm { \ m A } \bullet \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 } \ : \ : \mathrm { ] }$ ，the membrane-less V-Ti RFB displays an impressive performance as compared to existing works utilizing organic/hybrid setups [18-20,27], however,in terms ofcapacity utilization,and energy density,the battery showcases rather poor performance.

Overall,this study aids in advancing the realm of utilizing soluble inorganic redox active materials while synthesizing a biphasic aqueous membrane-lessredox flow battery,rather than utilizinga hybrid RFB. The prime advantage of utilizing a RFB is the decoupling of power and energy densities while offering scalability,which is possible with the incorporation of either organic/inorganic soluble redox active materials,rather than the hybrid ones.Further,various component combinations of abundant inorganic redox active materials such as,cerium, manganese,chromium,polysulphides,can be utilized to expand the OCP,nominal discharge voltage,capacity storage,and energy density.

The future works of the study include enabling the flow of liquid electrolytes through the cell to reduce ohmic $^ +$ mass transport resistances,along with evaluating the performance over varying operation parameters to enhance the EE $\%$ ,capacity storage,and thereby the energy and power density of membrane-less aqueous inorganic V-Ti RFB.

# 2．Conclusion

In this work,a novel membrane-less aqueous biphasic static-cell based exclusively on fully soluble inorganic redox-active materials has been demonstrated for the first time.By employinga LiTFSI-induced salting-out strategy,two immiscible aqueous phases were obtained, enabling spatial separation of $\mathrm { \Phi ^ { \bullet } T i ^ { 3 + } / T i ^ { 4 + , } }$ and $\mathrm { ^ { \circ } V ^ { 4 + } } / \mathrm { V } ^ { 5 + , }$ redox couples without the use of an ion-exchange membrane to facilitate the transport of ions.Physical characterizations revealed that the biphasic system is stabilized by a combination of differences in the density,dynamic viscosity asymmetry,and interfacial tension, leading to gravity-driven stratification.Furthermore,electrochemical testing of the static biphasic cell demonstrated stable cyclic operation over thousands of cycles with $\mathrm { C E } ~ \%$ consistently exceeding $9 0 \ \%$ ，directly evidencing effective crossover suppression. Increasing the $^ { \mathrm { \scriptsize ~ \mathfrak { o } } \mathrm { \Lambda } ^ { 3 + \cdot } }$ concentration in the‘TFSI’rich phase improved the capacity，nominal discharge voltage,and thereby $\mathrm { V E } \%$ ,with the cell achieving EE $\%$ sabove $5 5 \%$ at current densities of ${ \sim } 2 \ \mathrm { m A } { \bullet } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ .Nevertheless, voltage and energy efficiencies remain limited by the high dynamic viscosity of the electrolytes $( \sim 5 9 \mathrm { \ m P a } \bullet s )$ ,and the static nature of the cell configuration, which collectively contribute to significant ohmic $^ +$ mass-transport resistances.

Despite these limitations,the present study represents an important conceptual advancement in membrane-less RFB design.Unlike previouslyreported biphasic systems that rely on organic or hybrid redox chemistries,this work demonstrates that fully inorganic,aqueous redox couples can be successfully integrated into biphasic membrane-free architectures,rather than following a laminar flow design.

Future work will focus on implementing flow,optimizing electrode architecture,and reducing the viscosity of the electrolyte to effectively minimize the transport resistance and enhance the capacity utilization, thereby improving $\mathrm { V E } ~ \%$ ，power density,and energy density. Overall, this study lays the foundation for next-generation membrane-less inorganic redox flow batteries and expands the design space for scalable,and cost-effective grid-scale energy storage technologies.

# 3.2.Electrolyte preparation

The catholyte was prepared by dissolving 1 M $\mathrm { { ( m o l \cdot L ^ { - 1 } } }$ ${ \mathrm { V } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 5 } ,$ in an aqueous solution of $3 \mathrm { ~ M ~ H _ { 2 } S O _ { 4 } }$ and $1 \mathrm { ~ M ~ H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } }$ at room temperature while stirring for approximately $4 \mathrm { h }$ ,whereas,the homogenous solution of $\mathrm { T i C l } _ { 3 }$ was synthesized by dissolving 1 $\mathbf { M } \left( \mathbf { m o l } { \cdot } \mathbf { L } ^ { - 1 } \right)$ 0 $\mathrm { T i C l } _ { 3 }$ along with 3 MHCl in deionized water at room temperature.To the resulting solution of $1 \mathrm { ~ M ~ T i C l _ { 3 } }$ ,approximately $5 \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ LiTFSI has been added to induce the phase separation-by opting a‘salting-out' method,and produce TFSI" rich (dense $^ +$ hydrophobic),and $\mathrm { \mathrm { ^ { * } H _ { 2 } O / C l } } ^ { - , }$ rich phases.The TFSI'rich phase has been carefully separated,and the concentration of the resulting unknown solution has been verified with UV-Vis.Although the initial concentration of $^ { \mathrm { \scriptsize ~ \mathfrak { o } } \mathrm { \Lambda } ^ { 3 + } }$ was $^ { 1 \mathrm { ~ M ~ } }$ (prior to the addition of LiTFSI), UV-Vis analysis revealed that the effective concentrations of the dense bottom phases (at varying concentrations of LiTFSI) were approximately ${ \sim } 0 . 1 \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ ,and $0 . 2 3 6 \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ .Subsequently,the separated ‘TFSI’rich phase has been utilized as anolyte,while the ${ } ^ { \cdot } \mathrm { V } ^ { + 5 } { } ^ { , }$ acted as a catholyte.

# 3.3.Electrochemical characterization

Cyclic voltammetry (CV) measurements were conducted using a 2- electrode,and a 3-electrode configuration connected to a potentiostat (Palmsens MultiTrace 4).Graphite plates were employed as current collector plates in the electrolytes,and the CV has been recorded for the entire cell.Prior to each experiment, the graphite plates were washed thoroughly with ethanol and deionized water,followed by drying under ambient conditions.Cyclic voltammograms were recorded over an appropriate potential window $( \mathbf { f r o m } 0 . 0 \ V$ to $1 . 5 \mathrm { V } .$ at varyingscan rates $( 0 . 0 0 1 \ \mathrm { V } { \cdot } s ^ { - 1 } $ to $0 . 1 0 0 { \mathrm { ~ V } } { \cdot } s ^ { - 1 } \Sigma$ for 2-electrode setup.Further, 3-electrode configuration has been utilized to evaluate the redox potentials of $\cdot _ { \mathrm { T i } } 3 + \cdot \cdot$ in ‘TFSI’ rich phase,and the variation of peak current as a function of scan rates $( 0 . 0 0 2 5 ~ \mathrm { V } { \cdot } s ^ { - 1 }$ to $0 . 1 0 0 0 ~ \mathrm { V } { \cdot } s ^ { - 1 } )$ .The electrolyte consists of ${ \sim } 0 . 1 \mathrm { ~ M ~ } ^ { \cdot } \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + , }$ ，with a working electrode (graphite plate) active surface of $0 . 7 5 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$

All measurements were performed at room temperature under ambient atmosphere.

# 3.4. Battery testing

The biphasic membrane-less cell was assembled in a static cell configuration，wherein, the bottom phase consists of ${ \mathfrak { o } } _ { \mathrm { T } } { \mathfrak { i } } ^ { 3 + }$ ina ‘TFSI'rich phase, while the top phase comprises ${ } ^ { \cdot } \mathrm { V } ^ { + 5 } { } ^ { , }$ dissolved in an acidic aqueous solution. The phases have been carefully assembled to avoid causing an initial interfacial disturbances.The total volumes of the top,and the bottom phases corresponded to $7 ~ \mathrm { m L }$ with $3 . 5 ~ \mathrm { m L }$ each. Graphite plates were employed as current collectors which were positioned vertically as showcased in the schematic,along with an interfacial contact area of $0 . 5 2 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ .It is noteworthy to mention that,no membrane was used,and the liquid-liquid interface served as an intrinsic separator.Further,the charge and discharge studies were conducted by utilizing LANHE-CT3002A (LANHE,Wuhan, China) at an average charge and discharge currents of $1 0 \ \mathrm { \ m A } \bullet \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ，and 1.5 $\mathrm { \ m A } { \bullet } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ respectively. Unless otherwise specified, the cut-off voltage was fixed at $_ { 0 . 2 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ during the discharge, and $_ { 1 . 5 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ during the charging process at a charge capacity of $0 . 1 \mathrm { \ m A h }$ ,throughout this work.All the measurements have been performed at ambient temperature.
