# ABSTRACT

Keywords: Multi-redox organic species Aqueous redox flow batteries Molecular engineering

The pursuit of carbon neutrality demands scalable,safe energy storage,driving interest in aqueous organic redox flow batteries (AORFBs)for their sustainability and molecular tunability.Developing redox-active organic materials capable of multi-electron transfer ofers a highly promising route to overcome the limitations of energy density in AORFBs.This review comprehensively summarizes recent advances in multi-redox organic species for AORFBs,highlighting their critical rolein boosting energy density,and criticallyanalyze key material classes—including viologens,quinones,azines,and naphthalene diimides—alongside emerging molecules like azobenzenes，phenylamines,and fluorenones.The discusson focuses on molecular design principles，redox mechanisms,and structure-property relationships underpinning multi-electron transfer.Finally,the challenge and perspective on the development of multi-redox organic species for high-performance AORFBs are also provided.

# 1． Introduction

Against the backdrop of global efforts to achieve the goal of “carbon peaking and carbon neutrality,”the development and utilization of renewable energy sources such as solar and wind power have become key pathways for global energy transformation [1].In 2024,low-carbon electricity accounted for over $4 0 \%$ of the global power generation, with solar and wind energy contributing ${ > } 1 5 ~ \%$ to the global energy supply—a share that continues to grow rapidly.However,due to the intermittent and fluctuating nature of solar and wind power,their direct integration into the power grid may pose significant challenges to grid stability [2,3].This underscores the urgent need for large-scale,cost-- effective energy storage systems to decouple energy supply and demand spatially and temporally,while also facilitating flexible grid dispatch [4-6].

Among the various energy storage technologies,electrochemical methods—especially rechargeable batteries—are receiving increasing attention [7,8].Due to the high energy density,lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) have been widely adopted in electric vehicles and portable electronic devices,and more recently,have expanded into the grid-scale storages [9,lO]．Nevertheless,their flammability risks and limited cycle life pose major challenges for large-scale,long-duration，and safety-critical applications [11,l2].Alternative battery systems such as sodium-ion batteries (SIBs） and zinc-ion batteries (ZIBs) have been explored for grid storage due to advantages in cost and safety,however, they still face inherent limitations [13,14].SIBs generally exhibit lower energy density and unsatisfactory long-term cyclability[15],while ZIBs are plagued by zinc dendrite formation,shape change,and hydrogen evolution reactions,which compromise their efficiency and lifespan [16, 17]. These drawbacks hinder their reliability for long-duration energy storage.

In recent years,redox flow battery (RFB) has emerged as a highly promising alternative,offering exceptional scalability,long service life, and high operational safety [18-21]. Unlike conventional batery systems,RFBs spatially separate the reaction zone (cell stack) and the energy storage zone (electrolyte tanks) [22,23].The power is determined by the electrode area in the stack,while the capacity depends on the electrolyte concentration and tank volume [24].This decoupling of power and energy allows independent scaling of both parameters [25, 26].Additionally,RFBs exhibit fast reaction kinetics,minimal phase changes,long cycle life,high safety,and excellent thermal management capabilities [27,28].These advantages have led to growing academic and industrial interest in RFBs in recent years [29].

Depending on the solvent used in the electrolyte,RFBs can be categorized into aqueous and non-aqueous redox flow batteries.Nonaqueous redox flow batteries employ organic solvents asthe electrolyte [3O].The use of organic solvents expands the battery's operating voltage,however,this system exhibits low conductivity, generally poor rate performance,and poses certain safety risks during operation due to the flammability of organic solvents [31,32].Aqueous redox flow batteries (ARFBs) use water as the solvent,significantly reducing the risks of combustion and explosion during operation and endowing them with high safety [33].Additionally,the solvent is low-cost,easy to handle,and offers good conductivity,making ARFBs the mainstream technology for commercial development in recent years [34].

Traditional ARFBs typically employ redox-active materials derived frommetals or halogens， such asthe all-vanadium [35,36], iron-chromium [37],and zinc-bromine [38] systems.Although these systems have achieved early-stage commercialization, they face critical challenges including limited resource availability, fluctuating metal prices,solubility constraints,and insufficient redox activity,all of which hinder further technological promotion.Among them,all-vanadium RFBs are currently the most mature systems and feature relatively high energy efficiency and excellent cycling stability [39,4O]. However, its limited energy density and high system cost still hinder its widespread commercial adoption [41,42].

In contrast,aqueous organic redox flow batteries (AORFBs),which utilize water-soluble redox-active organic materials (ROMs) as redoxactive species,offer distinctive advantages in sustainability，safety, and molecular tunability [43-46].Composed of abundant and low-cost elements,ROMs have the potential to enable large-scale and economically viable energy storage [47-5O].Furthermore,the properties of ROMs,such as redox potential and solubility,can be precisely tailored through specific molecular modification, thereby enabling the development of AORFBs with potentially high energy density and low cost [51-53].

Despite these promising attributes conferred by molecular engineering，AORFBs are currently facing a major technical bottleneck—limited energy density [54,55].Theoretically，the energy density of AORFBs is determined by three key factors: the concentration of redox-active species,the cell voltage,and the number of electrons involved in the redox reaction per molecule. Unfortunately,the narrow electrochemical stability window of aqueous electrolytes $( \sim 1 . 2 3 \mathrm { ~ V ) }$ significantly restricts the operating voltage of AORFBs relative to their non-aqueous counterparts [56-58]．As a result, the choiceof redox-active materials compatible with aqueous environmentsis limited,and rational selection of ROMs is crucial for enhancing the energy density of AORFBs (Fig.1).

Within the constraints of a limited voltage window, improving the solubility of ROMs is considered an efective strategy to boost energy density [59-62]. Benefiting from the structural tunability of organic molecules,various molecular engineering approaches—such as the introduction of polar substituents and asymmetric molecular design-—have been proven to significantly enhance the solubility of ROMs, with concentrations exceeding $3 . 0 \ \mathrm { M }$ in some systems [47,51,62,63]. Nevertheless,highly concentrated electrolyte systems are often accompanied by increased viscosity and mass transport limitations,which can compromise battery performance and pose challenges for practical system integration,thereby limiting their large-scale deployment [64].

To overcome these limitations,beyond improving solubility and optimizing voltage,the development of ROMs capable of multi-electron transfer emerges as a promising strategy for further increasing energy density[65]． In principle,if a molecule can stably undergo multi-electron transfer redox reactions,the theoretical capacity density of electrolyte per unit volume can be significantly enhanced without additional concentration increases.Moreover,multi-electron transfer systems can achieve higher molecular utilization efficiency,which is beneficial for lowering system-level costs.In recent years, increasing attention has been paid to the development of multi-redox ROMs, involving various structural motifs such as quinones [66-69],viologens [70,71],azines [72-74],and other molecules.These systems provide ample opportunities for molecular design through modulation of molecular structures and redox sites.

Although numerous reviews have systematically summarized the development of AORFBs,a focused and comprehensive analysis of multielectron transfer ROMs systems—particularly their molecular design principles,structure-property relationships,and impact on device-level performance—is still lacking.This review aims to bridge that gap by systematically summarizing recent advances in multi-redox active spices (e.g.,quinone,viologen,azine,naphthalene diimide derivatives） with regardtostructuraltailoring，electrontransferpathways, electrochemical stability,and degradation mechanisms.We further discuss their potential and challenges in achieving high energy density and long cycle life in AORFB systems.We aim to provide both theoretical insights and practical guidelines for the rational design and application of multi-electron transfer ROMs,contributing to the development of next-generation，green，efficient，and sustainable energy storage technologies.

# 2．Fundamentals of AORFBs

ARFB system typically comprises two fundamental components: the cell stack and the electrolyte tank as depicted in Fig.2.The cell stack adopts a layered “sandwich”architecture consisting of plates,current collectors,bipolar plates，porous electrodes，and an ion-exchange membrane.The ion-exchange membrane serves to separate anolyte and catholyte,permiting selective ion transport while maintaining overall charge neutrality during operation.Electrodes,situated on both sides of the membrane,provide electrochemical interfaces where redox reactions take place.Bipolar plates are engineered with flow channels that facilitate the continuous circulation of electrolyte through the electrodes.Adjacent to these electrodes are current collectors,which are electrically connected to the external circuit, enabling electron transfer. The entire assembly is secured by end plates to ensure mechanical stability and proper sealing.

The electrolyte storage subsystem comprises separate reservoirs for the anolyte and catholyte,as well as connecting tubing and circulation pumps.Each reservoir stores redox-active electrolytes independently and is connected to the corresponding side of the cell via a closed-loop fluidic circuit. During operation,peristaltic or mechanical pumps drive the electrolytes through the flow channels,where they undergo oxidation or reduction at the respective electrodes before returning to the tanks.

The charge and discharge processes are governed by the redox behavior of the redox-active species.During charging,the redox-active species at catholyte undergo oxidation, releasing electrons:

$$
C ^ { n } - x e ^ { - } {  } C ^ { ( n + x ) }
$$

Simultaneously, the redox-active species at anolyte accept electrons via reduction:

$$
A ^ { n } + x e ^ { - } {  } A ^ { ( n - x ) }
$$

During discharge,the above reactions proceed in the reverse direction.The oxidized catholyte species undergoes reduction:

$$
C ^ { ( n + x ) } + x e ^ { - } \to C ^ { n }
$$

While the reduced anolyte species is oxidized:

$$
A ^ { ( n - x ) } - x e ^ { - } {  } A ^ { n }
$$

Electrons are transferred from one electrode to the other via the external circuit, passing through current collectors and bipolar plates. To preserve charge balance within the cell,counter-ions migrate through the ion-exchange membrane during redox cycling,thereby maintaining electroneutrality in the electrolyte solutions.

Fig.2. Schematic diagram of the typical structure of RFBs.

In AORFBs,three components critically dictate the system's performance:the redox-active electrolytes,the electrodes,and the ionselective membrane.Electrodes should provide a high surface area for electrochemical reactions,combined with excellent electrical conductivityand chemical durability to facilitate efficient redox process [75].It is worth noting that in AORFBs,the electrodes themselves do not participate in energy storage,and act solely as electron donors or acceptors [76]. In addition,because the redox kinetics of ROMs are typically much better than those of traditional inorganic couples (e.g., vanadium, zinc),the need for catalytic electrode modifications is greatly diminished [77,78].The researchers instead directs more attention toward overcoming challenges related to ROM stability and advanced membrane development.

The membrane functions as both a physical separator and an ionconductive medium.It prevents the crossover of active species,mitigates the risk of internal short circuits,and facilitates the migration of counter-ions to complete the ionic loop [79].Given the membrane's impact on current density,power output,and cycle life,the development of high-performance membranes is essential [8O].The core challenge in membrane design is directly dictated by the molecular properties of the ROMs.Severe crossover of ROMs leads to rapid capacity fade, driving membrane engineering strategies that exploit specific molecular characteristics.

A primary approach leverages electrostatic repulsion,which is most effective for highly charged,stable ROMs [81].By designing membranes with densely charged groups that match the charge polarity of the ROMs,crossover can be significantly suppressed. Conversely,for neutral or zwitterionic organic molecules,which are unaffected by electrostatic repulsion,strategies must shift to size sieving.This involves synthesizing large molecular weight ROMs or oligomeric/polymeric materials whose physical size exceeds the membrane's pore size.Furthermore,the development of bipolar molecules,which incorporate both positive and negative charges within a single molecule,presents a promising direction [82].Ultimately,advancing AORFB technology necessitates a co-design philosophy,where the molecular structure of the ROMs is engineered in tandem with the membrane's physicochemical properties to achieve optimal compatibility and long-term stability.

In aqueous media, an ideal ROM should exhibit a high redox potential (either anodic or cathodic), excellent water solubility, strong structural stability,fast electron transfer kinetics,favorable diffusivity, and economic viability.The cycling stability,energy density，power density of AORFBs are closely related to the properties of ROMs. Cycling stability is a pivotal factor for the commercial viability of AORFBs.It is primarily affected by the intrinsic chemical stability of the redox-active molecules,as well as their crossover and adsorption behavior at the membrane interface. Therefore,besides molecular modification, selecting membranes with appropriate ionic selectivity and chemical compatibility is crucial for extending cycle life.

According to energy-density equation: $E { = } \mathrm { Q V }$ (E represents energy density;Qis the quantity of electric charge contained unit volume,Vis the battery voltage),both the solubility and the number of transferable electrons per molecule of ROMs directly influence capacity density in AORFBs.Molecular solubility in aqueous media depends on the balance between hydrophilic and hydrophobic moieties and the compatibility with supporting electrolytes.Strategies to improve solubility include the incorporation of hydrophilic functional groups such as hydroxyl,amino, or sulfonic acid onto the redox-active core,or asymmetric molecular design to enhance polarity.Furthermore,certain co-solvents can promote solubility via hydrogen bonding—for example,methylene blue exhibits a solubility exceeding $^ { 2 \mathrm { ~ M ~ } }$ in acetic acid-water mixtures [83], and TEMPO's solubility increases from $0 . 0 5 ~ \mathrm { M }$ to $2 \textbf { M }$ in a $4 \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ LiTFSI aqueous solution [84]. Nevertheless,under real operating conditions, polarization,redox kinetics,and current limitations typically result in a practical capacity density lower than the theoretical maximum.

The open-circuit voltage (OCV) of the cell is determined by the potential difference between the catholyte and anolyte redox couples, which is intrinsically governed by their molecular structures.The introduction of electron-donating or -withdrawing groups can systematically modulate the redox potentials.Moreover,in proton-coupled electron transfer processes,the redox potential exhibits $\mathsf { p H }$ dependence,decreasing linearly with increasing pHas predicted by the Nernst equation.In practice,cell voltage is also influenced by internal ohmic resistance and concentration polarization effects,which can reduce the effective working voltage.

# 3．Research progress of multi-redox species for AORFBs

# 3.1． Viologens

Viologen (Vi),a class of organic cations composed of $^ { 1 , 1 ^ { \prime } }$ -disubstituted $^ { 4 , 4 ^ { \prime } }$ -bipyridinium units, has recently emerged as a promising class ofROMs as anolytes in AORFBs,owing to its facile synthesis,low redox potential, fast redox kinetics and excellent electrochemical reversibility [85-87]. Viologens typically undergo two sequential one-electron redox processes: $\mathrm { V i } ^ { 2 + }  \mathrm { V i } ^ { + }  \mathrm { V i } ^ { \circ }$ The first redox is highly reversible,but the second exhibits poor reversibility,which limits its practical application in two-electron redox process.The poor reversibility of the second electron transfer primarily stems from the severe structural reorganization and loss of aromaticity upon formation of the neutral $\mathrm { V i } ^ { \circ }$ state, which is highly susceptible to irreversible dimerization and decomposition reactions.To address this challenge,molecular engineering strategies have been developed，including: introducing hydrophilic substituents such as quaternary ammonium groups to enhance the solubility and stability of the neutral state; employing host-guest complexation with cyclodextrins to provide steric shielding; extending $\pi$ -conjugation or incorporating aromatic bridges to delocalize charge and stabilize the reduced state; and introducing steric hindrance groups to suppress parasitic reactions.These approaches have enabled the development of emerging viologen derivatives with high stability, which demonstrate efficient multi-electron storage.This represents a promising strategy to boost energy density (Fig.3),even though current research has predominantly centered on single-electron redox behavior [88,89].

The simplest and most extensively studied viologen derivative is methyl viologen (MV),or $^ { 1 , 1 ^ { \prime } }$ -dimethyl-4,4'-bipyridinium，which exhibits excellent aqueous solubility up to $3 . 0 \ \mathrm { M }$ in $\mathsf { 1 . 5 ~ M }$ NaCl solution [90].It undergoes two reduction steps at-0.45 V and -0.76 V(vs. SHE, the same below),respectively.However, the doubly reduced neutral form of MV has poor solubility in water and redox reversibility.To enable stable two-electron storage,researchers introduced hydrophilic substituents such as 3-(trimethylammonio)propyl (NPr） groups to enhance solubility and redox stability.Liu etal.were the first to achieve stable two-electron reversible storage in aqueous solution using viologen derivatives by synthesizing 1-methyl- $\cdot 1 ^ { \prime } .$ [3-(trimethylammonio) propyl]- $^ { 4 , 4 ^ { \prime } }$ -bipyridinium trichloride, $\mathrm { \small { [ ( M e ) ( N P r ) V ] C l _ { 3 } } }$ ，which displayed two distinct and reversible redox peaks at $- 0 . 3 9 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ and $- 0 . 7 8 { \mathrm { ~ V } }$ (Fig. 4a) [91]. The positively charged quaternary ammonium groups not only increased the solubility of the neutral state viologen but also enhanced its electrochemical stability during cycling via electrostatic repulsion.They further synthesized disubstituted derivatives like $\mathrm { [ ( N P r ) _ { 2 } V ] \mathbf { B } r _ { 4 } }$ and $\mathrm { \Delta [ ( N P r ) ( S P r ) V ] { B r _ { 2 } } }$ ，which,despite their lower solubility than MV,achieved theoretical energy density of $6 8 . 1 ~ \mathrm { W h } . \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 }$ for $[ ( \mathrm { N P r } ) _ { 2 } \ \mathrm { V } ] \mathrm { B r } _ { 4 }$ in neutral aqueous solutions when paired with (ferrocenylmethyl)trimethylammonium chloride (FcNCl) as the catholyte.In this paper, the theoretical energy density is calculated using equation: energydensity $\mathbf { \mu } = \mathbf { n C F V } / \mu _ { \mathrm { v } } ,$ where n is the number of electrons involved in the cell reaction,C is the concentration of active materials,F is Faraday's constant, $\mathrm { v }$ is the cell voltage,and $\mu _ { \nu }$ is a factor representing the overall volumes of anolyte and catholyte (with $\mu _ { \nabla }$ calculated as: $\mu _ { \mathrm { v } } = 1 +$ (max solubility of the less soluble electrolyte)/(max solubility of the more soluble electrolyte). The integration of these strategies is crucial for advancing viologen derivatives from promising laboratory materials to competitive candidates for large-scale energy storage.

# 3.2. Quinones

Quinone derivatives constitute a class of carbonyl compounds, featuring conjugated cyclic carbonyl groups $\scriptstyle ( \mathbf { C } = \mathbf { O } )$ as redox-active cores.They achieve reversible two-electron redox processes through enol-keto tautomerism in a single step.Quinone derivatives can be classified into benzoquinones (monocyclic),naphthoquinones (bicyclic),and anthraquinones (tricyclic) based on the number of aromatic rings (Fig.6),each influencing their redox potential, solubility,and stability.

A fundamental characteristic of quinone electrochemistry is its pronounced dependence on the solution pH,governed by proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET) mechanisms.This pH dependence profoundly impacts their applicability in AORFBs across several key aspects. Specifically,the redox potential of quinones exhibits a strong correlation with proton activity,typically shifting to more negative values as pH increases.This principle allows for the strategic tuning of cell voltage. For instance,benzoquinones can serve as high-potential catholytes in acidic media,while anthraquinone derivatives are often tailored for anolyte use in alkaline environments by leveraging this potential shift. In addition,pH dictates the dominant reaction pathway and rate,as optimal proton availability is often crucial for facilitating rapid and reversible redoxreactions.Most critically,pHisa primary determinant of molecular stability,directly influencing susceptibility to nucleophilic attack，irreversible dimerization，and disproportionation reactions. Consequently, the profound influence of pH on quinone electrochemistry makes it a central design parameter,governing the selection of molecular structures,the achievable cell voltage,and the long-term cycling stability.A successful design is,therefore,contingent on the careful matching of the molecular structure with the electrolyte environment.

The high redox potential of benzoquinones in acidic environments (0.5-1.1 V vs. SHE) makes them suitable as catholyte (Fig.7a).Narayanan etal. pioneered the construction of a quinone/quinone AORFB bypairing1,2-benzoquinone 3,5-disulfonic acid (BQDS) with anthraquinone-2-sulfonic acid (AQs) or anthraquinone-2,6-disulfonic acid (AQDS) in 1.0 M $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } S 0 _ { 4 }$ (Fig.7b） [1o8].The electrochemical behavior of BQDS exhibited a dependence on pHof electrolyte.Inacidic electrolytes with pH below 4,BQDS proceeded a reversible redox reaction. Consequently,BQDS was commonly employed in acidic AORFBs. However，Michael addition between BQDS and $_ \mathrm { H _ { 2 } O }$ progressively transformed BQDS into 1,2,4,6-tetrahydroxybenzene-3,5-disulfonic acid.This caused the molecular structure of BQDS undergoes changes during initial cycling stages,resulting in voltage decay and capacity fade (Fig.7c). To mitigate BQDS deactivation, DHDMBS was synthesized by introducing methyl groups at the 2,4-positions of the BQDS skeleton while retaining hydroxyl and sulfonic acid groups at the1,3,6-positions to block nucleophilic attacks on aromatic ring [lo9].The performance of DHDMBS-based battery was significantly improved compared to BQDS,indicating that the grafting of methyl groups effectively inhibits nucleophilic attack by water. Although no chemical degradation was detected, there was still a slow capacity decay of ${ \sim } 0 . 6 \%$ /cycleover30 cycles attributed to the high membrane crossover caused by the small molecular size of DHDMBS.Namazian et al. proposed two fluorinated hydroquinonederivatives，3-fluorocatechol $\mathrm { ( 3 F Q H _ { 2 } ) }$ and tetrafluoro-p-hydroquinone $\mathrm { ( T F Q H _ { 2 } ) }$ ，as positive ROMs in AORFBs [110]. ${ 3 \mathrm { F Q H } _ { 2 } }$ showed higherredox potential compared to $\mathrm { T F Q H _ { 2 } } ,$ but exhibited less efficiency when constructed AORFB. $\mathrm { T F Q H } _ { 2 }$ -based AORFB showed stable charge-discharge cycling over 40 cycles with the energy efficiency of $8 0 ~ \%$ and a negligible capacity loss at room temperature.

A vanillin-based 2-methoxy-1,4-hydroquinone (MHQ） was also designed as the catholyte of AORFBs,and its semiquinone intermediates at insufficiently low pH and quinoid radicals were identified as responsible for decomposition of MHQ under electrochemical conditions [111]. This side reaction could be hindered using $\mathrm { H _ { 3 } P O _ { 4 } }$ electrolyte,allowing for reversible cycling ina AORFB for 25O cycles with CE of over $9 7 ~ \%$ .Four tertiary amine groups were grafted onto the benzene ring to completely block nucleophilic sites,yielding 2,3,5,6-tetrakis ((dimethylamino)methyl)hydroquinone $\mathrm { ( F Q H _ { 2 } ) }$ [112].At $\mathrm { p H } = - 0 . 7 $ ， $\mathrm { F Q H } _ { 2 }$ exhibited a redoxpotential of $0 . 7 \mathrm { ~  ~ { ~ V ~ } ~ }$ Employinga three-compartment cell design with both AEMand CEM membranes and pairing with a $\mathrm { Z n / Z n ( O H ) \bar { 4 } }$ counter electrode,the full cell achieved a theoretical voltage of $2 . 0 \mathrm { V }$ with $9 9 . 9 2 \%$ capacity retention per cycle or $9 7 . 7 8 ~ \%$ capacity retention per day (Fig.7 d,e).Following similar strategy, Stahl's group developeda fully substituted BQ molecule with four sulfonated thioether substituents [113].The electrochemical tests showed that the tetrasubstituted hydroquinone containing four sulfonated thioether substituents exhibited best stability among the tested thioether substituted benzoquinone derivatives.This tetrasulfonated thioether-BQ retained over $9 8 \ \%$ capacity retention after 5O cycles, proving that full-site blocking effectively prevents Michael addition (Fig. 7 f, g).

Due to the proton-coupled electron transfer mechanism,the redox potential of benzoquinone decreases as the solution pH increases.Based on this,Aziz et al. developed 2,5-dihydroxy-1,4-benzoquinone (DHBQ) as an alkaline-compatible anolyte by replacing two hydrogen atoms on the benzene ring with hydroxyl groups [ll4].DHBQ's strong electron-donating hydroxyl groups form hydrogen-bonding networks, significantly enhancing water solubility of $4 . 3 1 \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ in $^ { 1 \mathrm { ~ M ~ } }$ KOH and substantially reducing its redox potential of $- 0 . 7 2 \mathrm { V }$ at $\tt p H 1 4$ (Fig. 7h). Priced far lower than anthraquinones,DHBQ-based cell paired with ferrocyanide catholyte achieved $9 9 . 7 6 ~ \%$ /cycle capacity retention over 150 cycles.Capacity fade primarily stemmed from molecular crossover and inherent instability.The team subsequently synthesized poly-DHBQ to block unsubstituted carbon atoms on the benzene ring,elevating capacity retention to $9 9 . 9 6 ~ \%$ /cycle over 400 cycles (Fig. 7i).The introduction of the electron-donating methyl groups could further reduce the redox potential of DHBQ [115].2,5-dihydroxy-3, 6-dimethyl-1,4-benzoquinone (DMBQ） exhibited redox potential of

Naphthoquinones (NQ) consist of two adjacent aromatic rings and two conjugated carbonyl groups. Compared to benzoquinones,they exhibit larger $\pi$ -conjugated systems,inherently higher structural stability,and reduced membrane crossover. NQ derivatives are commonly developed as anolytes to leverage their two-electron transfer capability $( \mathrm { N Q } \to \mathrm { N Q } ^ { . } \to \mathrm { N Q } ^ { 2 - } )$

The earliest studied NQ derivative in AORFBs was 2-hydroxy-1,4- naphthoquinone (lawsone,2-HNQ),notable for its abundant sources and synthetic simplicity.However,AORFBs employing 2-HNQ anolyte suffered from low volumetric capacity and severe capacity decay due to limited solubility and side reactions with oxygen.Michael addition at carbonyl-adjacent positions and tautomerism are considered as primary degradation mechanisms for 2-HNQ.

To block the site on NQ that is prone to side reaction and enhance its solubility，Jinetal.synthesized2-hydroxy-3-carboxy-1,4- naphthoquinone (2,3-HCNQ) by introducing a carboxyl group at the 3- position of lawsone [116].This derivative achieved a solubility of $\mathbf { 1 . 2 M }$ in $1 . 0 \mathrm { M } \mathrm { K O H }$ ,asignificantincrease from2-HNQ's O.48M.Inan AORFB using O.1 M 2,3-HCNQ as anolyte and potassium ferrocyanide as catholyte operated at $1 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A { \cdot } c m ^ { - 2 } }$ ,capacity retention reached $9 4 . 7 \%$ after 100 cycles,markedly outperforming unmodified lawsone (Fig.8a,b). Bislawsone was constructed by covalently dimerizing lawsone units to provide mutual steric protection of carbonyl-adjacent sites [117]. The dimer exhibiteda solubility of $0 . 5 6 \mathrm { M }$ in $\mathrm { { p H = 1 4 } }$ solution, equivalent to $\mathbf { 2 . 2 4 \ M }$ transferable electrons.At a current density of $1 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A { \cdot } c m ^ { - 2 } }$ ,the cell based on $0 . 5 \mathrm { M }$ bislawsone showed a capacity retention rate of 0.74 $\%$ per day (Fig.8d).NMR and MS analyses revealed that prolonged reduction induces enol-keto tautomerism in bislawsone,generating inert dihydro derivatives that precipitate from solution, limiting its cycling performance (Fig. 8c).

Anthraquinone (AQ) derivatives represent a class of quinone compounds featuring extended tricyclic conjugated frameworks.Compared to BQs and NQs,AQs possess expanded conjugated planes that confer lower membrane permeability and enhanced structural stability.Their 9,10-carbonyl groups undergo two-electron redox reactions,enabling theoretically high electron density.By introducing water-soluble substituents at various positions on the AQ conjugated skeleton,researchers systematically modulate redox potential,solubility,and cycling stability,developing AQ derivatives adaptable to diverse pH environments.

# 3.4.Naphthalene diimides

Naphthalene diimide (NDI) constitutes a class of planar,rigid,con-jugated aromatic molecules characterized by a naphthalene core flanked by imide groups (-CO—NR-CO-).The naphthalene ring and adjacent imide groups form an extended $\pi$ -conjugated plane,which underpins its favorable electron-transport capabilities and electrochemical activity. The strongly electron-withdrawing imide groups render the naphthalene core highly electron-deficient,endowing NDI molecules with the capacity for reversible two-step, two-electron redox reactions (Fig.15).

To explore NDI's potential as electrolyte in AORFBs,carboxylate groups were introduced to improve water solubility of NDI, yielding the water-soluble derivative $\mathrm { K } _ { 3 } – \mathrm { B N D I }$ with two reversible redox peaks at ${ \cdot 0 . 2 4 / - 0 . 4 9 \mathrm { V } }$ vs.SHE [161].During electrochemical reconstruction, $K ^ { + }$ ions triggered an ion-pairing mechanism through dynamic coordination. Phenylamine derivatives,though historically limited by redox irreversibility,have gained attentionas potential ROMs for AORFBs through targeted molecular engineering to optimize their redox activity and stability.Loh et al. transformed the redox-irreversible triphenylamine into a reversible two-electron transfer system based on enol/keto tautomerism by molecular engineering， synthesizing theparatrihydroxyl substituted derivative $4 , 4 ^ { \prime } , 4 ^ { \prime \prime }$ -trihydroxytriphenylamine (P3,Fig.17 e,f) [17O]. Despite their potential for two-electron reduction to alcohols,ketones have been largely excluded from AORFBs due to a critical bottleneck which arises because the electrochemical oxidation of the resultingalcohol back to the ketone typically requires high potentials or catalytic assistance,a characteristic that makes them impractical for energy storage.Wang etal.achieved reversible ketone-alcohol electrochemical conversion at room temperature without catalysts through molecular engineering of fluorenone (FL) [173]. By introducing strong electron-withdrawing groups into the FL skeleton,the benzylic $\mathrm { C - H } p K a$ of fluorenol (FL-OH) was significantly reduced,enabling deprotonation to fluorenolate in alkaline electrolytes and lowering alcohol oxidation potentials (Fig.l8a).Paired with ferro/ferricyanide catholytes,the cell based 1.36 M 4-carboxylic-7-sulfonate fluorenone (4C7SFL） anolyte cycled 1111 times (120 days) at $2 0 \ \mathrm { m A { \cdot } c m ^ { - 2 } }$ with a capacity decay of

$0 . 0 0 2 3 9 \ \%$ per cycle (Fig.18b).EPR and NMR results showed that charging involves FL reduction to radical anions,which disproportionate to FL/FL-OH,while discharging regenerates radicals via comproportionation of FL-OH for electrochemical oxidation.

Building on this foundational breakthrough in achieving reversible ketone-alcohol conversion through molecular engineering,the performance of FL-based systems was further optimized by targeting kinetic and solubility limitations via a proton-regulation strategy using $\beta$ -cyclodextrin ( $\mathrm { \Delta \ B }$ -CD) [174].DFT and NOESY NMR revealed that $\beta$ -CD forms directional H-bonds with FL-OH benzylic protons (Fig.18c), accelerating deprotonation by 1.6 times.Host-guest interactions increased 4C7SFL solubility to 1 $. 9 \mathrm { M } ( 1 0 1 . 8 \mathrm { A h } . \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 }$ theoretical capacity). # 3.5.4. Other molecules

Li et al.employed an in-situ electrolysis strategy within a flow battery system to induce molecular reconfiguration of pyrene precursors such as 8-hydroxypyrene-1,3,6-trisulfonic acid trisodium salt (HPTS) and 2-((dimethylamino)methyl)1-hydroxypyrene (DMAMPO),enabling the direct generation of stable redox-active molecules without the need for complex post-treatment (Fig.19a) [176].During the electrolysis process,the pyrene cores undergo hydroxylation，electrochemical oxidation,and subsequent chemical transformations including dimerization,isomerization,and asymmetric addition, ultimately forming a series of reversible redox couples composed of hydroxylated isomers at different positions and their corresponding quinone forms. Compared to HPTS,the DMAMPO precursor—with its bulky structure and positively chargedammonium group-effectivelysuppressesdimerization, resulting in an electron utilization rate of up to $9 5 . 5 \%$ and a theoretical electron transfer number approaching four. RFB based on the resulting pyrene-based catholytes deliver high volumetric capacities of up to 50 $\mathrm { \bar { A h } } . \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 }$ and exhibit exceptional cycling stability over 75oo cycles $( \sim 7 0$ days）at both room temperature and $5 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ ，with negligible capacity decay (Fig.19b).An asymmetric pyrene-4,5,9,10-tetraone-1-sulfonate (PTO-PTS) was further designed,in which the introduction of a single sulfonic acid group onto the pyrene core effectively disrupts molecular planarity,reduces intermolecular $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking,and significantly enhances solubility up to $^ { 1 \mathrm { M } }$ ,while preserving its four-electron reversible redox activity (Fig.19 c,d) [177].PTO-PTS exhibits excellent thermal stability,with its semiquinone radical intermediates remaining highly stable even under ambient air.The PTO-PTS-based AORFB demonstrated outstanding performance,deliveringa high volumetric capacity of $8 0 ~ \mathrm { \ A h { \cdot } L ^ { - 1 } }$ with nearly $1 0 0 ~ \%$ capacity retention over 60 days (Fig. 19e).

A four-electron transfer ROM based on spirobifluorene was developed as catholyte, named CSFA,with a high redox potential of 1.05 V vs. SHE[178].During the first charging cycle,the oxidized spirobifluorenyl ammonium salt (SFA) is nucleophilically attacked by chloride ions in the electrolyte.This attack occurs at the four $\beta$ -positions of the spirocyclic quaternary carbon center,resulting in chlorination and the formation of chlorinated spirobifluorenyl ammonium salt (CSFA).A battery using CSFA-Cl as the catholyte cycled stably in air for over 22 days.Although the study found that oxidized CSFA slowly reacts with water to produce oxygen,leading toa slight capacity fade of ${ \sim } 1 . 1 ~ \%$ /day,itsoverall stability significantly surpasses that of unchlorinated SFA,due to chlorination protection effectively inhibits further nucleophilic attack.

Bauer et al.developed a two-electron transfer anolyte based on thioindigo sulfonic acid [179]. Thioindigo- $^ { 5 , 5 ^ { \prime } }$ -disulfonic acid (TIDS) and thioindigo- $^ { 5 , 5 ^ { \prime } , 7 , 7 ^ { \prime } }$ -tetrasulfonic acid (TITeS） were synthesized via direct sulfonationreactions,can achieve reversible two-electron/two-proton transfer in acidic conditions at $3 4 0 ~ \mathrm { m V }$ and $4 0 7 ~ \mathrm { \ m V }$ ，respectively.When paired with BQDs,both electrolytes demonstrated stable cycling for 2Oo cycles.Although their long-term stability requires improvement, this work successfully demonstrates the feasibility of using thioindigo derivatives as anolytes in flow batteries (Table 5).

# 4.Conclusions and perspectives

This paper systematically reviews the research progress of various representative multi-electron transfer ROMs,including viologens, quinones,azines, NDIs, and other emerging redox-active molecules (e.g., azobenzene,phenylamine,fluorenone),and provides a comprehensive assessment of their design strategies,redox mechanisms,battery performances,and degradation pathways. In recent years,as a key strategy to enhance the energy density of AORFBs,multi-redox species have achieved notable breakthroughs.However,it must be acknowledged that their commercialization still faces substantial challenges.For them to become a viable practical technology,the following critical issues need to be adequatelyaddressed:

The design strategy for multi-electron redox-active species remains ambiguous,with the most critical molecular-level challenges,such as stabilizing radical intermediates,managing drastic solubility changes across redox states and overcoming kinetic hysteresis,collectively highlighting a fundamental trade-of by which strategies to boost capacity often exacerbate molecular instability and aggregation. Current research on multi-electron transfer molecules primarily focuses on three aspects:(1) Constructing molecular backbones with multiple independent and reversible redox sites—for instance,introducing multiple quinone groups,thiazine rings,or aza-structural units into a single molecule to enable stepwise or simultaneous electron transfer processes; (2) Incorporating conjugated extended structures and electron delocalization systems to facilitate more efficient electron distribution and transfer within the molecule,thereby stabilizing high-valent intermediates and reducing overpotential for each reaction step; (3) Regulating redox potentials and reaction pathways via intramolecular hydrogen bonding,electron donor/acceptor auxiliary sites,and $\pi { - } \pi$ stacking control to improve electrochemical stability and controllability. Despite progress in individual systems,a universal and clear design logic or predictive model linking "molecular structure to electron transfer behavior" is still lacking.

There is a scarcity of matched multi-electron transfer ROMs in acidic and alkaline systems.Although researches on multi-redox molecules have expanded,with an increasing variety of ROMs,alkaline systems predominantly focus on anode molecules (e.g., quinones,azines, fluorenones),while matched multi-electron transfer catholytes are absent. In full cell, ferricyanides are typically used as the catholyte material. However,limited by the single-electron transfer mechanism and solubility of ferricyanides,the catholyte volume is often much larger than that of the anolyte,reducing the overall energy density of the system. In acidic systems,multi-redox anolytes are relatively scarce,and vanadium is frequently employed as the anolyte.When cation exchange membranes such as Nafion are used,obvious crossover of vanadium ions leads to decreased battery efficiency.Additionally, inorganic anolytes like vanadium and heteropolyacids often exhibit low redox kinetics, limiting the rate performance of the battery.The core of this challenge lies in the dual dependence of the aqueous electrochemical window and molecular stability on pH.Froma thermodynamic perspective,the potentials for the HER and oxygen evolution reaction (OER） shift dramatically with pH. In acidic environments,the lower HER potential creates a strongly reductive atmosphere that severely threatens the stability of organic anolytes in their low-potential reduced states. Conversely,in alkaline environments,the lower OER potential constitutesa highly oxidative environment that readily degrades highpotential oxidized catholyte materials.Furthermore,the $\mathsf { p H }$ dependence of proton-coupled electron transfer processes and the chemical instability of key functional groups (e.g.,imides,esters) under extreme pH conditions further narrow the selection of available molecules.pH fluctuation in electrolyte induced by molecular structural changes or hydrogen/oxygen evolution in neutral systems could compromise molecular stability.During battery operation,hydrogen/oxygen evolution—caused by excessively negative/positive redox potentials—could trigger pH fluctuations in the solution. For viologen and NDI anolytes, hydrogen evolution increases the concentration of $\mathrm { O H } ^ { - }$ in electrolyte, which can attack molecules,causing side reactions such as substituent detachment and impairing battery cycle life.

High-concentration electrolytes lead to increased viscosity.Elevated viscosity and mass transfer limitations in high-concentration electrolytes are also core challenges for AORFBs based on multi-electron transfer ROMs.For example,the six-electron transfer molecule HATNTA exhibits a viscosity as high as $3 0 \mathrm { \ m P a } :$ sat $0 . 2 5 ~ \mathrm { M }$ ,and the resulting pumping losses cause battery capacity decay.These physicochemical bottlenecks at high concentration pose a significant barrier to achieving a reasonable full-cell volumetric capacity target of 40-60 $\mathrm { \mathbf { A } h \cdot L ^ { - 1 } }$ ,which would require operating multi-electron ROMs at concentrations near or above $1 . 0 \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ ，Furthermore,multi-electron transfer molecules exist in multiple redox states during cycling,with varying physicochemical properties (e.g.,solubility,aggregation state) across these states. For instance,the two-electron reduced state of viologen molecules has significantly lower solubility than the oxidized state; Under high-concentration conditions,mass transfer is markedly hindered,and precipitation is prone to occur,blocking flow channels.Devicestructure design (e.g.,，flowchannel geometry， electrode optimization) has not yet balanced mass transfer efciency and energy loss.

Moreover,the high cost of current ROMs restricts their large-scale application in flow batteries.Although organic molecules offer advantages over traditional metal redox materials—such as strong structural tunability and abundant sources—their synthesis pathways are often complex with multiple steps,and industrial-scale production lacks standardized, low-cost synthesis processes.Additionally,many highperformance organic molecules rely on expensive or rare starting materials,catalysts,or sophisticated post-treatment processes,further increasing electrolyte costs.

In summary,the commercialization of AORFBs is contingent upon simultaneously resolving the trilemma of stability,energy density and cost, which necesstates an application-driven selection of redox systems based on their distinct combinations of characteristics.However, translating this application-specific potential into practical reality requires bridging several critical gaps,including insufficient long-term chemical stability under continuous cycling conditions,poor air stability of reduced anolytes necessitating costly inert atmospheres,adverse physicochemical properties at high concentrations,and the lack of standardized,low-cost synthesis routes for scale-up.

While ROMs offer inherent cost advantages through earth-abundant elements,current AORFB systems face significant economic hurdles.For 4-hour storage applications,the estimated levelized cost of AORFBs remains over three times higher than that of lithium-ion batteries,primarily due to immature manufacturing scale and the high cost of ionexchange membranes.However,AORFBs’unique decoupling of power and energy,combined with their potentially superior cycle life and negligible capacity degradation,creates a compelling value proposition for long-duration storage.As discharge duration extends beyond $^ { 8 \mathrm { h } }$ ,this architectural advantage becomes decisive and bring AORFBs increasingly competitive economics due to their scalable energy capacity and long service life,positioning them as the suitable solution for grid-scale long-duration energy storage. Therefore,comprehensive technoeconomic assessment should extend beyond active material cost to include system-level parameters such as energy density,cycle life,and maintenance requirements. In addition，regulatory considerations including safety standards for large-scale electrolyte storage,environmental regulations governing electrolyte disposal,and grid interconnection requirements will significantly influence deployment timelines. Establishing industry-wide testing protocols and safety standards will be crucial for regulatory acceptance and insurability.

For grid-scale long-duration energy storage where ultra-long cycle life and minimal levelized cost are paramount, alkaline anthraquinone/ iron systems currently represent the most promising candidate.Their exceptional stability combined with the low cost of anthraquinone precursors and iron, positions them ideally for large-scale energy storage applications.Conversely,for applications demanding high power density and rapid response—such as grid frequency regulation—neutral pH systems pairing viologen anolyteswith TEMPO catholytes offer superior characteristics.These systems achieve high cell voltages $( > 1 . 4 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } )$ and exhibit excellent redox kinetics,while benefiting from the relatively low cost of viologen and TEMPO precursors.

Among other molecular families,azine derivatives present compelling alternatives to anthraquinones in alkaline media, offering more negative redox potentials for enhanced energy density.However, their current synthesis costs remain substantially higher, limiting immediate large-scale deployment. Similarly,NDI systems,while providing unique insights into aggregation control,face both cost challenges and technical hurdles including kinetic hysteresis and hydrolysis susceptibility.

Overall, this comparative analysis underscores that the development trajectory points toward a diversified ecosystem: cost-effective anthraquinone/iron systems for bulk storage,kinetically superior viologen/ TEMPO systems for frequency regulation,with phenazines and NDIs as future candidates pending cost reductions and durability improvements. The successful commercialization of these systems will ultimately dependonco-developingmolecules，cell architecture，and manufacturing processes within an integrated framework that addresses both technical performance and economic viability across different grid service applications,with AORFBs finding their optimal niche in 8-hour and beyond long-duration storage markets where their architectural advantages outweigh current cost premiums.

Thus,regarding future directions for multi-redox organic materials in ARFBs,we propose focusing on the following aspects (Fig.20):

Current molecular developments rely on trial-and-error and empirical rules,lacking universal predictive models for multi-electron transfer behaviors (e.g.，redox kinetics,intermediate stability).Artificial intelligence-driven molecular design is expected to be a core approach for establishing "structure-performance"correlations in multi-redox organic species.By integrating machine learning with high-throughput screening，we can construct an intelligent optimization system for "molecular structure-electron transfer characteristics-physicochemical properties," enabling virtual screening and directed generation of chemical space to rapidly identify molecular backbones with multielectron transfer capabilities,appropriate redox potentials,and high stability.This willalso reveal how key factors—such as functional group combinations,conjugation length,and substituent effects—influence critical properties like molecular stability and solubility. Combining robotic synthesis with high-throughput electrochemical validation can significantly accelerate the discovery cycle of high-performance molecules,shifting material development from experience-driven to modeldriven paradigms.

Developing compatible electrolyte additives. Electrolyte engineering is critical for overcoming stability and high-concentration mass transfer bottlenecks of multi-redox organic species.For neutral systems,novel adaptive buffer systems can be developed,or the hydrolysis window can be widened by increasing supporting electrolyte concentration to dynamicallymaintain electrolyte acid-base balance.To address aggregation and precipitation caused by solubility differences across multielectron states,solvation microenvironments can be synergistically optimized via molecular structure modification and functional additives,enhancing molecular thermodynamic stability and dispersibility to prevent aggregation and flow channel blockage.For viscosity increases and mass transfer limitations at high concentrations,lowviscosity molecular design concepts—such as ROMs with branched structures—should be prioritized to construct hydrogen bond networks and ionic interactions,ensuring high solubility while reducing rheological resistance.Additionally,optimizing supporting electrolyte concentration and ion type will balance conductivity and viscosity.

Establishing bipolar membranes and neutral pH systems.A multifaceted strategy that integrates innovative cell architectures with precise molecular engineering is essential to overcome the fundamental challenge of pH compatibility.The implementation of pH-asymmetric configurations,enabled by bipolar membranes,offers a systematic solution by decoupling the operational environments.This allows the anolyte and catholyte to function within their respective optimal pH regimes—alkaline for anolytes and acidic for catholytes—thus circumventing the inherent stability limitations of single-pH systems. Concurrently,molecular design must be tailored to address intrinsic vulnerabilities at $\mathsf { p H }$ extremes.Tailored molecular engineering addresses pH-specific challenges by implementing hydrolytically robust, proton-resistant motifs for acidic anolytes and integrating strong electron-withdrawing groups for alkaline catholytes,while strategically stabilizing reactive intermediates through extended conjugation or steric protection.In parallel,dedicated research on high-performance multi-electron pairs for neutral pH systems presents a complementary route to entirely bypass the corrosive challenges of extreme pH.This holistic approach,marrying system-level innovation with atomic-level molecular control,provides a comprehensive blueprint for nextgeneration,high-energy-density AORFBs.

Advancing in-situ characterization technologies.In-situ/operando characterization is key to revealing dynamic behaviors of multielectron transfer ROMs and guiding material and device optimization. Traditional characterization methods struggle to probe transient intermediates,aggregation dynamics,or side reactions during battery cycling.Developing_high spatiotemporal resolution platforms (e.g, NMR,UV-Vis,EPR,Raman) will enable real-time tracking of transient intermediate evolution during multi-step electron transfer,clarify how complex mechanisms (e.g.，proton-coupled electron transfer) regulate reaction properties,and analyze aggregation kinetics of ROMs under concentration gradients and potential changes.Accurately identifying deactivation pathways of ROM intermediates and aggregation-sensitive sites will effectively guide the design of multi-electron transfer molecules with high intrinsic stability and protective functional groups.

Developing low-cost,air-stable electrolyte systems.An ideal flow battery system should feature low capital costs and long cycle life.Unlike inorganic flow batteries using scarce electrolytes,AORFBs utilize materials primarily composed of earth-abundant elements (e.g.,carbon, hydrogen,oxygen，nitrogen)，promising significant cost reductions. Reducing ROM preparation costs while extending battery cycle life will balance the overall cost of AORFB systems.Molecule costs are closely linked to production scale,raw material prices,and post-treatment complexity,while energy storage system capital costs depend on performance metrics such as lifespan, capacity utilization,and energy density.In addition,most AORFBs require operation in inert atmospheres because reduced anolyte molecules are easily oxidized by air, increasing the difficulty of maintaining system hermeticity during battery manufacturing.Regulating molecular aggregation via molecular engineering is a promising solution for developingair-stable anolytes.
