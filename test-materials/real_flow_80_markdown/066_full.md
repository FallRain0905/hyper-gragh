Vanadium redox flow battry (VRFB)is a new type of high-efficiency energy conversion and storage device. Due to its independent battery output power and energy storage capacity,it is suitable for large-scale energy storage in renewable energy generation proceses such as wind and solar energy,as wellas grid peak shaving processes. However,due to the influence of operatingconditions,especially electrolyte flow rate,its performance is compromised.Under high flow rate and high current density,water migrates severely from the negative to the positive sides after multiple cycles,causing severe electrolyte imbalance and ultimately leading to areduction in available capacity.Therefore,byseting diffrent electrolyte flow rates,a pressure gradient is formed to suppress the large-scale migration of water.Experiments have shown that under the optimal asymmetric flow rate,the charge-discharge performance of the batery can be improved; Compared with symmetric flow rate,VRFB has significantly improved eficiency and capacity.Among them,Asymmetry-2 enhanced theaverage coulombic efficiency by $0 . 9 4 \%$ ，energy efficiency by $2 . 0 5 \%$ ，and capacity retention by $9 . 6 6 \%$ compared to Symmetry. Studies have demonstrated that an asymmetric electrolyte flow rate design can significantly enhance the overall performance of batteries.

# 1．Introduction

In recent years,with the proposal of the dual-carbon targets (carbon peak,carbon neutral),the new energy market has spurred significant growth,and various innovative energy storage technologies storage have emerged,such as vanadium redox flow battery (VRFB),sodium-ion battery, lithium-sulfur battery,and nickel-based battery.VRFB,which hasrecently entered the energy storage market,is reported to possess the capability of indefinite cycling and exhibit output power comparable to that of lithium-ion batteries [1-3]. VRFBs possess several advantages over lithium batteries,including a longer lifespan, superior scalability, higher efficiency,and markedly enhanced safety.These attributes make VRFBs particularly well-suited for the energy storage field [4,5]. Therefore,VRFBs have emerged as one of the most promising technologies for large-scale energy storage,with broad application prospects in peak shaving power systems,large-scale wind and solar power system energy storage,emergency power systems,and other fields [6-8].

Despite their advantages in large-scale,long-term energy storage, VRFBs face significant challenges due to their low energy density and high costs,which are key factors restricting their development and application of VRFB.To address these issues,many researchers have focused on enhancing VRFB performance by developing new materials, improving electrolyte solubility,enhancing electrode activity,utilizing highly selective ion membranes,and increasing current collector current density [9-11].However, the development of new materials is often characterized by long cycles,high costs,and significant risks.Consequently, optimizing operating conditions to ensure that VRFBs operate at their optimal state has become an effective and widely adopted method for improving VRFB performance [12-14].

In recent years,significant advancements have been achieved in enhancing the overall performance of VRFB through operational optimization.However,these improvements predominantly rely on symmetric materials,structures,and control methods,which have shown limited effectiveness.Consequently,to address the inadequacies of symmetric approaches in performance enhancement,asymmetric research methods have been introduced [15-17]. For instance,Agar et al. [18] employed an asymmetric electrode configuration comprising pristine and functionalized electrodes; Their study demonstrated that the performance of VRFB was primarily constrained by the negative half-cell reaction.Wei et al.[19] artificially induced the hydrogen evolution side reaction and constructed an asymmetric electrode structure; This approach not only enhanced the kinetics of simultaneous reactions at both electrodes but also minimized the kinetic disparities between them. Park et al.[2O] discovered that the imbalance crossover rate of vanadium material remained high due to the concentration disparity between the positive and negative electrolytes; During cell operation, an asymmetric volume ratio between positive and negative electrolytes was introduced, which significantly mitigated the capacity decay rate during early cycling stages.Wang et al.[21] developed a non-uniform electrode compression model that accounts for electrode shape deformation and the heterogeneity of physical properties, demonstrating that non-uniform compression can enhance the performance of VRFBs. Zeng et al. [22] introduced and fabricated an asymmetric porous polybenzimidazole film featuring an ultra-thin dense layer and a sponge-like porous layer, which exhibited high conductivity and superior stability. Shrestha et al. [23] utilized asymmetric anion and cation exchange membranes to mitigate battery capacity decay and improve capacity retention.Lu et al.[24] designed and optimized an asymmetric electrode structure to reduce capacity degradation and enhance battery charge-discharge efficiency.Yang et al. [25] investigated the impact of vanadium ion crossover on capacity fade, charge-discharge performance,and the effects of asymmetric electrolyte concentration and operating pressure strategies in VRFBs. Jing et al. [16] developed an asymmetric battery configuration for VRFBs by leveraging the significant diferences in cathode and anode dynamics, thereby substantially enhancing energy efficiency.Monalisa et al.[26] argue that one of the most significant challenges impacting the reliability of redox flow batteries (RFBs) is the imbalanced osmotic pressure between compartments,resulting from differences in molar concentration between the anode and cathode electrolytes.This imbalance leads to substantial water migration across the membrane. By developing an asymmetric electrolyte formulation, they were able to suppress water migration. Their findings suggest that adjusting electrolyte concentrations effectively mitigates this issue. Zhang et al.[27] reconstructed the asymmetric chemical potential on both sides of the heterogeneous interface,thereby accelerating electrochemical kinetics and significantly enhancing RFB performance.

The above research indicates that the asymmetric VRFB research method can effectively improve battery performance compared to the symmetric research method [28]. Flow rate control is crucial and can significantly enhance the VRFB's performance.This article establishes pressure gradients on the positive and negative sides by setting different flow rates in the positive and negative inlet, thereby suppressing water migration and preventing electrolyte imbalance. Consequently,asymmetric flow rate design methods positively impact the available capacity of VRFBs and enhance their overall performance.

# 2. Working principle and water migration

# 2.1．Principles of electrochemical reaction

The VRFB system primarily comprises a stack, storage tanks,circulating pumps,power supply,and load,as illustrated in Fig.l(a). The stack,a critical component,consists of a membrane,bipolar plates, collector plates,electrode frames,end plates,seals,and fasteners.In VRFBs, the positive side employs vanadium ion solutions in $\nabla ^ { 4 + }$ and $\mathsf { V } ^ { 5 + }$ as active material,while the negative side uses vanadium ion solutions in $\mathsf { V } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { V } ^ { 2 + }$ .These electrolytes are stored separately in dedicated storage tanks.During charge-discharge process,the positive and negative electrolytes undergo oxidation-reduction reactions on either side of the ion exchange membrane [29]. Simultaneously,the external pump continuously circulates the electrolyte from the storage tanks into the respective chambers to maintain ion concentration, thereby facilitating the charge-discharge process of the VRFB.

The electrochemical reaction process is illustrated in Fig.1(b).At the positive side, the reaction occurs between $\nabla ^ { 4 + }$ and $\mathsf { V } ^ { 5 + }$ ,while at the negative side, the reaction takes place between $\mathrm { V } ^ { 2 + }$ and $\mathsf { V } ^ { 3 + }$ . In a fully discharged state the positive electrolyte contains only $\nabla ^ { 4 + }$ ，and the negative electrolyte contains only $\mathsf { V } ^ { 3 + }$ .Conversely,ina fully charged state,the positive electrolyte contains only $\mathsf { V } ^ { 5 + }$ ,and the negative electrolyte contains only $\mathrm { V } ^ { 2 + }$ .The potential difference between the positive and negative electrodes during the entire reaction is 1.259V [30].

# 2.2.Analysis of water migration mechanisms

As shown in Fig.2,during the operation of VRFB,trace interactions and electromigration of positive and negative electrolytes across the ionconductive membrane are inevitable.The hydrated ions associated with vanadium ions in different oxidation states exhibit varying numbers and diffusion rates,leading to a gradual accumulation that can cause electrolyte imbalance over time.The most common forms of ion crossover and related water transfer to the positive half-cell include vanadium ions, bound water,and permeation-driven water [31-33]. In summary, transmembrane water migration results in electrolyte imbalance, thereby reducing the available capacity of VRFB.

# 3.Experimental section

# 3.1. Experimental design and process

An experimental study was conducted to verify that asymmetric control of electrolyte flow rates on the positive and negative sides of a vanadium redox flow battery (VRFB) enhances overall battery performance.The experimental design concept is illustrated in Fig.3(a). Initially,a thorough analysis of the electrochemical characteristics of VRFBs was performed to understand the mechanisms by which electrolyte flow rate influences battery performance and identify performance improvement methods.Subsequently,an asymmetric flow control strategy was proposed to address the issue of water transmembrane migration under symmetric electrolyte flow conditions,and experiments were designed to verify battery performance under asymmetric flow conditions.Finally,experimental data were collected and analyzed to refine and improve the method iteratively [37].

The experimental procedure is illustrated inFig.3(b).Initially,verify the functionality of all experimental apparatus to ensure they are erorfree.Subsequently,configure the density and cut-off voltage for the charge-discharge circuit. Activate the circulation pump and chargedischarge tester,allowing them to operate in an open-circuit state for a specified duration.Set the charging cut-off voltage to 1.65V,followed by a 3O seconds stabilization period post-charging.Next,conduct a discharge test with the cut-off voltage set to 1.oov,followed by another 30 seconds stabilization period.Finally,the flow rates of the positive and negative electrolytes are adjusted,12O cycles are performed under consistent operating conditions,and the data is collected and analyzed.

# 4.Results and discussion

# 4.1． Charge-discharge performance

It is the most critical indicator for evaluating the performance of VRFB,encompassing constant current mode,constant power mode,and constant voltage mode.The constant current mode is especially advantageous for assessing VRFB performance because it ensures a stable current throughout both charge-discharge processes.As illustrated in Fig.4(a),voltage and power levels rise during the charging process but decline during the discharging process.Given that the directions of current and power are opposite during charge-discharge,the variations in current and power are represented as absolute values.

From Fig.4(a),itis evident that different flow modes exhibit distinct battery charge-discharge performance due to localized phenomena such as concentration polarization,water migration,and electrolyte imbalance.Specifically, the charge and discharge performance in asymmetric mode surpasses that of symmetric mode,with Asymmetry-2 mode demonstrating the highest efficiency.The findings suggest an asymmetric flow design significantly enhances water molecule transmembrane transport.However,an optimal critical point exists for charge and discharge performance in asymmetric mode,which mitigates false peaks during the charging phase and extends the duration of the discharge phase.After multiple observation and statistical analysis cycles,the cumulative charge and discharge duration difference under various flow modes becomes increasingly pronounced,as illustrated in Fig.4(b). The duration difference between the Asymmetry-2 mode and the Symmetry,Asymmetry-1,and Asymmetry-3 modes increased from 390 seconds,240 seconds,and 90 seconds in the first cycle to 64,800 seconds,57,600 seconds,and 10,800 seconds in the 120th cycle, respectively. The results indicate that,compared with Symmetry, Asymmetry-1,and Asymmetry-3 modes,Asymmetry-2 mode extended a single cycle by 540 seconds,480 seconds,and 90 seconds,respectively. Studies have demonstrated that implementing asymmetric electrolyte flow rates on the positive and negative sides can effectively mitigate large-scale transmembrane migration of water molecules and suppress electrolyte imbalance,thereby significantly enhancing battery chargedischarge performance.

# 4.2. Change in internal resistance

During the operation of VRFB,electrochemical reactions can increase resistance,thereby shortening the battery's lifespan. Consequently, internal resistance is a critical parameter for evaluating battery performance.The total losses in the VRFB system consist of internal resistance losses and pump losses.Specifically，internal resistance comprises ion resistance and flow resistance.Ionic resistance depends on electrical factors and decreases with increasing state of charge (SoC), whereas flow resistance is influenced by flow rate and flow-related factors.As shown in Fig.5,altering the flow rates on both the positive and negative sides result in changes in internal resistance.Notably, the charging DC internal resistance initially decreases sharply with cycle accumulation,stabilizes,and ultimately increases.

From Fig.5(a),it is evident that the internal resistance under symmetrical flow rates reaches its peak,with an average value of $2 3 . 9 8 \mathrm { m } \Omega$ When the total flow rate remains constant, reducing the negative side flow rate while increasing the positive side flow rate decreases charging internal resistance.However,as the disparity between the positive and negative flowrates increases beyonda certain threshold,the internal resistance starts to rise again. The experimental results suggest that asymmetric flow control can reduce internal resistance,but only up to an optimal point,beyond which the internal resistance increases.This optimal point corresponds to the critical value of the positive and negative flow rates.

As illustrated in Fig.5(b),during the discharge phase, the concentration of vanadium ions in the electrolyte decreases over time due to the concentration gradient in the VRFB. Simultaneously,thermal accumulation within the VRFB increases internal temperature,enhancing electron migration.As the discharge progresses,the battery experiences a rise in resistance.Under asymmetric flow control, the increase in internal resistance is significantly lower compared to symmetric flow conditions due to improved mass transfer efficiency.Experimental data indicate that the internal resistance of Asymmetric-2 is the lowest at $3 2 . 0 8 \mathrm { m } \Omega$ ，followed by Asymmetric-3 at $3 2 . 4 3 \mathrm { m } \Omega$ ，while the highest internal resistance is observed in the symmetric flow mode at $3 6 . 8 6 \mathrm { m } \Omega$ Specifically，the internal resistance of Asymmetric-2 is $4 . 7 8 \mathrm { m } \Omega$ ， $3 . 3 1 \mathrm { m } \Omega$ ,and $0 . 3 5 \mathrm { m } \Omega$ lower than that of Symmetric,Asymmetric-1,and Asymmetric-3,respectively.Studies have demonstrated that appropriate asymmetric flow control can reduce mass transfer resistance and extend the battery's service life.

# 4.3. Cell fficiency analysis

Efficiency is a critical parameter for evaluating battery performance, encompassing coulomb efficiency (CE),energy efficiency (EE),and voltage efficiency (VE). These efficiencies can be quantified using charge-discharge curves [38].The specific calculations of CE,EE,and VE are shown in Eqs. (1), (2),and (3), respectively.

$$
C E = \frac { Q _ { d i s } } { Q _ { c h } } \times ~ 1 0 0 \% = \frac { \int I _ { d i s } ( t ) d t } { \int I _ { c h } ( t ) d t } \times ~ 1 0 0 \%
$$

$$
V E = \frac { \overline { { V } } _ { d i s } } { \overline { { V } } _ { c h } } \times ~ 1 0 0 \% = \frac { \int V _ { d i s } ( t ) d t / \int V _ { c h } ( t ) d t } { t _ { d i s } / t _ { c h } } \times ~ 1 0 0 \%
$$

$$
E E = \frac { W _ { d i s } } { W _ { c h } } \times ~ 1 0 0 \% = \frac { \int V _ { d i s } ( t ) I _ { d i s } ( t ) d t } { \int V _ { c h } ( t ) I _ { c h } ( t ) d t } \times ~ 1 0 0 \%
$$

where, $Q _ { d i s }$ is the charging capacity, $Q _ { c h }$ is the discharging capacity, $I _ { d i s }$ is the charging current density, $I _ { c h }$ is the discharging current density, $t$ is the charge-discharge time, $\overline { { V } } _ { d i s }$ is the discharging average voltage, $\overline { { V } } _ { c h }$ is the charging average voltage, $V _ { d i s }$ is the discharging voltage at any time, $V _ { c h }$ is the charging voltage at any time, $t _ { d i s }$ is the discharging time, $t _ { c h }$ is the charging time, $W _ { d i s }$ is the discharging energy,and $W _ { c h }$ is the charging capacity.

Generally,a higher CE indicates better ion selectivity;a higher VE results in lower battery resistance and improved proton conductivity; anda higher EE signifies enhanced energy conversion efficiency [39].

CE is used to measure the conversion eficiency of electrical energy during battery charge-discharge processes and is related to the transmembrane crossing of vanadium ions on both sides of the ion membrane.As shown in Fig.6(a),with the increase of cycle times,CE gradually increases.Due to severe water migration under symmetric conditions,vanadium ion crossing occurs,resulting in lower CE.The CE was calculated under various modes to achieve more accurate values, and the results are presented in Fig.6(b).The values for the Asymmetric-2 and Asymmetric-3 modes were higher than those of the Symmetric and Asymmetric-1 modes.Research has demonstrated that optimizing the flow rates of the positive and negative electrolytes can significantly minimize vanadium ion crossover.

The VE of VRFBs is closely associated with the battery polarization that occurs during the charge-discharge processes.Higher VE indicates lower battery polarization and reduced voltage loss.As illustrated in Fig.6(c),underall operating conditions,VE gradually decreases as the number of charge-discharge cycles increases.After12O cycles,the VEs for the four modes are $8 0 . 5 7 \%$ ， $8 2 . 1 1 \%$ ， $8 5 . 1 8 \%$ ,and $8 4 . 3 8 \%$ ,respectively.This suggests that asymmetric control of positive and negative electrolyte flow rates significantly outperforms symmetric control. Specifically,VE is primarily influenced by concentration polarization, which confirms that asymmetric control can effectively mitigate this phenomenon. These results indicate optimizing the positive and negative flow rates can enhance the battery's VE.In addition,the average VE of different modes,labeled as $8 6 . 5 2 \%$ ， $8 6 . 7 2 \%$ ， $8 7 . 8 1 \%$ ，and $8 7 . 5 6 \%$ ， were analyzed,as illustrated in Fig.6(d).Among these,Asymmetric-2 exhibits the highest VE value.As the difference between the positive and negative flow rates increases,VE starts to decline,suggesting the presence of an optimal critical value for the positive and negative flow rates.

EE is determined by both CE and VE,serving as a metric to evaluate energy conversion efficiency during the charge-discharge processes. Although CE increases with the number of charge-discharge cycles and remains high, VE gradually decreases due to concentration polarization, leading to a declining trend over time.As shown in Fig. 6(d), the average EE for the four modes is $8 1 . 2 5 \%$ ， $8 1 . 7 3 \%$ ， $8 3 . 3 0 \%$ ，and $8 3 . 0 8 \%$ ， respectively.Specifically，Asymmetric-2outperformsSymmetric, Asymmetric-1,and Asymmetric-3 by $2 . 0 5 \%$ ， $1 . 5 7 \%$ ，and $0 . 2 2 \%$ ,highlighting the asymmetric mode's superior energy conversion efficiency. According to Fig.6(d),extensive migration of water molecules and transmembrane crossing of vanadium ions contribute to decreased voltage efficiency as the number of cycles increases.Moreover,the decrease in VE exceeds the increase in CE,resulting in a net decrease in EE as the number of charge-discharge cycles accumulates.The experimental results demonstrate that EE can be significantly enhanced by strategically designing asymmetric flowrates，improving VRFB performance.

# 5．Conclusion

The electrochemical reaction in a VRFB necessitates a circulating pump to transport the electrolyte to the reaction area.The flow rate is a critical operating parameter that directly influences the battery's performance.Moreover, significant water migration can occur at high flow rates,resulting in electrolyte imbalance and irreversible capacity loss. Consequently,to mitigate water migration, it is proposed to implement asymmetric control of the positive and negative flow rates to establish a preemptive pressure gradient, thereby suppressing water migration.An experimental platform was constructed to validate the effectiveness of the asymmetric flow design method,and 120 cycles were performed under varying positive and negative flow rates.The results demonstrate that the charge-discharge performance,CE,VE,EE,and capacity retention rate of VRFB are markedly enhanced under asymmetric flow rate conditions.These findings suggest that the asymmetric flow rate control method is effective and can serve as a valuable reference for future enhancements in VRFB performance.
