# ABSTRACT

Keywords: Redox flow battery Deep eutectic solvent Organic active species Water content

To improve the performance of redox flow bateries (RFBs)using deep eutectic solvent (DES)electrolytes,this study systematically investigates the efect of water content on the physicochemical and electrochemical properties of aqueous DES electrolytes containing different active species.The redox couples studied include organic species-tetramethylpiperidine oxide (TEMPO)and quinoxaline-as wellas metal-based species (Fe and V ions). The experimentalresults show thatthe strategic additionof water effectively reduces viscosity and density,while significantly enhancing the ionic conductivity of DES electrolytes.Electrochemical analysis determines the optimal water content to be $1 0 \mathrm { \ m t { \% } }$ for inorganic species and $2 0 \mathrm { w t } \%$ for organic species.Furthermore,this work evaluates the performance of aqueous DES-based RFBs employing the aforementioned active species.Full-cell tests demonstrate that a TEMPO $\mathrm { . v c l } _ { 3 }$ hybrid RFB with $1 0 ~ \mathrm { w t \% }$ water exhibits the optimal overall performance, achieving $9 0 \%$ coulombic efficiency, $7 7 \%$ energy efficiency (at $5 ~ \mathrm { m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 } \gamma$ ,and excellent cycling stability.In contrast,the al-rganic TEMPO-quinoxaline RFB,despite its high discharge power density,sufers from poor cycling stabilitydue to material degradationand unavoidable side reactions.This study confirms thecritical role of water content regulation in optimizing DES-based electrolytes and suggests the promising application of organic-inorganic hybrid RFBs with aqueous DES electrolytes.

# 1． Introduction

To address the inherently intermittent and unstable problems of renewable energy generation (e.g.,solar energy，wind energy,tidal energy),various grid-scale energy storage solutions are undergoing rapid development to support the advancement of zero-carbon power generation technologies [1-3]. Thereinto,redox flow batteries (RFBs) represent promising candidates for integrating into the renewable power system,due to lower cost, higher safety,longer life-cycle and decoupling design of energy storage and power generation [4-6].Although numerous RFBs have been proposed over the past decade,all-vanadium RFB with aqueous electrolyte has received extensive attention,resulting from the same element as redox couples used in positive and the negative sides thus minimizing the crossover contamination [7].To date, all-vanadium RFB has successfully entered into commercial energy storage market [8,9].However,the soaring purchasing cost of vanadium raw materials has undermined the competitiveness of all-vanadium RFB relative to lithium-ion energy storage batteries.Additionally,the relativelylow solubility of aqueous electrolytes,stemming from their narrow electrochemical stability window,restricts further enhancement of their energy density and power density.

In 2003,Abbott et al.[1O] proposed an all-copper hybrid RFB utilizing a DES electrolyte. Being a green non-aqueous solvent,Deep eutectic solvent (DES) is formed through the association of hydrogen bond donors and hydrogen bond acceptors.DES-based electrolytes enable the maximized utilization of active species of RFBs owing to their advantageous properties,including a wide electrochemical window, high solubility，low flammability，and environmental friendliness [11-14].In recent years,diverse DES electrolyte-based RFBs have been developed to expand the variety of non-aqueous RFBs [15-18].Despite this progress,the practical application of DES electrolyte-based RFBs still faces significant obstacles,as most DESs exhibit unfavorable characteristics,such as high viscosity,poor mass diffusivity,and slow reaction kinetics.These disadvantages consequently introduce substantial mass transfer and electrochemical reaction resistances during electrolyte flow [19-22].As a consequence,the modification of DES electrolyte remains necessary to enhance the performance of DES electrolyte-based RFBs under development.

To overcome above inherent limitations of non-aqueous DESs, Alfurayj et al.[23] proposed a strategic incorporation of minor quantities of water employed into DESs.This approach is aimed atdisruptthe extensive hydrogen bond networks characteristic of DES components, thereby reducing viscosity and enhancing mass transport properties. This “Water-in-deep eutectic solvent”electrolytes,inheriting characteristics from aqueous and DES medium 24],have been proven to be capable of effectively enhancing reversibility and cycling stability of Zn-ion batteries [25] hybrid supercapacitors [26].With regard to RFBs, the experimental study from Prado et al. [27] showed that opportune addition of water into DES could significantly boost the conductivity, diffusivity and kinetic characteristics of Fe ions as active species,and decrease the viscosity of DES-based electrolytes.Moreover,this aqueous DES nevertheless achieved an electrochemical stability window closely comparable to that of non-aqueous DES electrolytes.Similarly,Protsenko etal.[28] investigated the electrochemical kinetics of Cr ions in hydrous DESsolvents. Experimental results demonstrated that increasing the water content in the solvent effectively enhances the mass transfer efficiency of chromium ions.However,the charge transfer resistance was conversely observed to decrease.Therefore, finding an appropriate water content for the DES solvent represents an essential approach to optimizing the performance of such aqueous DES electrolyte-based RFBs.Wang etal.[29] revealed that,a water-to-DES molar ratio of 1:3 leads to a significant enhancement in the conductivity and diffusion coefficient of the active species in solvent,thus resulting in a new,cost-effective,and highly efficient electrolyte for iron-vanadium RFB applications.Moreover,Nayanthara et al.[30] proposed DES-based all-vanadium RFB electrolytes which contain an optimal amount of aqueous acid and dispersed sulfonated multi-walled carbon nanotubes.This modified electrolyte exhibits a high-capacity retention $( 9 0 . 0 \%$ over 40o charge/discharge cycles)and energy efficiencies $( 7 0 . 0 \%$ over 400 cycles),thus proving the commercial potential of aqueous DES-based RFBs.

Asa consequence,aforementioned literature demonstrates the critical role of water incorporation at appropriate ratios in enhancing the performance of DES-based electrolytes for RFBs.However,current research has primarily focused on electrolytes utilizing metal-based redox species (e.g.,Fe,Cr,V),leaving a significant gap in systematic comparative studies investigating the distinct effects of water content on the electrochemical kinetic properties of DES electrolytes containing organic species versus metal-based active species.Furthermore,a screening study is also needed to explore the feasibility of utilizing aqueous DES electrolytes as both catholyte and anolyte to construct allorganic RFBs or hybrid (organic/inorganic) RFBs.Consequently,this work comparatively investigates the optimal water content for achieving the optimal kinetic properties in aqueous DES solutions containing different organic active species (including tetramethylpiperidine oxide i.e.,TEMPO and Quinoxaline) and metal-based active species (Fe ions and V ions).Based on the water content optimization results,this study proposes both an aqueous DES-based all-organic RFBs (utilizing TEMPO as the redox species of positive side and quinoxaline as the redox species of negative side,denoted as the T-Q RFBs) and an aqueous DESbased hybrid RFBs (utilizing TEMPO as the redox species of positive side and Vanadium as the redox species of negative side,denoted as the T-V RFBs)，and comprehensively compares the full-cell polarization behavior, coulombic efficiency,and voltage efficiency during chargedischarge cycles for these aqueous DES electrolyte-based RFBs.

# 3．Results and discussion

# 3.1. Viscosity,density,and electrical conductivity measurements

Viscosity $\left( \eta \right)$ ，density $( \rho )$ ，and electrical conductivity $( \sigma )$ are key transport properties for DES-based RFBs,as they directly influence battery performance by regulating pumping energy consumption and ohmic losses.This study reveals the effect of water content on these properties of DES electrolytes with the different active species,which are $\mathrm { F e C l } _ { 2 }$ ， $\mathrm { { V C l } _ { 3 } }$ ,TEMPO,and Quinoxaline respectively.

Fig.l(a) shows that the viscosity of all DES electrolytes decreases as water content increases.For example,DES electrolyte with $\mathrm { F e C l } _ { 2 }$ as the active specie has a viscosity of $5 2 . 6 \mathrm { m P a } s$ when water content is $0 ~ \mathrm { w t } \%$ While this viscosity decreases to $1 7 . 5 \ \mathrm { m P a } \ \mathrm { s }$ at $3 0 ~ \mathrm { w t \% }$ water content, which is a reduction of about $6 6 . 7 \%$ .This markedly decrease in viscosity happens because water molecules insert into the original hydrogen-bond network of the DES and weaken its structure [33,34]. Due to hydrogen bonds of DES are weakened and broken by the existing water molecules, the interaction between DES molecules and active ions decreases,thus causing a significant decline in viscosity at the macro level.Fig.1(b) shows that the solvent densities of all DES electrolytes decrease steadily as water content increases.This is because water dilutes the DES,which reduces the overall density of electrolytes.The density of the $\mathrm { F e C l } _ { 2 } .$ based DES decreases significantly from $1 . 1 1 \ \mathrm { g \ c m ^ { - 3 } }$ at $0 \ \mathrm { w t } \%$ water content to $1 . 0 8 \ : \mathrm { g c m } ^ { - 3 }$ at $3 0 \mathrm { w t } \%$ water content. Fig.1(c) shows that the electrical conductivity of all DES electrolytes increases significantly as water content rises.For the $\mathrm { F e C l } _ { 2 }$ -based DES,conductivity rises from $8 . 1 3 \mathrm { m } S \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ at $0 ~ \mathrm { w t } \%$ water content to $2 9 . 7 \mathrm { m S } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ at $3 0 \mathrm { w t } \%$ water content.The main reason for this increase is that water disrupts the strong hydrogen bonds in the DES components,promotes the ionic dissociation of ChCl,which enable the choline cation $( \mathrm { C h } ^ { + } )$ and chloride anion $( \mathbf { C l } ^ { - } )$ to dissociate more readily [35].This increases the concentration and migration rate of freely mobile ions within the electrolyte, thereby enhancing conductivity.Adding water changes the key transport properties of DESs electrolytes.It lowers viscosity and density while boosting electrical conductivity.This trend is seen in all DESs electrolytes with different active species.It shows that water plays a key role in adjusting the transport behavior of DESs electrolytes.These findings give a basic foundation for optimizing water content in DES-based RFBs.

# 3.2.Electrochemical characterization in half-cell test

The incorporation of water into the DES electrolyte markedly enhances its ionic conductivity,an effect attributed to reduced viscosity and the disruption of the hydrogen bond network.This improvement in transport properties is expected to positively influence the charge transfer dynamics at the electrode/electrolyte interface.Consequently, we employed electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) and cyclic voltammetry (CV) to investigate the interfacial electrochemical kinetics of the aqueous DES electrolytes.

EIS is a non-destructive analytical technique used to characterize electrochemical processes of batteries.It also helps identify and quantify the kinetic properties of different impedance sources that affect RFB performance.For DES with different active species (i.e., $\mathrm { F e C l } _ { 2 }$ ${ \mathrm { V C l } } _ { 3 } ,$ TEMPO,and Quinoxaline) and water content,EIS measurements were conducted at open-circuit potential to probe the electrochemical kinetics of electrolytes,as shown in the Nyquist plot (Fig.2). Table S1 presents the values of internal resistance $( R _ { s } )$ and charge transfer impedance $( R _ { c t } )$ for different active species at varying water content.The analysis covered a range of water content and revealed a consistent trend: as water content increased,charge transfer impedance decreased systematically in all electrolytes,especially for charge transfer impedance. Lower charge transfer impedance is conducive to enhancing the electrochemical reaction at the electrode interface and reducing activation polarization losses,which in turn improves the energy efficiency of the

DES-based RFB.These findings provide a fundamental basis for determining the optimal water content in DES electrolytes,and this is critical for rationally optimizing the performance of DES-based RFBs.

To evaluate the electrochemical stability of organic active species, long-term CV tests were performed on TEMPO and Quinoxaline in DES electrolyte,with the results of the lst and $1 0 0 0 \mathrm { { t h } }$ cycles compared. The test results are summarized in Fig. S2 and Table S2.As shown in Table S2, for TEMPO, $I _ { p a }$ decreases slightly from $0 . 5 7 8 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ to $0 . 5 5 7 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ,while $I _ { p c }$ remains essentially unchanged, $\varDelta E$ stays stable at $0 . 2 1 5 \mathrm { V }$ ,indicating excellent redox reversibility and electrochemical stability.For Quinoxaline, $I _ { p a }$ decreases from $0 . 7 2 3 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ to 0.697 $\mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ,and $I _ { p c }$ shifts from $- 1 . 0 2 3 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ to $- 1 . 0 5 5 \mathrm { ~ m A ~ c m } ^ { - 2 } \varDelta E$ value increases slightly from $0 . 7 0 0 \mathrm { V }$ to $0 . 7 4 4 \mathrm { V }$ ,which remains within an acceptable range.These results demonstrate that both TEMPO and Quinoxaline exhibit good electrochemical stability in the half-cell test. However,their suitability as active components for RFBs still needs to be further examined in subsequent full-cell tests.

Charge-discharge cycling efficiency and stability are the key metrics for the practical application of RFBs.The voltage efficiency during charge-discharge cycles directly reflects the retention capability of active species,electrode interface stability,and electrolyte polarization characteristics.Under controlled identical charge-discharge times,the short-term cycling tests were conducted to evaluate the voltage efficiencies of TEMPO-Quinoxaline and TEMPO $\mathrm { \nabla { \cdot } V C l _ { 3 } }$ DES-based RFBs with different added water content, aiming to reveal the role of water content in regulating cycling performance of RFBs.For the TEMPO-Quinoxaline RFBs as shown in Fig.6(a),increasing water content significantly enhances initial voltage efficiency:the T-O Q-O case achieves $7 5 . 6 5 \%$ firstcycle efficiency,while the T-20 Q-2O case reaches $9 3 . 8 4 \%$ in the first cycle.This aligns with the pattern where adding water reduces polarization losses of DES-based RFBs.However, the result exhibits accelerated voltage efficiency decay athigh water content conditions.Although the elevated water content enhances initial reaction efficiency,the cycling stability requires further optimization in conjunction with water content of electrolyte.For the TEMPO- $\mathrm { \nabla { C l _ { 3 } } }$ RFBs as shown in Fig. 6(b), the initial voltage efficiency increases markedly with rising water content: the T-O V-O case reaches $7 3 . 9 2 \%$ ,while T-10V-10 and T-20V-20 cases achieve first-cycle efficiencies of $8 6 . 4 8 \%$ and $8 7 . 7 1 \%$ ，respectively.Furthermore,cycling stability and voltage efficiency have achieved synchronous enhancement at an appropriate water content condition. To understand the deterioration mechanism of cyclic stability for TEMPO-Quinoxaline RFBs under high water content, three DES electrolytes,which have TEMPO with $2 0 ~ \mathrm { w t } \%$ water content, Quinoxaline with $2 0 \mathrm { \Delta w t \% }$ water content and $\mathrm { V C l } _ { 3 }$ with $2 0 \mathrm { ~ w t \% ~ }$ water content respectively,were tested using Raman spectroscopy before and after the reaction as shown in Fig.S5.On the one hand,Fig.7(a) shows that Raman spectra of Quinoxaline before and after the reaction show similar characteristic peak positions.This indicates its main molecular structure remains intact. It can be observed that the characteristic peaks of both active species before and after cyclingalmost completely overlap,indicating that the molecular frameworks of both species remain intact after multiple charge-discharge cycles,without irreversible decomposition or structural collapse.Then, within specific wavenumber ranges,slight changes in peak intensity appear in the spectra after cycling.These changes indicate that TEMPO and $\mathrm { { V C l } _ { 3 } }$ indeed participated in electron transfer during the charge-discharge process,and their internal chemical bonds underwent temporary adjustments.However,since the overall spectra after cycling are highly consistent with the initial spectra, these changes are considered reversible.Collectively,the Raman spectra demonstrate that TEMPO and $\mathrm { V C l } _ { 3 }$ exhibit good structural stability during electrochemical reactions.Allowing them to continue participating in redox reactions over multiple cycles and thus supporting cycling stability.

# 4.Conclusion

In this work,the role of water content in the physicochemical and electrochemical properties of DES electrolytes was systematically investigated to advance their application in RFBs.The introduction of water significantly reduced the viscosity and density of the DES electrolytes while enhancing their ionic conductivity, thereby improving mass transport and mitigating polarization losses.Electrochemical characterization revealed that the optimal water content is speciesdependent: $1 0 ~ \mathrm { w t } \%$ for inorganic active species $( { \mathrm { F e C l } } _ { 2 }$ and ${ \mathrm { V C l } } _ { 3 } .$ and $2 0 \mathrm { w t } \%$ for organic species (TEMPO and Quinoxaline),as determined by cyclic voltammetry and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy.In full-cell tests,hybrid TEMPO- $\mathrm { \nabla { \cdot } V C l _ { 3 } }$ RFB employing DES electrolyte with $1 0 \ \mathrm { w t } \%$ water demonstrated the optimal performance,achieving $9 0 \%$ coulombic efficiency and $7 7 \%$ energy efficiency $( 5 \operatorname { m A } \operatorname { c m } ^ { - 2 } )$ along with stable cycling behavior,that outperforms the cycling stability of allorganic RFB.Raman spectroscopy analysis indicated that the poor cycling stability of the all-organic system under high water content originated from irreversible structural changes in quinoxaline and competing side reactions such as hydrogen evolution.In summary, this study highlights the importance of optimizing water content in DES electrolytes according to the specific redox species involved.While allorganic aqueous DES electrolyte-based RFBs show potential for high power density output,their practical deployment remains constrained by limited cycling stability.Hybrid aqueous DES electrolyte,integrating organic catholytes and inorganic anolytes,represents a more viable route toward efcient and stable DES-based RFBs for practical energy storage applications.
