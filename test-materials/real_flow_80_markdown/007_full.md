# A High-Current, Stable Nonaqueous Organic Redox Flow Battery

ABSTRACT: Nonaqueous redox flow batteries are promising in pursuit of high energy density storage systems owing to the broad voltage windows $\scriptstyle ( > 2 \lor )$ but currently are facing key challenges such as limited cyclability and rate performance. To address these technical hurdles， here we report the nonaqueous organic flow battery chemistry based on $N _ { ☉ }$ -methylphthalimide anolyte and 2,5-di-tert-butyl-1-methoxy-4-[ $\mathbf { { \dot { 2 } } ^ { \prime } }$ -methoxyethoxy]benzene catholyte,which harvests a theoretical cell voltage of $2 . 3 0 \ \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ ，The redox flow chemistry exhibits excellent cycling stability under both cyclic voltammetry and flow cell tests upon repeated cycling. A series of Daramic and Celgard porous separators are evaluated in this organic flow battery, which enable the cells to be operated at greatly improved current densities as high as $\mathbf { 5 0 \ m A ~ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ compared to those of other nonaqueous flow systems. The stable cyclability and highcurrent operations of the organic flow battery system represent significant progress in the development of promising nonaqueous flow batteries.

Nonaqueous flow batteries，a departure from conventional aqueous systems, capitalize on the broader electrochemical window of nonaqueous electrolytes，which offer a promising alternative pathway in pursuit of high energy density.³ -6 Organic electroactive compounds have the advantages of greater diversity，availability，and tunability in chemical structures, resources，and redox potentials and therefore have been investigated in a variety of nonaqueous flow chemistries.7-11 High energy densities of ${ > } 5 0$ Wh $\bar { \mathrm { ~ L ~ } } ^ { - 1 }$ have been achieved in a few nonaqueous flow systems，which exemplifies the great prospects of nonaqueous flow batteries.12-16

However,a number of key challenges still exist. In general, the ionic conductivities of nonaqueous electrolytes are intrinsically lower than those of their aqueous counterparts and become even less favorable at high concentrations of redox materials.Moreover， high-performance membranes for nonaqueous flow batteries have yet to be developed. Ion exchange membranes and ceramic separators usually result in high cell resistance in nonaqueous electrolytes， low cost-effectiveness, and undesirable material-membrane interactions.7,17,18 As a result,current reported nonaqueous flow batteries are often operated at the current density of $< 0 . 5 ~ \mathrm { \ m A } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ Asa comparison，aqueous flow batteries can support current densities $> 1 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m } ^ { - 2 } ,$ 19 more than 2 orders of magnitude higher.Low operational current resulting from low electrolyte conductivity and high membrane resistance has become a major bottleneck of the development of nonaqueous flow batteries. In addition，the irreversible performance degradation has also been recognized as another one of the most critical technical hurdles for nonaqueous flow batteries.20-22 A major cause is the decomposition of redox materials especially in the charged state.Deliberate structural engineering of redox materials has been attempted to improve their chemical stability，which however often involves extensive material synthesis and induces additional materalcost2324Therefore,itisighlydesirableto develop stable nonaqueous flow chemistries with easily accessible redox materials.

In the present work, we demonstrate a nonaqueous organic flow battery employing commercially available $N .$ -methylphthalimide (MePh) as the anode side and readily synthesized 2,5-ditert-butyl-1-methoxy-4-[2'-methoxyethoxy]benzene (DBMMB) as the cathode side redox materials (hence denoted as MePhl DBMMB).The electrochemistry of this flow battery at both the anode and cathode sides is illustrated in Scheme 1.The cell chemistry involves the formation of radical ions for both redox materials.Following the use of porous separators in aqueous flow batteries,25,26 a series of Daramic (a registered trademark of Daramic LLC) and Celgard porous separators were tested to study the effect of key separator parameters (i.e., thickness and pore size) on flow cell performance. Due to the relatively large pore size, mixed-reactant electrolytes were used at both positive and negative half-cells to reduce the concentration gradients and mitigate the crossover of redox materials.27,28

Cyclicvoltammetry (CV） was recorded with repeated potential sweeping to identify the redox potentials and evaluate the electrochemical stability. Figure la shows the CV curves of a mixed-reactant electrolyte containing a 1:1 molar ratio of MePh and DBMMB at $0 . 1 ~ \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ concentrations.Both redox materials exhibit well-defined redox peaks，and their redox potentials can be calculated by averaging the oxidation and reduction peak potentials.With redox potentials of $- 1 . 7 9 \mathrm { V }$ for MePh and $0 . 5 1 ~ \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ for DBMMB versus $\mathrm { { A g / A g ^ { + } } }$ ，the MePhl DBMMB flow chemistry exhibits a cell voltage of $2 . 3 0 \mathrm { V }$ The single redox couple of the electrolytes containing only one redoxmaterial indicatesclean electrochemistry for bothof them in the full potential range (Figure $^ { \mathrm { 1 b , c } }$ ).Although the MePh has two redox pairs, inclusion of the more negative redox pair in the flow cell chemistry causes degradation to both redox materials under CV conditions (Figure S1 in the Supporting Information). Moreover, the CV curves during repeated scans (100 cycles）overlap with each other almost completely, suggesting high chemical and electrochemical stabilities of both redox materials under CV conditions.In addition,both redox materials exhibit good solubilities in the DME solvent,with ${ \sim } 0 . 7 \mathrm { M }$ for the MePh and high miscibility for the DBMMB(a liquid at room temperature).

The voltage curves of flow cells using these porous separators all display well-defined single plateaus (Figure ${ \tt S 2 a { - } e }$ in the Supporting Information)，and no obvious changes to these seperators were observed visually after cell cycling, indicating negligible redox material-separator interactions.Because the three Daramic separators have the same pore structures,the thickness plays a significant role in controlling both cell resistance and redox material crossover. When the thickness decreases from 800 to $1 7 5 \ \mu \mathrm { m } ,$ the flow cell ASR decreases from 18 to $3 . 8 ~ \Omega ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ (Figure 3a),which is reflected by greatly improved voltage efficiencies (VEs） of corresponding flow cells (Figure 3b).Meanwhile, thinner separators yield more material crossover and self-discharge reactions,as demonstrated by the lower Coulombic efficiencies (CEs) at the same tested currents (Figure 3c). Similarly, due to a significantly reduced thickness, both Celgard separators produce lower CEs than Daramic separators.For the Daramic separators, the thickness seems to have more impact on the VE than on the CE.Therefore, thinner separators (e.g.，Daramic-175）afford better rate capability for the flow cells,that is,higher energy efficiencies (EEs，see Figure 3d） and redox material utilization ratios (Figure S2f in the Supporting Information) especially at current densities $> 1 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ .On the other hand, the pore size of porous separators is another important parameter that dominates cell performance.It is expected that smaller separator pores yield less redox material crossover but higher membrane resistance.For example,even 7-fold thinner than Daramic-175,the much smaller pore size $( 2 8 ~ \mathrm { n m } )$ ofCelgard 2325 still leads to a slightly higher cell ASR $( 5 . 1 \ \Omega \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 } )$ and lower VEs at the same tested currents (Figure 3a,c). To summarize,there indeed exists a trade-off in the thickness and pore size of porous separators in order to achieve optimal EE and material utilization. Thin separators with large pores are generally favored for high current densities because of low membrane resistance,and vice versa.Among these separators, Daramic-175 almost always leads to the highest EEs and is especially advantageous for high-current operations. For example, the flow cell using this separator can operate at currents as high as $5 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m } ^ { - 2 }$ while still delivering an EE of $\sim 7 0 \%$ and a material utilization ratio of ${ \sim } 8 0 \%$ (Figures 3d and S1f).Such operational current densities are around 2 orders of magnitude higher than those of many nonaqueous flow batteries developed by other research teams10,37,38 and are comparable to or even better than those for neutral electrolytebased aqueous flow batteries.39,40

The cycling stability is a critically important performance parameter for flow batteries that guarantees the system to deliver consistent, durable energy storage service over extended operations. Capacity loss caused by unbalanced redox material transport can be possibly restored in certain aqueous flow bateries such as all-vanadium through electrolyte remixing maintenance or pressure regulation.4 However， performance degradation originating from irreversible decomposition of redox species is more detrimental because of the unrecoverable capacity decay leading to shortened cycle life of the flow battery system.This case is commonly observed in nonaqueous flow batteries because of the chemically active organic redox species. In this regard,we demonstrate that the MePhlDBMMB system exhibits excellent cycling stability even under battery-relevant conditions.As the redox concentration increases, the viscosity and ionic resistance of the electrolytes increase,which leads to higher cell polarization.This explains the lower VEs of the 0.3 M MePhlDBMMB flow cell than that of the $0 . 1 \mathrm { { M } }$ cell with the same Daramic-175 separator at all tested currents,as shown in Figure 4a. Also shown are the lower CEs due to longer charge/ discharge durations and more self-discharge. However, the EE of the $0 . 3 ~ \mathrm { M }$ flow cell still remains ${ > } 6 0 \%$ even at a current as high as $5 0 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ . Figure 4b depicts the cycling capacity and efficiency of the $0 . 3 \ \mathrm { \dot { M } }$ MePhlDBMMB flow cell using the Daramic-175 separator at $3 5 \mathrm { \ : \ : \ m A \ : \ : \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ The flow cell maintained constant capacities and efficiencies (CE $9 0 \%$ ，VE $7 7 \%$ and EE $6 9 \%$ ）throughout the S0 cycles.The average charge and discharge capacities reached 85 and $7 7 \%$ of the theoretical capacity,indicatinga high redox material utilization ratio. Capacity retention was also acquired in the $0 . 1 \mathrm { ~ M ~ M e P h } |$ DBMMB flow cell for 100 cycles with the Daramic-800 separator (Figure S3 in the Supporting Information). Stable cyclings at two different redox material concentrations provide strong evidence for the excellent stability of the MePhlDBMMB system. Such an impressive capacity retention greatly exceeds other reported nonaqueous flow batteries that showed continuous capacity fading over even shorter cycling.20-22 Notably, the performance parameters achieved in this system are even comparable to the recently reported organics-based aqueous flow batteries (Table S1 in the Supporting Information), in terms of theoretical energy density, operating currentdensieccaciee Although longer cycling may need to be demonstrated,this MePhlDBMMB flow system represents a significant advance in nonaqueous flow battery development.

The good cycling stability of the MePhlDBMMB flow cells is closely associated with the high chemical stability of both radical ion species, especially the $\mathrm { M e P h } ^ { \bullet - }$ radical anion, which essentially arises from a combination of environmental and structural factors. This argument has been confirmed in our previous study of another all-organic nonaqueous flow batery chemistryFirst,theDMEsolventandLiTFSIsalt structurally contain no electron-deficient functionalities and thus are less susceptible to nucleaphilic attacks from the $\mathrm { M e P h } ^ { \bullet - }$ radical anion than other solvents such as nitriles and carbonates and salts such as tetrafluoroborates. Figure Sa shows possible parasitic side reactions of the $\mathrm { { M e P h } ^ { \bullet ^ { - } } }$ with other solvents and salts. This is supported by the comparison between the cycling capacities in the 0.1 M MePhlDBMMB flow cels employing a variety of solvents and salts, shown in Figure Sb.In contrast to LiTFSI/DME that afforded constant capacity，use of acetonitrile (MeCN),propylene carbonate (PC),or tetraethylammonium tetrafluoroborate $\left( \mathrm { T E A B F _ { 4 } } \right)$ ）all led to substantial capacity fading. Second, the $\mathrm { M e P h } ^ { \bullet - }$ structurally benefits from high canonical resonance delocalizing the spin and charge from a single carbonyl bond into the fused bicyclic aromatic ring and the other carbonyl bond. Also,the electron-withdrawing imide $( - \mathbf { C } ( = 0 ) - \mathbf { N } - \mathbf { C } ( = 0 ) - )$ group decreases the electron density，thus offering additional stabilization．For the DBMMB\*+，in addition to resonance-induced stabilization, the close proximity of the two bulky t-butyl groups provides good shielding to the unpaired electron and offers strong steric protection against incoming attacking species. These factors, coupled with two electron-donating alkoxyl substituents decreasing the charge density， function synergistically to stabilize the DBMMB+.

In conclusion, we have developed an organic MePhlDBMMB nonaqueous flow battery chemistry that has a cell voltage of ${ \sim } 2 . 3 \ \mathrm { V } .$ RDE-based LSV studies revealed high diffusivity and fast kinetics for both organic compounds that are desired for promising redox candidates. Because of the favorable combination of pore structures and membrane thickness, selected Daramic and Celgard porous separators produced significantly low membrane resistance and flow cell ASRs of ${ < } 5$ $\bar { \Omega _ { \mathrm { ~ } } } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ .This benefit has led to high flow cell efficiency and redox material utilization $\left( { > } 7 0 \% \right)$ ，especially in high-current operations (e.g., $5 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ ).In addition, these separators are highly cost-effective $\left( < \$ 10\ : \ : \mathrm { ~ m ~ } ^ { - 2 } \right)$ ）compared to expensive perfluorinated ion exchange membranes such as Nafion ( $\cdot >$ $\bar { \$ 500 } \ m ^ { - 2 } ) , ^ { 4 4 , 45 }$ which leads tosignificant reduction in the membrane contribution to the overall system cost. More importantly，the MePhlDBMMB flow cells demonstrated excellent cycling stability both under CV and relevant flow cell test conditions. Constant capacity and efficiency were maintained for 50 galvanostatic charge/discharge cycles at 0.3 M redox concentrations. Flow cell cycling with a series of solvents and salts indicates that the chemical compatibility between the charged species (organic radical ions） and the supporting electrolyte may dominate the cycling stability of MePhlDBMMB flow cells. The organic redox materials may also harvest good chemical durability from their structural characteristics,such as electron delocalization, steric protection, and electron-tuning functional groups.

Indeed, the current flow battery is still facing challenges such as limited energy density compared to traditional aqueous systems (e.g.,all-vanadium),redox material crossover,and high electrolyte resistivity. In fact, these are general concerns for nonaqueous flow batteries.More engineering work is needed for future performance improvements to fully demonstrate the high potential of nonaqueous systems. For example,molecular engineering could introduce quaternary ammonium or oligo-(glycol ether） functional groups to the MePh to increase its solubility.Highly selective membranes that minimize redox material crossover need to be developed to eliminate use of mixed-reactant electrolytes and increase effective redox concentrations.The flow cell design could be further optimized to mitigate the elevated electrolyte viscosity at high redox concentrations.With these implemented，nonaqueous flow batteries are expected to become more competitive for gridattached energy storage applications.
