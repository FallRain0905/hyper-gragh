# ABSTRACT

The transition to renewable energy sources necesitates efficient energy storage solutions,driving research into redox flow batteries (RFBs).This review examines recent advancements in improving vanadium-based RFBs, focusing primarily on the electrolytes,electrodes,and membrane modifications.Innovations in electrolytes such as incorporating nanofluids,ionic liquids,and additives,significantly enhanced the overallelectrochemical characteristics of the battery.Nanofluids like reduced graphene oxide (rGO)and carbon nanotubes (CNTs) improved the electrode kinetics and energy density,while ionic liquids enhanced the solubilityof vanadium salts.In addition to these,additives such as sodium phosphate and chloride ions enhanced the capacity retention of VRFBs and redox couples'reactivity.Additionally,the membrane modifications were primarily aimed to enhance the ion selectivity and resistance to vanadium ion transport,thereby reducing the self-discharge of the battery.A variety of novel membranes prepared by the incorporation of polymers or ionic liquids showcased a significant improvement in ion selectivityand battery performance compared to conventional Nafion membranes which are explored in detail. These modifications to the existing redox flow battery technology ofer a promising alternative to the traditional ones,paving the path for improved performance and widespread adoption of RFB's technology in large-scale grid-based energy storage solutions.

# 1．Introduction

As the world accelerates its transition towards renewable energy sources,driven by the imperative to mitigate climate change and the gradual depletion of fossil fuel reserves,the need for efficient energy storage solutions becomes increasingly urgent. The sporadic nature of the renewable energy sources underscores the necessity for reliable storage systems to ensure a smooth,continuous,and an uninterrupted power supply.To achieve this,various energy storage devices (ESDs) can be utilized such as supercapacitors,aqueous batteries,and metal-ion batteries [1-5]. Although they exhibit superior power density and optimum energy density,they have their limitations in terms of energy storage capacity.This aspect restricts their suitability for large-scale grid-based energy storage applications.In contrast to these ESDs, emerging technologies like flow batteries offer promising alternatives due to their enhanced energy storage capacity along with the superior scalability of devices.

Prior to delving deeper into the modifications of the various components in the redox flow batteries (RFBs),it is crucial to understand their fundamental components and working principles.Typically,a RFB comprises two tankers consisting of liquid electrolytes which are referred to asanolyte and catholyte,and theyplaya pivotal role in storing and releasing energy during the chargingand discharging cycles of the battery by exhibiting various oxidation states.The volume of the liquid electrolyte in the tankers in turn defines the energy storage capacity of the RFB.The pumped liquid electrolyte gets soaked onto a porous carbon felt,and undergoes reversible redox reactions while being in the contact with the graphite plates,through which the electrons are transferred via the external circuit.Apart from the electrodes and electrolytes,the cell stack consists of a membrane that separates the anolyte and catholyte compartments within the stack.This membrane facilitates the transfer of either cations or anions between the electrolyte solutions while preventing their mixing i.e.,cross-contamination, thus maintaining the integrity of the redox reactions,and inhibiting the self-discharge of RFBs.

To comprise,the overall operation of RFBs involves a controlled flow of electrolytes through the cell stack,wherein electrochemical reactions occurs at the electrodes,while typically the proton transfer occurs through a cation-exchange membrane (Nafion),as showcased in Fig.1. This setup enables the storage and release of electrochemical energy in a flexible and scalable manner,making RFBs promising candidates for grid-scale energy storage applications.

However,due to the low dissociation potential of water $( 1 . 2 3 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ under standard conditions),water-based solvents cannot be stably utilized at cell voltages beyond ${ \sim } 1 . 5 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ ,as this leads to water electrolysis and gas evolution.This intrinsic limitation of water content restricts the achievable electrochemical stability window,thereby motivating the exploration of alternative non-aqueous solvents that avoid water dissociation [7]. The reactions occurring at the electrodes during the charge-discharge processes,include the generation of hydrogen and oxygen gases which can negatively impact the performance of the battery.These reactions are stated below,

$2 \mathrm { H } ^ { + } + 2 \mathrm { e } ^ { - }  \mathrm { H } _ { 2 }$ (HER, Cathode -0.00 V vs SHE).

and

$$
2 \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }  \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } + 4 \mathrm { H } ^ { + } + 4 \mathrm { e } ^ { - } ( \mathrm { O E R } , \mathrm { A n o d e } - 1 . 2 3 \mathrm { V } \mathrm { v s } \mathrm { S H E } ) .
$$

Furthermore,ina RFB,various redox couples,electrolytes,electrodes,and membranes can be utilized for the purpose of storing,and harnessing the energy，which are explored at a great length in this article.Prior to exploring the influence of individual components on the electrochemical metrics of RFBs,this section dwells into the evolution leading to the innovation of the state of the art,vanadium redox flow battery (VRFB).

In the late 197Os,Fe-Cr based RFB [8] was developed by NASA, which paved a path for the research and development of current RFBs. The iron-chromium RFB (ICRFB) stands out as one of the earliest examples of a RFB.Its design incorporates low-cost and readily available materials such as chromium and iron chlorides as the redox-active components,making ita highly cost-effective energy storage solution. The operation of an ICRFB generates a standard voltage of approximately $1 . 1 8 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ ,achieved through a series of redox reactions between chromium and iron.While specific reactions may vary depending on the exact composition of the electrolyte,a generalized representation of the redox processes occurring in an ICRFB can be outlined as follows:

$$
\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + }  \mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + } + \mathbf { e } ^ { - } , \mathbf { E } ^ { 0 } = 0 . 7 7 \mathrm { V } \mathrm { v s } \mathrm { S H E } ,
$$

and

The negative electrolyte,or anolyte,of the Fe-Cr RFB consists of chromous $( \mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } )$ and chromic $( \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + } )$ ions with a standard reduction potential of $- 0 . 4 1 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ In contrast,the positive electrolyte,or catholyte,

$$
\mathrm { E ^ { O C P } = E ^ { P O S } - E ^ { N E G } = 0 . 7 7 - ( - 0 . 4 1 ) = 1 . 1 8 ~ V } .

However,in addition to its relatively low OCP,a significant issue arises from the cross-contamination of the positive and negative electrolytes within the cell stacks.To address this issue and enhance the OCP of the flow battery,researchers turned their attention to the Brpolysulphide redox couple.

In the late 9Os,Br-polysulphide RFB was developed by utilizing sodium bromide,and sodium polysulphide as the positive,and negative electrolytes respectively.Due to their superior solubility in aqueous electrolytes,the volume of the catholyte and anolyte tanks required to store energy was reduced compared to the Fe-Cr flow battery.

The reactions occurring at the positive and negative electrodes are as follows:

$$
3 \mathrm { B r ^ { - } }  \mathrm { B r _ { 3 } ^ { - } } + 2 \mathrm { e ^ { - } } , \mathrm { E ^ { 0 } } = 1 . 0 9 \mathrm { V } \mathrm { v s } \mathrm { S H E } ,
$$

and

$$
2 \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } ^ { 2 - }  \mathrm { S } _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - } + 2 \mathrm { e } ^ { - } , \mathrm { E } ^ { 0 } = - 0 . 2 6 5 \mathrm { V } \mathrm { v s } \mathrm { S H E } .
$$

These reactions involve the conversion between different oxidation states of bromine and sulphur species,facilitating energy storage and release within the Br-polysulphide RFB system.

At the positive electrode,the bromine ions combine to form tribromide,and at the negative electrode,the polysulfide to sulfide shuttling takes place as illustrated in the above-mentioned equations. Furthermore,the overall open circuit potential difference across the flow battery corresponds to $1 . 0 9 - \left( - 0 . 2 6 5 \right) = 1 . 3 5 \ : \mathrm { V } .$

However,despite an increase in the OCP when compared to the Fe-Cr RFB,there is a possibility of cross-contamination amongst the positive and negative electrolytes resulting ina decrease in the battery's state of charge (SOC). In order to eliminate the cross-contamination,and to retain the capacity of RFB for numerous cycles,a single element exhibiting various oxidation states has been considered to function as a redox active material in the electrolytes.

In this regard,a RFB comprising vanadium in various oxidation states has been developed,wherein,the positive electrolyte consists of vanadium in $+ 4$ and $^ { + 5 }$ oxidation states,and the negative electrolyte comprises $+ 2$ and $\textsuperscript { + 3 }$ oxidation states.Moreover,since it is an allvanadium redox-flow battery (VRFB),there is no room for crosscontamination,unlike the Fe-Cr and Br-polysulphide RFBs.In a VRFB, usually, vanadyl sulfate salt $\mathrm { ( V O S O _ { 4 } ) }$ is dissolved in $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } S O _ { 4 } ,$ and water, which on discharging reacts with the electrodes,releasing electrons through the outer circuit and proton exchange through the cationexchange membrane (e.g. NAFION). The following reactions along with their standard potentials are as follows,

$$
\begin{array} { r } { \mathsf { V } ^ { 2 + }  \mathsf { V } ^ { 3 + } + \mathsf { e } ^ { - } , \mathsf { E } ^ { 0 } = - 0 . 2 6 \mathsf { V } \thinspace \mathrm { v s } \thinspace \mathrm { S H E } , } \end{array}
$$

and

$$
\begin{array} { r } { \mathsf { V O } ^ { 2 + } + \mathsf { H } _ { 2 } \mathsf { O }  \mathsf { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + } + 2 \mathsf { H } ^ { + } + \mathsf { e } ^ { - } , \mathsf { E } ^ { 0 } = 1 . 0 0 \mathsf { V } \mathrm { v s } \mathrm { S H E } . } \end{array}
$$

The OCP of the vanadium redox flow batteries results in $1 - \left( - 0 . 2 6 \right)$ $= 1 . 2 6 \ : \mathrm { V }$ ,and the overall reaction is as follows,

$$
\mathbf { V } \mathbf { O } ^ { 2 + } + \mathbf { H } _ { 2 } \mathbf { O } + \mathbf { V } ^ { 3 + }  \mathbf { V } \mathbf { O } _ { 2 } ^ { + } + \mathbf { V } ^ { 2 + } + 2 \mathbf { H } ^ { + } , \mathbf { E } ^ { \mathrm { O C P } } = 1 . 2 6 \mathbf { V } .
$$

On assuming there are no side reactions,the Nernst equation can be utilized to calculate the voltage of the cell as,

$$
\mathbf { E } = \mathbf { E } ^ { * } + \frac { R T } { F } \mathrm { l n } \left[ \frac { \alpha _ { p r } } { \alpha _ { r e } } \right] ,
$$

where $\alpha _ { \mathrm { p r } }$ and ${ \alpha } _ { \mathrm { r e } }$ represent the activity of products,and reactants during the charge-discharge cycles of the VRFB.However,despite its advantages of restricting the cross-contamination of the liquid electrolyte and retaining the SOC of the battery,along with its efficiency for numerous cycles； the widespread utilization of VRFBs is primarily restricted because of its low energy (the compactness of the energy storage in the liquid electrolyte -Wh/L) and power density (measure of how quick the stored energy can be delivered-W/L),and energy storage capacity (the total energy stored in the liquid electrolyte -Wh) when compared to other electrochemical devices.Overcoming these challenges is crucial forunlocking the full potential of redox flow batteries in large-scale energy storage systems and electric vehicles (EVs).

In efforts to overcome the limitations inherent in RFBs,the studies were focused on various aspects of the battery system,including electrodes,electrolytes,membranes, flow rates,and many more.In the upcoming section,we delve into the impact of different electrolytes on the performance of RFBs,followed by an examination of the influence of membranes.Finally,we conclude by exploring modifications to electrodes aimed at enhancing redox kinetics.Although several reviews have been published on vanadium redox flow batteries,most focus on individual aspects such as electrolyte chemistry,membrane development,or electrode activation.In contrast, this review provides an integrated summary of recent progress across all three critical components, with a particular emphasis on work published in the last two to three years.By systematicallyaddressing these aspects of RFBs,researchers aim to overcome existing limitations and unlock the full potential of this promising energy storage technology fora wide range of applications, from grid-scale energy storage to EVs and beyond.

# 2. Electrolytes

In the current landscape of the evolution offlow battery technology, the development of positive and negative electrolytes play a crucial role. In this regard, the usage of nanofluidic electrolytes,ionic liquids,and the incorporation of various solvents, showcased a promising alternative to the traditional ones by enhancing the active surface area of the electrodes which in turn aids in improving the redox reactions and facilitate anunhindered electron transfer.

Along these lines, Sadegh et al.,[9,1O] developed a RFB comprising reduced graphene oxide (rGO) nanofluidic vanadium electrolyte,and noticed a significant enhancement in the electrochemical characteristics along with a reduction in the electrolyte and charge transfer resistances. To investigate the influence of rGO in the vanadium electrolyte,various compositions of rGO ranging from 0.1 to $0 . 7 \mathrm { w t } \%$ were synthesized,and the GO powders were mixed with the vanadium electrolyte viz. $0 . 2 \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ vanadium in2M $\mathrm { H _ { 2 } S O _ { 4 } }$ ，which are later ultrasonicated at fixed time interval to avoid the precipitation of vanadium ions at a higher temperature.Furthermore,to evaluate the impact of various compositions of rGO on the nanoparticle scattering on electrode surface,SEM and TEM were performed as shown in Fig.2(a),along with the viscosity and electrochemical analysis.It can be depicted from the figure that,on increasing the concentration of rGO from 0.1 to $0 . 7 \mathrm { \ w t \% }$ (b-h),the nanoparticle aggregation，and accumulation increases causing the electrode fibres to become rough which in turn increases the resistance to the charge transfer kinetics.A composition of $0 . 4 \mathrm { \ w t \% }$ provided a uniform distribution of nanoparticles and showcased optimal electrochemical characteristics when compared to the other compositions. Furthermore,apart from the surface aggregation of nanoparticles on the electrodes,the viscosity of the rGO incorporated vanadium electrolyte plays an important role in the flow battery.It can be noticed from Fig.2 (b),on increasing the operation temperature,the viscosity decreases and at a fixed temperature,the viscosity increases along with the composition of rGO in the nanofluid.

On performing the electrochemical analysis on the various compositions of rGO in vanadium electrolytes,the cyclic voltammetry showcased an interesting result suggesting that, the peak current density can be altered with an increase in the rGO composition till $0 . 4 \mathrm { \ w t } \%$ ,and decreased on the further addition as displayed in Fig.2(c),corresponding to the blockage induced to the electrode due to the aggregation of the nanoparticles.Moreover,due to the presence of functional groups on the electrode surface as a result of the incorporation of nanofluids; when compared to the pristine electrode,the redox reactions are enhanced on the modified electrode surface.To conclude with,the incorporation of rGO in the vanadium electrolyte enhanced the current density rates by approximately $2 0 \%$ ,and the charge transfer resistance significantlyreduced byapproximately $9 9 \%$ by improving the diffusion of vanadium ions by $5 0 \%$

In a similar fashion,instead of coating the ionic liquid on the electrode surface,Xipeng et al.,[21,22] synthesized a membrane with the help of ionic liquids for selective proton transfer in a VFRB by mixing poly(oxyphenylene benzimidazole) with 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium tetra-fluoroborate.The increase in ionic liquid content resulted in elevated resistance to vanadium and reduced selective proton conductivity in the membrane,attributed to the Donnan exclusion effect between ionic liquid cations and vanadium ions.This effect restricts vanadium ion mobility near the membrane surface due to electrostatic repulsion,impeding their transport. Along similar lines, Zhang etal.,[24] incorporated chloride ions as an additive in the vanadium electrolyte in a VFRB to enhance the $\mathrm { V } ^ { + 4 } / \mathrm { V } ^ { + 5 }$ redox coupes reactivity which can in turn increase the energy storage capacity. Overall, these findings underscore the potential role of incorporating nanofluids,additives,and solvents to enhance the performance metrics of VFRBs,offering insights into optimizing electrolyte composition for improved energy efficiency and capacity retention in RFB systems.In essence,the incorporation of nanofluids,and ionic liquids play a critical role in tailoring the properties of liquid electrolyte to achieve enhanced battery performance.Nanofluids,consisting of nanoparticles dispersed in the electrolyte,as discussed throughout the section,can enhance the ionic conductivity,thermal conductivity,and mass transport of the electrolyte region, thereby reducing concentration polarization during operation.The increment in the ionic conductivity can be related to a decrease in the solution resistance of the electrolyte,thereby leading to an reduced ohmic drops.Similarly,ionic liquids are known for their wide electrochemical stability window,non-volatility,and high ionic conductivity,help to suppress side reactions,extend the operating voltage range,and improve safety.So,a suitable combination of ionic liquids along with solvents which can reduce the overall viscosity can significantly aid in improving the ionic conductivity while reducing the viscosity of the liquid electrolyte,and thereby maximising the efficiencies of the flow battery.

While such oxygenated groups catalysed the $\mathrm { V } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + }$ redox reaction,an excessive amount was found to impede the $\mathrm { V O ^ { 2 + } / V O _ { 2 } ^ { + } }$ reaction, suggesting that a balance between functionalization and surface roughness is indeed essential. Similar insights were obtained by the same author,Leuaa et al.,[39] where electrochemical impedance spectroscopy was employed to decouple charge-transfer and mass-transfer processes of the $\mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ couple, and the study emphasized that reliable kinetic information can only be obtained under equimolar conditions at equilibrium potential.In line with this,a recent study by Anand Kumar Tripathi et al.,[4O] on the $\mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ reaction highlighted deviations in the charge-transfer coefficient from the expected 0.5 and explained the overpotential dependence of kinetics using Marcus theory.Collectively，these studies [38-41] establish that surface chemistry,charge-transfer processes,and mass-transport limitations must be carefully optimized to achieve high performance in VRFB electrode.

Along the similar lines, Seong etal.,[42] conducted a study aimed at enhancing the performance of CF electrodes in VRFBs by altering their hydrophobic nature to hydrophilic and improving redox kinetics through doping with Boran-functionalized groups.Initially,the wettability of the Boron-functionalized CF electrode was assessed using a water droplet test,revealing a contact angle of $1 2 2 . 1 ^ { \circ }$ for the pristine CF electrode，whereas the Boron-doped CF electrode exhibiteda super-hydrophilicity by rapid waterabsorption.Subsequently,electrochemical tests were conducted on VRFB cells comprising Boron-doped CF electrodes,and the results were compared with cells using pristine CF electrodes.The B-CF-cell demonstrated significantly higher VE of $8 0 . 6 5 \%$ at a current density of $1 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ ,compared to the $6 3 . 4 0 \%$ VE observed for the pristine CF electrode.Moreover,during charge and discharge cycles at a current density of $6 0 \mathrm { \ m A \bullet c m ^ { - 2 } }$ over 100 cycles, the EE of the B-CF-cell was notably enhanced to $8 4 . 3 2 \%$ ,whereas the CF-cell exhibited an EE of $7 3 . 6 2 \%$ .These findings suggest that incorporating Boron into CF electrodes can effectively enhance their performance in VRFBapplications,offering improved voltage and energy efficiencies.

Similarly,Meng-Yue etal.,[43] synthesized an electrode by electrodepositing lead on to the existing graphite electrode,to increase the EE of VRFBs,and evaluated its characteristics in comparison to a pristine electrode.The lead deposited positive electrode displayed an improved redox kinetics of $\mathrm { V O } ^ { + 2 } / \mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ along with an improvement in the charge-discharge kinetics.Furthermore,the study proposed an innovative structure for VRFBs,featuring a sealing flow field plate to prevent electrolyte leakage and reduce resistance during charge-discharge cycles.The electrode is fabricated using a simple electrodeposition method,and its effectiveness is evaluated through cyclic voltammetry, charge-discharge measurements,and morphology analysis using field emission electron scanning microscopy.The results indicated that the electrodeposited-lead electrode significantly lowers peak potential difference by $1 0 3 . 7 \mathrm { m V } _ { \mathrm { 3 } }$ ,and peak current ratio by O.249,when compared to pristine electrodes,showcasing higher activityi.e.,an enhanced redox kinetics at the electrode,as shown in Fig.8.The reduced peak difference indicates a higher reversibility,and activity of the redox couple aiding in a better electron transfer.This improvement is attributed to lead particles adhering to carbon fiber,enhancing electrochemical reaction activity.Furthermore,the charge-discharge measurements demonstrated that the battery with the electrodeposited-lead modified electrode exhibited an improved average discharge capacity and a higher energy efficiency of $8 . 5 \%$ ，when compared to untreated electrodes at $6 0 ~ \mathrm { { m A } }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ .While electrodeposited-lead catalysts offer advantages in catalysing the $\mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ electrochemical reaction, their improvement in electrochemical reaction surface area is limited due to their single-material nature.

Furthermore,to address the cost and complex modification challenges associated with CF/GF electrodes, Zhang etal.,[44] synthesized a composite electrode consisting of melamine co-doped with Nitrogen, Sulphur,and Phosphorus,along with modified Carbon Nanotubes (CNTs).They then compared its electrochemical performance with that of a pristine electrode.The inclusion of CNTs in the composite enhanced conductivity and hydrophilicity,while,according to the DFT,the doped atoms improved catalytic activity by facilitating adsorption and accelerating electron transferat the electrode interface.In VRFB assembly, the modified composite melamine electrode demonstrated promising results,exhibiting an energy efficiency of $8 0 . 1 2 \%$ at $3 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and stable performance over 2Oo cycles.Following 20o cycles,the capacity decay was measured at $3 2 . 1 5 \%$ .These findings indicate that the synthesized composite electrodes [45-47] offer significant potential for improving VRFB performance,with enhanced energy efficiency and cycle stability,thereby addressing key challenges associated with CF/GF electrodes.

Furthermore,due to its poor electrochemical reactivity,and the wettability properties,the pristine carbon felt electrodes have been modified by utilizing various materials by various researchers [48,49]. Of which,Koble,K.et al.,[5O] synthesized a carbon-felt electrode by incorporating poly(o-toluidine） (POT), $\mathrm { F e { - } N { - } C \ + }$ carbonnanotube (CNT),and Vulcan XC 72R.Later,the wettability,and the permeability have been measured,as it greatly influences the saturation of the electrode (the degree to which the porous structure of the electrode is filled with the liquid electrolyte) with the liquid electrolytes thereby reducing the pumping costs involved.Wettability determines how easily the electrolyte can spread and penetrate into the porous structure.When the wettability is high, the electrolyte spontaneously fills the pores through capillary action.This reduces the hydraulic resistance and minimizes the need for high flow rates to drive the electrolyte into the porous electrode.As a result, the pumping power required to circulate electrolyte decreases.In contrast, poor wettability leads to incomplete wetting and higher flow resistance,which increases the pumping costs.Here,the incorporation of these materials on the pristine carbon-felt electrode has been performed with the help of electropolymerization and the impregnation process,which resulted in a successful,and a complete modification of the fibers in the electrode. Of the three modifications, Fe-N-C + CNT showcased an exciting result in the electrolyte saturation, and the wettability properties,whereas,the others displayed a resistance to the flow of liquid electrolyte through the electrodes.

In a similar vein,Q.-C. Jiang etal.,[5l] synthesized a graphite felt electrode by incorporating ultrafine $\mathrm { { \tt S n O } _ { 2 } }$ nanoparticles derived from metal-organic frameworks (MOFs).As compared to MOFs,the incorporation of metal oxide catalysts,often suffer from agglomeration and poor bonding with the electrode materials,yet they are still widely preferred over MOFs for simple electrode modifications due to their low cost,abundance of active reaction sites,and strong resistance to corrosion.However,the incorporation of MOFs to the electrode materials, offer several advantages,including a rich pore structure,enlarged active specific surface area of the electrodes for the redox reactions,and the controllable structure of the morphology.Moreover,by adjusting the amount of MOF precursors,it is quite possible,as mentioned in the literature,to obtain the products with varying microstructures [52-54].

In this regard,an innovative approach ensured uniform distribution of $\mathrm { { s n O } _ { 2 } }$ nanoparticles on the surface of the GF electrode which in turn aided in addressing the common issues like agglomeration and weak bonding of metal oxides on the surface of the GF electrode.Later,several characterization techniques were performed to confirm the stable integration and enhanced properties of the $\mathrm { { \tt S n O } _ { 2 } }$ -modified GF.The modified electrode exhibited a superior electrochemical performance,including higher peak currents,improved redox reaction reversibility,and lower charge transfer resistance.The enhanced hydrophilicity and increased active surface area of the modified GF electrodes led to a better interaction with the liquid electrolyte and a faster redox reaction kinetics. Further,in VRFB performance tests,the $\mathrm { S n O } _ { 2 } / \mathrm { G F }$ electrodes showcased ahigher discharge capacities and energy eficiencies,maintaining stable performance over 2Oo cycles with better energy efficiency retention compared to the unmodified GF electrodes.This study demonstrates that MOF modifications to the electrode material can significantly enhance the electrochemical and operational performance of VRFBs,offering a promising strategy for high-performance battery electrodes.Furthermore,apart from the MOFs,and metal oxide catalysts,various modifications to the electrodes can be performed by incorporating MXENES,as discussed earlier,to enhance its reaction kinetics,which has been extensively studied bya variety of researchers [55-59].

Thus,it can be concluded from that the energy storage capacity, energy density,electrode reaction kinetics,the ability of the liquid electrolyte to dissolve vanadium salts,and membrane modifications to enhance ion conductivity while resisting vanadium ion transport can be significantly improved by altering the component combinations of the materials being incorporated.While some materials added to the electrode,electrolyte,and membranes negatively impact VRFB performance, the majority enhance various efficiencies.Besides incorporating various additives and materials into the VRFB system,the primary area of research remains the electrolyte's ability to dissolve greater amounts of vanadium salts,which still needs significant focus.Additionally, exploring different solvents other than water,due to its limited working voltage window,is a promising direction.

Future research in this field should prioritize improving the solubility of vanadium salts in electrolytes,while enhancing the electrode reaction kinetics by incorporation various nanoparticles which do not result in an agglomeration over the electrode surface,and developing alternative solvents that offer better performance characteristics.In conclusion,by optimizing the material combinations,VRFBs can achieve superior performance,making them more viable for large-scale energy storage applications,possibly EVs in the near future.

# 5．Conclusion

In conclusion,the comprehensive exploration of advancements in vanadium redox flow battery (VFRB) technology elucidates a multifaceted landscape of innovation aimed at overcoming key challenges and enhancing performance metrics.From electrolyte modifications to membrane and electrode enhancements,researchers have pursued diverse strategies to optimize VFRBs for energy storage applications.

The integration of ionic liquids into the electrolytes has proven effective in improving the cell's potential and efficiency.In the similar lines,inorganic additives such as sodium phosphate and chloride ions have demonstrated the ability to enhance thermal stability,capacity retention,and redox couple reactivity,offering valuable insights into electrolyte optimization.

Furthermore,electrode enhancements utilizing materials like r-GO/ Mxene hybrids,MXene/CoO composites,and functionalized groups have significantly improved redox kinetics,conductivity,and catalytic activity,contributing to increased efficiency and stability of VFRBs. Additionally,membrane modifications using long side-chain imidazolium-functionalized poly(vinyl chloride) and sulphonated poly(ether sulfone) with poly(2-ethylaniline) have shown promising results. Similarly,controlled incorporation of ionic liquids into membranes has improved proton conductivity and stability，while also enhancing resistance to vanadium ion crossover.

These findings collectivelyunderscore the dynamic nature of VFRB research,wherein interdisciplinary approaches converge to address critical challenges and unlock the full potential of this technology.As the quest for sustainable energy storage solutions intensifies,the continued advancement of VFRB technology holds immense promise for realizing a more resilient and efficient energy infrastructure.In summary,the amalgamation of diverse methodologies and innovations presented in the various studies signifies a significant step forward in the evolution of VFRBs,offering a pathway towards scalable and sustainable energy storage solutions for the future.
