The vanadium redox flow battery (VRFB),regarded as one of the most promising large-scale energy storage systems,exhibits substantial potential in the domains of renewable energy storage,energy integration,and power peaking.In recent years,there has been increasing concern and interest surrounding VRFB and its key components.Electrolytes,serving as theenergy storage medium,playa key role indetermining the performance andcostofthebatery.Despitea great deal of research and development devoted to vanadium-based electrolytes over the years,the solubility of vanadium and its adaptability to varying temperatures have yet to meet the requirements,and the in-situ monitoring and recovery methods still need to be developed for high energy density,long cycle life,and cost-effective VRFB. This paper provides a review of electrolyte properties, supporting electrolytes,electrolyte additives,synthesis methods,and their impacton battery performance.Moreover,state monitoring and capacity recovery methods are summarized and discussed.This review aims to guide the development,optimization,and application of electrolytes for further improvement of flow batteries.

# 1． Introduction

With increasing energy consumption and limited fossil fuels, renewable energy is considered as one of the most promising solutions to address energy issues [1,2].However,renewable energy faces substantial challenges,particularly its intermittent production and inherent fluctuation [3,4],which pose a significant threat when integrating renewable sources directly into the grid.To mitigate these challenges, energy storage systems (ESS) have been developed to provide storage of electric energy from renewable sources and its on-demand release [3,5]. ESS assumes an important role in maintaining and enhancing grid stability,accommodating surplus energy,and ensuring secure and consistent electrical energy delivery within modern power grids [6-15]. Currently,several ESS have been developed, classified into mechanical, thermal, chemical,and electrochemical technologies [16-19]. Despite this,many existing ESS technologies fall short of meeting the requirements.For instance,compressed air technology has low initial cost and high capacity but has limited flexibility and requires specific geographic conditions [2O].Flywheel technology has a long lifespan but has a risk of mechanical failure and short storage time [21].Moreover, the supercapacitor has short charging time and response time but experiences low energy density, high self-discharge losses,and temperature impact[22,23].In recent years,electrochemical energy storage has attracted more and more attention due to its characteristics of no-terrain restrictions,short response time and construction period.Among them, rechargeable batteries [24-29]，such as lithium-ion batteries,are considered as the most promising candidate [3O,31].Due to relatively high energy density and long cycle life,lithium-ion batteries have been thought to the world-wide batteries,finding applications in mobile phones,cameras,laptops,and electric vehicles.However,the inherent security issues limit the long-term and large-scale application [3,32].

To meet the demands for large-scale energy storage systems,the redox flow battery (RFB) has emerged as a promising candidate,which has the advantages of decoupled energy and power, excellent scalability, flexible operation,long cycle life,high security,and environmentally friendliness,compared to lithium-ion batteries [33-39].RFB can be traced back to 1974 when L.H. Thaller first proposed the concept, consisting of positive and negative half-cells [4O,41]. Each half-cell is equipped with its redox pairs that cyclically transition between oxidation and reduction states,thus facilitating the conversion of electrical and chemical energy [42-44]. The electrochemical reaction can be briefly described by Eq.(1):

$$
A ^ { x } + B ^ { y }  A ^ { x - z } + B ^ { y + z } ,
$$

where A and $B$ are the active ions, $x$ and $y$ are the valences of active ions, and $\mathbf { z }$ is the number of electrons transferred.In recent years,many flow battery systems have emerged,such as Fe-Cr flow battery [45],Zn-Br flow battery [46,47],Zn-I flow battery [47,48], Zn-Ce flow battery [49],and so on.Among them,VRFB stands out as one of the most promising options [5O],which combines all the merits of RFB and has no cross-contamination issue due to its same elements on both sides [51-53].VRFB was first proposed by M. Skyllas-Kazacos at the University of New South Wales in the 1980s [54],and the schematic of VRFB was shown in Fig.1,with the electrochemical reaction described in Eqs. (2) and (3) [55]:

$$
\begin{array} { l } { { V ^ { 3 + } + e ^ { - }  V ^ { 2 + } , } } \\ { { { } } } \\ { { V O _ { 2 } ^ { + } + e ^ { - } + 2 H ^ { + }  V O ^ { 2 + } + H _ { 2 } O , } } \end{array}
$$

In VRFB,the biggest issue that hinders its large-scale commercialization is the high cost. Some important compositions of VRFB including electrodes,bipolar plates,membranes，and electrolytes canall contribute to the overall cost,with electrolytes accounting for a substantial share [56-6O].It is estimated that the electrolyte cost can account for $5 0 ~ \%$ of the entire system [61].Currently,the capital cost for VRFB ranges $7 1 3 { - } 1 3 5 9 \ \ S / \mathrm { k W h }$ ，whereas the cost of pumped hydro storage and compressed-air energy storage are 110-208 and 98-238 $\$ /\mathrm { k W h }$ ，respectively [61,62].Therefore,to improve the commercial competitiveness of VRFB,there is an urgent need to reduce its cost, especially electrolyte cost which can be further categorized into manufacturing and maintenance costs.Manufacturing costs include raw material expenses,supporting electrolyte costs,and resources consumed during the preparation process,including purification and electrolysis [63,1].Due to the low solubility of vanadium ions,and short temperature window [1,54],additives are an extra needed but increase the cost. Maintenance costs mainly depend on the actual performance and operational state of VRFB,which are primarily the additional costs associated with monitoring and recovering the condition of the electrolyte aftera long period of operation.Duringa long cycle,electrolyte state needs to be accessed to determine the extent of electrolyte imbalance,thus requiring the monitoring equipment. Additionally,the recovery methods are also important for high-capacity retention and long cycle life,therefore increasing the cost of electrolyte.Hence,there is a need to study the characteristics and requirements of the electrolyte to optimize its design and enhance its performance, thus reducing the contribution to the overall battery cost.

In recent years,the investigation and optimization of electrolytes in VRFB is continuouslya hot topic and several reviews have discussed some important aspects of it.For example, Skyllas-Kazacos et al.[64,65] introduced additives and physical characteristics of the electrolyte under different states.Du et al.[66] focused on the mixed acid supporting electrolyte of VRFB,including $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } S 0 _ { 4 }$ -HCl, $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } S 0 _ { 4 } – \mathrm { C H } _ { 3 } S 0 _ { 3 } ,$ and $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } S \mathrm { O } _ { 4 } { \cdot } \mathrm { H } _ { 3 } \mathrm { P O } _ { 4 }$ .Xiao et al.[67] studied the physicochemical and electrochemical properties of vanadium electrolytes at different temperatures.Nolte and Volodin etal.[68] summarized the currently available approaches for monitoring the state of electrolyte and discussed how to realize capacity auto-rebalance via battery management systems.Jirabovornwisut et al. [69] reviewed the causes,effects,measurements, and regeneration methods of the imbalanced electrolyte.Despite the progress,previous works have mainly focused on specific aspects of electrolytes while ignoring their interrelationships.However,as a whole,the parameters and properties of electrolytes are an indivisible unit that together influences the performance and reliability of VRFB. When investigating the electrolyte composition，physicochemical properties such as viscosity should be considered together to achieve stable and high-performance electrolytes.In turn，the optimized composition and properties will affect the long-cycle performance of the battery,thus encouraging the study of electrolyte monitoring,deterioration mechanisms,and recovery methods.Therefore,the investigation and optimization of the electrolytes should be carried out as a whole, avoiding a focus on only one specific aspect, thus better understanding and designing the future electrolyte of VRFB.

In this paper,some vital aspects such as physical properties, compositions,charge-discharge performance,monitoring，and recovery methods have been taken into consideration which provide an overview of vanadium electrolytes.The objective of this review is to provide a detailed discussion on why electrolyte properties are vital, how electrolyte compositions can be rationally designed,and what methods can be used to monitor and recover electrolytes.Firstly, the requirements of electrolytes are summarized theoretically.To better understand the electrolytes,the properties of electrolytes including ion conductivity, viscosity,and thermal stability are analyzed.The supporting electrolytes,additives,and synthesis methods are following summarized and discussed in detail. # 2.Requirements of electrolytes

In a flow battery,the electrolytes serve as the working solution carrying redox active substances,some vital parameters such as open circuit voltage (OCV),conductivity,viscosity,concentration,etc.will have great impacts on the battery.A proper understanding of these crucial parameters can provide more in-depth guidance on electrolyte requirements,gaining broad insight into electrolyte improvement and optimization.Here are some vital descriptions that highlight the vital role of certain parameters:

Firstly,when researchinga battery,the battery potential is a crucial parameter. In VRFB,the potential of positive and negative can be described by the Nernst equation [70,71]:

$$
E _ { p } = E _ { p } ^ { 0 } - \frac { R T } { n F } \mathrm { l n } \frac { c _ { V O ^ { 2 + } } } { c _ { V O _ { 2 } ^ { + } } c _ { H ^ { + } } ^ { 2 } } ,
$$

$$
E _ { n } = E _ { n } ^ { 0 } - \frac { R T } { n F } \mathrm { l n } \frac { c _ { V ^ { 2 + } } } { c _ { V ^ { 3 + } } } ,
$$

Based on the above equations,the potential of VRFB can be shown as:

$$
E = E _ { p } - E _ { n } = \left( E _ { p } ^ { 0 } - E _ { n } ^ { 0 } \right) + \frac { R T } { n F } \mathrm { l n } \frac { c _ { V ^ { 2 + } } c _ { V O _ { 2 } ^ { + } } c _ { H ^ { + } } ^ { 2 } } { c _ { V ^ { 3 + } } c _ { V O ^ { 2 + } } } ,
$$

where $E ^ { 0 }$ is the standard reduction potential, $R$ is the gas constant, $T$ is the ambient temperature, $F$ is the Faraday's constant,and $c$ is the concentration of ions in the electrolytes.Nevertheless,Donnan potential,determined by the difference in proton concentrations on both sides,is overlooked in Eq.(6).If the positive proton concentration is not consistent with that of the negative side,Donnan potential cannot be ignored.In this context, the Donnan potential can be described as [70]:

$$
E _ { m } = \frac { R T } { F } \mathrm { l n } \frac { c _ { H ^ { + } } ^ { + } } { c _ { H ^ { + } } ^ { - } } ,
$$

Thus, the Nernst equation is changed and shown as:

$$
E = \left( E _ { p } ^ { 0 } - E _ { n } ^ { 0 } \right) + \frac { R T } { n F } \mathrm { l n } \frac { c _ { V ^ { 2 + } } c _ { V O _ { 2 } ^ { + } } c _ { H ^ { + } } ^ { 2 } } { c _ { V ^ { 3 + } } c _ { V O ^ { 2 + } } } \frac { c _ { H ^ { + } } ^ { + } } { c _ { H ^ { + } } ^ { - } } ,
$$

where $n$ is the electron transfer number (For VRFB, $n { = } 1$ ).However, the actual OCV cannot equal with theoretical value due to the overpotential including activation,ohmic,and concentration overpotentials. Ohmic overpotential,comprising electrode,membrane,and electrolyte,is expressed as Eq. (9):

$$
\eta _ { o h m } = i \bigg ( \frac { d _ { e } } { \sigma _ { e } } + \frac { d _ { m e m } } { 2 \sigma _ { m e m } } + \frac { d _ { e l } } { \sigma _ { e l } } \bigg ) ,
$$

where $\eta _ { o h m }$ is ohmic overpotential, $_ i$ is the applied current density, $\sigma$ is the conductivity, $d$ is the thickness.The electrolyte conductivity $\sigma _ { e l }$ is defined as [72]:

$$
\sigma _ { e l } = \frac { F ^ { 2 } } { R T } { \sum _ { i } } _ { } ^ { 2 } D _ { i } ^ { e f f } C _ { i } ,
$$

$$
D ^ { e f f } = D \varepsilon ^ { 1 . 5 } ,
$$

where $\mathfrak { z } _ { i }$ is the vanadium valence, $D _ { i } ^ { e f f }$ is the effective diffusion coefficient of species, $C _ { i }$ is the concentration of species.Furthermore,the activation overpotential $\eta _ { A c t }$ is derived from the activation barrier in the electrochemical reactions [73],while the concentration overpotential $\eta _ { C o n : }$ ，influenced by the difference between the bulk electrolyte and surface concentration on the electrode,is described by Khazaeli [74]. Thus,the battery voltage in actual operating can be illustrated as:[75]

$$
U _ { c e l l } = E \pm ( \eta _ { A c t } + \eta _ { o h m } + \eta _ { C o n } ) ,
$$

From Eqs.(4)-(l2)，the battery potential and polarization are described,from which the type of active substances,the concentration of active ions,and the electrolyte conductivity are critical.The conductivity of electrolytes significantly influences ohmic polarization.Typically,VRFB electrolytes consist of sulfuric acid (supporting electrolyte) and vanadium sulfate [75,76].The protons provided by sulfuric acid are responsible for balancing the internal circuits and determining ionic conductivity.Hence,in the electrolytes,researching and optimizing the electrolyte ionic conductivity and its supporting electrolyte are significant aspects of VRFB.

Additionally,in VRFB,the electrolytes transport from the flow field to the electrode to occur redox reactions, thus the reactions and transportation characteristics of active ions in electrode is vital for electrolyte performance.The velocity of the electrolyte in the electrode can be shown as [77,78]:

$$
\begin{array} { r } { \frac { \rho } { \varepsilon } \left( \overrightarrow { u } _ { e } \cdot \nabla \right) \frac { \overrightarrow { u } _ { e } } { \varepsilon } = - \nabla p + \nabla \cdot \left[ \frac { \overrightarrow { \mu } } { \varepsilon } \left( \nabla \overrightarrow { u } _ { e } + \left( \nabla \overrightarrow { u } _ { e } \right) ^ { T } \right) - \frac { 2 \mu } { 3 \varepsilon } \left( \nabla \overrightarrow { u } _ { e } \right) \right] } \\ { - \frac { \mu } { K } \overrightarrow { u } _ { e } , } \end{array}
$$

where $u _ { e }$ is electrolyte velocity, $\mu$ is electrolyte viscosity, $P$ is pressure, and the $K$ is electrode permeability [77].In addition,in dilute-solution theory,the electrolyte flow through the electrode with flux of active ions described by the Nernst-Planck equation [79,80]:

$$
\overrightarrow { N } _ { i } = \ - D _ { i } ^ { e f f } \nabla c _ { i } - \frac { z _ { i } c _ { i } D _ { i } ^ { e f f } } { R T } F \nabla \oslash _ { s } + \overrightarrow { u } _ { e } c _ { i } ,
$$

where $c _ { i }$ is vanadium ion concentration, $\mathfrak { z } _ { i }$ is vanadium valence, $F$ is Faraday's constant, $\varnothing _ { s }$ is electrolyte ionic potential [81].From the above,it can be concluded that electrolyte viscosity can impact fluid electrolyte flow and its uniformity within the electrode,consequently influencing current density distribution to some extent.Moreover, when electrolyte flows through pipes,system efficiency must calculate the pump power consumption which is represented in pressure drop in pipes [82]:

$$
\begin{array} { l } { \displaystyle P _ { p u m p } = \frac { \Delta P _ { t o t a l } Q } { \alpha } , } \\ { \displaystyle \Delta P _ { p i p e } = \frac { 1 2 8 \mu L _ { m } Q } { \pi D _ { h } ^ { 4 } } , } \end{array}
$$

where $\alpha$ is pump efficiency, $L _ { m }$ is pipe length, $\mu$ is electrolyte viscosity, and $D _ { h }$ is the hydraulic diameter of the flow channel [82].

The electrochemical reaction of vanadium ions can be described by the Butler-Volmer equation. The transfer current density can be described as follows [71,83]:

For positive reaction:

$$
j _ { + } = a F k _ { + } \left( c _ { V ^ { 4 + } } ^ { s } \right) ^ { \alpha _ { 1 , c } } \left( c _ { V ^ { 5 + } } ^ { s } \right) ^ { \alpha _ { 1 , a } } \left[ \exp \left( \frac { \alpha _ { 1 , c } F \eta _ { + } } { R T } \right) - \exp \left( { - \frac { \alpha _ { 1 , a } F \eta _ { + } } { R T } } \right) \right] ,
$$

and the overpotential is $\eta _ { + } = \phi _ { s } - \phi _ { l } - E ^ { + }$

For negative reaction:

$$
j _ { - } = a F k _ { - } \left( c _ { V ^ { 2 + } } ^ { s } \right) ^ { \alpha _ { 1 , c } } \left( c _ { V ^ { 3 + } } ^ { s } \right) ^ { \alpha _ { 1 , a } } \left[ \exp \left( \frac { \alpha _ { 1 , c } F \eta _ { - } } { R T } \right) - \exp \left( { - \frac { \alpha _ { 1 , a } F \eta _ { - } } { R T } } \right) \right] ,
$$

and the overpotential is $\eta _ { - } = \phi _ { s } - \phi _ { l } - E ^ { - }$ . In Eqs. (17) and (18), $a$ is active specific area, $\alpha _ { 1 , c }$ and $\alpha _ { 1 , a }$ are positive and negative charge transfer coefficients. $\phi _ { s }$ and $\phi _ { l }$ are electrode and electrolyte potential. $c _ { i } ^ { s }$ shows vanadium ion concentration on the electrode surface.Additionally, $k _ { + }$ and $k _ { - }$ are rate constants of positive and negative reactions which are vital for the electrochemical reactions of VRFB.Active ions occur electrochemical reactions on the electrode surface and the rate constants $k _ { + }$ and $k _ { - }$ inflect their kinetics on the electrode.In this regard, enlarging $k$ will improve the operating current density of the battery, thereby enhancing the whole performance and power density [84]. Consequently,researchers consistently optimize the physicochemical properties and electrolyte composition to enhance the reactions.For example,adding additives can have diverse effects on electrolytes such as changing vanadium ions’structure,enhancing vanadium ions' adsorption on electrode,inhibiting crossover,and recovering the capacity [64,85]. In summary, the rate constant $k$ is influenced by several factors,and electrolyte as the key composition of VRFB can have a great impact on it. The design of electrolytes critically affects the charge-discharge performance of the battery,which can be attributed to the change of $k$

Furthermore,electrolytes not only transport in electrode butalso can crossover the membrane,causing unbalanced vanadium concentration on both side and subsequent capacity loss.Consequently,the models describing the transport of mass and charge of vanadium become crucial forunderstanding and relieving the capacity loss.When crossover occurs,some self-discharge reactions can happen [82,86]:

$$
\begin{array} { l } { { V ^ { 2 + } + 2 V O _ { 2 } ^ { + } + 2 H ^ { + }  3 V O ^ { 2 + } + H _ { 2 } O , } } \\ { { \ } } \\ { { V ^ { 3 + } + V O _ { 2 } ^ { + }  2 V O ^ { 2 + } , } } \\ { { \ } } \\ { { V O ^ { 2 + } + V ^ { 2 + } + 2 H ^ { + }  2 V ^ { 3 + } + H _ { 2 } O , } } \\ { { \ } } \\ { { V O _ { 2 } ^ { + } + 2 V ^ { 2 + } + 4 H ^ { + }  3 V ^ { 3 + } + 2 H _ { 2 } O , } } \end{array}
$$

Previous research highlights diffusion,migration,and convection as the principal mechanisms governing vanadium crossover in membranes. The Nernst-Planck equation also can show the flux of species at the electrolyte-membrane interface [71]:

$$
\overrightarrow { N } _ { i } = \ - D _ { i } ^ { e f f } \nabla c _ { i } - \frac { z _ { i } c _ { i } D _ { i } ^ { e f f } } { R T } F \nabla \mathcal { O } _ { s } + \overrightarrow { \nu } ^ { \cdot } c _ { i } ,
$$

In this equation,the first term describes the flux of species due to diffusion,which is proportional to the concentration gradient of vanadium ions.The second term describes the migration law of vanadium ions,which is dominated by electric filed and ions concentration.The third term represents the migration flux caused by electrolyte convection，where $\overrightarrow { \nu }$ is electrolyte convection velocity.From these three points,changing the effective diffusion coefficient is the effective method such as redesigning the solvent structure of vanadium.While extensive modeling and simulation have explored the mechanisms of this phenomenon, experimental validation remains crucial.To learn this phenomenon，diverse electrolyte monitoring methods have been developed including electrochemical methods,optical methods,and acoustic methods [68].Capacity fading,a significant consequence of ion crossover,has prompted the development of recovery methods [87,88], which will be discussed later.

Except for vanadium ions,water molecules in electrolytes can crossover the membrane,presenting a primary challenge in VRFB.The solvated structures of vanadium ions enable simultaneous water migration to the other side.The electro-osmotic of ions and pressure between positive and negative cells contribute to water crossover, resulting in electrolyte precipitation and reduced concentration.In this regard,some models that describe water generation,consumption,and transport are developed.The water balance equation is formulated as [71,89]:

$$
\begin{array} { r l } & { \frac { \partial \langle E C _ { w } \rangle } { \partial t } + \nabla \cdot \left( - D _ { w } ^ { \mathrm { e f f } } \nabla C _ { w } + \vec { u } C _ { w } \right) = S _ { w } , } \\ & { S _ { w } ^ { \mathrm { r e g e r } } = \frac { j _ { B F } } { F } , } \\ & { S _ { w } ^ { \mathrm { v o r e } } = \frac { 1 } { \delta _ { 0 g e } } \left( - 6 n _ { v _ { v } ^ { \mathrm { s a v } } } ^ { \mathrm { r e f f } } - 6 n _ { v _ { v } ^ { \mathrm { s a v } } } ^ { \mathrm { r e f f } } \ ~ | ~ 6 n _ { v _ { v } ^ { \mathrm { s a v } } } ^ { \mathrm { r e f f } } \ | ~ 6 n _ { v _ { v } ^ { \mathrm { s a v } } } ^ { \mathrm { r e f } } \right) , } \\ & { S _ { w } ^ { \mathrm { v o r e } } = \frac { 1 } { \delta _ { 0 g e } } \left( - 6 n _ { v _ { v } ^ { \mathrm { s a v } } } ^ { \mathrm { r e f } } - 6 n _ { v _ { v } ^ { \mathrm { s a v } } } ^ { \mathrm { r e f } } + 6 n _ { v _ { v } ^ { \mathrm { s a v } } } ^ { \mathrm { r e f } } + 6 n _ { v _ { v } ^ { \mathrm { s a v } } } ^ { \mathrm { r e f } } \right) , } \\ & { S _ { w } ^ { \mathrm { v o r e } } = - \frac { \nabla \cdot \left( n _ { v _ { v } ^ { \mathrm { s a v } } } ^ { \mathrm { e f f } } \right) } { F } , } \\ & { S _ { w } ^ { \mathrm { r e g e } } = - \frac { \nabla \cdot \left( n _ { v _ { v } ^ { \mathrm { s a v } } } ^ { \mathrm { e f f } } \right) } { F } , } \end{array}
$$

where $\varepsilon$ is the porosity, $D _ { w } ^ { e f f }$ is the effective diffusion coefficient of water molecular.From Eqs.(24)-(28),the first equation describes the diffusion and convection behavior which is dominated by concentration difference. The $\boldsymbol { S _ { w } }$ in Eq.(24) accounts for many contributions which includes the water consumption and production of the redox reaction in the positive electrode $( S _ { w } ^ { E C R } )$ ，the water production of side reactions $( S _ { w } ^ { X O V - } )$ and vanadium ions drag due to crossover $( S _ { w } ^ { X O V + } )$ ), and the water flux arising from electro-osmotic drag of proton $( S _ { w } ^ { E O D } )$ . Based on the above-mentioned equations,it has found that the electro-osmotic was symmetric in the charging-discharging process,while the diffusion in the membrane was irreversible,thus leading imbalance in both electrolytes [71,89].Additionally,these equations can also be used to analyze the water changes in long cycles,providinga theoretical basis for electrolyte imbalance problems.Therefore,itis suggested that water migration should be considered in combination with many factors and more eforts and numerical simulations are still needed to study and alleviate this phenomenon in VRFB.

In summary, from the above-mentioned theoretical model,electrolyte conductivity,concentration,composition，viscosity,etc.are the main points that effect the actual performance of electrolyte.Additionally,vanadium crossover and water transfer are not only vital focus and key issues for simulation but also sticky problems for battery tests, influencing capacity,efficiency,and cycle life.Therefore,to have a deeper study on electrolytes,some actual requirements need to be clear:

The conductivity is determined by the protons concentration that derived from the sulfuric acid,which can impact the polarization and performance of the battery.As illustrated by above-equations, viscosity is also an important parameter that can afect the pump power,mass transfer,and uniform distribution of electrolyte.Thus,the first requirementis to achieve a suitable electrolyte state,including high conductivity and suitable viscosity.

In VRFB,the temperature window is narrow and the“thermal precipitation”of $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ easily happens at elevated temperatures which can be described as [54]:

$$
( V O _ { 2 } ) _ { 2 } S O _ { 4 } + H _ { 2 } O {  } V _ { 2 } O _ { 5 } + H _ { 2 } S O _ { 4 } ,
$$

This reaction can significantly affect battery capacity and performance.Additionally,vanadium ions may crystallize at low temperatures，resulting in electrolyte freezing[9O,91]．Thus,another requirement is to enlarge vanadium temperature adaptation and stability at high concentrations,achieving more steady operation of VRFB [64].

Electrolyte composition is also a major topic,where raw materials, supporting electrolytes,additives,and synthesis method all need to be studied in depth.For example,raw materials such as vanadium oxide sulfate $\mathrm { ( V O S O _ { 4 } ) }$ andvanadiumpentoxide $\left( \mathrm { V } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 5 } \right)$ suffer high costs and complex purification processes [92],thus low-cost material and easy purification methods need to be developed.In electrolyte synthesis processes,chemical reducing agents are always used,resulting in long synthesis time,limited production volumes,and complex operation [1]. In the laboratory,the electrolysis methods are always employed,but sufer from elevated energy consumption and increased costs.Hence, the requirement is to continuously optimize the electrolyte composition, which needs to develop new raw materials,supporting electrolytes, additives,and appropriate synthesis methods,thus achieving low-cost and high-performance electrolytes for VRFB.

Moreover,vanadium ions can cross the membrane to the another side (Eq.(19)-(22)), leading to self-discharging of the battery [1]. Additionally,due to the solvation of vanadium ions $\mathrm { ( [ V ( H _ { 2 } O ) _ { 6 } ] ^ { 2 + } }$ ，[V $\left( \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } \right) _ { 6 } ] ^ { 3 + }$ ， $\mathrm { [ V O ( H _ { 2 } O ) _ { 5 } ] ^ { 2 + } }$ ， $\mathrm { [ V O _ { 2 } ( H _ { 2 } O ) _ { 4 } ] ^ { + } }$ ， $\mathrm { [ V O _ { 2 } ( H _ { 2 } O ) _ { 3 } ] ^ { + } ) }$ and different pressure of both sides [1,93],water molecules can be dragged or transported to the another side along with vanadium ions.Therefore, the requirement is to minimize vanadium crossover and water migration, achieving high-capacity retention and long cycle life,which is also needed to develop electrolyte monitoring and capacity recovery methods.

# 3.Properties of electrolytes

# 3.1． Ionic conductivity

Ionic conductivity significantly impacts battery polarization, particularly in ohmic polarization.The electrolyte typically consists of sulfuric acid and vanadium ions,where the proton from sulfuric acid enhances the ionic conductivity.Both proton concentration and state of charge (SOC) of half-cell electrolyte can impact the electrolyte conductivity.In the solution, ion-pairing reactions can be expressed as follows:

$$
\begin{array} { l } { { V O ^ { 2 + } + S O _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - }  V O ^ { 2 + } \cdots S O _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - } , } } \\ { { { } } } \\ { { V O _ { 2 } ^ { + } + S O _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - }  V O _ { 2 } ^ { + } { \cdots } S O _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - } , } } \end{array}
$$

Meanwhile,the sulfuric acid dissociation can be influenced by ionpairing reactions,which can be described as follows:

$$
H _ { 2 } S O _ { 4 }  H ^ { + } + H S O _ { 4 } ^ { - } \ \mathbf { k } = 2 . 4 \times 1 0 ^ { 6 } ,
$$

$$
H S O _ { 4 } ^ { - }  H ^ { + } + S O _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - } \ \mathbf { k } = 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 2 } ,

# 3.2. Viscosity

Viscosity is one of the most critical characteristics of electrolytes, playing a vital role in ensuring steady operation by efficiently pumping electrolytes into the cell.The electrolyte viscosity could be influenced by various factors such as concentration，temperature,etc.Early investigations were conducted by Rahaman and Skyllas-Kazacos etal.[94] to explore electrolyte viscosity,considering the impact of concentration, temperature,and environment.Their study focused on the viscosity of $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ solutions with varying concentrations of total sulfate/bisulfate (5-7 M),as shown in Fig.4a.As total sulfate/bisulfate concentration increased,the solution viscosity rose,which was more pronounced at higher vanadium concentration.In Fig.4b,viscosity exhibited a linear relationship with $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ concentration within the $2 { - } 3 . 5 \mathrm { M }$ range, beyond which the slope increased exponentially. One reasonable explanation is that the production of chain polyvanadic molecules contributes to the viscosity,while another reason is the transformation of $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ into $\mathrm { V _ { 2 } O _ { 4 } ^ { 2 + } }$ contributes to more combination between $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ and sulfate/bisulfate ions [97],thus increasing the viscosity.Moreover,external environment has a huge impact on viscosity.With increasing temperature,molecular thermal motion intensifies,resulting in a drop in viscosity. The Fig.4c demonstrated a $2 4 \%$ reduction in viscosity when the temperature increases from ${ 2 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } }$ to ${ } ^ { 2 5 } \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } .$ Li et al. [98] measured the ${ \tt V O S O 4 }$ solution viscosity within the range of $0 . 5 { - } 3 . 0 \ \mathrm { m o l / k g }$ at 10-50 ${ } ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ confirming that the viscosity decreased exponentially with increasing temperature.Xiao et al.[67] measured different types of electrolytes at variable temperatures (Fig.4d and $^ { 4 } { \bf g } )$ ,which showed the same trend with the above results and that $\mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + }$ had the highest viscosity due to its lowest diffusion coefficient.

# 3.3. Thermal stability

Among numerous parameters associated with electrolytes,temperature is one of the most critical factors [1].Nearly all physical quantities related to the electrolytes can vary with temperature which means it plays a central role in stabilizing the electrolytes and influencing the batteryperformance [56,1O2].In the early days,Skyllas-Kazacos etal. [103] studied the stability of $\mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ sulphate system at different temperature and SOC.The results are shown in Fig.5a,which demonstrated that in high SOC,the electrolyte was unstable,and the concentration of active ions decreased at high temperature.It could also prove that $\scriptstyle \mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + }$ is more stable than $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ at high temperature. To understand the causing of poor thermal stability of $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ , Vijayakumar et al. [102] used $^ { 1 7 } 0$ Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectroscopy to study the $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ solution, as shown in Fig. 5b. The chemical shift of $^ { 1 7 } 0$ NMR showed an irreversible change in $3 3 5 \mathrm { ~ K ~ }$ (around $6 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ with visible yellowish red particles appearing in the solution,which meant the $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ underwent irreversible structural transition. Based on this phenomenon, the author considered the $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ species existing as hydrated penta co-ordinated vanadate ion which was unstable at elevated temperature and easily transformed to $\mathrm { V } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 5 }$ via a deprotonation process.Except $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ in electrolyte,vanadium ions exhibit four distinct oxidation states,each characterized by different solubility and stability within the solution.In this regard, as shown in Fig. 5d-e, the $\mathrm { V } ^ { 2 + }$ ， $\mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + }$ ,and $\scriptstyle \mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + }$ would crystallize at low temperature,whereas $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ remained stable under this temperature range.Conversely, $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ would undergo the “thermal precipitation’reaction when the temperature exceeded $4 0 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ Forthe above reasons, the temperature window is limited in the range of 10-40 ${ } ^ { \circ } \mathbf { C } ,$ ，with a concentration of vanadium limited to $1 . 5 { - } 2 \textrm { ~ M }$ Skyllas-Kazacos etal.[1o3] recommended a suitable concentration of vanadium at $\mathsf { 1 . 5 ~ M }$ or lower,and that the SOC should be controlled at $6 0 \mathrm { - } 8 0 \%$ when the concentration of ions was higher.Due to the effect of temperature on vanadium ions,the battery performance could also be influenced. Just as shown in Fig. 5c [9O], with temperature increasing, the times of charge-discharge behavior were extended,and the battery overpotential was minimized to some extent,which means the activation of vanadium ions were increased due to the elevated temperature. Therefore,proper selection of battery operating temperature and vanadium ion concentration is one of the keys to achieving highly efficient and long term battery operation.

Moreover, from the point of thermal management requirement, better thermal stability of vanadium ions could reduce maintenance frequency,thus reducing the costs that associated with electrolyte replacement and capacity recovery methods. Zhang et al. [9O] showed that better electrolyte thermal stability not only reduced the capacity decay rate of the battery,but also contributed to battery fficiency, power density,and reduced pumping power losses,leading to simpler VRFB thermal management designs.In sum，investigating and researching vanadium thermal stability is significant in increasing energy density,enhancing electrochemical performance,and reducing maintenance costs.

# 4.1. Active species

In VRFB,the supporting electrolyte has two main functions: increasing electrolyte conductivity and supplying protons to balance charges.Sulfuric acid is commonly used as the supporting electrolyte in VRFB[75,76],and considering solution resistance and viscosity, the concentration is typically limited to the range of $1 . 5 { - } 3 \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ 、Toachieve high electrochemical performance,strong stability,and a wide temperature range,supporting electrolytes need to be researched and developed.For now，numerous supporting electrolytes have been developed for VRFB.In earlier research,PNNL conducted a series of studies into supporting electrolytes of VRFB [1O6-1O8]. # 4.3.Additives

# 4.3.1．Principle of electrolyte additives

Due to the limited solubility of vanadium ions,poor reversibility of kinetics,narrow window of temperature,and possible side reactions, the energy density of VRFB is extremely limited [116].As mentioned,the solubility of $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ can be improved by increasing the concentration of sulfuric acid and decreasing the operating temperature,while the other three ions show the opposite trend [56].Therefore,lots of additives have been developed, which are designed to widen the temperature window, inhibit precipitations,mitigate side reactions,and enhance reaction kinetics [64,117,118].The selection standards for additives of VRFB are as follows:

1）No side effects or reactions with vanadium ions;
2) Low electrolyte production cost;
3) No corrosion to the battery;
4) Stable solubility over a wide temperature range;

# 4.3.2. Stabilizing agents

In VRFB,the “thermal precipitation”occurs when the electrolyte temperature exceeds $4 0 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ while the vanadium ions freezingly precipitate when the temperature is low.To address this challenge,stabilizing agents can be introduced into the electrolyte,which can stabilize vanadium ions,inhibit the formation of precipitation,extend the formation duration,and widen the temperature range.The developed stabilizing agents fall into two main categories: organic additives,and inorganic additives.

Organic additives include organic acids,organic salts,carbohydrates,alcohols,and others.Among the organic acids, Zhang etal.[119] added the complexing agent 1-hydroxyethane-1,1-diphosphonic acid (HEDP) into electrolytes (Fig.7a) which could interact with $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ in a 1:1 binding stoichiometry to inhibit the precipitation and widen the temperature range.Kim et al.[115] used the MSA as an additive,proving that MSA could improve the stability of electrolytes,extend the time of precipitation,and enhance the electrochemical performance.Battery withMSA had higher energy efficiency,discharge capacity,and cycle stability compared to those without it.Wang et al.[12O] introduced various acids,including MSA,boric acid,hydrochloric acid,trifluoroacetic acid,polyacrylic acid,oxalic acid,methacrylic acid,and phosphotungstic acid.CV and EIS were used to research the electrochemical performance,revealing all the above-mentioned additives can improve the electrolyte performance.Furthermore,anthraquinone-2,6-disulfonic acid (AQDS) [121],taurine [122],3-aminopropylphosphonic acid [123],dipicolinic acid [124],quinolinic acid [124],phytic acid [125],and amino acids [l26] were all investigated as stabilizing agents for VRFB electrolyte.Abbas et al.[l27] used histidine in the positive electrolyteatelevated temperatures,finding the electrolyte had higher energy efficiency and capacity retention at $1 5 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - # 4.3.3. Immobilizing agents

Immobilizing agents can make vanadium ions reach higher concentrations without precipitation,which includes the gels,gums,starch, and kaline pectate [16l]．However,although such additives can improve vanadium stability and prevent electrolyte leaks,electrolyte viscosity will be increased,compromising its flow characteristics and electrolyte distribution.Based on that,as flow property is the most important characteristic of VRFB,immobilizing agents still need to be developed further.

# 4.3.4.Kinetics enhancers

For kinetics enhancers, substances are added into the electrolyte to improve the redox reaction, including organic compounds containing functional groups,metal ions,and metal oxides.These substances added to the electrolyte can be classified as deposited or non-deposited during battery operation.For non-deposited substances,Jia etal.[136] added glycerin and n-propyl alcohol to introduce hydroxyl groups,which improved the performance of electrolytes by attributing to more effective adsorption of vanadium ions.Additionally,for deposited substances,it can be electrodeposited on the electrode when charging, which can be viewed as a modification method to increase active sites on the electrode,thereby enhancing electrochemical performance [162, 163].For instance,Jiang and Wei et al. [164,165] added the $\mathbf { B i } ^ { 3 + }$ (Fig. 8a) and $\mathrm { { C u } ^ { 2 + } }$ (Fig.8c) into the electrolyte,respectively. Through electrodeposition methods,metal ions were deposited on the surface of the electrode.From Fig.8b,itcould be indicated that by adding O.001 M $\mathbf { B i } ^ { 3 + }$ into the electrolyte,the TCC (thermal-treated carbon cloth) exhibited higher peak current and reduced peak separation potential, meaning the reaction kinetics could be enhanced due to the catalytic activity of Bi.Additionally, since the redox peak of $\mathrm { B i / B i } ^ { 3 + }$ appeared to the left of $\mathrm { V } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + }$ redox peak, it meant the nanoparticles deposited on the electrode did not dissolve with the reaction, thus continuously catalyzing the redox reaction during battery operation.From Fig.8d,it could also be observed that the redox peak of $\mathrm { { C u / C u ^ { 2 + } } }$ was located between that of $\mathrm { V } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { V O ^ { 2 + } / V O _ { 2 } ^ { + } }$ ,thereby the kinetics of negative reaction could be enhanced by deposited Cu without dissolving in the electrolyte.

In addition to the ions described above, the $\mathsf { S } \mathsf { n } ^ { 2 + }$ was also investigated by Mehboob et al.[166] to be the kinetics enhancers of the electrolyte.In fact,the role of elemental Sn in the electrolyte is quite different from the two ions mentioned above.By CV curves,it could be seen that the tin deposition peak aligned with the $\mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + }$ reduction peak, which represented a phenomenal impact on the reaction kinetics of $\mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { V } ^ { 2 + }$ couple. Through this coupling, the peak separation potential was significantly minimized,and the peak current of reduction peak was also increased,resulting in faster reaction kinetics of negative redox reactions.However, since the oxidation and reduction peaks of $S _ { \mathrm { { n } } / \mathrm { { S n } } } ^ { 2 + }$ were located between the oxidation and reduction peaks of $\mathrm { V } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + }$ ,the deposited Sn would undergo a continuous dissolution and deposition process with the reaction,thus enhancing the oxidation reaction of $\mathrm { V } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + }$ by dissolving Sn and reduction reaction by depositing $\mathsf { S n } ^ { 2 + }$ ,as shown in Fig. 8f.

However,while these materials added in to the electrolyte could enhance electrochemical performance,and improve efficiency and stability of the battery,their inherent instability and cost may pose a challenge to long-term cycle and manufacturing cost of the battery [162].Hence,further optimization and development of such approaches are required such as developing low-cost,high-efficiency enhancers, optimizing the formulation ratios,and making adaptation in large-scale stacks, etc.

# 4.3.5.Electrolyte impurities and reducing agents

For the impurities,to some extent, some ions are introduced to the electrolyte during preparation progress due to raw material impurities. Some impurities can cause negative impacts on VRFB such as $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ .Ding et al.[167] investigated the influence of $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ on the electrolyte,discovering that $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ could decrease electrolyte conductivity and affect the stability of $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ ,but the battery could operate successfully within the temperature range of $^ { - 1 0 }$ to $4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ only the $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ concentration remained below $8 . 0 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \mathrm { M }$ However, not all impurities negatively impact the electrolyte, such as $\mathrm { C u } ^ { 2 + }$ and $\mathrm { M n ^ { 2 + } }$ ,which can enhance electrochemical performance without adverse effects.Song et al.[168] investigated the influence of $\mathrm { M n ^ { 2 + } }$ on VRFB, showing that the capacity decay rate with $\mathrm { M n ^ { 2 + } }$ was $1 1 ~ \%$ after 48 cycles, better than $2 5 \%$ of pristine electrolytes, thereby significantly improving the electrolyte utilization.It was noteworthy that in industrial electrolyte preparation from $\mathrm { V } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 5 }$ or vanadium extract solutions,considerable amounts of impurities were introduced such as $\mathrm { M n ^ { 2 + } }$ ， $\mathrm { N i ^ { 2 + } }$ ， $\mathrm { C u } ^ { 2 + }$ ， $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + }$ [65], which encouraged researchers to study electrolyte impurities combined with electrolyte synthesis methods.By optimizing synthesis approaches and improving the purity, harmful ions in the electrolyte should be minimized greatly. # 4.4.Synthesis methods of electrolyte

The electrolyte of VRFB is typically synthesized using $\mathrm { V O S O } _ { 4 }$ or $\mathrm { V } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 5 }$ ,and the methods can be categorized into electrolysis and chemical reduction. In the laboratory,electrolyte can be synthesized easily by using $\mathrm { V O S O } _ { 4 }$ .The procedure involves dissolving a specific quantity of ${ \tt V O S O 4 }$ in water with a predetermined volume of sulfuric acid in a specific ratio.Subsequently,divide the electrolyte equally and conduct the electrolysis.During this process, $\scriptstyle \mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + }$ is reduced to $\mathrm { V } ^ { 3 \bar { + } }$ and oxidized to $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ . Except electrolysis method,chemical reducing methods are developed to decrease vanadium valence and obtain the electrolyte.Heo et al.[169] compared the effect of Pt/C and PtRu/C catalysts and investigated the effect of formic acid,methanol acid,and oxalic acid, indicating the high-quality $\mathsf { V } ^ { 3 . 5 + }$ electrolyte could be made by utilizing formic acid and $\mathrm { P t / C }$ 、Lee et al.[170] used $\mathrm { V O S O } _ { 4 }$ as raw material, formic acid as a reducing agent,and carbon-supported platinum dendrite (PtD/C)as a catalyst to prepare electrolyte. The reaction rate was improved by $6 3 \%$ compared to the $\mathrm { P t } / \mathrm { C }$ method,with no difference in capacity and energy efficiency with pristine electrolyte.Li etal.[171] added oxalic acid into the positive electrolyte to reduce the $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ produced by electrolysis back to $\scriptstyle \mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + }$ ,with $\mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + }$ in the negative electrolyte. After charging,the SOC of electrolyte was $1 0 0 \%$ with $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + } \mathrm { i n }$ the positive electrolyte and $\mathrm { V } ^ { 2 + }$ in the negative electrolyte.Electrochemical test proved no side effect of oxalic acid on electrolyte.Furthermore, $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 }$ and sulfur as reducing agents have also been investigated for electrolyte synthesis [172,173].Wei etal.[174] used seawater to replace deionized water (DI),which increased ${ \tt V O S O 4 }$ solubility by $1 2 ~ \%$ and reduced overall cost by $3 . 6 \%$ ,indicating the potential of seawater in electrolyte synthesis.

In this regard,the purity of the electrolyte needs to be focused on, ensuring no harmful ions introduced in the electrolyte.During electrolyte optimization, the charge-discharge performance such as columbic efficiency,should also be guaranteed to be comparable with that of the standard electrolyte.

# 5. Influence of electrolytes on battery performance

# 5.1． Charge-discharge performance

During operation, battery performance can be evaluated by chargedischarge performance.As a vital component of VRFB,electrolytes play a key role in determining battery charge-discharge performance, with electrolyte parameters and composition significantly influencing the limited performance of VRFB.In detail, the different flow rates can influence the mass transfer of electrolyte within the electrode,significantly impacting concentration polarization and battery performance. Furthermore,electrolytes containing different supporting electrolyte andadditives can have variousimpacts on charge-discharge performance.

When studying electrolyte flow rates,two points should be considered: low flow rates will be employed when investigating novel channel or electrode structures to assess their potential to enhance mass transfer performance,while high flow rates will be employed to mitigate the effects of concentration polarization when striving to improve battery charge-discharge efficiency.Huang etal.[189] studied the electrolyte flow rate on battery discharge voltage under certain current densities and vanadium concentrations.Fig.1Oa revealed that at high SOC, varying flow rates has no significant effect on discharge voltage. How-ever,as discharge depth increasing, battry voltage reduced due to polarization which demonstrated that higher flow rates yield higher discharge voltages because of the reduced polarization. Fig.1Oc illustrated the overpotential in negative and positive electrode at different flow rates,showing that polarization especially concentration polarization significantly affected voltage at low flow rates,and higher flow rates mitigated this phenomenon, thus reducing battery overpotential. These findings demonstrated that flow rate could greatly affect charge-discharge performance,meaning a higher flow rate would generate higher efficiency.After learning the effect of electrolyte flow rates on battery,researchers desired to optimize flow rates in different situations and enhance battery performance [189-193]. Xiao et al. [191] proposed a novel variable flow rate control strategy based on pump eficiency and established a flow rate limitation region,which allowed system parametersdetermined by operating situation. Compared to constant flow rates,this method resulted in higher battery efficiency- ${ . 3 . 3 4 \% }$ higher in system efficiency and $3 . 8 4 \%$ higher in CE. Taking concentration overpotential and pump losses into account, Tang et al.[19O] studied the flow rate effect on battery efficiency (Fig.10b) in a 40-VRFB cell stack,which demonstrated that variable flow rates could ensure higher energy efficiency and system efficiency on the battery. The charge-discharge curves also revealed (Fig.1Od) that variable flow rate exhibited higher initial charge voltage but longer charge and discharge times and lower voltage at high SOC,which means variable flow rates could effectively mitigate the concentration overpotential, resulting in long duration, high CE,and improved system efficiency. Based on vanadium concentration estimated by a modified OCV cell (Fig.1Of),Jienkulsawad et al.[194] developed an online dynamic optimization approach to adjust flow rate,which results (Fig.1Oe and 10g) demonstrated that the flow rate could be adjusted in real time, thus ensuring the battery to adapt the changes happening during the opera-tion and making it more beneficial in long-term running.

# 5.2. Cycle stability

Asa medium to long-term EES,VRFB holds a promise for grid-side applications,expected to operate over extended periods.Therefore, cycle stability is a critical characteristic of VRFB's suitability in grid integration. In this context,electrolytes must exhibit robust stability, resisting changes in operating conditions,including no precipitation, minimized vanadium crossover and water transfer,and rapid responding to operating conditions.In the meantime,maintaining minimal capacity degradation is essential to maintain high energy levels and energy efficiency. Presently, challenges related to capacity retention and long cycle life still exist.From an electrolyte perspective,cycle stability is mainly influenced by vanadium ions’stability,crossover,and side reactions,which contribute to diverse supporting electrolytes and additives developed.As above-mentioned, forinstance,due to the formation of dinuclear-chloro complexes and vanadium-chlorine complexes [107], the electrolyte based on chloride could achieve only a $0 . 0 6 ~ \%$ degradation in initial capacity over 9O cycles,maintaining coulombic and energy efficiency stable.Zhang et al.[197] continuously optimized chloride ion concentration,revealing that $0 . 0 4 \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ chloride ion could achieve $8 3 . 1 ~ \%$ capacity retention after 160 cycles at $2 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ Additionally,electrolyte-added MSA had $7 4 \ \%$ capacity retention after 50 cycles [115],higher than $4 4 . 3 \%$ of pristine electrolyte.Furthermore, although some acid can have a positive effect on VRFB,some organic and inorganic additives also can enhance the cycle stability of VRFB [132,147,148]. In summary, Tabled 2-6 concluded some added materials and their effect on the cycle stability of VRFB.

It must be noticed that as the electrolyte is strongly acidic [1], some membranes cannot resist the corrosion, leading to vanadium ions crossover and side reactions in the long-term cycles [198-2OO],thus affecting the cycle stability and capacity.Therefore,optimizing the electrolyte composition represents an important part of enhancing the long-cycle performance of VRFB [2O1].In conclusion,cycle stability is an extremely important ability,ensuring normal operation under variable situations and conditions [2O2,2O3].By combining the research of electrolyte components,studying cycle and operating stability,and investigating battery failed mechanisms are the main topics related to the stability of VRFB.

# 6. State monitoring of electrolytes

During the charge-discharge process,the battery state needs to be monitored to gain the information of remaining capacity.The imbalance of both side electrolytes will cause battery capacity decay. Consequently,monitoring the electrolyte state becomes essential to assess the extent of electrolyte imbalance.In the actual experiments,the SOC of both sides are commonly referenced to infer the degree of imbalance that contributes to the development of SOC monitoring methods.As the SOC of one side cannot represent the battery SOC,the methods need to monitor both sides electrolytes independently.Moreover，real-time monitoring of the electrolyte is vital for researchers to study the imbalance mechanism,significantly important in rebalancing and electrolyte recovery.To date,researchers have developed numerous techniques for SOC monitoring，broadly categorized into electrochemical methods,optical methods,acoustic methods,and others.

# 6.1．Electrochemical methods

As the commonly used monitoring methods,the electrochemical methods include coulomb counting, OCV,amperometry,potentiometric titration, etc.

Coulomb counting,also known as the ampere-hour method,is a straightforward,cost-effective,and convenient monitoring method.The SOC can be calculated by the following equation:

$$
\mathit { S O C } ( t ) = \mathit { S O C } _ { 0 } + \frac { 1 } { \mathit { Q } _ { B } } \int _ { t _ { 0 } } ^ { t } \mathop { C E } ( \tau ) i ( \tau ) d \tau ,
$$

Before using this equation,certain parameters must be determined, including the initial SOC $( S O C _ { 0 } )$ ,battery capacity $( Q _ { B } )$ ,and coulombic efficiency (CE). These parameters need to be known continuously during the battery's operation.In this regard,the precision of SOC depends on the accuracy of current sensors and time span.Furthermore,various factors such as the initial SOC,self-discharge of vanadium ions,and the accuracy of CE can affect the result of this method [2O4].After a long time,this method may accumulate errors due to minor inaccuracies in current measurement, background currents,and unforeseen variations [68].Thus,while it may not serve as a standalone method for SOC measurement and electrolyte state monitoring,its short-term accuracy can be valuable for calibrating other methods and providing supplementary assistance to ensure measurement accuracy.Recently,Pavel and Dmitry et al. [2O5] introduced operando coulometric analysis to industrial VRFB (Fig.1lb),revealing battery capacity decay was attributed to an increase in the average oxidation state.

The OCV method, based on the Nernst equation, stands out as one of the most dependable techniques for SOC monitoring.This method needs an additional standard cell to measure the OCV between positive and negative electrolytes and the equation is as follows:

$$
\Delta E = \Bigl ( E _ { p o s } ^ { 0 } - E _ { n e g } ^ { 0 } \Bigr ) + 2 \frac { R T } { Z _ { e F } } \mathrm { l n } \biggl ( \frac { S O C ^ { B } } { 1 - S O C ^ { B } } \biggr ) ,
$$

Using this equation,the SOC can be calculated.However, the result remains applicable only when the positive SOC matches the negative SOC.Due to crossover and side reaction, the capacity of two sides is unequal,causing the calculated SOC less accurate.Furthermore,the reference voltage is required calibration to ensure SOC accuracy.To mitigate reference voltage drift and enhance efficiency,Stolze et al. [206] developed a symmetric OCV cell,making the electrolyte itself provide a stable reference potential for SOC monitoring.The method was validated using TEMPTMA and MV in a flow battery,demonstrating a reduction in reference potential drift.However,the above-mentioned method could only monitor the battery OCV but cannot monitor the half-cell potential to get each side SOC.Ngamsai et al.[2O7] added a middle half-cell between the negative and positive half-cells (Fig.11a), the relation of which potential and SOC of half-cell electrolyte (Fig.11c) could be explained by the Nernst equation.These results could also be used to study the efect of electrolyte imbalance on the energy capacity, thus detecting the level of electrolyte imbalance and capacity loss.

Except for the above methods,amperometry has been developed to monitor the electrolyte states.Based on the mass-transfer-limited amperometry,Stolze et al.[2o8] introduced a simple mathematical framework for SOC monitoring,which was independent of temperature, electrolyte composition,concentration,and calibration.The schematic is shown in Fig.11d.The potentiometric titration method is frequently employed to measure the composite and concentration of active ions in electrolyte.By calculating the amount of redox agent dripped into the solution, electrolyte composition can be determined. Typically,a known concentration of potassium permanganate solution is always used to calculate the concentration of active ions, thus accessing the electrolyte SOC.While this method is accurate,it is time-consuming and doesn't allow for in-situ monitoring,thus always used for validation.Nagesia et al.[2o9] designed a four-pole cell device to quantify electrolyte imbalance level, which was fabricated based two-pole cell and consisted of both outer and inner poles.The outer poles served to delivera constant current into the electrolyte,while the inner poles measured the potential difference.This design mitigated the polarization effect and accurately represented the actual voltage of the electrolyte solution which could be combined with SOC for electrolyte state monitoring. However,electrolyte concentration and temperature are the factors that can impact the result of this method.

To sum up,most of the electrochemical methods developed in the current stage are based on equations as well as additional electrochemical cells,which not only increase the complexity of the battery setup,but also require calibration and cooperation with some other methods.Additionally,due to lack of accuracy,while useful,some methods such as coulomb counting and potentiometric titration method cannot be used alone in monitoring the state of electrolyte but could combine with other methods for calibration and verification [68].In this regard,itis suggested that the electrochemical methods are still needed to optimize to enhance accuracy and device design,and are encouraged to be used in combination with other methods for more precise state monitoring.

# 6.2. Optical methods

Nowadays,various optical methods based on spectroscopy and photometry are widely developed,many of which enable in-situ monitoring.For instance,UV-Vis was widely employed. Skyllas-Kazacos et al. [95]observed varying absorbance in the electrolyte at different SOCs. Specifically,a linear relationship between the absorbance of negative electrolyte and SOC was identified at $7 5 0 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ wavelength,providing a basis for determining imbalance states of the battery (Fig.l2a).However，the absorbance of positive electrolyte was significantly high, featuring strong fluctuations across the UV-Vis spectrum, meaning UV-Vis method unsuitable for positive electrolyte analysis.Tang et al. [210] employed UV-Vis spectrophotometry to assess the SOC of VRFB electrolyte,revealing that only the absorbance of negative electrolyte was linear with SOC at $4 3 3 \mathrm { n m }$ ， $6 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ ,and $7 5 0 \mathrm { n m }$ (Fig.12b). Further investigation suggested that the presence of strongly absorbing species, possibly formed by $\scriptstyle \mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + }$ and $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ ions,deviated the absorbance and contributes to the non-linear SOC phenomenon in positive electrolyte. Shin et al.[211] also employed the UV-Vis method for SOC monitoring and introduced the concept of difference absorbance (Adiff) to monitor the SOC simple (Fig.l2c).However,due to the non-linear relationship at a certain wavelength,this method also cannot be achieved in positive electrolyte.

For in-situ monitoring the SOC of positive electrolytes,researchers have developed amethod that uses the overall spectra shape rather than a single fixed wavelength.Liu etal.[2l2] conducted SOC monitoring of VRFB by utilizing the transmission spectrum of positive electrolyte which addressed the complicated relations between spectrum and SOC. The database of transmission spectra was established by monitoring standard samples.As shown in Fig.12d,by comparing the tested spectra with the database,the electrolyte SOC could be determined. Zhang et al. [213] developed an online electrolyte spectroscopic monitoring system (OESM, shown in Fig.l2e),providing a tool for research on relationships between the spectra and the composition of the electrolyte.By incorporating the intensity-corrected correlation coefficient (ICCF) into this system and employing suitable calibration procedures,the SOC of monitored electrolytes could be calculated.

Pavel etal.[214] proposed a method for determining the SOC based on the UV-Vis spectra deconvolution,which combined the spectra of the electrolyte with the deconvolution procedure,allowing the analysis of more complex ion composition within the electrolyte.Additionally, infrared (IR) optical sensors can also be employed for SOC monitoring. Rudolph et al.[215] presented a high-precision real-time method using an IR source and detector which were based on the IR radiation absorption properties of vanadium ions.Through this approach,the real-time SOC measurement for both electrolytes were achieved, respectively.Recently,as shown in Fig.l2f, Vlasov etal.[216] proposed a new method based on the shift of electrolytes reflective index (RI).A simple sensor for RI measurements was proposed and the correlations between RI and OCV were derived for SOH monitoring,providing the last mean error of $1 . 8 3 ~ \%$ at $1 . 4 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ OCV.Furthermore,Raman spectroscopy,presenting another method,also could be utilized for electrolyte monitoring [217].

# 6.3.Acoustic methods

In addition to the electrochemical and optical methods,acoustic methods have also been investigated.Variations in ultrasound speed in electrolytes can reflect the changes in half-cell SOC.The acoustic method was initially employed in Li-ion and alkaline batteries [218,

219]，with the feature of non-invasive,highly sensitive，and non-polluting,providing a high level of spatial, temporal resolution and practicality [22O]. Chou et al. Although the ultrasound velocity could monitor the SOC of electrolyte,the temperature limited the flexibility (Fig.13d).To address the temperature limitation, Zang et al.[222] developed an online acoustic attenuation coefficient for SOC monitoring which was related to the molecular relaxation processes influenced by active ions and electrolyte concentration.After being validated by the titration method, the results indicated that this approach exhibited temperature insensitivity (Fig.13e) and offered superior accuracy compared with the velocity method. Davies et al.[223] accurately predicted Li-ion battery SOC by using simplified time of flight and total signal amplitude metrics,and a machine learning model was trained within an average error of ${ \sim } 1 ~ \%$ .In VRFB,machine learning models may combine with acoustic approaches to provide more accurate SOC prediction.Recently,Yan et al.[224] designed an ultrasonic and $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 }$ -generated detection device for SOC monitoring,which employed both sound speed and acoustic attenuation coefficient,as presented in Fig.13f.The author proved that variations in both sound speed and acoustic attenuation coeficient were correlated with $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 }$ generation and SOc changes.

# 6.4.Other methods

In addition to the typical three types described above,some other approaches for SOC monitoring have been investigated.Skyllas-kazacos et al.[95] introduced the conductivity measurements to independently measure the electrolyte SOC.In VRFB, the conductivity varies depending on different vanadium ions in the electrolyte,with $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ exhibiting the highest conductivity,owing to its high proton concentration.Due to ion-pairing interactions with $S 0 _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - }$ ,the conductivity of $\scriptstyle \mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + }$ and $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ is higher than that of $\mathrm { V } ^ { 2 + }$ and $\mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + }$ . However,due to the changed composition and temperature-sensitive characteristics,the calibration of conductivity is necessary.In this regard,researchers reproduced an empirical model, indicating that electrolyte conductivity could indicate the SOC directly [96].Nevertheless,as vanadium can crossover between membranes,another complementary method is necessary to combine with conductivity measurements,thus attaining a more precise SOC measurement.

Electrolyte density offers another choice for SOC monitoring.Ressel et al.[225] pioneered the use of temperature-corrected in-situ electrolyte density measurements which had a close linear correlation with SOC,making it viable for monitoring.However,density is also influenced by temperature,electrolyte composition，side reactions,and crossover,thus necessitating recalibration over long cycles.Electrolyte viscosity can also be used for investigating the electrolyte SOC.Li etal. Additionally,Li et al.[226] established an empirical neural network model linking SOC to viscosity and temperature.By introducing Darcy's law into the model, mappings between SOC,pressure,temperature,and flow rate (Fig.14d) were established.This method was validated on a 15-cell stack VRFB system,yielding promising results proving to its feasibility.Moreover,the extended Kalman filter (KF) method and various models have been developed to estimate and monitor the state of electrolyte in VRFB [68,73,227-230]. Bhattacharyya etal.[227]developed a detailed 2D model including main capacity fading mechanisms and a KF-based approach was developed for SOC estimating (Fig.14e). The approach used an autoregressive exogenous model where Akaike information criterion was for model selection,yielding satisfactory estimation for SOC.

# 7. Recovery methods of electrolytes

During the operation of VRFB, several challenges arise, including vanadium crossover,side reactions,and precipitation of $\mathrm { V } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 5 }$ Additionally,water in the electrolyte can cross the membrane with protons and vanadium ions due to osmotic pressure [88,175],causing volume change on both sides and imbalances in vanadium concentration and valence [231].Thus,the SOCs on both sides become mismatched, resulting in capacity degradation.To address these issues,various recovery methods have been developed to restore capacity and prolong the cycle life of VRFB.Currently,these methods can be classified into three categories: physical,chemical,and electrochemical methods. Furthermore,these methods are not interchangeable,meaning the selection of methods depends on the causes of electrolyte imbalance.For example,if capacity decayresults from crossover,it can beaddressed by remixing the electrolyte,while side reactions may necessitate chemical or electrochemical approaches.

# 7.1.Physical methods

Physical methods involve the straightforward remixing of imbalanced electrolyte,facilitating easy recovery of the SOC and capacity. However,the prerequisite of this method is using the same active ions on both sides [68],making it suitable for VRFB systems. Skyllas-Kazacos et al.[96] restored the capacity by remixing two half-cell solutions, achieving a capacity restoration from 300 to $1 5 0 0 \ \mathrm { m A h }$ (initial: 2000 mAh).Nonetheless,the capacity retention rate remained notably low, dropping to $7 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { m A h } }$ after just eight cycles.Luo and Li et al.[231] investigated the reasons for capacity decay over the charge-discharge cycle,identifying imbalanced active species and asymmetrical valence of vanadium ions as the primary causes of capacity fading.This condition was induced by ion transfer,driven by both concentration and potential gradients.Based on this phenomenon,a calculated amount of surplus active ions was physically transported from the positive side to the negative side,leading to capacity recovery (Fig.15a).

Wang et al. [88] adopted a novel electrolyte-reflow method by connecting the positive and negative reservoirs with a connector (Fig.15b) which could make the electrolyte from the positive side automatically flow back to the negative reservoirs.This method effectively maintained the electrolyte volume,mitigated capacity degradation,and extended the cycle life.Bhattarai et al.[232] introduced a hydraulic shunt between two reservoirs (Fig.15c) that achieved automatic rebalancing，showing a relative high-capacity retention and non-decaying efficiency (Fig.15d-e). Similarly, Schofield etal.[233] introduced the shunt concept in VRFB,achieving automatic electrolyte rebalancing.By designing different initial supporting electrolyte concentrations of both sides electrolytes, Shin et al.[2Ol] effectively mitigated the degree of imbalance,which showed a reduction in imbalance from $\pm 5 . 8 0 \ \mathrm { m l }$ to $\pm 0 . 7 \ \mathrm { m l }$ in $5 0 ~ \mathrm { m l }$ electrolyte tank.This concept was validated through 3D numerical simulations.Additionally,Lu et al. [234] increased the initial concentration of positive electrolyte,successfully mitigating capacity decay by enhancing the diffusion from the positive to the negative side.

In summary,physical methods involve automatic or manual remixing of the electrolyte,an external device for volume transfer,and modifications of initial electrolyte concentration.However,repeated mixing of the electrolyte can lead to a change in the average valence state and a decrease in the recovery effect,which limits the degree of capacity recovery and exhibits short cycle lives.Thus,physical methods still need to be further developed and investigated to achieve better recovery performance.

# 7.2.Chemical methods

Except for physical methods,chemical methods have been employed to recover battery capacity. The fundamental concept involves introducing redox agents into the solution to interact with active ions.It is crucial to avoid introducing unrelated ions that could react with active ions.Therefore,suitable substances include methanol,ethanol,oxalic acid,and some organic reducing agents [68,96],whose products are carbon dioxide and water,without side effects on active ions. Skyllas-Kazacos etal.[96] utilized physical methods for electrolyte but had unsatisfactory results (Fig.16a),which suggested that the capacity decay is not controlled only by vanadium crossover but side reactions. Consequently,by adding chemicals to positive half-cell electrolyte, nearly full capacity restorationwasachieved,along witha longer cycle life compared to physical methods (Fig.16b).

The tank used for reduction of positive electrolyte was showed in Fig. 17b, where $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ was reduced to $\scriptstyle \mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + }$ ,balancing the negative ions and recovering battery capacity.However,the reaction rate was slow at room temperature,and Pt-Ru was identified as a catalyst to accelerate the reaction.

Additionally, by combining with methods described previously, the capacity could be recovered better. Roznyatovskaya et al.[238] developed a three-step regeneration procedure.Initially,measuring the ratio of $\mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 \bar { + } }$ ; Subsequently,charging the electrolyte and obtaining $\mathrm { V } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ mixture; Finally, mixing $\mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ with initial electrolyte in a calculated ratio,ensuring complete reaction of all $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ Battery tests showed the electrolyte exhibited outstanding performance, making it a potential candidate alongside standard electrolyte.Li etal. [239] developed a method that combined reflow and online electrolysis techniques (Fig.17c),with increased positive volume reflowing to the negative side and accumulated $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + }$ electrolyzed to $\scriptstyle \mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + }$ .The electrolysis cell was connected to the battery，with positive electrolyte flowing inside,controlled by an online optical detection module.The tests showed the cycle number could reach to 468O after eight online electrolysis without needing electrolyte replacement.Pichugov et al. [87]also employed an electrolysis cell, incorporating with coulometric sensors to apply the required charge for reduction (Fig.17d).The results indicated that capacity could be recovered to the initial level,ensuring long-lasting high battery performance without costly maintenance (Fig. 17e).

In summary,the electrochemical method outperforms the other two approaches, exhibiting significant advantages in capacity recovery and cycle stability.Although it typically needs additional equipment and often needs to be combined with physical methods,it stands out in terms of effectiveness.However,these methods developed so far focused on single cells,thus further validation is required to assess their applicability to stacks.Simultaneously,efforts should be directed toward reducing the cost of these systems and carefully assessing their economic viability.

# 8. Concluding remarks and perspectives

In conclusion,the electrolytes play a vital role in impacting the efficiency,capacity,and cyclic stability of VRFB.To promote the developmentofnext-generationhigh-performanceVRFB,further investigations are needed to make the electrolytes more stable,more reliable,more efficient,and less expensive. In this regard,some considerations and perspectives are concluded as follows:

(1) The electrolyte composition has a vital influence on the performance of the battery.At present,it has been proved that changing supporting electrolyte and adding additives can effectively improve electrolyte performance.However，some added materials may face various issues such as gas extraction,side reactions,and crossover. Additionally,the conductivity and viscosity may also change due to the added materials,thus affecting the flow and reaction of active materials. Therefore,the electrolyte composition,including the concentration of active ions,the type and amount of supporting electrolyte,and novel additives,still needs to be further investigated.Meanwhile,machine learning offers a powerful tool for designing electrolyte composition and screening electrolyte additives,the wide adoption of which would undoubtedly accelerate the development of electrolyte.

(2) Electrolyte engineering needs to work in coordination with other components to make the battery operate properly.When optimizing the electrolyte, it is necessary to consider its suitability for components such aselectrodes and membranes.In VRFB,the electrode needs to be modified to match the changes in electrolyte.If the concentration of active ions increases,more active sites on the surface of the electrode are required.Additionally,parameters,such as porosity and permeability, are also demanded to be adjusted according to the physical state of the electrolyte.Moreover,when electrolyte composition is changed,such as adding specific additives,the membrane of VRFB needs to have the ability to keep them from freely crossing,thus ensuring the stability of electrolyte.Therefore,optimization of the electrolyte cannot be separated from the study of the important components of the battery and carried out independently.Instead,combination with important components should be considered together to achieve further improvements, thus realizing better performance of VRFB.

(3)For electrolyte monitoring,lots of methods have been developed, including electrochemical,optical,acoustic methods,and so on.For now,although such approaches have already achieved some progresses in electrolyte monitoring,there is still space for improvement in accuracy,responsiveness,stability,and economy.Additionally,some approaches require complex external instruments, thus increasing system complexity and overall costs.Therefore,future research directions need to focus on the development of methods and devices that can quickly and accurately monitor the electrolyte without complex assembly and high cost.In addition,some intelligent computer processing methods such as deep learning can be utilized to assist and improve electrolyte monitoring.For instance,image recognition and convolutional neural networks may be integrated into the instrument to achieve more accurate and quick monitoring.It is believed that these intelligent methods will certainly drive the further development of electrolyte monitoring methods.

(4) Issues such as vanadium crossover,water transfer,and side reactions can cause concentration and valence state mismatches on both sides of electrolytes,leading to a degradation in battery SOC and capacity.Thus,capacity recovery methods have been developed to increase capacity retention and prolong battery life.Nowadays, approaches such as the physical methods,chemical methods,and electrochemical methods were investigated.However，many developed methods may face complex operation processes or additional equipment, consuming additional energy and increasing maintenance cost.In this regard,it is recommended that the approaches should be further studied to achieve simpler and more efficient recovery results,and insitu capacity recovery is also vital in keeping battery long cycle life. Moreover,consideration can be given to incorporating computer artificial intelligence to control the switching of the in-situ recovery procedure to achieve more precise control and faster response.
