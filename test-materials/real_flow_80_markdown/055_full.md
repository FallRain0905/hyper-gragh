# ABSTRACT

Keywords: Distribution of relaxation time Organic redox flow battery Symmetric flow cell Long-term degradation

Organic redox flow batteries hold great promise as an energy storage technology,but their intricate chemistry makes them vulnerable to various degradation mechanisms.Monitoring this degradation is esential for identifying the limiting processes within the cels.Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS)ofers a straightforward,in-situmethod for measuring the total resistance ofanoperatingcell. However,to pinpointthe limiting processes during long-term cycling,EIS data must be complemented by other techniques.Distribution of relaxation time (DRT)analysis is particularly effective for differentiating resistance components.Inthis study,we performa comprehensive analysis of resistance evolution and the separation of anode and cathode contributions during long-term cycling of afullcellemploying7,8-dihydroxyphenazine-2-sulfonic acid (DHPS)as theanolyte. Separate analyses of the DHPS anolyte and feri-/ferrocyanide catholyte wereconducted using a symmetric cell setup.The relaxation times derived from symmetric cels facilitatethe identification ofpeaks in the DRT profiles from the full cell.Importantly,the DRT profiles indicate a correlation between the evolutionof charge transfer resistanceand the chemical degradation of DHPS.The methodologies and results outlined in this study offer significant insights for developing diagnostic tools applicable to other types of redox flow batteries.

# 1．Introduction

The development of low-cost grid-scale energy storage is essential for enhancing energy resilience and affordability.In this context,redoxflow batteries (RFBs） are considered a promising technology.RFBs offer the unique capability of independently scaling energy storage capacity (which depends on the volume of electrolyte reservoir and concentration of charge-storing species) and power (which is determined the size of the reactor cell stack).With inexpensive electrolytes,the cost of stored energy in RFBs can become competitive with that of conventional enclosed Li-ion batteries.

Over the past decades,although various flow battery chemistries have been introduced,only a few, such as,all vanadium (all-V), zincbromine,and zinc-iron hexacyanoferrate,have been scaled up to industrial scales $\mathrm { ( { > } k W ) }$ [1,2].The cost of these systems have been evaluated to range between USD $\$ 350 – 600$ per kWh [3],with the most significant cost arising from the expensive active materials,as indicated in cost breakdown reports [4].For instance,in the widely adopted all-V

RFBs,the electrolyte material,particularly vanadium oxide,constitutes about $3 2 ~ \%$ of the total cost of the system.To reduce expenses and enhance economic viability，new systems utilizing inexpensive, earth-abundant active materials or organic molecules have been proposed as pathways to lower-cost solutions [5-9]. The low cost of producing a generic organic active material (approximately $\$ 3.0/123$ compared to $\$ 25.5/123$ for vanadium) can potentially reduce the cost percentage of electrolyte material to $1 3 ~ \%$ [10].However,despite the low cost of electrolyte materials,most aqueous organic RFBs suffer from substantial capacity fade over time,largely due to the rapid chemical decomposition of their organic active materials.As redox-active organic molecules are susceptible to a variety of decomposition mechanisms [11],such as,nucleophilic attach,hydrolysis,identifying these decay mechanisms is a challenging task that relies on advanced ex-situ techniques.

In long-term cycling of aqueous organic RFBs,capacity fade can still be tracked and quantified without necessitating a detailed understandingofdecompositionmechanisms.In-situelectrochemical measurements,such as EIS,provide combined information about the two half-cell electrodes.The superimposed EIS data can be decomposed into half-cell contribution using DRT analysis.By employing a series of EIS measurements during long-term cycling of aqueous organic RFBs, it is possible to determine the resistance components of each half cell as a function of cycle number through the DRT technique.This deconvoluted resistance evolution across cycles can then be leveraged to predict the long-term performance of aqueous organic RFBs.

The DRT technique is a powerful method for deconvoluting electrochemical processes in two half-cells.This concept was first introduced in 19o7 [12].EIS is primarily measured and displayed in the frequency domain.The objective of the DRT technique is to transform the timescale characteristics of frequency-based EIS into the time domain.Practically,EIS data in frequency domain is fitted using series connected resistor (R)-capacitor (C) elements.This representation of impedance has been demonstrated across various electrochemical systems,including fuel cells,solid-state batteries,Li-ion batteries,and aqueous-electrolyte supercapacitors. The transformation of frequency data into time domain using the DRT technique is a mathematically ill-posed problem,requiring proper regularization to obtain physically relevant results. Several methods have been developed for this purpose, including Fourier transform [13],Monte Carlos sampling [14], maximum entropy[15], fractional algebraic identification [l6],and the popular Tikhonov regularization strategy [17].For more details on the application of DRT in electrochemical system,please refer to the reviews [18,19].

Despite the widespread application of DRT analysis for characterizing electrode materials in electrochemical systems,its use in RFBs remains limited. Schneider etal.[2O] conducted the first application of DRT analysis to monitor degradation processes in all-V RFBs.A generalized DRT model was developed to incorporate resistive-inductive effect and eliminate the need for data preprocessing in all-V RFBs [21]. However,the existing applications of DRT analysis in RFBs have been confined to all-V RFBs.In this study,we present the application of DRT analysis for monitoring the degradation process in organic RFBs.

An alkaline aqueous RFB using DHPS as the anolyte will be employed asan example for organic RFBs in this study.DHPS-based RFBs demonstrate stability at concentrations near their saturation limit and exhibits a high capacity of 67 Ah ${ \mathrm { l } } ^ { - 1 }$ [22].The DHPS anolyte is paired witha ferri-/ferrocyanide catholyte,anda series of EIS measurements are conducted during the long-term cycling of DHPS-based RFBs.DRT analysis is then applied to deconvolute resistance components and identify characteristic relaxation time.By analyzing the resistance evolution of the two half-cells as a function of cycle number,it becomes possible to predict long-term performance and identify the limiting

factors affecting cycling.

# 2.Theoretical formulation

The alkaline aqueous RFBs with DHPS as the anolyte and ferri-/ ferrocyanide as the catholyte operate with the following reactions in the full cell system:

$$
\begin{array} { l } { { { [ } F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } { ] } ^ { 4 - }  { [ } F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } { ] } ^ { 3 - } + e ^ { - } } } \\ { { { } } } \\ { { { [ } D H P S { ] } ^ { 3 - } + 2 H _ { 2 } O + 2 e ^ { - }  { [ } H _ { 2 } D H P S { ] } ^ { 3 - } + 2 O H ^ { - } } } \end{array}
$$

DHPS undergoes a two-electron redox reaction. The charge carriers through the ion-exchange membrane are $\mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ and $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ from the electrolyte in both half cells.

Based on nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) analysis of the anolyte solution after full cell cycling [23], the decomposition pathway for the anolyte is identified. The results reveal that the charged form of DHPS $\mathrm { ( H _ { 2 } D H P S ) }$ ）slowly undergoes desulfonation to DHP derivatives,which are also electroactive species involving two-electron transfer.More significantly, $\mathrm { H _ { 2 } D H P S }$ also undergoes deoxygenation and tautomerization (ring hydrogenation） reactions,eventually yielding hydrogenated derivatives that are electrochemically inactive.The decomposition pathway is shown in Fig.1.

The catholyte,ferri-/ferrocyanide,is also found to experience degradation [24],as below:

$$
4 F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } ^ { 3 - } + 4 e ^ { - } {  } 4 F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } ^ { 4 - }
$$

$$
4 O H ^ { - } {  } 2 H _ { 2 } O + O _ { 2 } + 4 e ^ { - }
$$

The reduction of ferricyanide to ferrocyanide is accompanied by the oxidation of water under alkaline conditions.This ferricyanide reduction is a chemical process rather than an electrochemical one.The rate of chemical reduction can be enhanced by the presence of carbon electrode.Additionally,high pH levels increase the favorability of the chemical oxidation of hydroxide in the ferri-/ferrocyanide solution. Therefore,it is anticipated that the parasitic reduction of ferricyanide is exacerbated by the presence of a carbon electrode and high pH condition.

The DRT frameworks assume that EIS spectra result from a series connected resistor-capacitor parallel circuits,implying that the impedance can be modeled as

$$
Z _ { D R T } ( f ) = 2 i \pi f L _ { 0 } + R _ { 0 } + \int _ { 0 } \frac { \gamma ( l o g \tau ) } { 1 + i 2 \pi f \tau } d l o g \tau
$$

where $L _ { 0 } , \ R _ { 0 } , \ f ,$ and $\gamma ( l o g \tau )$ are the inductance,ohmic resistance, externally excited frequency，and DRT with polarization resistance involved,respectively. It is noted that $f$ is determined by externally excited frequency spectrumusually between $0 . 1 \mathrm { H z }$ to $1 \ : \mathrm { M H z }$ ,whereas $\boldsymbol { \tau }$ isan intrinsically physical property of porous electrode interface, determined by the interfacial resistance and double-layer capacitance. In numerical simulation of Eq. (5), $\tau$ is set to a one-order wider spectrum than $1 / ( 2 \pi f )$ and is discretized with equal distance in logarithmic scale. The integrand in Eq. (5) represents a typical RC parallel circuit. The impedance of a typical RC circuitiscalculated as ${ \frac { R } { 1 + i 2 \pi f \tau } } ,$ where the time constant of this RC parallel circuit is $R C = \tau$ EIS frequencies are usually measured and displayed logarithmically; hence,the time constant is also presented on a logarithmic scale in Eq.(5).The details about the principle of DRT technique refer to the supplementary materials.

The last term in Eq. (5) represents the polarization resistance,

$$
\intop _ { 0 } ^ { \infty } \gamma ( l o g \tau ) d l o g \tau = R _ { p o l }
$$

This means that the area under the DRT profile equals the total polarization resistance $R _ { p o l }$ .The obtained distribution function requires a post-processing step involving peak analysis using Gaussian peaks [25]. Each Gaussian curve represents a separate polarization component in the electrochemical system.The area of each Gaussian curve corresponds to individual polarization resistance.

# 4.Results

# 4.1． Ferri-/ferrocyanide symmetrical polarization test

A series of steps are taken to analyze the raw EIS data.For example, in the case of the ferri-/ferrocyanide symmetric cell at a flow rate of 80 $\mathrm { { m l / m i n } }$ ，the raw EIS data presents both purely inductive behavior at high frequencies $( f > 1 0 0 0 \ : \mathrm { H z } )$ and resistive-capacitive behavioratlow frequencies $( f < 1 0 0 0 ~ \mathrm { H z } )$ .The purely inductive spectrum is usually ignored and thus subtracted from the measured spectrum.The resultant spectra are analyzed using a linear Kramer-Kronig test,with an acceptable residual error of less than $1 \ \%$ for both real and imaginary components.The residual error in Fig.3a shows much smaller value, indicating good compatibility of measured EIS with Kramer-Kronig analysis.Further analysis of the truncated EIS is performed using the DRT method.The measured impedance and the DRT-derived impedance demonstrate the approximate accuracy of the DRT analysis.The good match of Nyquist plot in Fig.3b indicates a good approximation of measured impedance by a series of RC elements.The DRT curve in Fig.3c shows four local maxima,each representing a resistance contribution of acell process to the total polarization resistance of the cell. The time constant $\left( \tau \right)$ is characteristic for each polarization process,and the area under the peaks represents the polarization resistance contribution of a specific reaction to the total cell polarization.Electron transport is usually the fastest process and has the smallest $\tau _ { i }$ ，such as distributed ohmic resistance.Distributed ohmic resistance results from the current distribution through both electronic and ionic phase of the porous electrode that minimize the total overpotential losses.Itis different from other ohmic resistance that stems from single source or element. Distributed ohmic resistance is the ‘system'ohmic loss in the electronic and ionic current network.Charge transferusually has an intermediate value of $\boldsymbol { \tau }$ ,such as anodic and cathodic charge transfer resistances.Ion diffusion is the slowest process and has the largest $\boldsymbol { \tau }$ ，such as concentration resistance.While the relative sequence is known,identifying each specific resistance component requires phenomenological analysis under various operation conditions,which will be covered in the following section.The four resistance components identified in DRT curve are projected onto the Nyquist plot in Fig.3d by their corresponding frequency.This shows that the resistance circles encompass the real impedance,and thus the sum of the sum of the deconvoluted resistance is consistent with the total resistance in the symmetric cell.

In the series of symmetric polarization tests,EIS measurements were taken at flow rates of $1 0 . 4 ~ \mathrm { m l / m i n }$ and $8 0 ~ \mathrm { m l / m i n }$ under open-circuit conditions.Additionally,after the completion of the polarization test, the cell was cooled down and EIS measurements were taken again at a lower temperature.All polarization tests were completed within 7 hours.Two potentiostatic polarization tests conducted atthe beginning and end of the test series,both ataflow rate of $8 0 \mathrm { m l / m i n }$ , showed very close measured currents $( \sim 9 9 ~ \% )$ as shown in figure S2, indicating a small diference in resistance $( \sim 1 ~ \% )$ over the measured period. In addition, the EIS impedance at those two tests with flow rate of $8 0 ~ \mathrm { m l / \Omega }$ min are also measured.The Nyquist plot in figure S3 shows the same shape of two EIS curves.It indicates an increase of membrane resistance and no change of other resistance. It also demonstrates the repeatability of the EIS measurement. This suggests that the sustained electrolyte compositions and hence negligible reduction of ferricyanide in the symmetric cell. The EIS impedance at $8 0 ~ \mathrm { m l / m i n }$ was measured before the EIS at $1 0 . 4 ~ \mathrm { m l / m i n }$ ,leading to smaller high-frequency resistance (HFR) in Fig. $^ { 4 } \mathbf { a }$ ，The smaller HFR is probably attributed to the less membrane fouling for early tests in the testing protocol. It corroborates with the shifted HFR for EIS with the same flow rate of $8 0 ~ \mathrm { m l / m i n }$ in figure S3. The resulting DRT representation in Fig.4c shows four peaks for both flow rates.The main diference comes from the peak at $\tau = 1 s$ denoted as $\mathrm { P } _ { 4 }$ .The corresponding resistances at $8 0 \mathrm { m l / m i n }$ and $1 0 . 4 \mathrm { m l / \Omega }$ min are $0 . 2 2 \mathrm { m } \Omega$ and $1 1 . 5 8 \ : \mathrm { m } \Omega$ ,respectively.It is well recognized that flow rate mainly influences concentration resistance,so it is safe to identify peak $\mathrm { P } _ { 4 }$ as corresponding to the concentration diffusion effect. The first peak, $\mathrm { { P } } _ { 1 } ,$ is most likely the distributed ohmic resistance,as it has the same resistance at different flow rates and the smallest relaxation time.Identifying the remaining two peaks $( \mathrm { P } _ { 2 }$ and $\mathbf { P } _ { 3 } )$ is not straightforward.It is widely recognized that two semi-circles in Nyquist plots represent different resistance components.The first semi-circle at highfrequency spectra usually represents distributed ohmic resistance and charge transfer resistance [27]. The second semi-circle at low-frequency spectra usually represents concentration resistance. Therefore, the size of the first semi-circle has little dependence on the flow rate.The shifted Nyquist plot of the two EIS measurements (figure S4) should have an almost overlapping first semi-circle.The division point between the first semi-circle and the second semi-circle is marked with a red marker in figure S4a, corresponding to a frequency of $1 0 \ \mathrm { H z }$ .The location of the projected peak circles in figure S4b reveals the resistance components. It is readily observed that $\mathrm { P _ { 1 } }$ represents the distributed ohmic resistance, $\mathrm { P } _ { 2 }$ represents the charge transfer resistance,and ${ \bf P } _ { 3 }$ and $\mathrm { P } _ { 4 }$ represent the concentration resistance.Interestingly,two peaks are attributed to concentration resistance.The occurrence of more than one peak related to concentration diffusion could be explained by the feature of a Warburg element, which is usually used to model concentration resistance and consists of a series of S-functions [28]. The same phenomenon has also been observed in vanadium redox flow batteries [20].

Similarly,the temperature effect on the DRT representation is shown in Fig.4d Even though the convoluted EIS shows negligible differences in the Nyquist plot in Fig.4b,the temperature effect can be differentiated in the DRT curves.The DRT curves show overall larger resistances for $\mathrm { P } _ { 2 } , \mathrm { P } _ { 3 } ,$ and $\mathrm { P } _ { 4 }$ atlower temperatures.Both charge transfer and concentration diffusion are temperature-dependent,and as the temperature decreases, the resistance increases.The trend observed in the DRT representations follows this common understanding and further validates the accuracy of peak identification in the symmetric cell.

The complementary polarization test provides additional means to further validate the accuracy of the deconvoluted resistance components.It should be noted that deconvoluted resistance components in DRT analysis are seldom validated in literature.Here,we compare resistance data from three sources:polarization tests,DRT analysis,and direct-current (dc) measurements in EIS.A series of flow rate conditions were considered in the symmetric test,as shown in Fig.5a.At high flow rate (i.e., $8 0 ~ \mathrm { m l / m i n } )$ ,therelation of applied potential and measured current is linear in the wide investigated range,indicating negligible role of concentration polarization.As the flow rate decreases,the measured current reaches asymptotic limiting condition at a lower potential condition, indicating dominance of concentration polarization.

The polarization test mimics the practical cycling conditions of the ferri-/ferrocyanide electrolyte.The total resistance $\mathrm { R } _ { \mathrm { t o t } }$ was calculated from the linear slope of the polarization curve, $\mathrm { R } _ { \mathrm { t o t } } = \mathrm { V } _ { \mathrm { p o l } } / \mathrm { I } _ { \mathrm { p o l } }$ The relation of $\mathrm { R } _ { \mathrm { t o t } }$ and Q is fitted bya power law,and the asymptotic value of $\mathrm { R } _ { \mathrm { t o t } }$ is used as the value with no diffusion limitation,as shown in Fig.5b The ohmic resistance $\mathtt { R _ { o h m } }$ is obtained from the high-frequency resistance (HFR) in the Nyquist plot.Therefore,the total resistance can be decomposed into three components: $\operatorname { R } _ { \mathrm { o h m } }$ ， $\operatorname { R } _ { \mathrm { c h } } .$ and $\scriptstyle \mathrm { R _ { c o n } }$ . The dc measurement in EIS records the direct current and voltage at each imposed frequency, shown in Fig.5c.The total resistance is calculated as $\mathrm { R } _ { \mathrm { t o t } } = \mathrm { V } _ { \mathrm { d c } } / \mathrm { I } _ { \mathrm { d c } }$ .It is noted that, due to the small excitation voltage ( ${ \sim } 5$ mV) under open circuit conditions,the calculated resistance is near the equilibrium state (i.e.,Nernstian state).In contrast, the calculated resistance from the polarization test is far from the equilibrium state. The total resistance from the dc source cannot be further deconvoluted without other advanced techniques and is presented as is for comparison.The comparison of resistance from the three sources is shown in Fig.5d It demonstrates good agreement of the deconvoluted resistance components from DRT analysis with the other two sources.Thus,the resistance deconvolution by DRT analysis is validated and credited.

There are several advantages to resistance deconvolution by DRT analysis.EIS measurement is a non-destructive testing method that has no influence on the electrolyte composition and state of charge,and it can seamlessly integrate with full cell cycling.Even small changes in resistance within the cell can be reflected in the EIS curve,making the deconvoluted resistance highly accurate.Moreover,EIS measurement is significantly simpler compared to conducting a series of polarization testsat different flowrates.

Prior to the DHPS symmetrical cycling test,a symmetric polarization test is conducted under a series of flow rate conditions.In the polarization tests,two tests ata flowrate of $6 0 \mathrm { m l / m i n }$ are conducted both at the beginning and the end.The measured current is very close,with a difference of $2 . 4 ~ \%$ .The testing protocol and the polarization curves at two flow rates of $6 0 ~ \mathrm { m l / m i n }$ are shown in figure S5.The full set of polarization tests is completed within 5 hours.Therefore,itis reasonable to state that the decomposition of DHPS is negligible over such a short testing period.Three EIS measurements are taken sequentially at flow rates of $6 0 ~ \mathrm { m l / m i n }$ ， $1 0 . 4 ~ \mathrm { m l / m i n }$ ,and $2 . 1 \ \mathrm { m l / m i n }$ .The HFR gradually increases as the same testing sequence in the protocol.The increased HFR is attributed to the gradual fouling of membrane as testing proceeds.The HFR shows the same order as depicted in the Nyquist plot in Fig.6a.Compared to the Nyquist plot of the ferri-/ferrocyanide symmetric cell in Fig.4a,the HFR in the DHPS symmetric cell is significantly larger. The HFR mainly comprises membrane resistance. The larger HFR of the DHPS symmetrical cell indicates greater membrane resistance,attributed to lower electronic conductivity of DHPS compared to ferri-/ferrocyanide.At first glance,the second semi-circle in the Nyquist plot is significantly affected by the flow rate.At high flow rates,the second semi-circle is relatively small,whereas at low flow rates,the second semi-circle becomes a straight line at a $4 5 ^ { \circ }$ angle to the abscissa,which is a characteristic feature of Warburg impedance due to the lack of species in front of the electrode surface.

The EIS data is processed using DRT technique.The DRT representation at three flow rates is shown in Fig.6b Three peaks are identified in the DRT profiles,with the flow rate mainly affecting peak $\mathrm { P } _ { 3 } ,$ as indicated by the arrow line.The other two peaks $( \mathrm { P _ { 1 } }$ and ${ \bf P } _ { 2 } )$ are not affected by the flow rate.Therefore,peak ${ \mathrm { P } } _ { 3 }$ is attributed to concentration resistance.For peak $\mathrm { P } _ { 3 } ,$ itisalso observed that the peak of the relaxation time shifts to the right at smaller flow rates.This is caused by the increased thickness of the diffuse layer near the electrode surface.At small flow rates,the diffuse layer is thicker,and it takes longer for species to reach equilibrium after perturbation,leading to a longer relaxation time at smaller flow rates.The equation of a finite-length Warburg impedance provides mathematical evidence for this interpretation [29].The identification of ${ \bf P } _ { 1 }$ and $\mathrm { P } _ { 2 }$ relies on the projection of the peaks onto the Nyquist plot (figure S6).Peak ${ \bf P } _ { 1 }$ is close to the intercept at high-frequency spectra,and it is attributed to distributed ohmic resistance in the symmetric cell,as supported by the analysis of other symmetric cels [27].The last peak ${ \bf P } _ { 2 }$ is due to the charge transfer resistance.

Another limitation of resistance decomposition from polarization tests is the assumption of constant ohmic resistance.In practice,the ohmic resistance changes between two EIS measurements,as evidenced by the increasing HFR in Fig.6a.However,this change can be represented by the DRT technique,and the deconvoluted resistance can reflect the increase in ohmic resistance.

The effect of these variations on the DRT profiles necessitates further in-depth investigation.

# 5．Conclusion

In this research,we utilized DRT analysis to investigate the performance degradation of ORFB and distinguish distinctive peaks in the DRT profiles to deconvolute the resistance components in the full cell. The peak identification relies on the peak relaxation times of electroactive species,determined from both DHPS and ferri-/ferrocyanide symmetric cells.The deconvoluted resistance components were validated using two other methods:polarization tests and dc measurements.This validation demonstrated the accurate determination of the resistance components from symmetric cells.

In the long-term cycling of the full cell,determining the resistance components in each half-cell is challenging without the use of a reference electrode.However,with DRT analysis,we can separate and quantify the charge transfer resistance in both the anode and the cathode.The changes in charge transfer resistance were correlated with new chemicals formed from electrolyte decomposition.This method provides the opportunity to monitor resistance evolution and identify the limiting factors in cell cycling,offering critical insights for optimizing ORFB performance and longevity.
