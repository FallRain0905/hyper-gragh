# ABSTRACT

The purposively tailored asymmetric half-cell structures enable beter matching of the difering mass-transfer requirements of the positive and negative half-cells,resulting in improved overall VRFB performance.In this study,drawing inspiration from existing serpentine and Biomimetic flow fields,asymmetric flow field configuration designs for VRFB are proposed.The study employed a three-dimensional multiphysics model to analyze electrolyte flow processes under diferent designs,complemented by single-celltesting that evaluated their impact on capacityand efficiency during cycling.The results indicate that the asymmetric flow field configuration has achieved certain improvements in optimizing electrolyte distribution and reducing pressre drop. Furthermore,experimentalresults demonstrated the well-balanced performance of Case 3:it maintained capacity decay rate below $1 6 . 8 0 \%$ while achieving CE of $9 7 . 0 5 \%$ .In addition,its VE and EE stabilized at $8 8 . 7 2 \%$ and $8 6 . 1 5 \%$ ,thereby exhibiting different relative advantages compared to different symmetric flow field configurations.The findings from this comprehensive analysis provide new research directions to guide subsequent efforts in VRFB optimization and operational management.

# 1． Introduction

Against the backdrop of accelerated global energy transition toward decarbonization, the large-scale development and utilization of renewable energy sources (including wind,solar,and tidal energy） has emerged as a critical pathway for achieving carbon neutrality goals [1-4].To address the inherent intermittency and volatility challenges of renewable power generation,establishing new power systems dominated by renewable energy has become a global consensus [5].In this context,large-scale energy storage technologies serve as the cornerstone for power system flexibility,requiring not only efficient energy storage and release capabilities but also compatibility with increasingly complex electricity market operations.As the proportion of renewable energy sources continues to rise,the flexibility and cost-effectiveness of energy storage solutions will become paramount. Among existing large-scale energy storage technologies,all-vanadium redox flow battery (VRFB) has been recognized as one of the most promising candidates due to its superior safety profile,environmental benignity, extended cycle life,design flexibility,and competitive cost-performance ratio [6].A comprehensive investigation into the operational mechanisms of VRFB,including the intrinsic properties of key materials and operating conditions,can provide both theoretical foundations for performance optimization and technical support for engineering applications. Such research is instrumental in enhancing overall system efficiency,reducing operational costs,and prolonging service life in practical energy storage deployments.

In recent years,significant research efforts have been devoted to investigating critical battery components,including electrolyte solutions,ion exchange membranes,and electrode materials，yielding numerous breakthroughs.However,redox flow battery (RFB) systems represent complex systems characterized by the coupling of multiple physicochemical processes.Notably,the design of the flow field structure within these batteries exerts a substantial influence on electrochemical reactions,mass transfer phenomena,and the distribution and diffusion of electrolyte solutions [7].For VRFB,mass transfer polarization is strongly coupled with electrolyte flow dynamics and reactant concentration distributions.Consequently,the investigation of flow field structures has emerged as a key research focus in VRFB development.The most conventional flow field designs-namely the interdigitated flow field (IFF),serpentine flow field (SFF),and parallel flow field (PFF)—were initially introduced to replace traditional flow distribution methods in flow batteries [8-11]. Subsequent studies on the structural configuration of the battery and flow field geometry have predominantly centered on comparative analyses of these three fundamental flow field types under varying operational conditions. Wang et al. [12] established a three-dimensional steady-state multiphysics model inte-grating fluid dynamics, mass transport, and electrochemical processes using COMsOL Multiphysics.Their study systematically evaluated VRFB employing the SFF configuration with eight distinct trapezoidal cross-sections and one rectangular cross-section under varying flow rate conditions.The simulation results demonstrated that the optimized trapezoidal cross-section design achieved $1 2 \%$ reduction in both pres-sure drop and pumping power consumption. Furthermore, this configuration enhanced the $\scriptstyle \mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + }$ concentration uniformity,leading to improved mass transport characteristics within the cell.Notably，at elevated current density of $6 0 0 \mathrm { m A } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ ,the modified flow field design exhibited $0 . 9 ~ \%$ improvement in voltage efficiency. Li et al.[13] conducted a comprehensive investigation of their novel spiral flow field (NSFF) design through three-dimensional transient modeling. Their computational analysis revealed significant performance enhancements at operational parameters of $1 8 0 ~ \mathrm { m L / m i n }$ flow rate and $9 0 \mathrm { \ m A } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ current density. Specificall, the NSFF demonstrated notable improvements in energy efficiency,surpassing conventional SFF and PFF by 3.65 $\%$ and $9 . 8 ~ \%$ ,respectively.Furthermore,the NSFF exhibited superior cycling stability,maintaining $7 2 . 1 8 ~ \%$ capacity retention rate after multiple charge-discharge cycles,representing $9 . 9 7 ~ \%$ and $3 2 . 1 2 ~ \%$ improvements over SFF and PFF configurations,respectively. In separate development, Sun et al.[14] introduced an innovative fan-shaped flow field structure for VRFB,expanding the design possibilities for enhanced electrochemical performance. Yang et al. [15] introduced an innovative biomimetic flow field (BFF) design for VRFB and established a three-dimensional multiphysics coupled model to systematically compare its performance with conventional SFF.Their investigation incorporated detailed analysis of electrolyte flow rate effects on multiple key performance indicators.The computational results demonstrated that the biomimetic configuration of the BFF achieved substantial reductions in pressure drop, with particularly pronounced improvements observed at elevated electrolyte flow rates.Meng et al. [16] developed an innovative trapezoidal flow field battery(TFB) for VRFB systems. Through coupled numerical simulation with charge-discharge test of the magnified stacks,their results demonstrated that the optimized TFB structure exhibited: enhanced mass transport characteristics,superior cell performance metrics, and significantly improved voltage efficiency and electrolyte utilization.

The single cell represents the fundamental operational unit of VRFB, where the performance of its constituent components under various operating conditions critically influences both power output and overall system eficiency [17,18].Despite significant advancements in VRFB design [19-22],studies indicate that structural asymmetries between the positive and negative half-cells remain critical challenge requiring further optimization.Cyclic voltammetry analyses reveal markedly slower reaction kinetics at the negative electrode compared to the positive electrode [23-26],resulting in fundamentally distinct electrochemical behaviors between the two half-cells.This intrinsic kinetic asymmetry induces non-uniform polarization distributions that ultimately degrade overall battery performance.However, this inherent asymmetry presents unique opportunities for battery optimization. By purposively engineering asymmetric half-cell structures,the differing mass-transfer requirements of the positive and negative half-cells can better matched, thereby enhancing VRFB system performance.Wei et al. [27] fabricated asymmetric electrode structure from two distinct composite materials.The substantial differences in the properties of the positive and negative electrodes served to enhance the respective reaction kinetics while reducing the kinetic disparity between them, consequently improving the overall battery performance.Jing etal.[28] also designed asymmetric electrode configuration for VRFB,capitalizing on the pronounced disparity in reaction kinetics between the positive and negative electrolytes，which substantially enhanced energy efficiency.Park et al. [29」 proposed an innovative asymmetric operational approach involving diferential positive and negative electrolyte volumes.Their investigation demonstrated that optimizing the electro-lyte volume ratio substantially mitigated the initial capacity decay rate while enhancing long-term capacity retention. Notably, this strategy yielded remarkable improvement in practical energy density，with measurements increasing from 15.5 Wh/L to $2 5 . 2 \mathrm { W h / L }$ following $2 5 \%$ reduction in positive electrolyte volume. Shrestha et al.[3O] adopted a strategy of using asymmetric anion and cation exchange membranes, thereby mitigating battery capacity decay. Knehr and Kumbur [31] demonstrated that employing asymmetric electrolyte flow rates between the positive and negative half-cells efectively mitigates vanadium crosover. Their study revealed that this strategy regulates the transmembrane pressure differential while suppressing osmotic convection effects,thereby significantly reducing vanadium ion migration. Conse-quently, this approach was shown to markedly attenuate the discharge capacity decay during cycling. Wang et al.[32] proposed asymmetrical variable flow-rate (AVF) control strategy that integrates asymmetric and variable-flow operations to dynamically counteract electrolyte migration during cycling. Experimental results demonstrated that this approach significantly mitigated electrolyte crossover，increased discharge capacity,and delayed capacity decay.Lu et al. [33-35] developed a two-dimensional transient isothermal model for VRFB that explicitly accounts for vanadium ion cross-diffusion mechanisms, including transmembrane diffusion,convection,and migration, while incorporating cell contact resistance effects.Through systematic numerical investigations employing this model, the researchers examined the effects of several key operational and design parameters on battery performance， including asymmetric electrolyte concentration and operating pressure conditions，multi-step electrolyte replenishment during extended cycling, and asymmetric electrode structure configurations.The results demonstrated that carefully implemented multi-step electrolyte addition strategy,involving periodic introduction of 1.04 mol/L $\mathrm { V O ^ { 2 + } }$ and $\mathsf { V } ^ { 3 + }$ electrolytes to the respective half-cels, reduced capacity decay rate by $1 0 . 8 ~ \%$ ，The study further revealed that increasing the positive electrolyte concentration while maintaining constant negative electrolyte concentration significantly mitigated long-term capacity loss.Additionally,elevated positive electrode outlet pressure was shown to efectively suppress capacity decay. Notably, the investigation of electrode design parameters established that both asymmetric compression of uniformly thick electrodes and uncompressed electrodes with asymmetric initial thickness provided comparable benefits in terms of capacity retention.Huang et al.[36] suppressed significant water migration by pre-establishing pressure gradient via diferential positive and negative electrolyte flow rates, with experimental results confirming that the optimal asymmetric mode improved the average coulombic eficiency by $0 . 9 4 \%$ ,energy efficiency by $2 . 0 5 ~ \% ,$ ，and capacity retention rate by $9 . 6 6 ~ \%$ over the symmetric mode.Zhang et al.[37] proposed a new nanointerfacial electric fields (E-fields),which accelerated electrochemical kinetics by reconstructing the asymmetric chemical potentials across the heterogeneous interface, resulting in significantly enhanced VRFB performance.

Analysis of the existing literature indicates that appropriately designed asymmetric structures can effectively enhance VRFB performance.In conventional flow fields,the differing reaction intensities at the positive and negative electrodes cause uneven distribution of active species within the electrolyte,consequently leading to mismatch in electrochemical reaction rates between the two half-cells.The underlying principle asymmetric flow field configuration is,therefore,to select suitable flow field structures to the specific operational requirements of each individual half-cell.Guided by this principle,our work focused on asymmetric flow field structures for the positive and negative electrodes in VRFB.It should be noted that Lu et al.[38] have conducted research on asymmetric serpentine flow fields optimized for net discharge power. In order to extend the scope of this research area, we examined asymmetric configurations combining SFF and BFF in this work.Using three-dimensional numerical model,we microscopically analyzed four distinct designs to assess their effects on electrolyte flow. In addition,the VRFB single-cell platform was employed to compare theircycling performance—including capacity and efficiency—against that of symmetric configurations to discuss the relative advantages of asymmetric flow field configurations.This work provides novel insights into asymmetric flow field optimization for VRFB,offering promising strategies to improve overall electrochemical performance.

# 2.Numerical model and experiment

# 2.1．Principle of electrochemical reaction

A typical VRFB single cell consists of an ion exchange membrane, electrodes,electrode frames,bi-polar plates,current collectors,and end plates,as schematically illustrated in Fig.1.During operation, the positive and negative electrolytes are circulated through their respective half-cell reaction zones via external pumps and piping systems before returning to storage tanks,completing closed-loop recycling process. Positioned at the core of the single cell,the ion exchange membrane serves as the battery's pivotal functional component,simultaneously fulfilling two essential roles: preventing electrolyte crossover between the positive and negative half-cells while selectively facilitating ion transport to maintain electrolyte electroneutrality and ensure internal circuit conductivity [39]. The porous graphite felt electrode functions as the primary site for electrochemical reactions,offering abundant active sites and efficient mass transport pathways for active ion redox processes [40].

VRFB employ $\mathsf { V } ^ { 2 + } / \mathsf { V } ^ { 3 + }$ and $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + } / \mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + }$ as active species in the negative and positive electrolytes,respectively,with $\mathrm { V O } _ { 2 } ^ { + } / \mathrm { V O } ^ { 2 + }$ predominantly present in the positive electrolyte and $\mathsf { V } ^ { 2 + } / \mathsf { V } ^ { 3 + }$ in the negative electrolyte.During charging,the positive electrolyte undergoes oxidation by electron loss while the negative electrolyte experiences reduction through electron gain.The discharge process reverses these electrochemical reactions [41,42],resulting in valence state variations of vanadium ions in both half-cells.The typical charge/discharge reactions are as follows:

Positive reaction:

$$
\mathrm { V O ^ { 2 + } + H _ { 2 } O \xrightarrow { \overbrace { \phantom { \left. \sum _ { \alpha = 1 } ^ { \mathrm { C h a r g e } } \right.}  } } V O _ { 2 } ^ { + } + 2 H ^ { + } + e ^ { - } }
$$

Negative reaction:

$$
\smash { \mathsf { V } ^ { 3 + } + \mathsf { e } ^ { - } \xrightarrow [ \hdots ] { \mathrm { C h a r g e } } \mathsf { V } ^ { 2 + } }
$$

Overall reaction:

$$
\nabla { \bf O } ^ { 2 + } + { \bf V } ^ { 3 + } + { \bf H } _ { 2 } { \bf O } \overbrace { \underbrace { \mathrm { D i s c h a r g e } } _ { \mathrm { D i s c h a r g e } } } ^ { \mathrm { C h a r g e } } { \bf V } ^ { 2 + } + { \bf V } { \bf O } _ { 2 } ^ { + } + { \bf 2 } { \bf H } ^ { + }
$$

During operation of VRFB,the positive and negative electrolytes circulate continuously through closed-loop piping system via pumpdriven flow,ensuring sustained transport of active species to electrode surfaces for maintained electrochemical reaction kinetics.This process facilitates the reversible valence-state transitions of vanadium ions that constitute the fundamental energy storage mechanism,enabling efficient bidirectional conversion between chemical and electrical energy.

The investigated flow field structures are fabricated on graphite bipolar plates through precision etching.By interchanging bi-polar plates with different flow field configurations,electrolyte flow characteristics and distribution profiles within the cell could be modulated.The SFF is a hydrodynamically designed flow field commonly used in electrochemical systems such as VRFB,as shown in Fig.2a.Through its unique meandering channel structure,it can effectively promote electrolyte flow path optimization, enhance the mixing effect and minimize stagnation zones,thereby achieving electrolyte distribution uniformity. The BFF design in VRFB employs biologically inspired architectures to enhance electrolyte flow dynamics and reaction kinetics.This biomimetic approach simultaneously optimizes electrolyte distribution while minimizing flow resistance,thereby improving reaction uniformity and overall energy conversion efficiency.The BFF implemented in this work is bioinspired by plant leaf venation patterns,representing a novel architecture specifically developed for VRFB applications Reference[15],as shown in Fig.2b The implementation of distinct flow field structures at the positive and negative electrodes modifies the electrolyte transport dynamics through the electrode, thereby enhancing performance in vanadium redox flow batteries.

# 2.3. Experimental design and process

The impact of different configurations on battery cycle performance can be evaluated through single-cellcharge-discharge cycling tests from long-term operational perspective.The experimental platform used in this study is the integrated redox flow battery single-cell test system,and contains bolt-type single-cell test fixtures,peristaltic pumps,fluidic pipelines,energy storage tanks,an integrated base,and charge/ discharge testers and other components.And the charge/discharge cycling tests were conducted using CT-4008-5V/6A battery tester, which features three-tier architecture comprising upper,middle,and lower computer control systems. The complete VRFB single-cell experimental setup is illustrated in Fig.3a. The single cell assembly comprises the following components:all-fltmsulphonie acid ion exchange membrane,graphite felt electrodes,insulated electrode frames,graphite bipolar plates,gold-plated copper current collectors,and aluminum alloy end plates,as shown in Fig. 3b The module is securely fastened using socket head cap screws with sealing gaskets to ensure proper compression and electrolyte containment,while permitting convenient component replacement when required.

Inaddition,the battery storage tanks store the vanadium-based solution for redox reactions of the battery,consisting of $1 . 5 \mathrm { m o l / L }$ vanadium ions in $4 . 5 \mathrm { \ m o l / L \ H S O _ { 4 } ^ { - } }$ aqueous solution.During operation, the peristaltic pump drives electrolyte circulation through the flow circuit, delivering the solution to respective half-cells via the piping system.The clockwise pump direction enables electrolyte supply to the cell,while anticlockwise operation facilitates post-experimental drainage to prevent graphite felt degradation from prolonged electrolyte exposure during storage.Magnetic Stirrers are employed to maintain homogeneous electrolyte mixing and prevent vanadium salt precipitation.

In order to systematically investigate the influence of flow field configurations on VRFB performance，this study designed four comparative experimental schemes.These same configurations were also used in the simulation analysis.As illustrated in Fig.4, the experimental design encompasses both conventional symmetric flow fields and novel asymmetric configurations,with particular emphasis on evaluating their effects on battery capacity decay and critical performance metrics.

1） Experimental Case 1: Under constant-current conditions, the longterm charge-discharge cycling test is conducted with SFF.
2)Experimental Case 2: Under constant-current conditions, the longterm charge-discharge cycling test is conducted with BFF.
3) Experimental Case 3:The positive electrode uses SFF,while the negative electrode uses BFF for long-term charge-discharge cycling tests under constant-current conditions.
4)Experimental Case 4:The positive electrode uses BFF,while the negative electrode uses SFF for long-term charge-discharge cycling tests under constant-current conditions.

We first leveraged three-dimensional multiphysics model to analyze four design schemes,aiming to investigate the effects of different flow field configurations on electrolyte flow processes.The model was solved numerically using the COMsOL Multiphysics platform.In order to establish its validity and reliability,the simulated discharge voltage - SOC curve was systematically validated against experimental measurement.For model validation,the operating conditions were set as follows: electrolyte flow rate of $1 2 0 \mathrm { m L / m i n }$ ,current density of $6 0 \mathrm { m A } / \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ ,and initial vanadium concentration of $1 5 0 0 \mathrm { m o l } / \mathrm { m } ^ { 3 }$ As shown in Fig.5, the simulation results closely align with the experimental voltage-SOC trend,demonstrating maximum deviation of $1 . 3 5 \ \ \%$ .This minor discrepancy likely originates from model simplifications and unaccounted electrolyte properties. Overall, the model exhibits high degree of reliability and accuracy in its simulation outcomes.

# 3.1.2. Concentration distribution and overpotential

The distribution of ion concentration within the battery effectively reflects the performance of the flow field structure in distributing the electrolyte,while also influencing the calculation of battery voltage. The molar concentration contours in Fig.6 reveal that the SFF enables more uniform electrolyte penetration through the porous electrode,yielding higher average molar concentration.This finding further confirms the essential role of flow field design in controlling active ion distribution.In addition,more uniform distribution of vanadium ion concentration contributes to mitigating capacity decay,as will be demonstrated in the subsequent experimental research section. Collectively, these results demonstrate the performance of SFF over the BFF in promoting vanadium ion transport within the electrode.

# 3.1.3. Influence on pump

Pressure drop serves as a critical performance metric when evaluating batteries employing different flow field structures.A primary advantage in optimizing flow field design lies in its ability to minimize pressure drop,consequently reducing pump power consumption. In order to research the influence of flow field designs on pressure drop and associated pump power losses,multiphysics simulations were performed across electrolyte flow rates ranging from 30 to $1 8 0 ~ \mathrm { m L / m i n }$ . As shown in Fig.8a,marked reduction in pressure drop is observed for BFF,which derives from its unique structural design.Relative to Case 1,Case 2 reduced the pressure drop by $5 2 . 8 0 \%$ at $6 0 ~ \mathrm { { m L / m i n } }$ and by $5 4 . 2 2 \%$ at $1 8 0 ~ \mathrm { m L / m i n }$ ，demonstrating its pronounced advantage in minimizing flow resistance.This result aligns with the pressure drop characteristics reported by Yang et al.[15] for similar flow field designs.The battery system pressure drop is governed by the half-cell requiring higher driving pressure [45]. Consequently, the pressure drops in Case 3 and 4 substantially exceed that of Case 2and approach the level observed in Case 1.In addition,fluid mechanics dictates that at constant flow rate, increased fluid viscosity elevates flow resistance,thereby amplifying the pressure drop.This mechanism may account for the marginally higher pressure drop observed in Case 4 compared to Case 3 in the simulation results.As shown in Fig.8b,with increasing electrolyte flow rate, greater pressure drop clearly corresponds to higher pump power loss, and the pump loss performance of Case 2 was significantly superior to that of the other schemes.

# 4.Conclusion

This study designed asymmetric flow field configurations to exploit the distinctive electrochemical characteristics of the positive and negative half-cells,thereby enhancing the performance of vanadium flow batteries.To this end,a comprehensive three-dimensional multiphysics model was established to investigate the performance of vanadium flow batteries with four different configurations.The simulations revealed that,compared to the SFF,the BFF configuration exhibited more dispersed vanadium ion distribution across the electrode surface, coupled with lower average molar concentration.Despite this,Case 2 was notably superior in mitigating pressure drop and pump losses, especially under high electrolyte flow rates.The asymmetric flow field configuration synthesizes the merits of both designs that achieves improvements in electrolyte flow distribution, overpotential,and pressure drop compared to symmetric flow field structures.Subsequent experimental investigation using the VRFB single-cell platform subjected the different flow field configurations to 5O charge-discharge cycles for performance analysis. Experimental result revealed the superior performance of the symmetrical SFF in maintaining discharge capacity and mitigating capacity decay.After fixed number of cycles under identical conditions,Case 1 exhibited $5 . 1 9 \%$ lower capacity decay rate than Case 2 and $1 . 5 2 ~ \%$ lower rate than Case 3.In terms of efficiency,Case 3 achieved $2 . 4 1 ~ \%$ higher CE relative to Case 1,albeit with lower VE. When benchmarked against Case 2,it demonstrated $2 . 9 2 ~ \%$ improvement in VE and substantial gain in EE.In addition,Case 4 exhibited significant efficiency fluctuations,indicating poor cycling stability and disqualifying it as an optimal configuration. Based on battery capacity decay rates and three key eficiency metrics,Case 3 exhibited improvements in different performance metrics relative to the different symmetric flow field configurations.

The different performance characteristics revealed by combining simulation and experimental results underscore a critical optimization challenge in flow field design:achieving synergistic balance between enhanced mass transfer,minimized flow resistance,and maintained high system efficiency.Case 1 prioritizes mass transfer,as its high distribution uniformity effectively suppresses capacity decay,albeit at the expense of increased pump power losses.In contrast, Case 2 emphasizes flow resistance reduction,attaining the lowest pressure drop but compromising long-term efficiency stability.In this work,the asymmetric configuration proposed represents a compromise strategy. While it does not excel in any single performance metric,it achieves more practical and superior synergistic enhancement in overall system performance. It demonstrates particular potential as an effective strategy for concurrently improving battery efficiency and extending service life. Future work will involve extended lifetime testing of Case 3 under realworld operating conditions to validate its long-term performance for energy storage applications.Thus,appropriate flow field configurations can be implemented according to particular needs in actual batery operation.In addition,the viability of asymmetric flow field configuration also hinges on whether the flow structures selected for each halfcell are compatible with their distinct electrochemical characteristics. Adopting ill-suited configuration,as exemplified by Case 4,can have detrimental effects on overall battery performance.A promising direction for future work may also focus on advancing simulation and experimental techniques to decipher the mechanistic link between the flow field selection in asymmetric configurations and overall battery performance.In the development of future energy storage solutions, the selection of configurations with demonstrated long-term stability will enhance both the operational efficiency and economic viability of energy storage systems,while simultaneously guiding subsequent technological optimization efforts.
