# 1．Introduction

With the rapid development of global industry and population growth,the demand for energy has increased dramatically [1,2].In recent years,approximately $8 2 \%$ of the energy has been supplied from fossil fuel sources (coal,oil and natural gas),which has caused many environmental problems [3-7].Renewable energy sources such as solar and wind power have attracted more and more attention due to their advantages of low cost,cleanliness and no pollution [8,9].However, the inherent indirectness and volatility of the renewable energy sources result in a mismatch between energy supply and energy demand, inhibiting their direct integration into the grid [1O-12].Redox flow batteries (RFBs) can realize grid-scale power storage (up to MW/MWh) due to their unique structure,showing the advantages of good scalability,reliable safety,and independent decoupling of power and energy [11,13-17]. Several aqueous inorganic metal flow batteries are currently under extensive investigation,including the Zn-Mn [18],S-Mn [19], Zn-Fe [20,21],and S-Fe [22] flow batteries.Vanadium redox flow batteries (VRFBs) are currently the most advanced and commercially viable technology in the field.However,their large-scale implementation is limited by the crossover of catholyte and anolyte,as well as the high cost of vanadium-based materials [23,24].

The redox active materials in aqueous organic redox flow batteries (AORFBs) are constituted by earth-abundant elements (C,H, O,N), whose redox potentials and solubility can be regulated by molecular engineering，providing more promising prospective applications than aqueous inorganic RFBs [11,16,25].A considerable number of electrolyte materials for AORFBs,such as viologen [13,15,26,27],anthraquinone [28-31],phenazine [32,33],azobenzene [34],phenothiazine [35], 2,2,6,6-tetramethyl-1-piperidinyloxy (TEMPO） [23,36-39], $4 , 4 ^ { \prime } , 4 ^ { \prime \prime }$ -trihydroxytriphenylamine [4O]，and ferrocene derivatives [11,41-44], have been recommended in recent years and demonstrated excellent battery performance.However,the cost-effectiveness of the electrolytes remains an issue that inhibits the widespread application of AORFBs [45].Numerous natural dyes have been discovered to have active centers that can generate electrochemical redox and have the possibility to be employed in AORFBs [46,47].Aziz et al. [48] constructed a symmetric battery using a naturally derived dye (alizarin）as the redox-active substance for both catholyte and anolyte,achieving an open-circuit voltage of $1 . 0 4 \mathrm { V }$ and a capacity of $1 6 3 \mathrm { m A h } \mathrm { g } ^ { - 1 }$ . Zhu et al. [49] achieved an energy efficiency of $7 7 \%$ at a current density of $4 0 \mathrm { m A }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ by pairing IC-H, the acidic analogue of the natural organic dye IC-Na $( 5 , 5 ^ { \prime }$ -indigodisulfonic acid sodium salt),with $\mathrm { B r _ { 2 } / H B r }$ However, these AORFBs based on dyes also had the drawbacks of low solubility and poor stability.The alizarin-based symmetric battery showed only 45 $\%$ capacity retention over 1Oo cycles,and the solubility of IC-Na was only $1 0 \ \mathrm { g } \ \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 }$ , which was still a long way from commercialization.

In the past few decades, people have become more and more interested in biomimetic chemistry,and some progress has been made in the research of biomimetic energy materials and catalytic materials [50]. Biological metalloenzymes,such as hydrogenase,cytochrome c oxidase (CcO),and carbon monoxide dehydrogenase (CODH),are highly efficient,fast,and selective in energy conversion and storage [51].Reactivity of metalloenzymes is determined by the synergistic effectbetween the first coordination sphere (ligands that bind directly to the active center of the metal),the secondary coordination sphere (consisting of functional groups that can interact with,but do not bind to,the metal), and the outer coordination sphere (functional groups outside the second coordination sphere that affect the catalytic efficiency of the metalloenzyme） [52-54].Significant progress has been made in the biomimetic synthesis of the first coordination sphere of hydrogenase [55, 56]. It is worth noting that non-covalent interactions in the secondary coordination sphere of metalloenzymes also play a crucial role in determining activity and selectivity.Wu et al.[57] constructed the secondary coordination sphere of electron donors $\left( \mathrm { H A } ^ { - } \right)$ to CdSe QDs, which significantly improved the catalytic hydrogen production efficiency of [FeFe]-H2ase simulants.Sun et al. [58] utilized the secondary coordination spheres formed by the hydrophobic cavity of cyclodextrin to create a hydrophobic microenvironment for [FeFe]- $_ { \mathrm { H _ { 2 } a s e s } }$ in aqueous solution,which improved the stability of the catalyst and achieved high hydrogen evolution eficiency. The application of biomimetic energy active materials in the field of AORFBs has also been progressively investigated.Wooley et al.[59] designed a biomimetic metal-free batterywith biodegradable polypeptide backbone modified viologen and nitroxide free radicals,with a maximum charge capacity of $3 7 . 8 ~ \mathrm { m A h }$ $\mathbf { g } ^ { - 1 }$ ，and the active ingredients could be degraded on demand to generate amino acids and other structural units. Ji et al. [6O] reported a biomimetic ultra-stable AORFB with amino acid functionalized phenazine (AFP),and systematically studied the effects of different amino acids and substitution positions on AFP,in which 1,6-DPAP $\mathrm { \langle K _ { 4 } F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } }$ AORFB exhibited very low capacity fading rate of $0 . 5 ~ \%$ per year.

Naphthol green B (l-nitroso-2-naphthol-6-sulfonic acid sodium iron salt,NGB),a commercially important green nitroso dye with iron complex structure and excellent redox properties, possesses the properties of the catholyte for AORFBs.Therefore,we propose a positive material with a multistage coordination sphere for AORFB,which includes ligands bound to the active center atom iron of NGB as the first coordination sphere,the hydrophobic cavity of cyclodextrin (CD) as the secondary coordination sphere,and the modified functional group on cyclodextrin as the outer coordination sphere (Fig.1).Iron is the most abundant cheap transition metal in nature,and the valence state changes of iron ions are commonly present in processes such as catalysis and energy transfer in nature.The low price of NGB might significantly reduce the cost of AORFB,offering the possibility of its commercialization.Electrochemical measurements were performed on the NGB by cyclic voltammetry (CV),exhibiting a high redox potential $_ { ( 0 . 7 3 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ VS. NHE).The transformation of the Fe(II)/Fe(IV) electroredox couple was confirmed through analysis of oxidized and reduced NGB using ultraviolet-visible spectroscopy (UV-vis） and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS).The envelope interaction _of hydroxypropyl-$\beta$ cyclodextrin (HP $\boldsymbol { \cdot } \boldsymbol { \beta }$ -CD) on NGB was evidenced by $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ nuclear magnetic resonance $\mathrm { ( ^ { 1 } H \ N M R ) }$ ,nuclear overhauser effect spectroscopy (NOESY) and differential pulse voltammetry (DPV),which greatly enhanced the electrochemical kinetics of NGB,resulting in a pair of symmetric redox peaks.The all-Fe symmetric $\mathrm { N G B } / \mathrm { K _ { 3 } F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } }$ battery with HP $\boldsymbol { \cdot } \boldsymbol { \beta }$ -CD performs up to nearly $1 0 0 \%$ Coulombic efficiency (CE)and low capacity decay rate $( < 0 . 0 1 2 \%$ per cycle). The full-battery NGB/ $\mathrm { ( } \mathsf { S p r ) _ { 2 } V }$ AORFB with HP $\cdot \beta$ CD exhibits excellent rate performance and cycle stability, with $9 9 \%$ of initial capacity after 8oo cycles at current density of $1 0 \mathrm { m A }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ and a lower capacity fade rate of $0 . 0 0 1 2 5 \%$ per cycle.

# 2.7.Density functional theory

Theoretical calculations were performed using the Gaussian 09 software package to optimize the structures of NGB, $\alpha$ -CD,HP $\cdot \beta$ -CD,and $\gamma { \mathrm { - C D } }$ at the MO6-2X level of theory with $6 { - } 3 1 { + } \mathrm { G }$ (d,p) basis set [61]. All geometries were optimized in water as the implicit solvent.To model the implicit solvent,the universal solvation model based on solute electron density (SMD) was used.Conformational search and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were used to obtain the lowest energy complex structures.Frequency calculations for each optimized structure were performed at the $\mathbf { M } 0 6 { - } 2 \mathbf { X } / 6 { - } 3 1 { + } \mathbf { G }$ (d, p) level of theory. No imaginary frequency was found for the minimum energy configurations of any of the optimized structures.Based on the optimized structure of the molecules,the RESP charge and dipole moment were analyzed using the Multiwfn 3.8 analyzer [62],VMD 1.9.3 software and the cubeman utilization in the Gaussian O9 [63,64].

# 3．Results and discussion

# 3.1．Physical and electrochemical properties of NGB

NGB,a widely used commercial metal complex dye,is a pseudooctahedral Fe(II) coordination compound consisting of 1-nitroso-2- naphthol-6-sulfonic acid as a bidentate ligand.There are distinct UV-vis characteristic absorption peaks in aqueous solution (Fig.2a), among which the absorption peaks at 232 and $2 8 2 \ \mathrm { n m }$ are for the naphthalene ring and the conjugate bond on the ring,and the broad peak at $7 1 4 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ is caused by nitroso group.The color of the cathode solution (oxidized NGB） changed remarkably from dark green to yellowish brown when the NGB was charged as the positive electrode (Fig.2a),and the characteristic absorption peaks of the NGB disappeared almost completely in its UV-vis absorption spectra while a new absorption peak emerged at around $4 0 0 \ \mathrm { n m }$ .To investigate the change of NGB before and after charging,NaClO was added to the aqueous solution of NGB,and the color of the solution changed in the same way as the oxidized NGB,while the UV-vis absorption spectra was consistent. Gupta et al. [65] had reported that NaClO could oxidize Fe (III) in the coordination species to Fe(IV) in the higher valence state.

Single point energy calculations were conducted on the NGB before and after charging,with energies of $- 4 9 0 1 . 2 7 6$ au and -4901.309 au,respectively (Fig.2h).Meanwhile,the stability of the ligand 5-nitroso-6-oxonaphthalene-2-sulfonate $( \mathrm { N O S } ^ { 2 - } )$ ，which is a ligand of NGB,was investigated as shown in Figs.S2 and S3.The absence of redox peaks in the CV curves of $\mathrm { N O S } ^ { 2 - }$ as well as the presence of a large HOMO-LUMO energy level difference confirmed the good stability of $\bar { \bf N } O S ^ { 2 - }$ . # 3.5.Battery experiments

A flow battery was constructed using 0.O1 M NGB with 0.O2 M HP-$\beta$ -CD and 0.01 M ${ \mathrm { K } } _ { 3 } { \mathrm { F e } } ( \mathrm { C N } ) _ { 6 }$ in $\boldsymbol { 1 . 0 \mathrm { M } }$ NaCl supporting electrolyte for the catholyte and anolyte,respectively.The $\mathrm { N G B } / \mathrm { K _ { 3 } F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } }$ AORFB used a piece of Nafion 212 membrane as the separator to allow the exchange of $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ cation (Fig.11).To confirm the stability of NGB during battery charging/discharging,bipolar cycling tests were conducted on NGB/ $\mathrm { K _ { 3 } F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } }$ AORFB.The current rate performance of the $\mathrm { N G B } / \mathrm { K _ { 3 } F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } }$ AORFB was studied from $5 \ \mathrm { m A } \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ to $3 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ (Fig. 12a). Five cycles of galvanostatic charge and discharge tests were performed with cutoff voltages at $0 . 8 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ for the charging process and $- 0 . 8 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ for the discharging process at each current density. The flow battery capacity remained essentially stable at every current density.At $5 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ,the flow battery presented a capacity of $3 . 0 4 \mathrm { m A h }$ $( 7 5 . 6 \%$ of the theoretical capacity, $4 . 0 2 \ : \mathrm { m A h }$ ),which decreased to $1 . 6 3 ~ \mathrm { m A h }$ $( 4 0 . 5 ~ \% )$ when the current density was increased to $3 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ (Fig. S34) due to the increased electrochemical polarization and Ohmic polarization.The battery demonstrated an almost $1 0 0 ~ \%$ CE at each current density,as shown in Fig.l2b.The long-term cycling performance of the $\mathrm { N G B } / \mathrm { K _ { 3 } F e }$ $( \mathrm { C N } ) _ { 6 }$ AORFB was investigated,cycling at a current density of $1 0 ~ \mathrm { { m A } }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for 100 cycles.The flow battery exhibited good CE with almost $1 0 0 \%$ per cycle as shown in Fig.S35a.After 1O0 cycles of galvanostatic charge and discharge,the capacity of the battery increased from the initial $2 . 4 ~ \mathrm { m A h }$ to $2 . 6 \ \mathrm { m A h }$ (Fig. S35b),which might be caused by the crossover of the reduced species ${ \mathrm { K } } _ { 4 } { \mathrm { F e } } ( { \mathrm { C N } } ) _ { 6 }$ at low current density. To prevent the crossover of the reduced species,the current density was increased to $3 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ .This resulted in a stable cycling performance of the flow battery with no noticeable capacity decay in 1Oo cycles (Fig.l2c).Additionally,the flow battery achieved an average CE of almost $1 0 0 ~ \%$ per cycle.

To further investigate the chemical stability of the active substance NGB,we further assembled a $\mathrm { N G B } / \mathrm { K _ { 3 } F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } }$ symmetric AORFB employing 0.01 M NGB and 0.01 M ${ \mathrm { K } } _ { 3 } { \mathrm { F e } } ( \mathrm { C N } ) _ { 6 }$ with 0.02 M HP $\cdot \beta$ -CD as both anolyte and catholyte.The charge and discharge capacity curves at different electrical densities from $1 0 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ to $8 0 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ in Fig. S36 indicate that the symmetric flow battery has good current rate performance.At each current density,the symmetric flow battery delivered stable capacity retentionand a nearly $1 0 0 ~ \%$ average CE (Fig. S37).As shown in Fig.S38,the symmetric flow battery recorded capacities of 5.6, 5.5,5.3,5.1,5.0,4.8,4.6,4.3,4.0,3.7 and $3 . 3 \mathrm { m A h }$ at current densities of 10,15,20,25,30,35,40,50,60,70 and $8 0 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ . # 4. Conclusions

In summary,we have developed the metal complex dye NGB with good stability and electrochemical performance as the cathode electrolyte solution of AORFBs.Meanwhile,the mechanism of the improvement of the electrochemical properties of NGB by the secondary coordination sphere was investigated by experimental characterization and theoretical simulation.The valence shift of NGB from Fe(III) to Fe (IV) in the cathode electrolyte has been demonstrated for the first time through UV-vis,XPS,XANES,and Mossbauer analysis of NGB in the charged state.The secondary coordination sphere structure is confirmed to be in the form of the naphthalene ring of NGB penetrating into the interior of the HP $\boldsymbol { \cdot } \boldsymbol { \beta }$ CD cavity，as evidenced by the lowest energy structure obtained through MD simulation and the 2D NOESY NMR spectra of NGB and HP $\beta$ -CD.By comparing the bond length and bond angle changes,molecular frontier orbitals,RESP and dipole moments before and after the interaction of NGB with the three CDs,it is shown that HP- $\beta$ -CD can form a strong secondary coordination sphere with NGB,and that the electrochemical performance of NGB has the most significant improvement compared with ${ \mathfrak { X } } ^ { - }$ and $\gamma$ -CDsas electrolyte additives.Adding HP $\cdot \beta$ -CD could increase the solubility of NGB and slow down the transmembrane crossover of NGB,ensuring a steady operation of the AORFBs.The diffusion coefficient and the electron-transfer rate constant of NGB were estimated by RDE.The excellent battery performance of $\mathrm { N G B } / \mathrm { K _ { 3 } F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } }$ symmetric AORFB with HP- $\cdot \beta$ -CD demonstrates that NGB exhibits remarkable electrochemical stability.The 0.01 $\mathrm { ~ M ~ N G B / ( S p r ) _ { 2 } V }$ full-battery AORFB with HP $\beta$ -CD demonstrated excellent cycling stability,maintaining its capacity over 8oO cycles at $1 0 \mathrm { m A }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ,and exhibited good current rate performance ranging from 5 to 30 mA $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ．At the same time,the high concentration NGB/ $\mathrm { ( } \mathsf { S p r ) _ { 2 } V }$ AORFB at $0 . 2 ~ \mathrm { ~ M ~ }$ also displayed outstanding battery performance. Therefore,NGB provided multiple alternatives for the research of AORFBs and the potentiality of applying AORFBs to large-scale energy storage.
