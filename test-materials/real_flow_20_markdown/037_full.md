With the rapid expansion of renewable energy,iron-chromium redox flow bateries (ICRFBs) have garnered significant attentiondue totheirlowcost,high safety,and longcycle life.However,the slugish kineticsof the chromium redox couple $( \mathbf { C r ^ { 2 + } } / \mathbf { C r ^ { 3 + } } )$ ,coupled with the pronounced hydrogen evolution side reaction $\mathrm { ( H ^ { + } / H _ { 2 } ) }$ ， severely limit the energy efficiency(EE)and hinder the commercialization of ICRFBs.Inthis study,we present a novel strategy involving the applicationof high-entropy aloys (HEAs)—specifically BiInSnFeMoand BiInSn-FeTi—to modify thermaly treated graphite felt (TGF)electrodes.This approach enables synergistic and balanced regulation of the $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ redox reaction and the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) through multi-component cooperative effects.The HEAs were uniformly immobilized on the TGF surface via a surfactant-assisted solidstate sintering method.Owing to the functional contribution of titanium in BiInSnFeTi,the TGF-T $\mathrm { i } @ \mathrm { H E A } _ { 1 }$ electrode exhibits an optimal balance between enhanced charge transfer for the $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ redox process and effective suppression of HER.As a result, the ICRFB achieves a high energy efficiency of $7 5 \ \%$ after 400 chargedischarge cycles at a current density of $1 2 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ and an operating temperature of $4 0 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ ，These results demonstrate that BiInSnFeTi-modified TGF electrodes offer a promising pathway for advancing the lowtemperature performance and practical application of ICRFBs."

# 1．Introduction

With the continuous increase in global energy demand and growing concerns regarding climate change,the development and utilization of renewable energy sources,such as wind and solar power,have become central to the global energy transition [1,2]. However,these energy sources are typically characterized by strong intermittency and poor controllability,which pose severe challenges to the stable operation of power systems.Therefore,efficient and flexible energy storage technologies are required to ensure their large-scale deployment [3].

Among electrochemical energy storage technologies,lithium-ion batteries (Li-ions),sodium-ion batteries (Na-ions),and redox flow batteries (RFBs) are currently the three most representative systems [4]. Benefiting from a well-established industrial supply chain and continuous cost reductions,Li-ions have emerged as the dominant solution for short- to medium-duration (typically ${ \tt < } 4 { \tt - } 1 0 \mathrm { h } { \tt }$ ）energy storage applications on both the utility and customer sides,owing to their high energy density,excellent round-trip efficiency,and fast response capabilities [5].Na-ions are considered a promising alternative for large-scale, cost-sensitive deployments due to their abundant raw materials,low potential material costs,and favorable safety profile.However, challenges remain in terms of energy density and the technological maturity required for widespread commercialization.At present, they are better suited for medium-duration stationary energy storage where a balanced trade-off between cost and safety is critical.RFBs have attracted considerable attention as promising candidates for large-scale energy storage due to their decoupled energy and power design,scalability,and inherent safety advantages [6-8].Among them,the iron-chromium redox flow battery (ICRFB) has drawn significant interest for its low-cost iron-chromium electrolyte,excellent electrochemical reversibility,and environmental friendliness [9].

Despite these theoretical advantages,the commercialization of ICRFBs still encounters significant challenges,primarily the relatively low energy efciency (EE) caused by slow electrochemical kinetics of negative half-cell reaction [1O,11].The sluggish electrochemical response of the $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ redox couple is primarily attributed to the limited catalytic activity exhibited by conventional graphite felt (GF) electrodes [12-14]. In practical applications,maintaining the battery operating temperatureatapproximately $6 5 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ is commonly required to mitigate the occurrence of chromium aging to some extent [15]. Meanwhile,the hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) tends to occur in highly acidic electrolytes,which further compromises the selectivity and cycling stability of the reaction [16].In practical operation, the thermal management system of an ICRFB has a substantial impact on its performance.Appropriate temperature regulation can effectively accelerate electrochemical reaction rates and improve stability,while also preventing electrolyte decomposition or hydrogen evolution caused by overheating. This extends the service life of the ICRFB and improves its efficiencies [17,18].Hence,enhancing electrode activity and suppressing HER are regarded as key strategies to improve the performance of ICRFBs,particularlyat temperatures below $6 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ [19].

To address these issues,many researchers have attempted to introduce catalysts such as metals nitride (e.g.,TiN [2Ol),alloys (e.g.,Ag-Bi [21]),and carbon-based materials (e.g.，pomelo peel [22]） onto GF electrodes to promote $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ redox kinetics.For example, Xie et al. constructed a $\mathbf { P b } / \mathbf { B i }$ bimetallic electrocatalyst on GF via electrochemical deoxidation,which simultaneously improved $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ kinetics and suppressed HER.The corresponding ICRFB achieved an $1 8 \%$ increase in EEat $1 2 5 \mathrm { m A c m } ^ { - 2 } [ 2 3 ] .$ Niu et al. developed an in situ electrodeposition method by directly introducing $\mathrm { P b } ^ { 2 + }$ into the electrolyte,which effectively enhanced the $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ reaction while inhibiting HER,achieving an EE of $8 3 . 9 0 \%$ at $1 4 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ with stable cycling over 400 cycles [24].Similar strategies employing In catalysts have also shown promising results[25]．Nevertheless， the suppression of HER by single-component catalysts remains limited due to the similar redox potentials of chromium and hydrogen ions [26,27].To address this limitation, Yu et al. proposed improving hydrogen utilization at the negative side of the ICRFB by integrating with iron-hydrogen batteries [28,29].

In recent years,high-entropy alloys (HEAs) and their compounds have received increasing attention in energy storage and catalysis because their multi-element composition introduces high structural disorder and multiple active sites [3O,31].HEAs exhibit tunable electronic structures,strong corrosion resistance,and multivalent active centers,making them well suited for catalyzing complex reactions in strongly acidic media [32,33].Demeku etal. pioneered the application of a hexaelemental high-entropy oxide (BiZrMoWCeLa) $^ { 1 } 2 0 5$ as a catalyst for vanadium redox flow battery (VRFB） electrodes,demonstrating unique catalytic activity.Owing to the synergistic effects of multiple metal sites,the activation energy was significantly reduced, leading to an exchange current density of $8 . 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \mathrm { A c m } ^ { - 2 }$ nearly three orders of magnitude higher than that of traditional carbon materials.At $1 2 0 ~ \mathrm { { m A } }$ $\mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ ，the VRFB employing this catalyst achieved an EE of approximately $8 0 \%$ [34]. Similarly, Tiwari et al. prepared a high-entropy alloy oxide $\mathrm { ( V N b M o T a W O _ { x } ) }$ coating on GF using magnetron sputtering. The uniformlydistributed metal elements formed a stable single-phase solid solution with nanoscale active sites,enabling the VRFB to reach an EE of $7 7 . 2 ~ \%$ at $1 2 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ with a performance degradation rate of only $0 . 2 5 ~ \%$ per cycle after 250 cycles [35].These studies preliminarily confirm the excellent electrocatalytic properties of HEAs and their oxides in VRFBs,indicating their potential application in ICRFBs.

Based on these insights,this paper employs a surfactant-assisted solid-state sintering method to design and synthesize novel HEAs (BiInSnFeMo and BiInSnFeTi),which are uniformly loaded onto the surface of thermally treated graphite felt (TGF) to form TGF@HEA. By simultaneously regulating the $\bar { \mathrm { C r } } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ redox pair and $\mathrm { H } + \mathrm { \Omega } / \mathrm { H } _ { 2 }$ reactions,TGF $\ @ \mathrm { H E A }$ constructs a composite electrode with both catalytic activity and HER inhibition.The morphology,electrochemical behavior, and practical performance of the composite electrodes in ICRFBs were systematically investigated.In particular,the performance of the ICRFB was evaluated under low-temperature conditions $( 4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ ，confirming the outstanding stability and efficiency of the TGF $@$ HEA electrodes in such environments.By revealing the synergistic mechanism of highentropy effects in promoting redox kinetics and suppressing HER, this study provides both theoretical guidance and experimental support for the rational design of advanced electrode materials for RFBs.

# 2.3. Characterization and computation

The morphology of the samples was examined using SEM (JEOL, JEM-780o F),while their elemental composition was analyzed by means of energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS)． XRD (SHIMADZU-7000S) was used for analyzing phase composition and crystal structure. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) (ThermoVG,EscaLab 250) was employed to evaluate the binding energies between atoms on the material surface.

The DFT calculations presented in this study were carried out using plane-wave methods implemented in the VASP software package (Version 5.4.4).As illustrated in Figure S1,the thermodynamically stable and commonly adopted low-index surfaces—graphite C (001), body-centered cubic Mo (11O),and hexagonal close-packed Ti(101)— were selected as model substrates.To ensure comparable exposed surface areas and maintain low coverage of hydrogen adsorption (one adsorbate per supercell, $\leq 0 . 0 5 \mathrm { M L }$ ， $5 \times 5 \times 1$ supercells were utilized for the construction of the C(Ool) and Mo (11O) slabs,while a $2 \times 4 \times 1$ supercell was employed for Ti (1o1). Each slab comprised 4-6 at.layers, with the bottom two layers fixed to simulate bulk-like constraints and the upper layers fully relaxed during structural optimization.A vacuum layer of at least $1 5 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ was incorporated along the surface normal direction to eliminate unphysical inter-slab interactions.The adsorption energy is defined as $\mathrm { E _ { a d s } = E _ { t o t a l } - E _ { s u b s t r a t e } - E _ { c l u s t e r } , }$ where $\mathrm { E _ { t o t a l } }$ denotes the total energy of the system,Esubstrate represents the energy of the substrate surface,and Ecluster corresponds to the energy of the adsorbate. Simultaneously, based on the optimized stable configuration,the density of states (DOS)and charge-density-diference (CDD) are calculated to conduct an in-depth analysis of the system's electronic structure characteristics and charge transfer behavior.All geometric structures are visualized and post-processed using VESTA software.

# 3.3.Side reaction

In Fig.5a, the LSV curves of the samples further illustrate the influence of HEAs on the HER.The results show that the hydrogen evolution potentials of HEA-modified TGF electrodes are all lower than that of pristine TGF,suggesting that HEAs also exhibit catalytic activity toward the HER.Among them，TGF- $\mathbf { \cdot M o @ H E A _ { 1 } }$ and TGF $\mathbf { \cdot M o @ H E A _ { 2 } }$ display lower hydrogen evolution potentials compared with TGF-$\mathrm { T i } @ \mathrm { H E A _ { 1 } }$ and TGF-T $\begin{array} { r } { \mathrm { i } @ \mathrm { H E A } _ { 2 } , } \end{array}$ whereas $\mathrm { T G F - T i @ H E A _ { 1 } }$ and TGF-$\mathrm { T i } @ \mathrm { H E A _ { 2 } }$ exhibit potentials closer to those of TGF,particularly in the case of TGF- $\mathrm { T i } @ \mathrm { H E A _ { 2 } }$ .This trend is consistent with findings reported in the literature,which indicate that pristine $\mathbf { M o O } _ { 2 }$ exhibits intrinsic HER activity.Furthermore,studies have demonstrated that vanadium (V) doping reduces its HER performance, suggesting that modulation of the electronic structure around Mo sites can significantly influence HER behavior [51].To further clarify the roles of Mo and Ti in regulating the HER,the calculated hydrogen adsorption energies on C,Mo,and Ti surfaces are shown in Fig.5b.The results indicate an adsorption strength order of $\mathrm { T i } ( 1 0 1 ) > \mathrm { M o } ( 1 1 0 ) > \mathrm { C } ( 0 0 1 )$ ，suggesting that Ti exhibits stronger hydrogen atom adsorption and a higher hydrogen evolution barrier,thereby effectively suppressing the HER[52].Inaddition,In and Sn in $\mathrm { T i } @ \mathrm { H E A }$ modulate the adsorption energy of hydrogen ions, weakening their adsorption strength on the alloy surface.These metals also present relatively high HER potential barriers,making the HER less favorable [25]. Meanwhile, Bi and Fe in Ti@HEA serve as the primary active sites for catalyzing the $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ redox reaction, as they can modulate the D-band electronic structure and thus enhance the redox activity[53,54]． Furthermore， theDOSandCDDprovide electronic-structure evidence for Ti and Mo as shown in Figure S10. Figure S1Oa illustrates a substantially reduced DOS near the Fermi level $\left( \mathrm { E _ { F } } \right)$ on the C (Ool) surface,with the density of states $\mathrm { { N } ( E _ { F } ) }$ approaching zero,indicating non-metallic behavior.The limited availability of electronic states for bonding and antibonding interactions restricts the capacity of C (oo1) surface for electron donation and acceptance toward hydrogen, leading to the weak hydrogen adsorption and a high energy barrier for the Volmer step.In contrast,Figure SlOb reveals that the Mo (110) surface displays a prominent DOS peak near $\mathrm { E _ { F } }$ ,witha higher N $\left( \mathrm { E _ { F } } \right)$ and Mo-d states closely aligned to $\mathrm { E _ { F } }$ .This electronic structure facilitates near-thermoneutral hydrogen adsorption and promotes synergistic electron transfer and adsorption-desorption processes,thereby readily initiating HER,which is consistent with the recognized intrinsic HER activity of Mo-based oxides [51].As illustrated in Figure S1Oc, the Ti (101） surface exhibits a pronounced pseudogap near $\mathrm { E _ { F } } .$ ，witha significantly lower $\mathrm { { N } ( E _ { F } ) }$ compared to Mo and a downshifted D-band center $( \varepsilon _ { \mathrm { { d } } } )$ .This electronic configuration results in reduced occupancy of antibonding states and facilitates stronger,more stable H-Ti bonding.

While hydrogen adsorption is enhanced,the kinetic barriers associated with the Heyrovsky and Tafel steps are increased, thereby more effectively suppressing the HER.The adsorption strength sequence derived from DOS analysis follows the trend ' $\Gamma \mathrm { i } \ ( 1 0 1 ) > \mathbf { M } \circ ( 1 1 0 ) > \mathbf { C }$ (001), which is consistent with the hydrogen ion adsorption energy calculations presented Fig.5b.

Such electronic distribution patterns are widely recognized as characteristic signatures of suppressed HER activity.

In summary,Ti serves as an "electronic structure regulator" in HEA by lowering the D-band center $( \varepsilon _ { \mathrm { { d } } } )$ and reducing the $\mathrm { { N } ( E _ { F } ) }$ ，thereby partially passivating the HER activity of Bi and Fe.Concurrently,the incorporation of In and Sn induces charge redistribution,which further attenuates the adsorption strength of hydrogen intermediates.As a result,an optimal balance between suppression of HER and facilitation of $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ charge transfer is achieved, as ilustrated in Fig.5c. # 4.Conclusion

This study employed a surfactant-assisted solid-state sintering method to synthesize novel HEAs modified TGF electrodes applied in ICRFBs,specifically BiInSnFeMo@TGFand BiInSnFeTi@TGF.Structural characterization confirmed the formation of nanostructured HEA particles exhibiting a multiscale disordered architecture on the TGF surface, demonstrating multimetallic synergistic effects and multivalent electronic states.Mechanistic analysis further revealed that Bi and Fe in BiInSnFeTi primarily enhanced the $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ redox kinetics, while In, Sn,and Ti in BiInSnFeTi effectively suppressed HER.Notably，Ti contributed to improved catalytic activity and selectivity，thereby enabling BiInSnFeTi $@$ TGF to achieve an optimal balance in the negative half-cell of the ICRFB.Therefore,the ICRFB developed in this study demonstrated efficient operation at a relatively low temperature of $4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ ,while maintaining excellent EE and cycling stability.At a current density of $1 2 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ ,the ICRFB employing BiInSnFeTi $@$ TGF as the negative electrode exhibited an EE of $7 5 \ \%$ after 400 charge-discharge cycles,significantly surpassing the performance of both the unmodified TGF and other HEA-modified electrodes.These findings indicate that BiInSnFeTi@TGF effectively balances the suppression of hydrogen evolution with the preservation of $\mathrm { C r } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { C r } ^ { 3 + }$ redox activity at $4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ Overall, this study offers novel insights into the application of HEAs as electrodes,providing both theoretical foundations and experimental evidence to support the enhancement of ICRFB performance and its commercial viability. Future research should aim at further optimizing the composition and microstructure of HEAs to explore their broader applicability and engineering potential in RFBs.Furthermore, the proposed HEA modification strategy is built upon a controllable solid-phase sintering process and cost-effective precursors,offering high technical feasibilityand reproducibility,thereby enabling large-scale electrode fabrication. The exceptional structural stability and tunable composition further present a promising pathway for the industrial application of electrodes in RFBs.
