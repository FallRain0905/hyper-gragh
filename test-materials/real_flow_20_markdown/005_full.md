# 1．Introduction

The global shift towards sustainable energy systems requires robust and scalable energy storage solutions to effectively integrate intermittentrenewable sources such as solar and wind power into the electrical grid.Redox flow batteries (RFBs) have emerged as a promising technology for large-scale energy storage systems due to their design flexibility and inherent safety features,which arise from the physical separation of active materials in external tanks from the powergenerating cell stack [1-3].This modularity allows for easy scalability to meet the demands of grid-level applications [4].

Among the various RFB options,aqueous organic redox flow batteries (AORFBs) stand out as an innovative and increasingly appealing choice.These systems utilize organic species as active materials dissolved in aqueous electrolytes,offering distinct advantages over traditional metal-based systems,such as all-vanadium RFBs.Organic active materials can be synthesized from abundant elements like carbon, oxygen,nitrogen, and sulfur,enhancing sustainability and reducing dependence on critical raw materials.Additionally, employing water as a solvent ensures environmental friendliness,non-flammability,and high ionic conductivity,which can lead to improved safety and potentially lower system costs.However,despite these compelling advantages,the widespread commercialization of AORFBs faces significant challenges.One key issue is the relatively low energy density,often constrained by the aqueous solubility of the organic active materials. More critically,the chemical and electrochemical stability of organic molecules in aqueous environments remains a substantial hurdle. Organic active species can degrade through various mechanisms, including nucleophilic reactions,disproportionation,dimerization,and tautomerization,influenced by pH,which can lead to irreversible capacity fade over time[5-8].Another major challenge is the unwanted transport of redox-active components,known as crossover, through the ion-selective membrane.This phenomenon directly contributes to capacity decay and limits battery lifetime [9,10].

While organic materials present potential cost advantages, achieving cost competitiveness with established technologies like vanadium RFBs necessitates materials that not only demonstrate high solubility and stability but also possess high open-circuit voltages (greater than 1.1 V) and reasonable round-trip efficiencies (over $7 1 \%$ ).Current research efforts in AORFB development focus on molecular engineering strategies to address these challenges.State-of-the-art organic active materials encompass various classes, including quinones (e.g., benzoquinone and anthraquinone derivatives like BQDs,AQDS,and DHDMBS), viologens, aza-aromatics (phenazines and phenothiazines),TEMPO derivatives, and iron coordination complexes [11-17].Each class has its own distinct advantages and disadvantages regarding redox potential,aqueous solubility,and chemical stability.For instance,TEMPO derivatives are recognized for their high intrinsic power densities,whereas phenazine derivatives show promise for high solubility and excellent capacity retention [17]. Some quinone-based electrolytes,such as bislawsone, have demonstrated an open-circuit voltage of $1 . 0 5 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ alongside a high power density [18,19].Researchers are actively exploring molecular design principles—such as incorporating hydrophilic functional groups, extending $\pi$ conjugation， breaking molecular symmetry， facilitating multi-electron transfer，and polymerization—to improve solubility, stability,and overall performance [2O-23].The ability to tune the chemical and electrochemical properties of organic molecules through structural modifications is a significant advantage that enables the targeted design of solutions to the identified limitations in AORFB development. This approach turns challenges into opportunities, facilitating the strategic engineering of active materials.The functionalization of the naphthoquinone core plays a critical role in its chemical stability. Unmodified quinones are known to undergo rapid decomposition in alkaline electrolytes (1.0 M KOH) primarily through nucleophilic Michael addition of hydroxide ions $\left( \mathrm { O H } ^ { - } \right)$ to the unsubstituted carbon positions.While various stabilization strategies including the incorporation of zwitterionic amino acids,branched alkyl chains,intramolecular hydrogen bonding,or micellization have been explored, these approaches often entail trade-offs such as complex multi-step synthesis, reduced aqueous solubility,or compromised ionic conductivity [24-26].

This work introduces the rational design and characterization of a di (hydroxyethyl)amino-functionalized organic molecule,2-bis(hydroxyethyl)amino naphthoquinone (HEANQ)，as a robust anolyte for aqueous organic redox flow batteries (AORFBs).The functionalization strategy was specifically selected to strike a practical balance between chemical stability and synthetic scalability.The bulky di(hydroxyethyl) amino group $\mathrm { ( - N ( C H _ { 2 } C H _ { 2 } O H ) _ { 2 } ) }$ provides essential steric protection against nucleophilic addition-elimination reactions,and the pendant hydroxyl moieties ensure high aqueous solubility in alkaline media without the need for solubilizing counter-ions.This approach establishes HEANQ as a viable alternative to synthetically demanding analogues by offering both stable cycling performance and favorable reaction kinetics. Torigorously validate this molecular design,a comprehensive suite of computational and experimental techniques was employed. These methodologies include Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations [27] for theoretical insights as well as structural confirmation via proton and carbon nuclear magnetic resonance( $\mathrm { ^ { - 1 } H }$ and $^ { 1 3 } \mathrm { C }$ NMR),Fourier transform-infraredspectroscopy(FT-IR)，andultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) spectroscopy.Additionally,detailed electrochemical analysis was performed using cyclic voltammetry (CV),electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS),and rotating disk electrode (RDE) [28].The study concludes with an assessment of the practical performance and long-term stability of HEANQ in a full AORFB cell to highlight its potential as an efficient active material for next-generation energy storage systems.

# 2.1． Synthesis of HEANQ

HEANQ was synthesized through a series of reactions.First, 2-bromo-1,4-naphthoquinone $( 3 . 0 8 \mathrm { ~ \small ~ \displaystyle ~ g ~ } , 0 . 0 1 3 \mathrm { ~ \small ~ \ m o l ~ }$ ， $2 9 8 \%$ ，Tokyo Chemical Industry,Tokyo, Japan) was added to liquid diethanolamine $( 1 5 ~ \mathrm { g } , 0 . 1 3 ~ \mathrm { m o l }$ ， $2 9 8 \%$ ,Sigma-Aldrich,St.Louis,MO,USA) in a prepared round-bottom flask.Ethanol ( $5 0 ~ \mathrm { m L }$ ， $\geq 9 9 . 8 \%$ ,Merck,Darmstadt, Germany）was then added as the solvent.The flask,containing the mixture,was purged with argon gas.The solution was stirred at $5 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 8 \mathrm { h } }$ to facilitate the nucleophilic substitution reaction,during which the nucleophilic nitrogen atom from diethanolamine attacked the electrophilic carbon bonded to the bromine atom in 2-bromo-1,4-naphthoquinone (2-BrNQ).After the reaction, ethanol was removed using a rotary evaporator (R-3oo Rotavapor,BUCHI,Switzerland） for $^ { 1 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ Methylene chloride $( \geq 9 9 . 9 \%$ , Sigma-Aldrich,St.Louis,MO,USA) and deionized water were added to the resulting mixture to separate it into two liquid layers in a separatory funnel. The remaining solution was then dried in a vacuum oven for $^ { 1 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ to eliminate any residual solvent.The final product,HEANQ,was obtained as a dark yellow powder with a total isolated yield of $7 8 . 5 3 \%$ . All synthesis steps were carried out with appropriate safety precautions for handling chemicals.The synthesis scheme is illustrated in Fig.1.

3.1.DFTcalculation for molecular structure and characteristics

The electrostatic potential (ESP) map of the optimized molecular structure is depicted in Fig.2(a).Additionally,the dipole moment shown in Fig.2(b) and the molecular polarizability in Fig.2(c) were computed to elucidate the molecular stability and solubility characteristics.The dipole moment of HEANQ is 2.07221 Debye as detailed in Table 1, which is 1.5 times higher than that of NQ.This increase is attributed to the nitrogen atom in di(hydroxyethyl)amino group,which strongly pushes electrons toward the naphthoquinone ring through an intramolecular inductive effect.The resulting local charge separation alters the charge distribution within the quinone molecule, thereby increasing the dipole moment size [43]. Additionally,the molecular polarizability of HEANQ is 183.0972 a.u.,which is 1.70 times higher than that of NQ. Fig.8(c) displays the CE,VE,and EE over 2OO cycles,showing no visible turbulence in the overall efficiencies.This evidence supports the use of HEANQ as a stable and reversible active material for AORFBs.As shown in Fig.8(d),the average CE,VE,and EE over 20o cycles were $9 7 . 7 4 \%$ ， $8 1 . 5 9 \%$ ,and $7 9 . 7 5 \%$ ,respectively.WhileHEANQ maintains a robust EE of nearly $8 0 \%$ ,lawsone suffers from compromised suffers from severely compromised CE and EE of under 60 and $4 7 \%$ ,from the very onset of cycling due to irreversible parasitic side reactions.These results mean that the adoption of HEANQ (functionalized with di(hydroxyethyl) amino functional group) successfully provide stabilization effect protecting naphthoquinone core from decomposition.

To rigorously ascertain the chemical stability of HEANQ during longterm operation,a comprehensive post-mortem analysis was conducted on the electrolyte after 2Oo charge-discharge cycles.Fig.1O presents the spectroscopic and electrochemical comparison of the electrolyte before cycling and the electrolyte recovered from the anolyte reservoir after cycling.Fig.10(a) and (b) display the $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ NMR spectra of the HEANQ electrolyte before and after cycling.The characteristic signals assigned to the naphthoquinone backbone protons remain well-defined and chemically shifted at their original positions because of solvation effect. Furthermore,the targeted aromatic and quinoid signals are associated in the 6.5-8.O ppm region and show no broadening or intensity loss.The absence of any new impurity peaks confirms that the molecular structure of HEANQ remained intact without undergoing side reactions during the continuous oxidative and reductive stress of cycling. This structural integrity is further corroborated by the UV-Vis spectroscopic data shown in Fig.1O(c).The absorption spectrum of the electrolyte after cycling perfectly overlaps with that of the electrolyte before cycling.The retention of the characteristic absorption bands indicates that the conjugated $\pi$ -system of the naphthoquinone core was preserved. There is no evidence of chromophore destruction or the formation of irreversible byproducts that would alter the optical properties of the solution. The electrochemical reversibility of the species after cycling was evaluated using CV as shown in Fig.1O(d).The voltammogram of the electrolyte after cycling retains the characteristic redox signature of the fresh anolyte including well-defined oxidation and reduction peaks.

This nucleophilic ligand exchange not only releases free cyanide ions but also leads to the irreversible precipitation ofiron hydroxides,causing significant capacity decay on the catholyte side [6].This chemical degradation is further accelerated by photochemical decomposition upon prolonged exposure to ultraviolet or visible light [54,55].To elucidate the mechanism behind this remarkable stability,Fig.1O(e) illustrates the proposed degradation pathway for lawsone alongside the protective stabilization mechanism of HEANQ.In practical alkaline environments,trace oxygen can generate highly reactive hydroperoxide anions (HOO-),which typically attack the highly electrophilic C-3 position of unsubstituted naphthoquinones or lawsone.This initiates an irreversible epoxidation and subsequent nucleophilic addition by hydroxide ions (OH-),ultimately destroying the redox-active core.In contrast, the spatial volume of the secondary amine branches of HEANQ creates an immense steric shield directly over the vulnerable C-3 carbon,physically obstructing the approach of bulky nucleophiles like HOO-[19].Also,the electron-donating nitrogen atom delocalizes its lone-pair electrons into the quinone $\pi$ -system, creating a region of exceptionally high electron density at the C-3 position.This electronic saturation inherently deactivates the electrophilicity of the double bond, thermodynamically repelling nucleophilic attacks [13].

While many organic moleculederivatives have shown promise, achieving high round-trip efficiency，power density，and long-term stability simultaneously has remained a challnge. For instance, optimizedanthraquinone-basedmoleculeslikeanthraquinone-2,7- disulfonic acid (AQDS) and 2,6-dihydroxy-1,4-anthraquinone (2,6- DHAQ) exhibit exceptional rate capabilities,typically maintaining EE between $6 0 \%$ and $8 2 \%$ [14,15]. However,achieving long-term stability with these unhindered structures is highly problematic.2,6-DHAQ, for example, suffers from a rapid capacity fade rate of $0 . 3 9 \%$ per cycle, which is 7.8 times higher than that of HEANQ.Regarding the performance of other naphthoquinone derivatives,2-hydroxy-1,4-naphthoquinone (lawsone) is highly vulnerable to nucleophilic conjugate addition. Consequently, unmodified lawsone can sustain only about 15 cycles, suffering asevere capacity fade of $5 0 \%$ per cycle accompanied by exceptionally low CE and EE as mentioned above [13,24,56]. Other naphthoquinone derivatives,such as 2-hydroxy-3-carboxy-1,4-naphthoquinone (2,3-HCNQ) and 3-(2-chloro-1,4-naphthoquinon-3-ylamino)propanoic acid (3-AFNQ),exhibit relatively low EE compared to HEANQ [57,58]. In contrast, highly engineered molecules like benzoic acid-functionalized NQ (BANQ) and bislawsone (a naphthoquinone dimer） exhibit exceptional capacity fade rates of $0 . 0 0 2 8 \%$ and $0 . 0 0 0 1 8 \%$ per cycle, respectively,alongside high EEs of $7 7 \%$ and $8 2 \%$ ,even at high current densities exceeding $1 0 0 \mathrm { \ m A \ c m ^ { - 2 } }$ Meanwhile,unhindered benzoquinone (BQ) systems, such as 2,5-dihydroxy-1,4-benzoquinone (DHBQ), also undergo rapid capacity fading (approximately $0 . 2 4 \%$ per cycle) due to chemical instability [12]. While attempts to stabilize BQs via polymerization (poly(BQ)) successully halt membrane crossover, the resulting massive increase in ohmic resistance causes their EE to plummet below $4 0 \%$ ，Synthetically，employing HEANQ as an anodic species, the AORFB can demonstrate a unique combination of high stability driven byits rational functional groups and suitable redox potential along with a suppressed capacity fade rate. This is owing to its ability to suppress the degradation pathways seen in lawsone and BQs,allowing it to maintain enhanced stability without requiring additional additives or stabilization methods. This robustness is a critical characteristic for grid-scale applications,positioning HEANQ as a competitive alternative to traditional metal-based RFBs particularly regarding sustainability and efficiency. However,the practical implementation of HEANQ still requires improvements in low rate capability and higher concentration application.Additionally,the susceptibility of the ${ \mathrm { K } } _ { 4 } { \mathrm { F e } } ( { \mathrm { C N } } ) _ { 6 }$ catholyte to structural degradation must be considered. Ferrocyanide is prone to photochemical decomposition upon prolonged exposure to ultraviolet or visible light,which can lead to the release of free cyanide ions and subsequent capacity decay on the catholyte side. These operationallimitations can be addressed in the future by adopting suitable selection of catholyte counterparts and refining overall cell

architecture [59].

# 4.Conclusions

This paper presents a novel 2-bis(dihydroxyethyl)amino naphthoquinone derivative (HEANQ, $\mathrm { C _ { 1 4 } H _ { 1 5 } O _ { 4 } N ) }$ featuring a di(hydroxyethyl)amino group,which undergoes a highly stable and reversible two-electron redox reaction as a soluble organic active species for aqueous redox flow batteries.A key highlight of this study is the incorporation of the electron-donating and highly hydrophilic di (hydroxyethyl)amino functional group onto the naphthoquinone backbone,which enhances hydrogen bonding and solvation effects in aqueous media.

To confirm the molecule's identity and purity,we conducted structural elucidation of the proposed HEANQ using FT-IR, $^ 1 \mathrm { H }$ ， $^ { 1 3 } \mathrm { C }$ NMR, and UV-Vis spectroscopy.DFT calculations further provided accurate predictions of its electronic structure and redox properties.Additionally, the enhanced chemical and electrochemical stability of HEANQ was demonstrated through CV,RDE,and EIS tests in $\boldsymbol { 1 . 0 } \mathbf { M }$ KOH.These tests indicate that the di(hydroxyethyl)amino functional group attached to the naphthoquinone contributes to highly reversible and stable redox behavior,rapid electron transfer kinetics,and low internal resistance. Importantly,cycling tests of the $\mathrm { H E A N Q / K _ { 4 } F e ( C N ) _ { 6 } }$ flow cell demonstrated significant stability and reversibility,achieving an average coulombic efficiency of $9 7 . 7 4 \%$ over 2OO cycles and retaining areliable $9 3 . 6 4 \%$ of the initial discharge capacity by the 2Ooth cycle.These results suggest that the introduction of the di(hydroxyethyl)amino functional group into the naphthoquinone facilitates the redox reaction pathway, overcoming the inherent stability and reversibility limitations of hydrophobic naphthoquinone in aqueous solutions. Consequently,this study offers new insights into naphthoquinone as an organic active material for AORFBs,highlighting its high performance and potential for long-term operation.
