ABSTRACT: The redox flow battery (RFB) is one of the most promising systems for large scale electrochemical energy storage applications. The development of redox-active materials is an essential part of RFB research. Commercial RFBs utilize redox-active inorganic ions, which have several issues such as expensive and toxic active materials, crossover of redox species,and high cost of the ion exchange membrane. The incorporation of redox-active polymeric materials is an intriguing solution because low-cost polymeric redox-active materials and size-exclusion porous membranes formed by commodity polymers could be applied to replace expensive inorganic redox-active materials and ion exchange membranes,respectively. The development of polymer-based redoxactive materials in RFB application (PRFB) has advanced rapidly in the past few years. In this review, the recent progress of PRFB is summarized. Three major categories based on materials are discussed: the aqueous redox-active polymers, the nonaqueous redox-active polymers and oligomers，and polymer suspension systems.This review not only provides comprehensiveinformationonsytheticstrategofpolymericredox-activematerialsandtheirpropertiessuchasredox potentialand solubilityinelectrolytebutalsoreports theperformanceofrecentPRFBcels，includingcyclingstability，cellvoltage，and implementation of size-exclusion porous membranes. Finaly,a short future perspective of PRFB development is provided.

1.1.Overview of Redox Flow Batteries. The fast growing renewable energy technology in the past few decades accelerated the study of large-scale electricity energy storage technology. The currently dominant large scale electricity storage technique, pump hydroelectric energy storage (PHES), consists of $9 0 \%$ grid storage capacity.1 However, the capitol cost required for PHES construction is very high,and the system is largely restricted by geographical conditions. The development of renewable energy techniques requires a more flexible， cost-effective solution for grid energy storage. Due to this main feature,RFB can be easily sized for different grid storage applications.It shares similar feature as fuel cells where the redox-active materials are also stored externally. Therefore, the device capacity of RFB is controlled by the volume of storage vessels for redox-active material solution. On the other hand,RFB has the same characteristics as batteries as the electrochemical reactions of the redox-active materials are reversible, rendering them as essentially regenerable fuel cells. With the development of organic-material-based RFB and the driving force of further reducing the cost of the cell, the polymeric redox-active material-based RFB (PRFB） was proposed. In inorganic materials and small organic moleculebased RFBs，the crossover of redox-active materials isa significant issue,which reduces the coulombic efficiency (CE) of the cell and leads to capacity degradation during long-term cycling.53 Furthermore, the contamination of the separator also results in the increase of internal resistance within the cell.17

To mitigate the crossover issue in RFB, modification of the ion exchange membrane is often necessary.54 However，the commercial IEMs such as Nafion, Fumasep，and Selemion comprise a relatively high portion of the cell stack cost, impeding the scalability of RFBs.55 Therefore, the incorporation of polymeric redox-active materials is an intriguing solution for the RFB community because a low-cost sizeexclusion porous membrane formed by commodity polymers such as polypropylene，polyethylene，and cellulose acetate could be potentially applied to replace expensive IEMs.A list of commercially available membranes is summarized in Supporting Information Table S1. From the table， the commonly used proton exchange membranes such as Nafion $\left( \$ 500 /\mathrm { m } ^ { 2 } \right)$ are at least five times more expensive than dialysis membranes $\left( { \$ 20 - 100/ \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 } } \right)$ and 25 times more expensive than porous membranes $\left( { \$ 20 } / \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 } \right)$ . PRFB provides an option to build flow batteries with cost-effective porous membranes. However,as summarized in the later sections,there are still great challenges for the PRFB system. Several issues,such as sluggish redox kinetics of polymeric materials,high viscosity of electrolytes,and limited solubility，need to be addressed by creative design of new redox-active polymers.

A number of comprehensivereviews2,56-6lhave been published recently to categorize the development of redox materials in RFBs.In addition to the reviews focusing on the materials，review reports were published for the engineering aspects of the RFB，including cell/stack design and osri pilot-scale RFB construction,65and membrane design.66-70 However, there is no comprehensive review for PRFB up to the present. This review overviews the recent progress of PRFB, including aqueous-,nonaqueous-,and suspension-media-based PRFBs.A short future perspective of PRFB is also provided.

# 2.1.Development of Polymer-Based Aqueous RFB.

Redox-active polymers as the active material in energy storage devices have been explored by the electrochemical society. Conjugated polymers (poly(pyrrole),71,72 poly(thiophene), 73 poly(aniline),7etc.）doncojugated plmers (bonl group-based polymers,76-78 organosulfur-based polymers,79,80 etc.）have been investigated for solid-state polymer batteries fora few decades.Researchers have proven that redox-active polymers could possess good electrochemical and chemical stability as well as tunable structures to engineer critical parameters such as redox potential and solubility. Because the commercial RFBs are all based on the aqueous supporting electrolyte，water-soluble redox-active polymer is favored for RFB application. The earliest concept of water-soluble redoxactive polymer was reported during the study of electrically conducting polymers.A thiophene-based polymer with a sulfonate functional group was synthesized and investigated by Heeger in 1987.81 This polymer is soluble in concentrated sulfuric acid and exhibited bulky conductivity between $1 0 ^ { - 7 }$ and $1 0 ^ { - 2 } \mathrm { S } / \mathrm { c m }$ .However, the main research focus on its redox behavior was concentrated on solid state materials rather than corresponding solution.23 Although there were some reports regarding water-soluble redox-active polymers in the last few decades，only in recent years have researchers incorporated those polymers for RFB application.

# 3.CONCLUSION AND PERSPECTIVE

The crossover of redox-active materials in RFB triggered the research of polymeric redox-active materials for RFB application. Replacing the expansive ion exchange membrane with cost-effective size-exclusion membrane or even porous separator is attractive for RFBs which are designed for large scale grid energy storage systems. Over the past several years, the development of redox-active polymers for flow battery advanced rapidly. Table S2 summarized the properties of reported redox-active polymers,including molecular weight, solubility，specific capacity，redox potential，viscosity，and diffusion coefficient. Table S3 reported the testing results of polymer-based RFBs.The tables were arranged to follow the category of aqueous polymer， nonaqueous polymer， and polymer suspension-based systems.For aqueous PRFB,the specific capacity(solubility）of redox-active materials in supporting electrolyte was improved significantly in several recent reports.As water is the most economical and environmental friendly solvent for RFB，the potential of aqueous PRFB is significant. In addition， recent studies on aqueous PRFBs also greatly improved their cycling performance.The polymer suspension-based cells exhibited the best cycling performance in PRFBs.However, the current density used in polymer suspension-based RFBs was significantly smaller than that in other RFBs.Nonaqueous PRFBs had the highest cell voltage;however, it is still challenging to achieve long cycle life in nonaqueous flow cell with the reported redoxactive polymers.

Flow batteries based on the polymeric redox-active materials and size-exclusion membrane have been demonstrated,and some systems showed promising cycling performance. However,compared to inorganic RFBs or even small organic redox molecule-based RFBs,the development of PRFB is still in its infancy.A comparison of representative polymer-based RFB and small organic molecule-based RFB is plotted in Figures S1 and S2 in terms of energy density and specific capacity，respectively. There are several issues on current PRFBs which need to be addressed with significant efforts: (I) Incorporation of novel redox center in polymers.Most reported polymeric redox-active materials were based on a few limited redox-active centers such as viologen, TEMPO, and ferrocene.With the successful validation of polymer RFB and size exclusion membrane concept, the future work could focus on introducing a well-designed, stable redox center in the polymer to construct a flow battery with high cell voltage and long cycle life. The first generation of redox centers such as

TEMPO,viologen， ferrocene,and various conjugated molecules are either sensitive to the environmental conditions or prone to side reactions during the cycle. As_a result, many reports of PRFBs used a static cell to evaluate the cycle performance, and the capacity fading of cells was significant under flow conditions.More robust redox centers are required to provide the desired performance of polymer RFBs. (II) The solubility of polymeric redox-active materials in electrolyte needs to be significantly improved. Many redox-active polymers have achieved high solubility in pure solvent. However， the salt-out effect is more challenging in polymer solution as compared to the case of small organic molecules. High solubility of redox-active materials in supporting electrolyte is crucial for PRFBs to reach high specific capacity $\left( \mathrm { A h / L } \right)$ . Compared with small organic molecule-based RFBs, where the specific capacity can easily exceed $2 0 \ \mathrm { A h / L } ,$ the polymer-based RFBs typically have specific capacity under 10 $\operatorname { A h } / \operatorname { L }$ .It is worth pointing out that a recent report of a zwitterionic polymer showed that it reached exceptional specific capacity of $2 0 ~ \mathrm { A h / L }$ in supporting electrolyte.86 It is therefore promising to improve the solubility of polymers in electrolyte by introducing new chemistry. As the solubility of polymers can be largely affected by their molecular weight, controlled polymerization methodssuch as living polymer-ization may provide a viable way to achieve these new redoxactive polymers. (III） Mechanistic study of polymer-based RFBs is required for the design of polymeric redox-active materials. A fact of many PRFBs is the low current density applied in the cell，which is_critical for materials utilization during the flow-state of the cell To improve the power density of PRFBs， a better understanding of charge transport mechanism in the cell is pivotal. However, the charge transport mechanism of the polymer in supporting electrolyte is complicated. Most redox-active polymers are nonconjugated to separate the redox center and obtain unique redox potential. The charge transport theory of conjugated polymers， which received significant development in the past few decades, could not be applied on nonconjugated polymers. Novel models of charge transport of nonconjugated redox-active polymers in electrolyte need to be developed. A recent work20has first provided a new diffusion-cooperative model to describe the charge transport in those cases.However, significant advances in theory,characterization,and experiments are still required for PRFB research.On the other hand, the dynamics of polymer molecules in electrolyte is intriguing but not welldesrihed in the literature.esneriallv fnr the rases where hnth redox-active polymer and supporting electrolyte salt are under high concentration. Recent work53,94 on modulation of the electrochemical reactivity of redox-active polymer through polyelectrolyte dynamics shed light upon such research (Figure 12).Advances of characterization work for redox-active polymers in electrolyte solutionlo3 are pivotal to understand the polymer RFB system and provide rational design of new redox-active polymers.92 (IV) Although the PRFBs are stil in their early stage,reports for polymer RFB devices concerning operation prameters such as viscosity,21 electrode design,12 and flow rate123 1 have emerged. Those works will become more significant with the successful development of polymeric redoxactive materials. Overall, with the development of redox-active polymers, the PRFB systems could provide an opportunity to develop future robust and low-cost RFBs for grid energy storage.
